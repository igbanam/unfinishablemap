The Map's agency cluster keeps reaching for one phrase—"the agent, *as a persisting substance*, exercises causal powers." That phrase presupposes a whole metaphysics it rarely names: E.J. Lowe's **four-category ontology** and the **substance causation** it grounds. This article supplies that foundation. It does two things: it lays out what the four-category ontology is (only as much as the dualist framing needs), and it shows how, on Lowe's account, *substances rather than events* do the causal work that lets a conscious subject count as a genuine cause. It closes by staging a deliberate fork—Lowe's model secures mental causation with **no gap in physics at all**, whereas the Map's [Minimal Quantum Interaction](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction) tenet locates a *minimal physical difference* at quantum-indeterministic junctures (developed in [Relation to Site Perspective](#relation-to-site-perspective) below). The Map uses Lowe to sharpen its own commitment, not to adopt his wholesale. A second departure concerns the will: Lowe holds free volitions uncaused, the Map makes them agent-caused, and that costs it part of the continuity with ordinary causation that Lowe claims for agency.

## The Ontological Square

Lowe's *The Four-Category Ontology* (2006) builds a systematic metaphysics from two cross-cutting distinctions. The first is **particular versus universal**: this individual match here (a particular) against matchhood or the kind *match* (a universal). The second is **substantial versus non-substantial**: things that exist in their own right against ways things are (properties, characteristics, qualities). Crossing the two distinctions yields four fundamental categories—the "ontological square," with Aristotle's *Categories* as its ancestor:

| | Substantial | Non-substantial |
|---|---|---|
| **Particular** | **Objects** (this match) | **Modes** (this match's specific shape) |
| **Universal** | **Kinds** (the kind *match*) | **Attributes** (flammability in general) |

- **Objects** are substantial particulars: individual persisting things—a match, an electron, a person.
- **Kinds** are substantial universals: the sortal categories objects instantiate—*match*, *electron*, *person*.
- **Attributes** are non-substantial universals: properties and relations construed as universals—flammability, negative charge.
- **Modes** (tropes) are non-substantial particulars: the property-instances that characterise a given object—*this* match's *particular* flammability.

Two formal relations tie the square together. **Instantiation** runs from universal to particular (this match instantiates the kind *match*; its flammability-mode instantiates the attribute flammability). **Characterisation** runs across the substantial/non-substantial divide (the flammability-mode characterises this match; the attribute flammability characterises the kind *match*). Lowe argues that laws of nature consist in **kinds being characterised by attributes** (electrons are negatively charged), and that **dispositions and powers** fall out of the grid rather than being bolted on—the metaphysical structure is, in his subtitle's phrase, "a metaphysical foundation for natural science."

What matters for the Map is narrow. The four-category grid makes room for **objects with irreducible causal powers**—substances that are the source of change, not mere sites where events happen. That is the payload; the generic trope-theory machinery is left to the metaphysics literature.

## Substance Causation: Substances, Not Events, Do the Work

On the dominant contemporary view, the causal relata are **events**: one event causes another according to laws. Lowe inverts this. In *Personal Agency* (2008) he defends **substance causation** as the primary form: strictly speaking there is no event causation, only substances (animate and inanimate alike) exercising their powers. A match does not undergo an igniting-event that causes a burning-event; *the match*, exercising its power to ignite, meets *the wood*, exercising its liability to burn. Causation is the manifestation of a **power** meeting a reciprocal **liability**.

Lowe argues the reduction runs one way only. An event-causal claim, such as that an explosion collapsed a bridge, is elliptical for a claim about a substance: the bomb collapsed it (Lowe 2008: 5; see Griffith 2009). The reverse analysis fails because causing is a kind of doing, and events do nothing; they only happen. If he is right, substance causation is the primitive and event-causation is at best a *façon de parler*.

For Lowe, this reframes agency. **Agent causation is not a special, sui generis kind of causation**—it is simply substance causation with a rational agent as the substance. What makes human agency distinctive is not a new causal relation but the *rational* character of the powers exercised. Chief among these is **volition**: on Lowe's account a volition is a basic mental action, a "spontaneous" exercise of the will's **two-way power**—one an agent can exercise or refrain from exercising in light of reason. Free agents are, in the book's abstract, "unmoved movers, or initiators of new causal chains." Their volitions are wholly uncaused: agents cause their bodily movements by willing, and because agents cause only by acting, an agent-caused volition would start a regress (Lowe 2008: 7). The Map [departs here](/topics/philosophy-of-action-under-dualism/): it makes the volition agent-caused and so must deny that premise.

Lowe's 2013 chapter "Substance Causation, Powers, and Human Agency" restates the view within a general powers-based theory of substance causation: the will is "a unique kind of spontaneous power", a two-way power that can be exercised "in the light of reason" (chapter abstract). The Map's separate [causal powers](/concepts/causal-powers/) article covers powers-realism generically (Bird, Mumford, dispositions); Lowe's distinctive contribution is making substances—not properties or events—the *bearers and exercisers* of those powers, which is what an agent-causal position needs.

## The Fact-Causation Model: Mental Causation Without a Physics Gap

Lowe's most consequential move for interactionists is his reply to the **exclusion problem**. Kim's argument runs: physical causal closure says every physical event has a sufficient physical cause; if a distinct mental cause also produced that same physical effect, we would have either systematic overdetermination or an excluded mental cause. The dualist appears trapped.

Lowe denies the argument's shared premise—that the mental and physical causes target *the same effect under the same causal relation*. His model, set out in "Non-Cartesian Substance Dualism and the Problem of Mental Causation" (*Erkenntnis*, 2006) and *Personal Agency*, distinguishes **fact-causation** from **event-causation**:

- A decision or volition is causally responsible for **the fact that a movement of a certain *kind* occurs**.
- The **specific** neural-muscular event—the particular trajectory of this arm—is caused by neural events.

The two do not compete for one effect. The mental cause **structures** what kind of movement happens; the physical chain supplies the specific movement. Because the relata differ (facts versus events) and the roles differ (kind-determination versus specific-production), the exclusion premise—"one effect, two sufficient causes"—is never triggered. Lowe holds that mental causes exploit no gaps in the physical (Lowe 2008: 117), so on this model the mental **adds no energy to the brain and redistributes none**. That is how Lowe claims compatibility with everything currently known about the central nervous system: his dualism requires **no rewiring of neuroscience and no gap in the physical chain**.

Lowe pairs this with **Non-Cartesian Substance Dualism (NCSD)**: the self is a distinct substance from the organic body—not identical to it or to any part—yet not necessarily separable from it, and bearing both mental and physical properties. The distinctness rests on a **unity argument**: I am the subject of all and only my own mental states, which neither my body nor any part of it could be (Lowe 2008: 96). A separate **entanglement argument** targets mental and neural *causes*: the neural causes of several parallel bodily movements can be causally entangled with one another, while their mental causes are not, so the mental causes cannot be identical to the neural ones (Lowe 1993; reconstructed in Kistler 2022).

## Relation to Site Perspective

The four-category ontology is a **metaphysical framework the Map leans on**, not empirical evidence for dualism. It earns its place because it grounds a phrase the agency cluster already relies on—the agent *qua* substance exercising causal powers—and because, on Lowe's terms, it makes agent causation continuous with ordinary causation rather than metaphysically exceptional. That fits **[Tenet 3 (Bidirectional Interaction)](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction)** without evidencing it: if all causation is substances exercising powers, a conscious subject causing physical events is the same *type* of causation as anything else, so [interactionism](/concepts/interactionist-dualism/) loses the charge that it *couldn't* be causation at all. The Map does not inherit all of this. It keeps substance causation, the volitionist structure and the two-way power, but Lowe earns the continuity from the premise that agents cause only by acting, which the Map's agent-caused volition denies. An agent that causes its volition without acting is the classical agent-causal picture Lowe declines, so what carries over is the general claim that substances are causes, not Lowe's ground for calling the agent's own causing ordinary. It also gives a substance reading of **[Tenet 1 (Dualism)](/tenets/#dualism)**.

Two framework-boundary qualifications keep this honest. First, NCSD is **a congenial option, not the Map's mandatory commitment**. The Dualism tenet is officially neutral between substance and property dualism (see [substance vs property dualism](/concepts/substance-property-dualism/) and [where the substance commitment enters](/concepts/where-the-substance-commitment-enters/)); the Map's substance-leaning is downstream of the agent-causal commitment, not of the tenet itself. Lowe supplies the strongest *substance* version of that lean, without making it obligatory. Second, the framework is scaffolding for locating consciousness, not an argument that consciousness exists—it says *how a subject could be a genuine cause*, given that there is one.

**The deliberate fork.** Here the Map parts company with Lowe, and the departure is instructive. Lowe buys causal-closure compatibility by making the mental cause **structuring rather than energetic**: on fact-causation, consciousness determines *which kind* of movement occurs while making **no difference to the physical event-chain**—no gap, and so no energy and no deviation. The Map's [Tenet 2 (Minimal Quantum Interaction)](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction) takes the other branch. It accepts a **minimal physical difference**, located precisely where physics is *already indeterministic*: at quantum junctures, physics fixes probabilities but not outcomes, so there is no sufficient physical cause of the specific outcome, and consciousness biases *which* outcome becomes actual within the Born distribution. That escapes closure stated as sufficiency; closure stated as chance-fixing survives wherever the Born measure is exactly preserved, and whether settling an outcome without shifting its chance counts as causing it is part of the Map's open [mechanism debt](/positions/quantum-interface/#mechanism-debt). The two routes share a goal—avoiding a crude interventionist "gap" that would violate the empirical record—but they answer the crux question oppositely: does the mental make *any* difference to the physical event-chain? Lowe says no (the difference is at the level of facts about kinds); the Map says yes, but only a minimal one at indeterministic points, invisible to unconditioned aggregate statistics.

Neither route is obviously forced. Lowe's has the advantage of touching no physics at all. Its cost shows in Max Kistler's reconstruction: Lowe's arguments do show a decision to be a cause specific to the movement it produces, where the neural cause is not, but that establishes only a weak property dualism a physicalist can accept, not the irreducibility Lowe claims (Kistler 2022). The Map's route makes a concrete physical claim about outcome-selection and so takes on empirical risk: a consciousness-conditioned departure from Born statistics would test it, though no such test has yet run (see the [Tenet 2](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction) discussion of empirical risk). The Map takes its branch because it wants consciousness to be a token-level *difference-maker* to the physical world, not only a determiner of facts about kinds—and because a purely structuring cause can look uncomfortably close to the epiphenomenalism the Map rejects. Staging the choice this way is the point: Lowe's fact-causation is the sharpest available foil for saying exactly *what* the Map's minimal-interaction commitment adds, and *why* it pays the empirical cost that Lowe's route avoids.

A residual worry the Map inherits rather than settles: Lowe's "spontaneous" volition faces Libet-style timing objections (neural readiness potentials appearing to precede conscious will). The Map's treatment of that data lives in its [agency and will positions](/positions/agency-and-will/) and [luck objection](/concepts/quantum-indeterminacy-free-will/) material; the four-category framework tells us *what a volition is metaphysically*, not whether the timing evidence undercuts its spontaneity.
