## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Montero and Papineau 2005 overstated (L103).** The page said they "defend an inductive case that it is" (the non-mental is closed). I read the open-access text (Zenodo record 849905, *Analysis* 65.3 pp. 233–237). It disclaims exactly that: "The point of this note was not to vindicate this inductive argument, which would be a substantial empirical task, but merely to establish the far more modest point that Hempel's Dilemma does not undermine the via negativa". They also say the evidence is the failure of "no type of empirical investigation", not of physiology alone. **Fixed:** they defend the inductive case "against the dilemma only", and the induction itself would be "a substantial empirical task".
- **Tenet 1 quotation spliced in its subject (L37).** The page said the tenet "describes consciousness as something that 'cannot be fully explained by physics alone'". In tenets L53 the subject of that predicate is "something beyond the material—whether substance, property, or aspect". **Fixed:** "posits something beyond the material that …". L107 already had it right.
- **NFM label credited to the SEP (L73).** The page said "what the SEP calls 'the 'no fundamental mentality' constraint'". The SEP reads "what Wilson 2006 calls the 'no fundamental mentality' constraint". **Fixed:** "Wilson calls her refinement the 'no fundamental mentality' constraint, as the SEP reports."

### Medium Issues Found

- **Tenet 5 overstated (L115).** The page said the tenet "denies that the razor tracks truth". The tenet says "Simplicity is not a reliable guide to truth when knowledge is incomplete" and calls the razor "a useful heuristic". **Fixed:** "denies that such razors reliably track truth when knowledge is incomplete".
- **The lead ran the charges together in the Map's own voice (L35).** "Empty, because no one can say …, and it might contain mental items" folded indeterminacy, triviality and the fundamental-mentality worry into one sentence. Yet the page's own thesis is that a user of the dilemma "has to say which charge it means". **Fixed:** the horns are now attributed to the SEP's reconstruction, with its word "trivial". The Three Charges section then separates them.
- **Description overclaimed.** "Shows that defining 'physical' by physics fails on either horn" contradicts Montero's "not fatal" and the page's "the dilemma refutes none of them". **Fixed:** "challenges defining 'physical' by physics on either horn".
