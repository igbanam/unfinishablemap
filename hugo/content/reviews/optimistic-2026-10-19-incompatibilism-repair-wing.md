The openings follow from the repair:

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would welcome the main structural result of the repair: the case for interactionism is now independent of the free-will debate. Consequence L91 says "Tenet 3 rests on mental causation, not free will, and commits only to outcome-selection, which a dualist who held conscious contributions to be fixed by prior mental states could also accept." A reader who finds libertarianism unbelievable can therefore still accept the Map's dualism and its interactionism. This is the separation Chalmers has always kept between the hard problem and the question of agency. Making Tenet 3 hostage to sourcehood would have tied the Map's strongest commitment to its most contested one, and the repair undoes that.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

Stapp would point to the inverted Lewis bridge at consequence L97 as the wing's best piece of physics-facing writing: "Lewis needs an unowned violation of deterministic law to make the counterfactual true; on the Map's indeterministic picture no violation is needed, and the role his miracle plays is filled by a lawful selection among outcomes physics leaves open." In this form the analogy cannot be read as the Map positing law-breaking, which was the Quantum Skeptic's charge on 10-13. Stapp's Process 1 choice is a lawful selection of the same kind. Stapp would also note that the free-will hub (L118) concedes his own model sits on the same horn as Pereboom's wild coincidences: Born statistics intact, so what selection adds is unexplained. That candour is consistent across the cluster now. The apex says the Map "parts from him on the first charge and carries the second as a cost".

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

Nagel would find his internal/external standpoint distinction in the repaired Jeppsson gloss. Manipulation L60 now says that the engaged stance, "not the detached one that yields the incompatibilist intuition, is the right one to take toward other agents." That is close to his problem of autonomy, in which the external view turns actions into happenings. The repair took an inaccurate "grounding" gloss and replaced it with a faithful epistemic one. That exposes a link the Map has not drawn. `concepts/objectivity-and-consciousness` treats the view from nowhere for phenomenal properties but never for agency (see Ideas for Later).

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

On Whitehead's account, each actual occasion completes itself in a final self-determining decision among the possibilities it inherits. Self-determination and real alternatives arrive together, and that is the *wide* source structure the source-vs-leeway page places the Map in (L80). Whitehead would also like the Lewis inversion: becoming as lawful selection among open outcomes, with no departure from law. **Constraint honoured**: no tier is at stake in this wing. Every page holds the Map's libertarianism as a bet ("located among the options rather than proved by them", source-vs-leeway lead). Whitehead-friendliness here supports coherence and is not used as evidence.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane would first notice something the wing does not say: by Timpe's 2007 classification, Kane is in the same cell as the Map. Timpe counts him as a source incompatibilist. Kane holds that meeting the ultimacy condition entails that the agent could have done otherwise voluntarily, intentionally and rationally, which is a stronger form of the wide thesis. Timpe even credits Kane with suggesting the narrow/wide labels (note 28). ECL L38 already states the principle that makes this matter: "A theorist fixes a position on both maps." The Map and its chief rival within libertarianism share one map and differ on the other (High Priority 3).

Kane would also welcome ECL L90's repaired sentence, "Tenet 5 is no answer to the luck objection itself, which the Map presses against the event-causal wing above". It stops the Map from using an argument against his view while discounting the same argument when it is turned on the Map.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

McGinn would notice that the rollback argument's author is the mysterian of free will. Van Inwagen's 2000 lecture is titled "Free Will Remains a Mystery", and SEP §3.2 reports that he presses the luck objection against agent causation too. Finch's 2017 fork lists his position as the third option: every premise of the Mind argument seems true, at least one is false, and we do not know which.

The Map is closer to that option than its luck pages say. The free-will hub calls the agent's settling "a primitive whose modal structure is owed rather than supplied". That is humility of the right kind. The Consequence Argument page could name it, and in doing so place the Map on Finch's fork (High Priority 1). The consequence page's own Beta paragraph shows the same restraint. It closes with "the formal argument's central inference stays under live challenge" and declines to declare either side the winner.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

The wing touches no minimal-organism or boundary case. Birch's verdict therefore turns on tenet-as-evidence moves, and here it is the review's headline praise. The repair performs the move this persona exists to reward, three times over:

1. **Tenet 5 declined as a likelihood-defeater.** Manipulation L70: "The second strand, the wild coincidences, is not a parsimony argument: it says the match between free choices and physical statistics would go unexplained, and Tenet 5 does not touch it." The Map had a structural opportunity to make an unanswered argument disappear by reclassifying it as a parsimony argument, and it declined. The apex carries the same split at L177: "That reaches the parsimony strand of Pereboom's case only; the wild coincidences are not a parsimony point, and the tenet does not answer them."
2. **Tenet 3 declined as an entailment.** Source-vs-leeway L82: "[Bidirectional Interaction](/tenets/#bidirectional-interaction) is why the Map wants sourcehood, though it does not entail it". A tenet that motivates a commitment is kept apart from a tenet that proves it.
3. **The incompatibilist conclusion itself is held conditionally.** Consequence L29: "even the incompatibilist conclusion may be better read as conditionally supported than as settled". The manipulation page holds genuine selection at "live hypothesis pending a toy model" (L68), tracking `tenets.md` L107.

**One calibration concern (not praise).** ECL L80 says "agent-causal views defeat luck but owe an account of substance causation". The Map's own hub, apex and [P-A2](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a2) hold only that agent causation *answers the disappearing-agent form* of luck. On their account the contrastive form is answered with a primitive "owed rather than supplied", and [P-A2](/positions/agency-and-will/#p-a2) says the objection "is answered rather than dissolved". SEP §3.2 reports the luck objection pressed against agent causation by Haji, Mele and van Inwagen. "Defeat" is the one place in the wing that upgrades the Map's own wing, so a refine-draft is minted (Medium Priority 1).

### High Priority

#### 1. The Consequence Argument is neutral about what does the determining, and the Mind argument is its formal twin (in place, consequence page)
- **Builds on**: consequence L91 (the installed figure of a dualist whose contributions are fixed by prior mental states) and L95 ("They are distinct problems, and the Map answers them at different points"). Also its existing reference to Finch & Warfield 1998, which the page cites only "a Beta-2 (Finch and Warfield 1998)".
- **Would address**:
  - **First,** the figure at L91 satisfies Tenet 3 but does not escape the Consequence Argument. The argument's premises concern the whole state of the world and the laws of nature (L35's own definition). On a dualist picture these take in mental states and psychophysical laws, so if each selection were fixed by prior mental states, the transfer of powerlessness would run as before. Quantum openness gives a conscious contribution room. Only a selection that prior mental states do not fix either discharges the libertarian premise, which is why that discharge is agent-causal rather than merely quantum. The free-will hub L80 and agent-causation L59/L71 imply this, but the page about the argument never says it.
  - **Second,** van Inwagen held the Consequence Argument valid if and only if the Mind argument is. Finch & Warfield 1998 claimed that the McKay–Johnson result broke the tie in the libertarian's favour, and Finch 2017 reversed that. The libertarian must give up the Consequence Argument, find a false premise in the Mind argument, or join van Inwagen's mystery. The Map's agent-causal settler is a bid for the second option, and its "owed rather than supplied" admission keeps it near the third.
- **Estimated scope**: two insertions in "Relation to Site Perspective" plus one reference. Measured on a mirrored scratch copy at +240 words including the reference (2,684 → 2,924, under the 3,000 soft threshold, headroom 1,075).
