## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Internal contradiction (presence vs the efficacy-of-evidence premise).** The presence channel grants that the *direct phenomenal belief* is secured by constitution with no causation (Chalmers 2003 p. 32). The premise section then stated the relocated premise for "a belief, inference or assertion" without qualification. Read literally, that covers the direct belief too. Relation to Site Perspective also said the premise concerns "holding, reasoning from and voicing experience", and "holding" again takes in the conceded presence channel. **Fixed:** "a downstream belief, inference or assertion" (the word the article and `phenomenal-acquaintance` L130 already use for post-presence judgement), and "holding," was deleted from the Relation paragraph.
- **Possibility/probability slippage (two sites).** (1) The lead said self-stultification now shows "only that their theory is not reasoned from, or voiced from, that knowledge". That reads as an established result, but the article itself says the result rests on an owed premise that faces an unsettled externalist-parity objection. **Fixed:** "at most that". (2) The efficacy section ended "The argument narrows and survives." on the same unsettled premise. **Fixed:** "narrows and, if the efficacy-of-evidence premise holds, survives." A reviewer who accepts the tenets would flag both, because the support is an open premise the article says it has not settled.
- **Source/Map conflation (Jackson).** "He placed causation in the use channel himself, inside the mind" assigned the article's channel taxonomy to Jackson, who only allows that qualia may make "a difference to other mental states". His stated motive is *awareness* of qualia, which sits nearer the presence channel than use. **Fixed:** "On this article's reading, he kept the use channel causal but inside the mind."
- **Unverifiable attribution (Yetter-Chappell).** "the judging subject is non-physical and relates to its experience by acquaintance" goes beyond what can be verified: the body is closed access (Springer, PhilArchive and the Miami repository all blocked this run, as on 2026-10-16), and the abstract as collated by `deep-review-2026-09-21-self-stultification` does not mention acquaintance. **Fixed, length-neutral:** "the judging subject is non-physical, and its phenomenal self-knowledge is then unproblematic". The abstract does support this: it says there is nothing paradoxical about the epiphenomenalist's understanding of "phenomenal judgments or phenomenal self-knowledge".

### Medium Issues Found

- **Quote order in Robinson's parity argument (SEP §2.4).** The article linked the two quotes with "so": the P3 event lacks any property carrying its causal history, *so* denying it knowledge would reject interactionism. In the SEP the rejection follows from denying knowledge to P3 *on the ground that it is not directly caused by M*, and the no-information point is a further step. **Fixed:** the two clauses are reordered to match the source. Both quotes are unchanged and verbatim.
- **De Brigard's "must posit".** De Brigard gives the epiphenomenalist two options: posit a non-causal nomological relation (the parsimony cost), or deny that a nomological connection is needed ("risky", p. 126). "Must posit" dropped the second option. **Fixed:** "the epiphenomenalist's best repair requires".
- **Bayne 2001 left hanging.** The article reported Bayne's certainty worry but not that Chalmers 2003 n. 14 answers it as "a natural misreading of my argument against reliabilist accounts". **Fixed:** "reports and rejects" (+2).

S. Robinson, who has defended epiphenomenalism against this objection, concludes in his encyclopedia entry that…".
- **Shoemaker 1975 reference** lacked the issue number. Crossref gives 27(5): 291–315. **Fixed.** The research note's alternative range 279–315, taken from Yetter-Chappell's reference list, is wrong.
