### 1. "Forbids" holds only for a static, history-independent production model
- **Flagged by**: chatgpt, gemini
- **Verification**: Clean on the kernel. ChatGPT's counterexample is verified on disk: with τᵢq̇ᵢ = p − qᵢ and A (θ 0.40, τ 0.10), B (θ 0.45, τ 0.20) at r = 1, A fails first (0.50 < 0.65) and recovers first (0.30 > 0.25). It is direction-blind, has one threshold per channel, and none of the 22 earlier reviews of the article raised it. Gemini's "Premise 2" paraphrases L46 accurately: the same profile orders failure and recovery, so a clean reversal is forbidden. Its objection, that a history-dependent system need not retrace its path, holds against L46. The material wrapped around that kernel is disputed and was not counted. Gemini's formalised conclusion ("production models are falsified") is the reverse of L74. Its "naive unified-switch" label is [filter-theory](/concepts/filter-theory/) wording. Its listed "Observation" (paradoxical kinesia, the hysteresis gap) is not an argument the article makes.
- **Quotes**:
  - **ChatGPT 5.6.sol Pro**: "Even before considering neural hysteresis, attractors, pharmacokinetics, plasticity, adaptation, or active arousal circuits, the article’s claim that the ordering is “forbidden” by substrate-symmetric production fails."
  - **Gemini 2.5 Pro**: "Premise 2 is demonstrably false in any physical system exhibiting hysteresis, plasticity, or metastability. The brain is not a memoryless, Markovian system; its current state and its trajectory through state space are strictly dependent on its history."
- **Two mechanisms, one defect.** ChatGPT's mechanism is unequal channel time constants under finite-rate forcing. Gemini's is multistable path dependence, which ChatGPT §3.1 also raises against L70 ("a multistable transition graph can produce ... path-dependent rank changes"). This matters for the fix. A ramp-rate control and long dwell times rule out lag (a reversal that shrinks as r → 0 is lag). They do not rule out multistability. The P1's Notes already say this ("rules out simple lag, but not multistability or active emergence circuitry"), and the rewritten L74 must keep it.
- **Task action**: Recorded convergence; **no upgrade** (already P1): "`topics/direction-dependent-discriminating-test-design` says substrate-symmetric production 'forbids' a non-mirror channel order…". ## Divergences

- **ChatGPT vs Gemini: what the article claims.** ChatGPT reads the article as candid but overreaching: "The article is admirably explicit that the filter model ... forbids no ordering", and its complaint is that the stronger labels survive the concessions. Gemini reads it as claiming that asymmetry "serves as decisive evidence for a dualist filter" and that hysteresis is "definitive evidence of an active 'reboot mechanism'". - **ChatGPT vs Gemini: can the design be saved?** ChatGPT §13: "A useful study could still be built from this idea, but its target should initially be direction-dependent cognitive dynamics, not dualism". It sets out a model ladder (static thresholds, lag, adaptation, multistability, active emergence circuits, a parameterised interface model) and ramp-rate, dwell-time and neural-state-matching controls. Gemini: "scientifically barren and philosophically hollow ... the manuscript must be rejected", because any asymmetry "merely tests for the presence of non-linear state transitions". The open P1 follows ChatGPT's route (restrict "forbids" to the quasi-static model; add the ramp-rate and dwell-time controls). Gemini's rejection rests largely on sections that audit other pages, so it is weak evidence against the article as such. If the P1 executor finds the quasi-static reversal still has no rival-excluding interpretation once multistability is admitted, the honest outcome is ChatGPT's improvement 1: a retitle to a test of static-threshold versus direction-dependent dynamics, with the slug unchanged.
