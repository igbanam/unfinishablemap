## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Dropped qualifier in the Frede attribution (L58).** "Michael Frede (2011) located the first notion of a will in Epictetus's view that adults choose to assent" dropped the object of the choice. Blackson's abstract (the only source the research note reached for Frede; Frede 2011 itself is `[not reached]`) reads "Epictetus did not believe, as Frede argues, that adult human beings choose to assent to *impulsive impressions*". In an article about belief, a thesis about assent to action-prompting impressions read as a thesis about assent in general is a meaning-changing omission, and the sentence also presented Frede as read directly. Resolution: restored "to impulsive impressions" and marked the formulation as Blackson's report of Frede.
- **Dropped qualifier in the Boespflug 2018 quotation (L96).** The article quoted "pervasive" and the list of authors but omitted Boespflug's definition of the thing that was pervasive: "the view that agents are able to exercise *direct* voluntary control over their beliefs". The omission mattered because the article's §What the History Shows argues the opposite reading ("Pre-modern 'voluntary' mostly meant answerable") and, without the definition, a reader could take Boespflug as endorsing it. Resolution: inserted the definition at the quotation, and added one sentence to the Map-reading paragraph stating that Boespflug counts the same authors as direct voluntarists and that the Map's narrower reading rests on the qualifications quoted in the body. Source/Map separation is now explicit at the point of divergence.
