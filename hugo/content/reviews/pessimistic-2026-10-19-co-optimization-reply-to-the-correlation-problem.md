## Executive Summary

The article's calibration is real. Its weak point is the one positive argument it makes for interactionism, the "why this specific pairing?" dilemma and the economy point drawn from it in Relation to Site Perspective. That argument has three problems, each checked at source or against the Map's own later pages:

1. It omits the reply printed in the article's own cited source. Robinson's SEP entry answers James's version of the inverted-valence challenge with a reference-fixing move that is neither horn of the dilemma.
2. It charges co-optimization with a psychophysical debt that standard interactionism also carries. Mørch (2017), Zietsch (2024) and the Map's own `inheritance-problem-for-the-psychophysical-law` all say so.
3. It runs two different opponents together under one name. The dilemma bites the property-dualist epiphenomenalist, while the "same behaviour by construction" tie holds only against the constitutive physicalist.

The evidence section also overstates what the dissociations rule out and calls the wanting/liking test "genuinely discriminating". Its Cox (2006) gloss asserts elevated mortality, which the paper itself contradicts.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

"Your explanandum is a folk unit that affective neuroscience has already broken up. You lean on Berridge, whose life's work splits 'pleasure' into incentive salience, hedonic reaction and learning. The Map's own wanting/liking page reports that the human pharmacology does not even keep the dopamine/opioid split clean. The inverted-valence organism needs a unitary 'felt badness' that can be swapped while everything else stays fixed. Nothing in the science you cite has that grain." This is bedrock as a rejection of phenomenal valence, and is not flagged. It has one article-internal consequence, Finding 4: the page treats Berridge's "liking" as felt valence, which Berridge does not.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

"The inverted-valence organism is an intuition pump. A pain that the organism seeks out, and that drives none of pain's dispositions, is not pain wearing a pleasant mask. Your scenario has no fact of the matter for it to describe. You half-admit this yourself when you say the necessity horn 'edges toward a functionalism for which there is no separable quale.' For me that is no cost. It is the right view, and it is the predictive-processing story you call 'precisely co-optimization-compatible'." The rejection is bedrock. The structural point is not: the article treats the functionalist as one more co-optimizer while conceding that, for the functionalist, the dilemma has nothing to bite on (Finding 3).

### The Quantum Skeptic

"Relation to Site Perspective says selection shapes valence 'through that causal channel'. For that to work, felt valence has to change how often fitness-relevant outcomes occur, in warm, wet tissue, through a channel your own tenets require to be minimal and Born-preserving. Your sibling page on the inheritance problem admits that the more strictly Born preservation is read, the less evolution can see. So the economy point you score against co-optimization is a cheque the quantum tenet has not cashed. And if the channel does make a selectable difference, you cannot also say the rivals 'predict the same behaviour by construction.'" This feeds Finding 3.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

"Your interactionist explanation of the alignment presupposes that there is a single outcome for felt valence to select. On an Everettian picture every branch's behaviour is fixed by the unitary dynamics, and valence selects nothing. The alternative explanation the page offers for the correlation is therefore conditional on rejecting my view, and the page does not say so." This is fair but minor. The page's economy point is implicitly conditional on the No Many Worlds tenet. One wrinkle cuts the Map's way: an anthropic reply to "why this pairing?" does not help epiphenomenalism. Inverted-valence organisms would be observers too, so observer selection cannot favour the aligned pairing. No task.

### The Empiricist

"Fault line 3 says no behavioural measure distinguishes your view from predictive processing 'by construction'. Fault line 2 offers a conditional that would favour you if behaviour tracked 'felt liking' over 'wanting', but no result that would count against you. Berridge's own conflict cases have already come in: addicts work harder for a dose they report as 'empty'. You call the discriminators 'open research questions' while the one you name has a published answer pointing the other way, and the only in-principle discriminator, an outcome-frequency deviation conditional on valence, goes unmentioned." This drives Findings 3 and 4.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

"You treat felt badness as an own-nature (*svabhāva*) sitting in the experience, detachable from what it arises with, so that it could be swapped while everything else stays put. On dependent origination, feeling-tone (*vedanā*) arises dependent on contact and conditions craving. It is relational through and through. Your 'inverted' organism reifies a quality that has no standing apart from its conditions." This is close to Robinson's reference-fixing reply, from a different direction (Finding 1). The Map's answer has to say why valence is intrinsic to felt character and not relational. The value-in-experience reading assumes that; it does not argue it here.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The dilemma omits the reply in the article's own cited source

- **File**: `obsidian/topics/co-optimization-reply-to-the-correlation-problem.md`
- **Location**: The "Why This Specific Pairing?" section. Text: "The co-optimization model therefore cannot explain *why* actual organisms exhibit the aligned pairing rather than an inverted or scrambled one—unless it smuggles in a constraint that makes the alignment non-arbitrary" and "There are two ways the epiphenomenalist can answer, and each carries a cost:". Also the Co-Optimization Model section: "the version Robinson (2023) surveys as the standard target of the evolutionary objection".
- **Problem**: Robinson's SEP entry (substantive revision 2023-06-15, §2.2 "Natural Selection"), which the page cites, states James's argument in the page's own inverted-valence form: "there would seem to be no reason why we might not abhor the feelings that are caused by activities essential to life, or enjoy the feelings produced by what is detrimental." It then gives the epiphenomenalist reply, which has two parts. (I) is a sensory-system / reward-system distinction. (II) is "What 'pleasure' refers to in any possible world is the effect in consciousness of the events in (b) that contribute to continuance or repetition." This is a third answer, neither a brute necessity-law nor indifference. Reference-fixing turns the inverted organism into a redescription instead of a possibility. The page does not mention it. It also cites Robinson only for the "cruder" brute-luck target, while the sophisticated reply it names "co-optimization" is the one Robinson himself defends (SEP points to Robinson 2007 and 2019 for "fuller discussion of James's argument"). The position is never attributed to anyone. Mørch's (2017) abstract lists Broad 1925, Jackson 1982, Robinson 2007 and Corabi 2014 as the critics of the evolutionary argument. The Map's dilemma therefore looks like an in-framework argument, but it skips the opponent's published in-framework move, which is a substitution failure.
- **Severity**: High.
- **Recommendation**: Add a third option, reference-fixing, stated fairly with the SEP (I)/(II) wording. Then give the Map's answer inside the opponent's framework: the move secures the alignment by making valence a *relational* feature of an experience (being the conscious effect of reward-system events), not a matter of how the experience feels. The inverted-valence question comes back unchanged as "why does that effect feel like *this*?". A qualia-realist epiphenomenalist who holds valence to be intrinsic to felt character cannot take the move without giving up the explanandum. Say openly that this answer assumes valence is intrinsic, which is the value-in-experience commitment, and attribute the reply to Robinson. The same edit can retire the "The challenge is not that co-optimization is incoherent. It is that…" construction, which the style guide bans.

### Issue 2: The debt charged to co-optimization is one interactionism shares

- **File**: same
- **Location**: Relation to Site Perspective: "since interactionism explains the alignment directly: valence tracks fitness because valence drives the behaviour, and selection shapes valence through that causal channel". Also: "Whether that debt is worse than interactionism's own commitment to [a quantum interaction channel](/tenets/#minimal-quantum-interaction)…". The necessity horn's cost reads "reintroduces a brute psychophysical regularity at the level the hard problem already flagged".
- **Problem**: Interactionism needs its own psychophysical laws. One is an *outbound* law: felt badness pushes toward avoidance, not approach. That law faces the same choice between necessity and indifference. One is an *inbound* law: these neural states present these feelings. That law carries harmony's contingency in full. I verified Mørch (2017) in the OpenAlex abstract index. She concludes that criticisms of the evolutionary argument "are indeed fatal to the argument if it is understood, as it traditionally has been, as an argument for any standard version of non-epiphenomenalism such as physicalism and interactionism." Only her phenomenal-powers view, on which phenomenal properties necessitate their effects "in virtue of how they feel", restores the argument. Zietsch (2024, abstract verified) argues that physicalism, dualism and panpsychism all fail to explain the correlations "via adaptation". The Map's own [inheritance-problem page](/topics/inheritance-problem-for-the-psychophysical-law/#harmony), written four months after this one, accepts this. On that page the inbound law "keeps its contingency, and Cutter and Crummett's challenge applies to it in full". Phenomenal powers removes "at most, the arbitrariness of the outbound law". The co-optimization page's economy point therefore holds only for an interactionism whose outbound law is the phenomenal-powers necessity, which the inheritance page calls "the Map's construction". Without that, the brute-regularity cost is counted against one side only.
- **Severity**: High. This is the page's only argument for interactionism, and a later Map page contradicts it.
- **Recommendation**: Make the economy point conditional on the phenomenal-powers reading (cite Mørch 2017 and link the inheritance page's harmony section). State that the inbound harmony debt is common to both views. Restate the comparison at the end of the Occam paragraph so that the shared law is not counted against co-optimization alone. The differential is the epiphenomenalist's need for a harmoniously *shaped* inbound law, against the interactionist's outbound law plus the quantum channel.

### Issue 3: Two opponents under one name, and "by construction" assigned to the wrong one

- **File**: same
- **Location**: The description says "Physicalism's strongest answer to why valence tracks fitness". The lead says "the strongest live physicalist answer". The predictive-processing paragraph says "It is precisely a co-optimization-compatible story." Fault line 3 says "No current behavioural measure distinguishes these, because they predict the same behaviour by construction." Against these, the dilemma says "on the epiphenomenalist's own terms, pleasantness and unpleasantness are causally inert", and the necessity horn concedes "a functionalism for which there is no separable quale to be epiphenomenal about".
- **Problem**: The Map's `concepts/epiphenomenalism` calls epiphenomenalism "one of three dualist positions". The predictive-processing account holds that felt awfulness is "constituted by" the valence-prior's computational role. On that view felt awfulness drives avoidance because it *is* the role. It is not epiphenomenalist, the inverted-valence organism is impossible for it by identity, and the necessity horn is its thesis, not its cost. So the dilemma's cost is earned against the property-dualist epiphenomenalist, the "constrains but does not discriminate" tie is earned against the constitutive physicalist, and Relation to Site Perspective merges the two. Second, "by construction" is true of the constitutive rival but not of the epiphenomenalist. The Map's selectability constraint (inheritance page) says an evolutionarily visible selector "can be practically hidden but not hidden by construction". If selection shapes valence "through that causal channel", the channel changes fitness-relevant outcome frequencies and is detectable in principle. The page cannot keep both the selection-based explanation and an in-principle tie against epiphenomenalism. This is also an epistemic/metaphysical equivocation. The lead's "on the present evidence, appears underdetermined" is a contingent claim, while "by construction" is a claim in principle, and the page never says which applies to which rival.
- **Severity**: Medium-High.
- **Recommendation**: Name the two rivals once: the property-dualist epiphenomenalist co-optimizer (Robinson's position), and the constitutive physicalist co-optimizer (the predictive-processing version). Assign each verdict to its rival. Against the constitutive rival, the remaining dispute is Dualism, not causal efficacy, and its cost is the generic explanatory gap, not a debt specific to co-optimization. Against the epiphenomenalist, the in-principle discriminator is physical (outcome frequency conditional on valence), not a behavioural dissociation. Change "physicalist" to "epiphenomenalist" in the description and lead. Nothing in the fix should upgrade the verdict toward "establishes".

### Issue 4: The evidence section overstates what is constrained, mislabels Berridge, and misreports Cox

- **File**: same
- **Location**: (a) "They rule out the crudest epiphenomenalism (valence-fitness alignment as brute luck) by showing the affective layer is systematically tied to protective behaviour." (b) "are the genuinely discriminating tests", "if behaviour tracks felt liking rather than computational wanting when the two conflict", and in Relation to Site Perspective, "felt-quality/functional-role mismatches like wanting-without-liking—remain open research questions rather than results". (c) "with elevated morbidity and mortality (Cox et al., 2006)".
- **Problem**:
  - (a) The lesion data show that feeling varies with neural state. The brute-luck epiphenomenalist says the same, since "luck" concerns the *shape* of the psychophysical law, not whether feeling depends on its substrate. So the dissociations cannot rule out the crude version; the correlation itself does that work. Berthier et al. (1988, PubMed abstract) also record that the deficit extended "to threatening gestures" and that "Five patients also failed to react to verbal menaces". Asymbolia therefore does not isolate felt pain-badness, as the Map's own `concepts/pain-asymbolia` says and Klein (2015) argues.
  - (b) The page itself calls wanting and liking "two physically separable variables", so a co-optimizer can explain behaviour that follows either one physically. That is the same structure as fault line 1, which the page has just ruled non-discriminating. Berridge (2009, PMC full text) defines "liking" as "the core process of hedonic pleasure", operationalised by facial reactions, not as felt liking. The Map's [wanting-liking-and-the-value-in-mechanism-fork](/topics/wanting-liking-and-the-value-in-mechanism-fork/) says "A mechanism-only physicalist reading appears to explain every Berridge finding without remainder". The cited source's own conflict case also runs against the page's conditional: addicts given a dose that "feels empty" "still work substantially more to receive more of the same 'empty' dose."
  - (c) Cox et al. (2006, PMC full text) say that Nav1.7-null mice die shortly after birth, "in stark contrast to the human families where there is no reported increase in early mortality." The morbidity is supported: lip and tongue injuries, bruises, cuts, and fractures or osteomyelitis diagnosed in retrospect. "Mortality" is not.
- **Severity**: Medium. (c) is a factual misattribution, live and cheap to fix.
- **Recommendation**: Restate what the cases constrain: the affective substrate is tied to the protective-behaviour system, which every surviving story meets. Add a clause on the breadth of the threat-response deficit, linking pain-asymbolia. Downgrade "genuinely discriminating" to "the closest available test", change "felt liking" to hedonic "liking", note that the existing conflict data show pursuit following "wanting", and link the fork page. Make the same change in Relation to Site Perspective. Replace "with elevated morbidity and mortality" with the morbidity Cox actually reports.

## Counterarguments to Address

### Robinson's reference-fixing reply
- **Current content says**: The epiphenomenalist must choose necessity (a brute law) or indifference (no explanation).
- **A critic would argue**: "Pleasure" rigidly names whatever conscious effect reward-system events have, so the aligned pairing is guaranteed without a brute law (SEP §2.2 "Natural Selection", reply (II)).
- **Suggested response**: The reply buys alignment by making valence extrinsic to felt character. The "why does it feel like *this*?" question survives as the inbound harmony question, and an epiphenomenalist who is a realist about qualia cannot accept the move without changing the subject.

### Zietsch's developmental reply
- **Current content says**: Nothing. The page names no non-adaptive answer.
- **A critic would argue**: "Sensational associative learning" during development makes sensations that are paired with fitness rewards or threats "come to be interpreted as good or bad" (Zietsch 2024). On that view the correlation comes from learning, not selection.
- **Suggested response**: Zietsch himself says his view is compatible with physicalism, panpsychism and dualism "(though not epiphenomenalism)". That supports the page's thesis against the epiphenomenalist, but it also removes the evolutionary argument's claim that interactionism is uniquely placed. This could be a one-sentence mention, and is not tasked.
