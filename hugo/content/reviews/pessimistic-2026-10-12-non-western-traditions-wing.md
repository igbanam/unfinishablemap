## Executive Summary

Two attributed quotations do not exist in the sources they are attributed to:
- **al-Ghazālī's** "so utterly different in their essences that one cannot affect the other" is a Wikipedia sentence. It describes the *Cartesian* motive for occasionalism, not al-Ghazālī's argument.
- **Davis (SEP)**'s "explicitly rejected simple monism, prioritizing radical individuality within interconnectedness" appears in none of seven retrievable editions of the entry. A positive control from the same entry turns up in every edition.

Both quotations passed earlier citation ledgers that recorded them as "prior-verified" or "faithful" without giving a locus.

Structurally, all three basin-edge articles explain the non-converging traditions by the *framework-dependence* reading. That reading concedes that every converging tradition shares one premise, the mind-matter partition, and that this premise produces the gap. The articles then say the core inference is "untouched" and that the dissociation "partly discharges" the common-cause objection. On the reading invoked, it relocates the common cause rather than discharging it.

The Islamic article also sets fanā' ("heightened awareness") beside *nirodha-samāpatti* as a parallel. The Map's own [cessation-versus-plenitude](/concepts/cessation-versus-plenitude/) puts *nirodha-samāpatti* at the opposite pole. The article then turns reports of "awareness without an experiencer" into an "irreducible subject".

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
Churchland would say the wing's *own* data favour her. The one tradition that never adopted a folk mind-matter partition (Chinese organicism) never found a hard problem. The traditions that did adopt it found one. That is what you would predict if the "gap" were a feature of a contingent folk taxonomy rather than of the world. The articles name this as the "framework-dependence reading" and hold it open. But their Relation-to-Site sections then credit the Chinese and Nahua cases as evidence *for* the convergence's independence. She would call that taking credit for the experiment that came out against you (Issue 3).

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
Dennett would target the Sufi material. "Awareness without an experiencer, cognition without a cogniser" (Islamic L85) is a report produced after the fact by a practitioner whose framework already tells them what fanā' is. Converting it into "the irreducible subject" (L87) is exactly the inflation of introspective report into metaphysics the Map's own [evidential-status-discipline](/project/evidential-status-discipline/) forbids. And the report contradicts the conclusion: if there is no experiencer, the residue cannot be a subject (Issue 4).

### The Quantum Skeptic
Tegmark's only purchase is the Islamic article's Tenet 2 paragraph. It says Avicenna's "holistic" mental causation "resonates" with probability-biasing at the quantum level. The "holistic" feature belongs to a 2019 *Bohmian* model by Jamali, Golshani & Jamali, not to Avicenna. In the authors' companion paper, Avicenna's own model works "through an intermediate substance". A deterministic pilot-wave mechanism is an odd ally for an indeterministic selection tenet (background finding B3).

### The Many-Worlds Defender
Deutsch has little to say here. The articles keep Tenet 4 thematic: the Islamic L107 says the tradition "neither refutes nor supports many-worlds interpretations". That is correctly scoped.

### The Empiricist
Popper's ghost would ask what result the convergence argument *forbids*. If traditions that pose the problem converge, that supports irreducibility. If a deep tradition does not converge, that "lowers the claim to universality without touching the core inference". Both outcomes leave the core intact. The articles have made the core inference immune to every tradition-level observation. The only way to restore content is to say what the non-converging cases *do* cost, which is Issue 3's fix.

### The Buddhist Philosopher
Nāgārjuna would say the Islamic article recruits the Buddhist case backwards. *Nirodha-samāpatti* is the cessation of perception and feeling, not heightened awareness. The Map's [cessation-versus-plenitude](/concepts/cessation-versus-plenitude/) says so, and says the Theravāda and Madhyamaka sources *deny* the substantialist reading that a persisting witness is present but unreported. L87's "witness that persists … is the irreducible subject" is that denied reading, presented as "the Map's framework suggests". He would accept the article's own description, awareness without an experiencer, and reject the subject it then installs.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: al-Ghazālī is given a Cartesian quotation, and his contemplative-epistemology passage is pinned to the wrong work (High)
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/islamic-sufi-philosophy-of-consciousness.md`
- **Location**: L49 (primary); L75 (secondary)
- **Problem (L49)**: The article attributes this quotation to al-Ghazālī: mind and matter are "so utterly different in their essences that one cannot affect the other." That string is the opening sentence of the **§Dualism** section of Wikipedia's "Occasionalism" article. In full: "One of the motivations for the theory is the dualist belief that mind and matter are so utterly different in their essences that one cannot affect the other." That section describes the Descartes-to-Malebranche motive.
  - The same Wikipedia article's §Islamic theological schools gives al-Ghazālī's actual argument: God's power and agency, illustrated by fire and cotton ("the cotton is burned not because of the heat of the fire, but through God's direct intervention").
  - al-Ghazālī's argument in *Tahāfut* XVII is a general denial of necessary connection between natural causes and effects. It is not an argument from mind-matter heterogeneity.
  - **Provenance**: `git log -S` shows the string entered via [research/interaction-problem-non-western-philosophy-2026-02-17.md](/research/interaction-problem-non-western-philosophy-2026-02-17/) L99 (still live and public), then the 2026-03-08 create. The 06-25 ledger called it a "primary-source rendering", and the 07-17 ledger called it "prior-verified". Neither names a work or page.
  - **The cluster contradicts itself**: [interaction-problem-across-traditions](/topics/interaction-problem-across-traditions/) L74 and [occasionalism](/concepts/occasionalism/) L45 both give the correct gloss ("causal regularity is God's habitual practice, not a power residing in things").
- **Problem (L75)**: The article says that "in the *Iḥyā' 'Ulūm al-Dīn*" al-Ghazālī argues that knowing what intoxication is differs from being intoxicated, and adds the health analogy.
  - Both the drunkenness image and the health/satiety image occur together in the ***Munqidh***, §4 "The Ways of Mysticism". Watt's translation, verified on ghazali.org: "What a difference there is between *knowing* the definition of health and satiety … and *being* healthy and satisfied! What a difference between being acquainted with the definition of drunkenness … and being drunk!"
  - I have **not** established that the *Iḥyā'* lacks a parallel. The claim is narrower: the passage the sentence paraphrases is verifiable in the *Munqidh*, which the References already carry. The ledgers "spot-checked" the *Iḥyā'* pin without quoting any locus.

### Issue 3: The basin-edge articles concede a premise-level common cause and then call it a discharge (Medium-High)
- **Files**: [topics/chinese-philosophy-of-mind.md](/topics/chinese-philosophy-of-mind/) L91, L95 (primary). Same claim at [topics/mesoamerican-nahua-philosophy-of-mind.md](/topics/mesoamerican-nahua-philosophy-of-mind/) L75; [topics/japanese-philosophy-of-mind-kyoto-school.md](/topics/japanese-philosophy-of-mind-kyoto-school/) L80; the hub `cross-traditional-convergence-on-consciousness-irreducibility.md` L135 (and L122).
- **Problem**: Each article explains its non-converging tradition by the framework-dependence reading: the finding of irreducibility is "conditioned on framework rather than forced by the raw phenomenology" (Chinese L91; Nahua L75 nearly verbatim). The hub defines the basin as traditions "that begin from a mind-matter-distinguishing framework" (hub L68). It states the framework-dependence reading as "the gap was created by the distinction itself" (hub L94).
  - Put those together. The traditions that *do* converge are premise-diverse on substance, self and causation, but they all share the mind-matter partition. On the reading the articles invoke, that partition is the generator of the finding. That is a premise-level common cause.
  - Yet the articles say:
    - "without touching the core inference from broad, premise-diverse convergence among the traditions that *do* pose the problem" (Chinese L91; Nahua L75 "untouched"; Kyoto L80 "without touching the core inference")
    - the dissociation "on the framework-dependence reading, partly discharges the common-cause objection" (Chinese L95; hub L135 "doubles as a partial discharge")
  - On that reading, the dissociation discharges only the *architecture* version of the null. It installs a *framework* version that is just as deflationary: the convergence would not track reality. It counts in the convergence's favour only on the depth-of-inquiry reading. The current L95 has the assignment backwards ("on the depth-of-inquiry reading leaves it more open").
  - It also contradicts the hub's own verdict that "premise-level common causes [are] substantially weakened" (hub L136, L138). That verdict cannot stand alongside a basin condition that is itself a shared premise.
- **Not pre-empted.** The 07-27 Chinese stability note fences the *standoff* between the two readings ("must not resolve it in either direction"). The fix below resolves nothing. It states what each reading implies for the core inference, conditionally. No prior review (searched `reviews/` for "premise-level common cause", "framework common cause", "shared mind-matter", "basin condition") has raised the point that the basin condition is itself a shared premise.
- **Severity**: Medium-High (the wing's central inferential claim is overstated in four files).
- **Recommendation**: Priority item 3, at the Chinese article (the wing's anchor leaf, with 1,359 words of headroom). **The hub cannot absorb additions**: it is at 3,995 words against a hard threshold of 4,000, so headroom is 4 words. Its L135 counterpart must be fixed length-neutrally in a separate pass. The Nahua L75 and Kyoto L80 counterparts are one-clause follow-ons.

### Issue 4: The fanā' paragraph inverts the cessation parallel and installs a subject its testimony denies (Medium)
- **File**: `obsidian/concepts/islamic-sufi-philosophy-of-consciousness.md`
- **Location**: L85 (last sentence); L87
- **Problem**: L85 argues that fanā' differs from dreamless sleep or anaesthesia because practitioners report "*heightened* awareness". It then calls the "parallel to Buddhist descriptions of *nirodha-samāpatti*" "notable". But the Map's own pages place *nirodha-samāpatti* at the opposite pole:
  - [cessation-versus-plenitude](/concepts/cessation-versus-plenitude/) L43: "the complete stopping of perception and feeling (*saññā-vedayita-nirodha*)"
  - [comparative-phenomenology-of-meditative-traditions](/topics/comparative-phenomenology-of-meditative-traditions/) L125: "the complete stopping of perception and feeling"

  So the parallel undercuts the paragraph's own argument: *nirodha* is the case where the reported state is closest to the sleep/anaesthesia contrast it is meant to escape. *Turīya* (plenitude) is the genuine parallel.

  L87 then concludes that "the witness that persists through fanā' is the irreducible subject". L85 had just described the state as "awareness without an experiencer, cognition without a cogniser". That is an epistemic-to-metaphysical slide: from report content to the constitution of a subject. The conclusion also contradicts the description it rests on. [cessation-versus-plenitude](/concepts/cessation-versus-plenitude/) L67 records that the Theravāda and Madhyamaka sources reject exactly this substantialist reading.
- **Severity**: Medium (factual inversion against the Map's own corpus; interpretive equivocation in the tenet-bearing paragraph).
- **Recommendation**: Priority item 4.

## Counterarguments to Address

### "Posing the problem is not the same as finding irreducibility"
- **Current content says**: the convergence is "premise-diverse" among traditions that pose the problem.
- **A critic would argue**: the one premise they share is the one that, on the framework-dependence reading, manufactures the result.
- **Suggested response**: the strongest available reply is that posing the problem does not *entail* the finding. Western analytic philosophy poses it and contains type-A and type-B physicalists who reject irreducibility. So the partition is necessary but not sufficient, and convergence *given* the partition still carries some weight. That reply should be made, but in the hub, which has no headroom; it needs a length-neutral swap. It does not rescue "untouched".

### "A basin edge cannot also be a convergent data point"
- **Current content says** (Kyoto L82): the Kyoto case "only strengthens rather than confirms" the convergence argument, because it shares the convergent traditions' method, and "adds weight to the pattern".
- **A critic would argue**: L80, and the 09-11 deep review's own stability note, define a basin edge as a tradition that did *not* reach the finding. A non-converging case cannot add weight to the convergence through shared method. Shared method plus *non*-convergence is evidence that the method does not force the finding. That is the dissociation polarity the Nahua L77 states correctly ("cuts cleanly only as a dissociating basin edge").
- **Suggested response**: see background B1.

  The cited Preprints.org paper returned 403 to both WebFetch and curl, so I have not seen its text. This finding should be confirmed before it is acted on. If it holds, L47 transposes a modern Bohmian model's feature onto Avicenna, and the "Modern interpreters" are its authors.
