## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **Empirical result misreported (daSilva Morgan et al. 2025).** The page said the study reports "lowered phosphene thresholds" as a group finding. The full text (PMC11813974) says: "No significant differences in phosphene threshold were observed between CBS … and ED-controls … (p = 0.162)". What the study found is that within the CBS group, lower thresholds went with weaker V1/V2 BOLD activation (rs = 0.649) and with greater hallucination severity on the NPI (rs = −0.618 and −0.650). **Fixed**: the sentence now separates the absent group difference from the within-group association.
2. **Dropped qualifier on the same paper.** The abstract says the hallucinations "have been posited to arise because of spontaneous, compensatory, neural activity". The page said this activity "is precisely what generates" them. **Fixed**: the page now calls it "the posited cause" and adds "on this hypothesis".
3. **Modal strengthening (Reichert et al. 2013).** The abstract says homeostatic plasticity "could serve to make the learnt internal model robust … but overcompensate". The page said it "keeps" the model robust and "will" overcompensate. It also said the abstract "reads CBS" as indication of a generative model, where the abstract says CBS "could be taken as indication". **Fixed**: "could keep … yet 'overcompensate …'", and "proposes that CBS 'could be taken as indication …'".
4. **Misattribution (Corlett et al. 2019).** The page said Corlett et al. "hold that interactions within and between hierarchies of inference can reconcile apparent disconnects of this kind", which reads them as answering the insight objection. In the full text (PMC6368358) that reconciliation addresses a different tension, strong priors in hallucination against weak priors in psychosis. The paper never mentions insight or reality monitoring. Its Outstanding Questions ask whether the account applies "trans-diagnostically (… Charles Bonnet Syndrome …)". The paper also predates Schmid and Kriegleder (2024). **Fixed**: the page now names the actual tension and the open question. "The dispute is open" stands, now better supported. The same stretch sat in the research note (L177) and was corrected there as well.
5. **Calibration: L73 vouched for the squinting case.** This was raised in tenet-check 151 Warning 1. The page said the behavioural half of `perceptual-failure-and-the-interface`'s Tenet 3 paragraph "is untouched by anything here". The squinting case does not depend on CBS, but it does face its own rival reading. A reviewer who accepts the tenets would still flag the endorsement, so this is a calibration error and not a bedrock disagreement. **Fixed**: the page now says "does not rest on the syndrome, and nothing here vouches for it: the blurred retinal signal would drive the same correction on a physicalist or epiphenomenalist account". This agrees with the pending P3 refine-draft on `perceptual-failure-and-the-interface` L134 and does not touch that file.

### Medium Issues Found

- **Coiner versus populariser (Cogan 1973).** The page said the release reading was "established by Cogan (1973)". Release theories of hallucination are older; West (1962) set out a "perceptual release" theory. Cogan is the standard ophthalmological citation, and daSilva Morgan's text and the research notes both use him that way. **Fixed**: "established in ophthalmology by Cogan (1973)".
- **History compressed (de Morsier 1936).** The page said de Morsier "attached Bonnet's name to the syndrome", meaning the eye-disease syndrome this page defines. ffytche (2005, abstract) says the eponym "was intended to eponymize the association of visual hallucinations with age, but evolved into one describing their association with eye disease". ffytche (2007, full text) says de Morsier held that CBS "had nothing to do with eye disease". **Fixed**: de Morsier's syndrome is now described as hallucination in the cognitively intact elderly, and the eye-disease meaning is dated as later.
- **Unsupported claim.** "A patient who has never met a costumed figure sees one anyway" had no source. **Replaced** with "The same categories recur from patient to patient, whatever scene each has lost", which Santhouse et al.'s cross-patient factor analysis supports.
- **Two triggers not in the source (Teunisse 1996).** The page said "Episodes favour darkness, fatigue and low arousal". The abstract says "sensory deprivation and a low level of arousal", and the full text is paywalled, so darkness and fatigue could not be checked. **Fixed** to match the abstract.
- **Universal claims about the literature.** "Every account in the clinical and computational literature agrees" became "The clinical and computational accounts reviewed below agree". "Has no support in the literature" became "finds no support in that literature". "No external source supports that wording" became "No source consulted for this article supports that wording".
- **Possible internal tension in the Tenet 5 paragraph.** The paragraph said "where nothing is incomplete in the relevant respect", directly after a section listing four open questions. **Fixed**: the page now says what remains open (timing, susceptibility, direction, labelling) and that none of it concerns where the content comes from.
- **PAD model compressed (Collerton et al. 2005).** "an attentional-binding deficit" became "a combined perception-and-attention deficit". The model pairs impaired attentional binding with poor sensory activation of a proto-object.
