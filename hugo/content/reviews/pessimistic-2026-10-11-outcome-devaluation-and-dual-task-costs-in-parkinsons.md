## Executive Summary

The page's design holds up well. It states the rival's prediction in the rival's own terms, tests it, leaves the anatomy standing and blocks its own promotion in the Construct Gap section. But its headline instrumental verdict rests on two misreadings of the two studies it calls direct.

**First, Mi et al. (2021).** The page credits them with finding that "habit formation was not impaired" (L29, and copied into `paradoxical-kinesia` L82). Their abstract concludes "the formation of both goal-directed and habitual control are impaired in PD patients". Their Discussion presents this as a departure from de Wit et al.: "Despite prior speculation that PD subjects have selective deficits of habitual control, we demonstrate that the formation of both habitual control behavior and goal-directed action are impaired."

**Second, the test the page says "comes out against" the habitual reading (L29) was never run.** The page states that test as one "on a well-practised task" (L35, table L73), and the kinesia page named it "in well-practised tasks". de Wit et al. trained for 96 trials and Mi et al. for 72. de Wit et al.'s own Discussion says their data "do not exclude the possibility that PD is accompanied by deficits in training-induced habit formation". The page treats Redgrave's identical reply as merely "available" (L47).

A third, smaller problem is that the page asserts in its own voice that model-free learning is the same thing as habit (L51), an identification that is contested and that Redgrave et al. never make. With all three corrected, the page's "parity" verdict needs re-deriving. All the fixes fit inside the 18-word headroom with funded cuts; the edit set below was applied to a scratch copy and measures 3,997.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

The page's two "readings" are folk-psychological glosses on one circuit. "Willed" and "habitual" are labels for task conditions. The "domain-indexed" result (L31) is what you would predict when folk categories are projected onto a mechanism that does not respect them: the circuit story (dopamine loss, posterior putamen first, a reinforcement signal) is unified, while the folk vocabulary breaks in two. Churchland would add that the willed-deficit reading names no mechanism beyond its label, whereas the rival's lineage names one (Hernandez et al. 2019's "critical functional stressor"). The page concedes the metaphysical half of this in its Relation section ("non-reductive physicalism with mental causation predicts as readily"). That concession is honest and is bedrock.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

The page puts the remaining Map/physicalist disagreement "in why goal-directed control is *felt* as effortful" (L97). Dennett would call that the intuition pump talking. Felt effort is the brain's cost signal and is reportable, so nothing is left over to explain. He would also press the page's own concession harder than it does. In reward learning, the attended, effortful, goal-directed system is the dopamine-dependent one that fails with severity (L97: "complicates that comfort"). If the Map locates felt effort there, then the felt system degrades with a neuromodulator. The page records this. It does not say what the Map would need in order to resist the obvious reading.

### The Quantum Skeptic

Nothing quantum is at stake, and the page says so (L97: "Nothing for Tenet 2"). Tegmark's only point would be that levodopa restoring model-based learning (Sharp et al. 2016) is a pharmacological fact with a classical mechanism. Any dualist gloss that later borrows this page as interface evidence would have to explain why the remedy works through a neuromodulator rather than through selection. The page does not attempt such a gloss.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

The page does not touch Tenet 4, and correctly says so. No critique.

### The Empiricist

This is the sharpest critic of the page, on three counts.

1. **The falsified prediction was conditional, and the condition was not met.** de Wit et al. (2011) state the habitual reading's devaluation prediction for "S→R habit formation through extensive practice". Their own task gave 96 training trials ("eight 12-trial blocks"); Mi et al. gave 72 ("six blocks… a total of 72 trials"), and Mi et al. note that "With only limited training performance should, however, be predominantly controlled by the goal-directed system." So the result refutes the habitual reading for conflict-induced, briefly trained habits. It does not refute the reading the kinesia page and L35 state. de Wit et al. concede exactly this in their Discussion. Popper would call the page's L47 move ("The reply is available; it leaves the direct devaluation test… pointing against their reading") a refutation claimed against a hypothesis the experiment did not test.

2. **The named test may not be runnable in humans.** de Wit et al. (2018) report "five failed attempts to demonstrate that overtraining instrumental behavior leads to the development of inflexible habits in humans". The failures included an appetitive slips-of-action task and two attempts to replicate the original demonstration. They conclude that outcome-devaluation procedures "may be insensitive to duration of stimulus-response training in humans". If so, the habitual reading's devaluation prediction is currently untested in patients rather than falsified. The page should say which.

3. **The willed-deficit reading risks almost nothing where its own construct lives.** In three of the four motor rows of the table (L76, L77, L79), it makes "No specific prediction". It predicts in the instrumental rows, which the Construct Gap section (L85) says do not measure its construct, and in one motor row (cueing, L78). A reading that is silent where it is about and predictive where it is not cannot lose on this evidence. The page's verdict of "compatible, not supported" absorbs this honestly. The "parity" verdict does not (Issue 4).

### The Buddhist Philosopher

"Self-initiated" in the page means endogenously timed, meaning movement without an external trigger (L85). Nothing in the evidence locates a self that initiates. The willed/habitual dichotomy is itself what the domain-indexed result dissolves: actions arise from conditions (cue, practice, dopamine tone, competing demand), and which "system" controls them is a description of which conditions dominate. The page does not reify a selector, and the Construct Gap section declines to identify either construct with conscious selection. Nagarjuna's critique therefore lands on the Map's wider programme, not on this page.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Mi et al. (2021) are credited with the opposite of their formation conclusion (High; citation framing, propagated)

- **File**: [topics/outcome-devaluation-and-dual-task-costs-in-parkinsons.md](/topics/outcome-devaluation-and-dual-task-costs-in-parkinsons/). The loci are L29, L31, L41 and L45. The same error is copied into [topics/paradoxical-kinesia.md](/topics/paradoxical-kinesia/) L82 and the research note at L208 and L220.
- **Location**: L29: "in the two direct patient studies located as of 2026-09-30, habit formation was not impaired and goal-directed control was what tracked disease severity (de Wit et al. 2011; Mi et al. 2021)". L41: "Both point the same way." L45: "Instrumental learning was slower in patients but was acquired with practice." L31: "habit is spared or over-weighted".
- **Problem**: Mi et al.'s abstract reads: "PD patients had impaired learning not only of the standard and congruent associations (mediated by goal-directed system), but also the incongruent association (mediated by habitual control system)… These results collectively demonstrate that the formation of both goal-directed and habitual control are impaired in PD patients." Their Discussion frames this as against the selective-deficit view and records de Wit's contrary finding. The underlying statistics have the same shape in both studies:
  - **Mi**: a main effect of Group on training accuracy (66.37% vs 78.47%) and no Group × Category interaction.
  - **de Wit 2011**: discriminative performance "negatively affected overall in the PD patients relative to the CS group, F(1, 42) = 3.99… p = .05", with no Group × Discrimination interaction.

  The two teams read the same pattern in opposite directions on habit formation. The page reports only de Wit's reading and attaches both names to it. On *formation* the two studies disagree. They agree only at *test* (expression), where the page's claim is sound.
- **Severity**: High. This is the lead sentence, it is a direct contradiction of a cited abstract, and it has been copied to the page that originally named the test.
- **Recommendation**: Priority List items 1a–1c and 1e for the target. Then `paradoxical-kinesia` L82 K2 (separate task). The research note's L49/L77/L208/L220 should be corrected in a later pass (would-mint P3; not minted).

### Issue 2: The "well-practised" test the page says came out against the habitual reading was not run (High; overclaim of headline verdict)

- **File**: the target at L29, L47 and table L73; `paradoxical-kinesia` L82.
- **Location**: L29: "the outcome-devaluation test… comes out *against* the habitual reading". L35: "patients should be *more* outcome-sensitive than controls on a well-practised task". L47: "The reply is available; it leaves the direct devaluation test, the one the kinesia page named, pointing against their reading." Table L73: "Outcome devaluation, well-practised task". Kinesia L82: "in well-practised tasks… The devaluation test ran and reversed".
- **Problem**: de Wit et al. (2011), in their Discussion, say: "it remains possible that conflict-induced habit formation relies on processes that are different from those underlying habit formation in the context of extensive training, and our data, therefore, do not exclude the possibility that PD is accompanied by deficits in training-induced habit formation." Redgrave et al.'s reply (L47) restates the primary authors' own caveat. It is not merely "available"; the authors of the test grant it.

  The training lengths confirm the point. de Wit: "Discrimination training consisted of eight 12-trial blocks." Mi: "six blocks… a total of 72 trials", with the comment "With only limited training performance should, however, be predominantly controlled by the goal-directed system". Neither study ran the well-practised version the page and the kinesia page name.

  In fairness, de Wit et al. also defend the sensitivity of their task. They argue "incongruent performance relies on habit formation", since incongruent devaluation-test performance "did not differ from chance level". So the result does count against the habitual reading for *conflict-induced, briefly trained* habit. The fix scopes the verdict; it does not reverse it.
- **Further context (not budgeted)**: de Wit et al. (2018), *J Exp Psychol Gen* 147(7), 1043–1065, https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000402, report "five failed attempts to demonstrate that overtraining instrumental behavior leads to the development of inflexible habits in humans". This explains *why* the named test is unrun and may be unrunnable. A sentence and a nine-author reference cost about +72 words, which the page cannot fund. A research-note addition or a future length decision is the place for it.
- **Severity**: High. The page's opening verdict claims more than its sources allow, and the primary authors disclaim the stronger reading.
- **Recommendation**: Priority List items 2a–2c. Kinesia K1 (separate task).

### Issue 3: "the same distinction": model-free learning asserted to be habit, in the page's own voice (Medium)

- **File**: the target at L51 and table L74 (and by extension the Wunderlich half of L75).
- **Location**: L51: "The reinforcement-learning decomposition of the same distinction gives the same answer." Table L74, habitual-reading column: "Model-free impaired".
- **Problem**: The model-free = habit mapping is a modelling identification, and it is contested. Miller, Shenhav and Ludvig (2019, *Psychological Review* 126(2), 292–311) say that "key computational models have conceptualized habits as arising from model-free reinforcement learning mechanisms", but that "Traditionally, however, habits have been understood as behaviors that can be triggered directly by a stimulus, without requiring the animal to evaluate expected outcomes". They model habits as value-free. Redgrave et al. (2010) do not make the identification either. Their text cites Daw et al. (2005) for goal-directed/habitual arbitration, but uses "model-free" zero times and "model-based" once, in a figure caption about Albin's basal-ganglia model. (As a positive control on the same text, "habit" appears 103 times.) So the table's habitual-column prediction "Model-free impaired" is the page's inference, not the rival's stated prediction. Two of the page's three instrumental rows (L74, and Wunderlich in L75) rest on that inference. L81 counts all three as reversing "the habitual reading's edge".
- **Severity**: Medium. The Sharp result is real and correctly quoted. What is overstated is how much weight it carries against habit.
- **Recommendation**: Priority List items 3a–3c.

## Counterarguments to Address

### The run test was the wrong test
- **Current content says**: the devaluation test ran, and it reversed the habitual reading (L29, L47; kinesia L82).
- **A critic would argue**: the prediction was for extensively trained habits. Both studies trained briefly, the primary authors say their data "do not exclude" a deficit in training-induced habit, and overtraining has failed five times to induce habits in healthy humans (de Wit et al. 2018).
- **Suggested response**: scope the verdict to conflict-induced, briefly trained habit (2a–2c). State that the well-practised version was not run, and keep de Wit's sensitivity rejoinder (L43 already explains the incongruent condition).

### The two "direct" studies disagree where it matters to the rival
- **Current content says**: "Both point the same way" (L41).
- **A critic would argue**: on habit *formation*, which is the rival's original claim (the Knowlton line at L47), Mi et al. side with the rival.
- **Suggested response**: 1b–1c. The page's claim survives at test (expression). The L47 sentence "de Wit et al. and Mi et al. both replicate slower instrumental acquisition in patients while finding intact or dominant habit expression" already contains the correct split and needs no change.

### Model-free is not habit
- **Current content says**: the RL decomposition is "the same distinction" (L51).
- **A critic would argue**: value-free habit models (Miller et al. 2019) dissociate the two. Redgrave et al. never predict a model-free deficit.
- **Suggested response**: 3a–3c.

### The willed-deficit reading is predictively idle in movement
- **Current content says**: in three of the four motor rows of the table the willed-deficit reading makes "No specific prediction" (L76, L77, L79).
- **A critic would argue**: a reading that predicts only outside its construct cannot lose here.
- **Suggested response**: none needed in this pass. The "compatible, not supported" tier (L93) already prices this in. Issue 4 is where it bites.
