## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

**1. Both Schwitzgebel arguments were stated without their naturalism premise (dropped qualifier). FIXED.**
The existential-reading paragraph said "Schwitzgebel (2023) argues that some systems have no determinate fact as to whether experience is present", with no condition attached. The publisher abstract (OpenAlex record for doi:10.1007/s11098-023-02042-1) gives the argument as conditional: *"Assuming mainstream naturalism about consciousness, we ought to reject the first three options, which forces us to the fourth, indeterminacy."* The author's manuscript (faculty.ucr.edu, BorderlineConsciousness-211220.htm, grep-verified) sets the dualist option aside explicitly: *"Apart from rejecting naturalistic theories of consciousness altogether, I see two ways to resist this conclusion"*. Schwitzgebel & Nelson's abstract is conditional in the same way: *"on a wide range of naturalistic views of consciousness, the processes underlying consciousness are sufficiently complex to render it implausible that conscious subjects must always arise in determinate whole numbers."*

The omission mattered most in this article, because the article is a dualist one. Without the qualifier, a reader cannot tell that both papers argue from a premise the Map rejects. That leaves out the most important fact about the disagreement.

The fix had to avoid the opposite error: treating the rejected premise as if it settled the matter, which would be possibility/probability slippage. So the new text adds the qualifier to both papers and then says what the dualist escape does *not* achieve. Dualism denies that graded physical bases *constitute* consciousness, but the Map's coupling still *depends* on graded architecture (`universal-coupling-response` L60: "a continuum"). The worry therefore returns at the trigger, a problem the article already treats in its own §"The Trigger Problem". A tenet-accepting reviewer would not flag the new wording as overstated: it removes a defeater and explicitly declines to count that as evidence.

- Old: "Schwitzgebel (2023) argues that some systems have no determinate fact as to whether experience is present, and Schwitzgebel & Nelson (2026) extend the indeterminacy to subject-*counting*, holding that the number of conscious subjects need not always be a determinate whole number. Anyone who accepts that will refuse the step,"
- New: "Schwitzgebel (2023) argues, assuming mainstream naturalism, that some systems have no determinate fact as to whether experience is present, and Schwitzgebel & Nelson (2026) extend the indeterminacy to subject-*counting*, holding that on a wide range of naturalistic views the number of conscious subjects need not always be a determinate whole number. Both arguments run from the graded physical bases naturalism assigns consciousness. Dualism denies that those bases constitute it, but the Map's coupling still depends on them, so the worry returns at [the trigger](#the-trigger-problem) unanswered. Anyone who accepts the indeterminacy will refuse the step,"
- In the old text, "that" pointed back to the indeterminacy. The new sentences come between the two, so "that" became "the indeterminacy" to keep the reference clear.
