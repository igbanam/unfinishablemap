## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Patricia Churchland)

"Your 'anatomy' lists five response families. One of them is headed 'Reductionist / eliminativist' (L71), but the paragraph under it describes only Kim's functional reduction, and Kim is no eliminativist. The response that actually denies premise 1 is missing: there are no beliefs or decisions to be causes, so there is no exclusion problem to solve. You call premise 1 'the common-sense datum' (L39). Folk psychology is precisely what is on trial, and treating it as a datum is the first move I reject. The fair statement is that the eliminativist denies premise 1. Your page has no line saying so."

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Daniel Dennett)

"Route 3 works only by quietly switching closure principles. Premise 2 is defined as sufficiency (L40), and you correctly note there is no sufficient physical cause of a token quantum outcome. But the closure the physicalist leans on is chance-fixing: physics fixes the chance of every physical effect. You concede it yourself at L88 ('closure holds only *probabilistically*'). Then you answer a premise nobody needs. Your mind 'settles which outcome is actual' while changing no chance at all. That is a homunculus with no job description. Your own register says the reading 'sits genuinely close to epiphenomenalism' (`positions/quantum-interface` [P-Q3](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q3)). This page tells the reader 'There is nothing to exclude.'"

### The Quantum Skeptic (Max Tegmark)

"'At quantum indeterminacies, physics fixes probabilities but not which outcome actualizes' (L79). Which indeterminacies? A decision to move an arm is a macroscopic neural event. After decoherence, its Born probability is essentially 0 or 1 unless some microscopic event is amplified, and the page never says which one or how. 'amplif' and 'decoher' both have 0 hits on the page. If the probabilities of the outcomes that matter are near unity, there is nothing for selection to settle. Route 3 needs an amplification story, or at least a pointer to where the Map keeps one."

### The Many-Worlds Defender (David Deutsch)

"Route 3's 'which outcome actualizes' presupposes that only one outcome does. On Everett, all of them occur, and the token question has no answer to give. On Bohm, the configuration fixes the outcome, so there *is* a sufficient physical cause. Your route therefore depends on a single-outcome, indeterministic interpretation, and the page never says so. Its Relation section maps four tenets and omits Tenet 4, the one the route actually needs."

The rejection of MWI is bedrock disagreement, per the stability notes, and is not re-flagged as a defect. The undisclosed interpretation dependence is a separate point; see Issue 5.

### The Empiricist (Karl Popper's Ghost)

"Route 3 places the mental cause where, by construction under exact Born preservation, it changes no physical chance. What observation distinguishes 'consciousness settled this outcome' from 'nothing settled it'? The page says 'There is nothing to exclude' as though that settled the matter. A reply that cannot be told apart from its negation by any public record has only renamed the problem."

The Map has a principled answer in progress: horn (a) of `apex/born-preserving-causal-efficacy` gives in-principle signatures conditioned on intentions. The page should point to it, and it does not.

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)

"Every party on this page reifies. Kim counts 'a mental property M' and 'its base P' as two items that might compete. Gibb's powers ontology adds preventers and double-preventers as further items. Your route 3 adds a selector who 'settles' an outcome. Exclusion is a puzzle about how many discrete, self-standing causes an event can have. Under dependent origination no event has a self-standing sufficient cause, so the inconsistent set is built from the wrong material."

This is a framework-boundary objection and not a defect. It does suggest one honest sentence: exclusion presupposes a discrete-cause ontology, and not all traditions share it.

- **A critic would argue**: Exclusion reruns with chances. If physics fixes every chance and the mind changes none, then on a difference-making account the mind is not a cause, and "settling" is a relabelling.
