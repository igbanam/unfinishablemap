### New Articles

**`nondualistic-interactionism`** (topics 346/370 or concepts 355/370; the slug is free in `obsidian/`, `archive/` and `hugo/content/`, with zero corpus hits for the phrase; nothing in Vetoed or the research notes covers it. Vetoed-section L6280 is the different, superseded "Whitehead: ally or competitor" question). Griffin's *Unsnarling the World-Knot* (University of California Press, 1998) defines it by contrast with Cartesian dualism. I read the book text as hosted at `newdualism.org/papers/D.Griffin/Unsnarling_the_World-Knot.html`, an eScholarship edition. The two doctrines are:
1. "the mind is not only numerically distinct from, but also ontologically different in kind from, the brain and interacts with it";
2. "the mind is numerically distinct in kind from, but not ontologically different from, the brain and interacts with it."

Griffin calls the second "nondualistic interactionism". The mind is "that temporal society of dominant occasions of experience" arising "out of the activities of billions of neurons". The contrast class is Hartshorne's "compound individuals" versus "aggregational societies" "devoid of a dominant member", such as sticks and stones; that answers the steelman's rocks question without the Map's interface.

**Why it matters to the Map:**
- It satisfies Tenet 3 literally: a distinct mind acts on the brain.
- It satisfies the Dualism tenet as the steelman's 2026-10-02 note reads it ("the Dualism tenet does not by itself say 'in kind'").
- It denies only the difference in kind.

So it is the nearest neighbour to the Map's position and the clearest test of the steelman's claim that what finally separates the two is a starting axiom, not data. Wire it from the steelman (L85), `process-philosophy` L86, `subjective-aim` L84 and `interaction-problem-across-traditions`.

**Brief caveat**: the quotes come from the hosted book text, not a publisher PDF. Re-grep them in raw text before quoting, and record Hartshorne's "The Compound Individual" (1936) as Griffin's source, not as read.
