## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **Dropped qualifier ("strict") changed what Davidson is said to hold.** In three places the article had Davidson concluding there is *no* psychophysical law at all: the lead ("governed by no psychophysical law at all"), "Where they split" ("there is *no* psychophysical law") and Relation to Site Perspective ("takes the no-law exit"). That contradicted the article's own quotation of principle 3, which denies only *strict* laws. Raw "Mental Events" allows "rough heteronomic generalizations, which are lawlike in that instances make it reasonable to expect other instances to follow suit". The 1974 essay allows "rough correlations". According to SEP *Anomalous Monism* (§6, citing Davidson 1993, 9–12), Davidson "allows for *ceteris paribus* psychological and psychophysical laws". SEP *Mental Causation* adds: "Davidson appears to think so (1993, p. 10), as does McLaughlin (1989), who also appeals to hedged laws." **Fixed**: the contrast now runs between Davidson's rough or hedged generalizations, with all causal backing done by strict physical laws, and the Map's posited law that is meant to carry causal work itself. The lead, "Where they split", the Tenet 2 paragraph and the description were all rewritten to match. "Lawless" at L37 and L43 became "escape strict law" and "falls under no strict law".
2. **Source/Map conflation on scope.** "Where they agree" said the Map accepts, "with Davidson", that no type-type reduction *of experience* is on offer. But Davidson's mental is marked by intentionality (raw "Mental Events"), and his anomalism argument concerns the propositional attitudes. SEP (Yalowitz, rev. 2025) puts it verbatim: anomalous monism "does not address the status of mental events such as pains, tickles and the like ... It is concerned exclusively with sapient mental events." **Fixed**: the sentence now says the Map "holds for experience what he argued for the attitudes". A new short paragraph, "Where the scopes differ", quotes both sources and links [articulability-of-q1](/concepts/articulability-of-q1/#the-anomalism-objection), which already makes the narrower-ground reply and records the pressure that survives it.
3. **Tenet calibration: "consciousness biases the probabilities of otherwise-indeterminate quantum outcomes".** This contradicts [P-Q2](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q2), where outcome frequencies stay Born-exact and only *which* outcome becomes actual is selected. **Fixed**: "consciousness settles which otherwise-indeterminate quantum outcome becomes actual, within a corridor that leaves the unconditioned Born statistics intact ([P-Q2](/positions/quantum-interface/#p-q2))". This was the only "biases the probabilities" string in concepts/topics/apex/voids (corpus grep).
4. **Originator claim.** The lead said "first pressed by Honderich in 1982" and the body said "Ted Honderich launched it". SEP *Mental Causation* §5.2 lists "Stoutland 1980; Honderich 1982; Sosa 1984" for the property-irrelevance charge. Stoutland 1980 is real: *Synthese* 43(3), 351–367, doi:10.1007/BF00870293 (Crossref). SEP *Anomalous Monism* calls Honderich "An early critic". **Fixed**: the lead now reads "pressed early by Honderich in 1982" and the body "gave it one of its earliest statements". Stoutland was not added; his paper is paywalled, so its content against Davidson was not read.
5. **Honderich's example detail.** The article had pears that "make the scale read one pound", taken from IEP's "1 lb.". Honderich's own text (homepages.ucl.ac.uk/~uctytho/AnMon.htm, section "(1) THE ARGUMENT FOR ANOMALOUS MONISM") reads: "something green and French did cause the pointer to move to the two-pound mark". **Fixed** to "moves the pointer to the two-pound mark". "Grocer's" (IEP's embellishment) was dropped.
6. **McLaughlin "argues elsewhere" that efficacy in virtue of physical properties does not entail efficacy *only* in virtue of them.** This could not be traced to any locatable work. SEP places McLaughlin's defence in the *same* 1989 paper: "exploiting *ceteris paribus* covering laws for psychophysical causal relations, claiming that this allows mental properties to be sufficient for their effects (McLaughlin 1989; Fodor 1989, 1991)". **Fixed**: "the same paper appeals to hedged, *ceteris paribus* psychophysical laws to argue that mental properties can be causally sufficient for their effects".
7. **Unverifiable quotation: "cross-classify".** It was quote-marked as if Davidson's. It has zero hits in the raw "Mental Events" scan and zero in two Google Books indexes of *Essays on Actions and Events*. **Fixed**: the article now uses Davidson's verified one-line summary instead, and "cross-classify" stands as the article's own word, unquoted.
