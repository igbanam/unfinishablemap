## Executive Summary

The citation scare is a false alarm: all fourteen Merlo 2021 quotations and all five Lipman 2023 *Ergo* quotations are verbatim in the raw full texts (Wayback copy of the Wiley HTML; Wayback copy of the *Ergo* PDF). One quotation is spliced (Kinkaid's abstract), one 2023 citation is ambiguous between two Lipman papers, and Merlo 2016 footnote 19 remains unverifiable at raw. The real problems are structural.

1. The primary article locates the Map wrongly on its own map. It says the Map "stands with the egalitarians against Hare and Merlo" on parity while keeping "the fact *simpliciter* about which subject is this one". A view that affirms every mind is on a par *and* a privileged fact about which subject is lived is Merlo's two-level subjectivism in all but name — Merlo himself keeps parity at the objective level. The article then says the Map "declines" the egalitarian exit at a price, whereas the two sibling pages it cites say the Map's commitments *force* that exit. Both cannot be right, and the article does not notice.
2. The void article's lede states that every self-locating rule "carries a reductio its own defenders accept as one". Defenders of SIA (Olum 2002) and of SSA (Bostrom, with reference-class relativism) do not accept the Presumptuous Philosopher or Doomsday as reductios; they are reductios *pressed by critics*. The same overstatement has already been propagated into `concepts/indexical-knowledge-and-identity` L130 by today's integration edit, and L156 of that same page makes the evidential move the void says the Map forgoes.
3. The Charles Bonnet article says the compensation reading is retired and "the other loci now converge on that wording". Two hosts do not: `perceptual-failure-and-the-interface` L96 still attributes hypnagogic imagery to "the interface" one sentence after the correction, under a bold head that still reads "Compensatory phenomena", and `dualist-perception` L66 and L136 still count "compensatory generation" and "the interface generating phenomenal content" as converging evidence for the interface.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Patricia Churchland)
"Feltness", "the lived perspective", "the fact simpliciter about which subject is this one": the whole article is an argument about where to file a posit that neuroscience has no use for. Merlo's Exercise 1 is introspection treated as a measuring instrument; Kinkaid's reply is a second introspection. The article's one reply with teeth — that the Appearance/Reality Principle is shared, so the dispute is about what appears — concedes her point: once appearance is the only tribunal, no mature science of the brain can adjudicate, and the Map has chosen to decide its metaphysics in a court she does not recognise. *Map response available*: bedrock, and the article says so ("contested at bedrock"). But see Issue 1 — the Map has not yet said which seat in that court it occupies.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Daniel Dennett)
The article runs Merlo's inference — "it is part of how they appear that only my experiences have feltness" (how things seem) to "at the level of reality my experiences differ" (how things are) — and never names it as the step where an epistemic datum is cashed as a metaphysical fact. It reports the Appearance/Reality Principle as the bridge, which is right, but the Map's *own* primitive "I" rides the same bridge: the "vertiginous datum" is a seeming that is being asked to pay for a fact simpliciter. Nothing in the article checks that the datum survives the heterophenomenological move (describe the seeming, withhold the fact). This is the epistemic/metaphysical equivocation check from the review protocol; the article passes it only because it attributes the step to Merlo rather than making it. When the Map makes the same step on its own behalf (lede, "the fact *simpliciter* about which subject is this one"), the hedge is one clause, "contested at bedrock", and the bridge is unnamed.

### The Quantum Skeptic (Max Tegmark)
No decoherence content; the article is correctly silent on mechanism. One thing he would notice: the Bidirectional Interaction paragraph says the pairing of "each subject with one physical channel" is why fragments-within-one-world and many-physical-worlds "stay separable". That is an appeal to a law the Map admits it does not have ([P-SC2](/positions/subject-census/#p-sc2), "owed"). The separability is being secured by an IOU.

### The Many-Worlds Defender (David Deutsch)
The article is unusually honest here and he would say so: it concedes that if the Map took the egalitarian exit, the dissolution of "why am I this subject?" becomes "available word for word to the Everettian for 'why am I this branch?'". He would press the converse. If the Map instead keeps a primitive fact *simpliciter* about which subject is lived, why may the Everettian not keep a primitive fact simpliciter about which branch is lived? "Fragmentalism multiplies stacks of facts about one history, not histories" is a difference in *what* is multiplied, not an argument that only one kind of multiplication may carry a primitive. The article's one answer — the pairing law — is the IOU noted above.

### The Empiricist (Karl Popper's Ghost)
A subjective fact is defined as a fact that obtains from some points of view but not all; no third-person observation can register one by construction. The article should say plainly that nothing in it is falsifiable and that the "verdict" is a bookkeeping result about the Map's own dependency graph. It very nearly does ("This is a verdict about the Map's own dependency structure, not a refutation of either side"). What he would strike is the lede's "the egalitarian view handles other minds *at least as well as* the Map's own posit" — a comparative empirical-sounding claim about explanatory performance with no criterion stated for "handles".

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)
Standpoint pluralism is the nearest thing in analytic metaphysics to dependent origination for subjects: a self that obtains only relative to a standpoint, with no self simpliciter. The article's reason for declining it — "the Map keeps that fact because Tenet 4 needs it" — is an admission that the self is being retained for the sake of an argument elsewhere. He would call that clinging with a footnote. The tenets page already concedes the Madhyamaka exit at the framework boundary (tenets.md L121); the article should cross-reference that concession instead of treating the deflationist as only Lewis and Perry.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The Map's own position is Merlo's two-level subjectivism, and the article files it with the egalitarians
- **File**: `obsidian/topics/subjective-facts-and-the-metaphysical-problem-of-other-minds.md`
- **Location**: lede paragraph 2 ("what it costs the Map is the fact *simpliciter* about which subject is this one … The Map keeps that fact"); "Implications → Other minds — calibration" ("on parity it already stands with the egalitarians against Hare and Merlo"); "Relation to Site Perspective → No Many Worlds" ("The Map declines it because Tenet 4 needs the fact").
- **Problem**: Merlo's position, which the article reports accurately in "Merlo's Third Problem", is: at the objective level "All minds are 'on a par'"; at the subjective level "a spotlight of feltness shines on all and only my experiences". That is *exactly* the pair the Map holds — every subject fully conscious (parity) plus a fact simpliciter that *this* subject is the lived one (the spotlight). So the Map does not stand "with the egalitarians against Hare and Merlo"; it stands with Merlo, and inherits the "fine line" Merlo concedes "may be debated whether [it] can be coherently sustained". The article quotes that concession and never applies it to the Map. Worse, the quadrilemma the article invokes in its lede says a fact simpliciter of this kind cannot be held together with non-solipsism and one world: `vertiginous-question` L107 says the Map "must accept fragmentation", and `egocentric-presentism` L81 says that "a fragmentalism that privileges one fragment collapses back towards Hare". The new article says the Map *declines* the fragmentalist exit at a price. The sibling pages say it is *forced* onto it. Both are live on the site today.
- **Severity**: High. This is the article's thesis sentence, and it contradicts the two pages it cites for support.
- **Recommendation**: Priority item 1. Replace "stands with the egalitarians against Hare and Merlo" with the accurate location: the Map's posit is Merlo-shaped (parity at one level, a privileged fact at another) and so inherits Merlo's fine line. Then state the fork the quadrilemma forces and pick a wording: either (a) the Map's "fact simpliciter" is a *privileged-fragment* fragmentalism, in which case it should say it is Hare-adjacent and that parity holds only at the objective level, or (b) the Map concedes the triad is not yet consistent and the primitive "I" is held pending that choice. Either is honest; "declines at a price" is not available. Amend the lede's "handles other minds at least as well as" to a non-comparative statement (see Language Improvements). Headroom 762; the fix is one paragraph of roughly 120 words plus two sentence edits.

### Issue 2: "A reductio its own defenders accept as one" is false of the defenders, and has been propagated
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/observer-selection-void.md` (lede, and "The Rule Face" last sentence: "The two best-developed rules escape each other's reductio by accepting their own"); sibling `obsidian/concepts/indexical-knowledge-and-identity.md` L130 ("each carry a reductio their defenders accept").
- **Problem**: The Presumptuous Philosopher is Bostrom's argument *against* SIA; Olum (2002, *PQ* 52(207), Crossref-checked) defends SIA and accepts the presumptuous verdict as correct rather than as a reductio, which is why Bostrom and Ćirković (2003) had to reply. Doomsday is likewise pressed against SSA by critics; Bostrom's own SSA-with-reference-class-relativism treats the argument as inconclusive, not as an absurdity he accepts. Neal's full non-indexical conditioning does not accept any reductio at all. The honest claim is that each rule carries a consequence *its critics present as a reductio and its defenders bite*. The void's whole "rule face" rests on this characterisation, and today's integration edit copied it into the indexical-knowledge page.
- **Severity**: Medium (the void's overall conclusion survives; the sentence that states it is wrong about three named authors).
- **Recommendation**: Priority item 2. Void headroom is 115, so the fix must be length-neutral: "carries a reductio its own defenders accept as one" becomes "carries a consequence its critics call a reductio and its defenders bite" (+1 word); "by accepting their own" becomes "by biting their own". ## Counterarguments to Address

### Hellie's "egalitarianism" is aimed at a dualist, not at Lewis and Perry
- **Current content says**: the "Deny subjective facts (Lewis, Perry …)" branch of the decision tree "is Benj Hellie's target in 'Against Egalitarianism' (2013), which coined the label".
- **A critic would argue**: Hellie's paper is a critical notice of Chalmers 2010 (its OpenAlex title is "Against Egalitarianism [on Chalmers (2010)]", and the abstract traces "a range of worries about Chalmers's philosophical system … to a background presupposition of egalitarianism"). Hellie's target is an egalitarian *property dualist* — a view with irreducible phenomenal facts and no privileged stream. That is structurally the Map's own Tenet 1 position minus the spotlight. Filing Hellie under the deflationist exit hides that the vertiginous charge lands on egalitarian dualism as much as on deflation, which is the pressure that produces Issue 1. "Coined" is unsupported: Builes says only that Hellie "argues against 'egalitarian' views"; Merlo's "Specialness and Egalitarianism" is the same year.
- **Suggested response**: move Hellie out of the deflationist bullet into a sentence of his own: his egalitarian is any view, dualist included, that treats streams as on a par; name Chalmers as the stated target; replace "coined the label" with "gave the label its currency".

### The Everettian gets the same primitive
- **Current content says**: fragments within one world "remain distinguishable from many physical worlds — fragmentalism multiplies stacks of facts about one history, not histories — but the indexical lever shortens".
- **A critic would argue**: as Deutsch above. If a primitive fact simpliciter is admissible for subjects, it is admissible for branches, and the Map's reason for denying it to the Everettian cannot be that it is a primitive.
- **Suggested response**: say what the Map's actual ground is — the single-outcome actualisation posit (background commitment 2), which is a claim about physics rather than about primitives — and cite [P-I2](/positions/individuation-and-subjecthood/#p-i2)'s "Would shift if", which already books that the tenet may have to stand on "single-outcome actualisation, the probability problem" alone.
