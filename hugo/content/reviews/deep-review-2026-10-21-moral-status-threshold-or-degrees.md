## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **McMahan's three-tier refinement: a concession he does not make (attribution / position strength).** Old: "his three-tier refinement — an intermediate status whose constraints vary with capacity (pp. 98–99) — concedes that the line does not hold." McMahan offers the refinement because "Some of these challenges could be addressed by refining the account" (p. 97). The refinement *keeps* a higher threshold, with "maximum inviolability" and equality above it (p. 98). His own verdict is "While the three-tiered account addresses some of the objections … there are some that it does not address at all. And it may well be that some of these other challenges cannot be answered" (p. 101). **New**: "Three arguments tell against the second line. McMahan's own challenges attack the threshold of respect; his three-tier refinement — … (pp. 98–99) — answers only some of them, and he allows that others may be unanswerable (p. 101)." The framing sentence "Three arguments say the second line cannot be held" became "Three arguments tell against the second line", because McMahan's limb is a "may well be".
2. **DeGrazia 2012 conflated with Douglas by "likewise" (attribution, and a missed ally).** Old: "Douglas (2013) argues that Buchanan's response fails, grants that status-raising enhancement is possible …; DeGrazia (2012) likewise holds post-persons possible." The Crossref-deposited abstract shows DeGrazia grants a higher status *only* on a respect-based model: "A better option, I tentatively suggest, is to drop the idea of levels of moral status, accept that all sentient beings have moral status…". That is the Map's verdict, reached in reply to Buchanan. "Likewise" placed him on Douglas's side. **New**: "DeGrazia (2012) holds post-persons possible but would tentatively "drop the idea of levels of moral status" — the Map's verdict." **Upstream source found**: the research note's L196 "Sides" line lists "DeGrazia 2012 (possibly yes)". It was annotated in place so the error does not propagate again (see Sibling Sweep).
3. **Internal tension: the Map's position was stated as McMahan's lower tier *wholesale*, while the article holds the constraints question open (internal contradiction, plus a source finding).** L79 said "the Map's position is that tier extended to everyone with standing". McMahan's lower tier is "governed solely by a proper concern for their good"; its interests "may be treated in a consequentialist manner" (p. 98). He names exactly this extension and prices it: "One possibility would be to extend the principles that … govern the treatment of individuals below the lower threshold so that they instead govern the treatment of all individuals … This option would involve repudiation of all constraints on action other than those imposed by considerations of consequences" (p. 101). Adopting the tier wholesale therefore closes the non-aggregative flank that L89 declares open. **New L79**: "the Map's position extends that tier's measure of stakes to everyone with standing, with the respect tier deleted." **New L89** (flank kept open, and now honest about what the source says): "McMahan notes that extending his lower tier's principles to all would repudiate every constraint not imposed by consequences (2008, p. 101); whether one threshold with graded stakes escapes that result is open." The old "can be married to constraints" became "escapes that result".
