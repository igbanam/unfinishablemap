# Referee Report: "Designing a Direction-Dependent Discriminating Test for the Interface Model"

## 1. Introduction and General Assessment of the Manuscript

The manuscript under review, "Designing a Direction-Dependent Discriminating Test for the Interface Model," outlines a proposed empirical methodology intended to adjudicate between materialist ("production") and non-reductive, dualist ("filter" or "transmission") accounts of consciousness. The central thesis posits that experiments featuring bidirectional, reversible perturbations of consciousness—specifically, those tracking direction-dependent orderings in the loss and subsequent recovery of cognitive and phenomenal states—can isolate empirical signatures that are uniquely predicted by a dualist, consciousness-first framework. By analyzing biological edge cases and altered states, including anaesthetic hysteresis, paradoxical lucidity, and psychedelic phenomenology, the author claims to have identified structural asymmetries that production models inherently fail to anticipate, thereby providing empirical validation for an interactionist dualist "interface".

Subjecting this manuscript to rigorous philosophical and neuroscientific scrutiny reveals profound conceptual, methodological, and empirical failures that entirely invalidate its central thesis. The paper suffers from a crippling epistemological circularity, relies upon a deeply antiquated and scientifically illiterate strawman of physicalist theories of mind, and betrays a comprehensive ignorance of the recent, paradigm-defining advances in computational neuroscience, dynamic systems theory, and multivariate information topology that have emerged in the 2020s. Specifically, the manuscript systematically mistakes the well-documented complex, non-linear dynamics of biological neural networks for non-physical "interface friction". Furthermore, the text repeatedly frames empirical data using a vocabulary that assumes the very dualist ontological gap the proposed test is ostensibly designed to establish.

The remit of this review is to rigorously audit the manuscript's empirical claims against the 2020–2025 literature, evaluate its logical inferences, and expose its systematic failure to engage with contemporary physicalist counterarguments. The ensuing analysis demonstrates that every "direction-dependent asymmetry" the author attributes to an irreducible, multi-channel dualist filter is not merely compatible with modern materialist frameworks, but is actively and mathematically predicted by them.

## 2. Epistemological and Methodological Failures: Circularity and Falsifiability

The foundational failure of the manuscript is located in its epistemological framing. For any empirical test to be genuinely "discriminating" between two competing metaphysical paradigms, it must offer a strictly falsifiable prediction that cleanly partitions the hypotheses. The author proposes that a "direction-dependent" asymmetry—a scenario wherein the sequence of cognitive degradation during a state perturbation (such as anaesthetic induction) is not the precise mirror image of the sequence of cognitive return during recovery (emergence)—serves as decisive evidence for a dualist filter.

However, the author's formulation betrays a fatal logical circularity. The manuscript posits that a naive "unified-switch" production model must inherently predict a perfectly symmetrical degradation and return of consciousness, while a filter theory naturally accommodates asymmetry because "different channels" of the putative interface reopen under varying constraints and friction. This rhetorical setup establishes a false dichotomy. The author acknowledges internally that the philosophical dispute is "underdetermined by the evidence the two frameworks share", noting explicitly that "what is at issue is which description of the same facts to treat as fundamental". If the debate is genuinely underdetermined by the empirical data at the level of foundational axioms, then no direction-dependent ordering can serve as a strict empirical discriminator.

This conceptual oversight violates the "hard criteria for empirical theories of consciousness" firmly established in the contemporary literature. Doerig, Schurger, and Herzog (2021) demonstrate compellingly that empirical theories of consciousness must provide rigorous, mechanistic explanations for paradigm cases without collapsing into unrestricted underdetermination or relying on unfalsifiable axioms. The manuscript's proposed discriminating test is entirely unfalsifiable in practice. If an asymmetrical recovery gradient is found, the author claims it is evidence of "interface friction"; if no asymmetry is found, it can simply be argued post hoc that the specific "filter channel" under investigation happens to operate symmetrically. Because the filter theory lacks independent, physical criteria defining *why* and *when* a metaphysical constraint should loosen rather than tighten, it functions as a fluid post-hoc redescription rather than a predictive, testable scientific hypothesis.

A rigorous physicalist model does not require the assumption of an ontological gap to explain asymmetrical recovery gradients. The manuscript's assertion that "the gap reappears as a question about the gradient" is a philosophical sleight of hand. It operates on a premise heavily contested by modern eliminative materialists, deflationists, and illusionists: the assumption that a "hard problem" of consciousness exists as a concrete physical datum rather than a linguistic or conceptual confusion born of folk psychology. The proposed discriminating test does not test for dualism; it merely tests for the presence of non-linear state transitions in the brain, relabeling ubiquitous complex physical dynamics as metaphysical "transmissions."

## 3. Non-Linear Dynamics and the Strawman of Monotonic Production

To sustain the illusion that direction-dependent asymmetry poses a fatal threat to materialism, the author relies entirely on a caricatured, strawman version of physicalist theory. The manuscript repeatedly contrasts the "filter theory" (tracing its lineage back to William James, Henri Bergson, and Aldous Huxley) with a "naive unified-switch production model". In this naive model, consciousness is viewed as a monolithic block generated in a strictly linear, monotonic relationship with neural substrate activity: more substrate activity equals more consciousness, and any perturbation must result in a symmetrical, linear degradation and recovery.

By targeting this simplistic "unified-switch" model, the author orchestrates a hollow victory for the filter framework. The manuscript completely ignores the last two decades of non-equilibrium thermodynamics, critical brain dynamics, and complex systems theory. In computational neuroscience, the brain is modeled as a highly recurrent, non-linear dynamical system operating near criticality. In such systems, the relationship between structural substrate and emergent functional state is inherently non-monotonic.

The logic of the manuscript's proposed test can be formalized as follows:

1. **Premise 1:** Production models dictate that state A (consciousness) is entirely and directly determined by physical substrate state X (neural activity).
2. **Premise 2:** Therefore, if X is perturbed away from its baseline and then subsequently returns to its exact baseline configuration, A must degrade and return in a perfectly symmetrical, order-dependent manner.
3. **Observation:** Empirical data (e.g., anaesthesia hysteresis, paradoxical kinesia, varying recovery orders) demonstrates that the return path from Xperturbed to Xbaseline is profoundly asymmetrical and state-dependent.
4. **Conclusion:** Therefore, production models are falsified, and a non-reductive "filter" or "multi-channel interface" must be mediating the interaction.

Premise 2 is demonstrably false in any physical system exhibiting hysteresis, plasticity, or metastability. The brain is not a memoryless, Markovian system; its current state and its trajectory through state space are strictly dependent on its history. The trajectory of a dynamical system moving through a multidimensional state space during a perturbation (e.g., the progressive saturation of varied receptor affinities across distinct cortical layers during anaesthetic induction) will naturally and inevitably carve a completely different topological path during emergence, because the network is initiating its return journey from an entirely different attractor basin.

To claim that this trajectory asymmetry is evidence of a non-physical "interface" is an astonishing category error. It reveals a profound lack of familiarity with continuous differential equations, non-equilibrium thermodynamics, and basic computational modeling. The manuscript effectively demands that a physicalist brain must behave like a simple digital light switch, and upon discovering that it behaves instead like a complex, recurrent, history-dependent neural network, falsely declares an empirical victory for dualism.

## 4. Anaesthetic Hysteresis and the Misappropriation of Neural Inertia

The most egregious empirical distortion in the manuscript is its treatment of anaesthetic hysteresis, commonly referred to in the literature as "neural inertia." The author leverages the well-documented pharmacological phenomenon wherein the drug concentration required to induce unconsciousness is significantly higher than the drug concentration at which consciousness eventually returns. The manuscript frames this "recovery-order reversal" and "stochastic emergence" as definitive evidence of an active "reboot mechanism" unique to a bidirectional mind-brain interface.

The underlying logic asserted by the author is that because emergence is not the mere pharmacokinetic reverse of induction, the brain cannot simply be "producing" consciousness based on raw substrate availability. Instead, the author argues, consciousness is actively struggling against "interface friction" to reopen a high-cost channel. This interpretation is utterly dismantled by the 2020–2025 neuroscientific literature on neural inertia, which provides comprehensive, strictly physicalist mechanisms for this hysteresis without any need for dualist intervention.

### Thermodynamic Bistability and Attractor Dynamics

Physical systems exhibiting hysteresis are ubiquitous in nature (e.g., ferromagnetism, phase transitions in water) and absolutely do not require dualist interfaces to explain their asymmetrical state changes. In modern computational neuroscience, anaesthetic state transitions are strictly modeled as a system poised between bistable attractor states. A brain transitioning into and out of general anaesthesia is undergoing a phase transition governed by neural network dynamics.

Kim et al. (2021) and subsequent researchers have provided direct empirical evidence supporting stochastic models of neural inertia, demonstrating that the transition barrier between the awake and anaesthetized states is a highly conserved, quantifiable physical property of the mammalian brain. When anaesthetic concentration increases, the robust, highly integrated neural network resists state changes until a critical thermodynamic threshold is breached, at which point the system plummets into a low-activity, low-entropy attractor state. During emergence, the network remains trapped in this energetic valley even as the drug concentration drops below the initial induction threshold. It requires a distinct level of activation energy to exit the well. This is a classical thermodynamic phase transition. The manuscript's assertion that hysteresis defies physicalist production is akin to claiming that because water freezes and thaws at slightly different energetic thresholds under specific pressures, a non-physical "water-spirit" must be struggling against the structural constraints of the ice.

### Distinct Neurobiological Pathways for Induction and Emergence

Furthermore, the manuscript entirely omits the recent, highly specific empirical discoveries isolating the exact neuroanatomical substrates responsible for asymmetrical emergence. The asymmetry exists for a very simple, physical reason: the brain does not use the identical neural circuitry to wake up as it does to fall asleep.

Current evidence shows that multiple brain nuclei and circuits play divergent, non-overlapping roles in anaesthesia induction versus emergence. For instance, orexinergic pathways, which control the stability of sleep/wake dynamics, heavily and specifically influence the hysteresis gap during emergence, but play little role in the initial loss of consciousness. More recently, the inhibition and activation of specific paraventricular hypothalamic corticotropin-releasing hormone (PVH CRH) neurons have been shown to directly modulate the state of consciousness and the stress response specifically during the emergence from sevoflurane general anaesthesia. Optogenetically activating these specific PVH CRH neurons accelerates the recovery process without equivalently altering the induction process.

The manuscript's attempt to brand this asymmetry as a "direction-dependent discriminating test" for dualism completely collapses in the face of these precise cellular findings. Physicalist models predict exactly the complex, multi-pathway, temporally disjointed recovery order that is observed clinically. The manuscript's failure to engage with the actual neuropharmacological mechanisms of neural inertia—preferring instead to lazily cite the macroscopic phenomenon as a generic "signature" of an interface—renders its empirical claims obsolete.

## 5. The Evasion of Predictive Processing and Active Inference

The manuscript's reliance on a strawman version of materialism leads to its second fatal omission: the complete evasion of Predictive Processing (PP) and the Free Energy Principle, which currently dominate the cognitive science of consciousness.

The core conceptual insight of the manuscript's dualist filter theory—the idea that the brain acts as a "reducing valve" constraining cosmic awareness to a biologically useful subset, and that reducing brain activity can therefore paradoxically "expand" experience—is presented as a mortal challenge to physicalism. In reality, it is the foundational premise of predictive processing.

### The Brain as a Statistical Inference Filter

In the predictive processing framework, elegantly detailed by Seth and Bayne (2022) in their comprehensive review of theories of consciousness, phenomenal experience is grounded in allostatic, control-oriented predictive inference. The brain does not passively "produce" a unified conscious picture by simply building up sensory data from the bottom up. Rather, it is a proactive inference engine that constantly generates top-down predictions (priors) to suppress, constrain, and explain away bottom-up sensory prediction errors.

Under this model, the material brain *is literally a filter*. It filters out the overwhelming, chaotic noise of the exteroceptive and interoceptive environment by imposing rigid, biologically constrained internal models. Physicalism does not need to posit an immaterial soul to explain why the brain acts as a constraint on experience; the constraint is the very mechanism of perception.

### Psychedelics and the REBUS Model

This omission becomes glaring when the manuscript attempts to use psychedelic states as a "window into the interface." The author argues that the expansion of consciousness amidst decreased default-mode network (DMN) activity supports a non-reductive filter theory, pointing out that psychedelics dissolve ego boundaries while enhancing phenomenal intensity. The author explicitly admits that this phenomenon is accommodated by the physicalist REBUS (Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics) model, but hastily and illegitimately dismisses REBUS by claiming it "risks predictions filter theory does not make," without ever elaborating on why the dualist theory provides a mathematically or empirically superior explanation.

The REBUS model, advanced by Carhart-Harris and Friston, provides a mathematically rigorous, fully physicalist account for the exact phenomenological changes the manuscript attributes to dualism. Serotonergic psychedelics like DMT, psilocybin, and LSD act as agonists primarily at the 5-HT2A receptor, which is heavily concentrated in high-level associative networks like the DMN. The primary computational effect of these psychedelics is to decrease the precision weighting of high-level priors.

When these top-down constraints (the physical filter) are relaxed by the drug, bottom-up sensory and limbic information floods the conscious workspace. This results in an increased entropic state, heightened neural signal diversity, and a flattening of the predictive cortical hierarchy. This neuropharmacological mechanism accounts for the "expanded perception, heightened emotional depth, and ego dissolution" perfectly within a materialist paradigm.

The manuscript's assertion that "on production models, disrupted neural organization should produce disrupted consciousness" is a blatant misrepresentation of contemporary systems neuroscience. Disrupted high-level neural organization (such as the DMN) disrupts *inhibition* and *constraint*, thereby radically *increasing* lower-level neural variance and signal complexity. The dualist filter-loosening argument is entirely redundant. Physicalism already possesses a robust, computationally sound filter-loosening model (REBUS), making the author's appeal to interactionist dualism a direct violation of Occam's Razor.

Furthermore, recent, high-resolution neuroimaging studies of DMT in the human brain (Timmermann et al., 2023, 2024) demonstrate profound, measurable changes in brain wave frequencies, such as the emergence of prominent theta/delta rhythmicity and heavily reduced alpha/beta rhythmicity. This specifically reflects a switch from processing exogenously incoming information to processing endogenously driven information, akin to the mechanisms of REM sleep dreaming. The manuscript fails entirely to engage with the reality that these supposedly "expanded" or "cosmic" states correlate directly with highly specific, measurable alterations in physical signal diversity and connectivity. There is no empirical justification whatsoever to posit an "outbound leg" where consciousness independently acts back upon the brain to explain psychedelic phenomenology.

## 6. The Architecture of Asymmetry: Multivariate Information and IIT 4.0

The manuscript's argument rests heavily on the concept of "independent dissociation". The author notes that during altered states, different components of consciousness—such as phenomenal presence, voluntary attention, memory, and agency—degrade at different rates. The author claims this proves the existence of a "multi-channel interface" rather than a "unified product of neural activity".

This argument constructs yet another strawman, assuming blindly that a materialist brain must generate consciousness as an indivisible, unified block that degrades uniformly across all dimensions simultaneously. In doing so, the manuscript completely ignores the last decade of network neuroscience, topological data analysis, and multivariate information theory, which mathematically define exactly why network degradation is inherently and necessarily asymmetrical.

### Multivariate Information Theory and Synergistic Subsystems

Recent advances in multivariate information theory, most notably the work by Varley et al. (2023), have uncovered that the human cerebral cortex is composed of highly synergistic subsystems. Traditional analyses of the brain, which the manuscript relies upon for its critiques, relied on pairwise functional connectivity, which can only capture redundant information sharing between two nodes. However, utilizing advanced measures like O-information, researchers have demonstrated the widespread existence of higher-order interactions where information is strictly *synergistic*—meaning the information exists only in the joint state of multiple variables and cannot be found in any individual constituent part.

Consciousness, in modern physicalist theories, is deeply and inextricably tied to these higher-order synergistic structures. When the brain is subjected to a perturbation (e.g., general anaesthesia, psychedelics, or neurodegeneration), these synergistic subsystems do not collapse in a linear or symmetrical fashion. Because synergistic information is topologically distributed, the removal or suppression of specific nodes (via targeted receptor agonism or antagonism) causes highly non-linear network reconfigurations.

The manuscript makes much of the fact that propofol (a GABA-A agonist) and ketamine (an NMDA antagonist) produce vastly different phenomenological outcomes despite both causing behavioural unresponsiveness. The author attributes this to the selective tuning of distinct channels in a "dualist interface". However, multivariate information theory and dynamic causal modeling provide the precise, mechanistic physical explanation: GABA-A agonism globally suppresses cortical integration and synergistic information sharing, causing true unconsciousness; NMDA antagonism (ketamine) uncouples frontal-parietal top-down connectivity while preserving, or even hyper-activating, localized sensory generators in the posterior cortex, leading to a dissociated state rich in unconstrained phenomenological content. No metaphysical interface is required. The specific chemical topology of the physical network fully dictates the specific asymmetry of the resulting conscious state.

### Integrated Information Theory (IIT 4.0)

The manuscript's failure to engage deeply with structural, information-based theories of consciousness is most glaring in its complete omission of Integrated Information Theory (IIT) 4.0. Albantakis et al. (2023) recently formulated IIT 4.0, which proposes that the properties of phenomenal existence are identical to the properties of a physical system's maximally irreducible cause-effect structure (the Φ-structure).

IIT explicitly predicts that consciousness is specific, integrated, and definite, and that it fluctuates dynamically based on the precise architecture of the neural substrate. Under IIT, the "independent dissociation" of consciousness components (e.g., the loss of agency while retaining phenomenal presence during a psychedelic trip or dream state) is a direct, mathematically calculable result of the Φ-structure fracturing along specific structural fault lines (partitions separating weakly interacting parts of the system).

The manuscript attempts to argue that because the loss of substrate does not always lead to an immediate, symmetrical loss of phenomenal experience, production theories are thereby falsified. But IIT 4.0 mathematically demonstrates that certain types of network degradation can actually *increase* integration (and therefore, theoretically, specific aspects of phenomenal intensity) if the degradation selectively prunes noisy, non-contributing nodes and leaves a more densely interconnected core complex. Physicalist models have moved far beyond the "less substrate = less consciousness" monotonic equation that the manuscript relies upon to justify its dualism. By ignoring IIT 4.0 and O-information metrics, the manuscript audits a version of materialism that has not existed in serious academic discourse for twenty years.

## 7. The Misappropriation of Terminal Lucidity and Anoxic Depolarization

To further cement the argument for a dualist interface, the manuscript frequently invokes the phenomena of "terminal lucidity" and the surge of high-frequency gamma activity observed in the dying brain. The text highlights recent high-profile findings by Vicente et al. (2022) and Xu et al. (2023), arguing that this "dramatic cognitive return strains the production model's monotonic prediction that less substrate means less capacity". The author attempts to frame this phenomenon as empirical proof of consciousness returning in full force as the biological filter permanently fails at the moment of death.

This interpretation represents a gross mishandling of the cited clinical literature and a fundamental misunderstanding of the neurophysiology of brain death. The manuscript omits the explicit conclusions and caveats of the very authors it cites, committing a severe error of omission that borders on academic malpractice.

### Hypoxia and Excitotoxic Noise

Vicente et al. (2022) recorded continuous electroencephalography (EEG) from a patient during cardiac arrest, observing a transient increase in absolute EEG power in the gamma band immediately following bilateral hemispheric global hypoxia. However, the biomedical literature does not treat this as the "return of coherent consciousness" or the lifting of a dualist veil. It is exceptionally well-documented that this transient gamma surge is driven by terminal anoxic depolarization.

As the brain is deprived of oxygen (global hypoxia), the energy-dependent sodium-potassium pumps that maintain cellular resting membrane potentials rapidly fail. This leads to a massive, unregulated, and pathological release of excitatory neurotransmitters (primarily glutamate) across the cortex. This excitotoxic flood transiently generates high-frequency oscillations (gamma waves) before the neurons permanently depolarize and undergo necrosis. To equate this pathological, disorganized electrical storm with "phenomenal presence" or a "loosening of the filter" is highly speculative, biologically illiterate, and empirically unjustified.

### The Dying-Moment Dream Hypothesis

Even if we charitably grant that this terminal gamma surge possesses some organized cognitive content (as some symbolic transfer entropy studies in animal models of cardiac arrest suggest), it is easily accommodated by physicalist models without resorting to dualism. The "Dying-Moment Dream Hypothesis" synthesizes these mechanisms into a neurocognitive model grounded in predictive processing.

Under extreme metabolic stress, the breakdown of inhibitory interneuron networks leads to a transient, hyper-associative REM-like state. The brain, entirely cut off from exogenous sensory input, relies entirely on endogenous priors—deeply encoded memories and affective states—to generate a final, closed-loop simulation before total system failure.

The manuscript's core claim that a terminal surge "points away from the production reading" because "less substrate means less capacity" relies on an elementary misunderstanding of brain energy budgets. The healthy brain is an actively inhibited system. It expends massive amounts of metabolic energy keeping excitatory neurons *quiet* and tightly regulated. When metabolic energy is removed via hypoxia, this active inhibition fails first, leading to a paradoxical, transient spike in unregulated excitatory activity. Physicalism predicts this exact non-monotonic physiological spike perfectly. The author's attempt to hijack this physiological death-throe as evidence for a dualist interface is both scientifically inaccurate and logically spurious.

## 8. Structural and Methodological Weaknesses: The Problem of Selective Bounding

Beyond the conceptual overreach regarding neurobiology, the manuscript suffers from structural and methodological defects that clearly preclude its publication in any top-tier philosophy of mind or cognitive science journal. Chief among these is the author's blatant use of selective bounding to immunize the filter theory against falsification.

The manuscript's treatment of Locked-In Syndrome (LIS) perfectly exemplifies this confirmation bias. The author admits that LIS—where consciousness persists fully intact behind a broken, paralyzed output channel—is a negative or "bounding case" where the concept of "filter-loosening" does not apply. The author frames this admission as a mark of intellectual honesty.

However, structurally, this move is highly convenient and epistemologically suspect: whenever biological damage results in the preservation or theoretical expansion of experience (as in psychedelics or terminal lucidity), it is triumphantly lauded as evidence for the filter model. Yet, whenever massive biological damage results in the total severance of output without any "paradoxical release," expansion, or dualist leakage (as in LIS, or deep coma), it is neatly cordoned off as a mere "bounding case" rather than acknowledged as counter-evidence showing that consciousness is strictly tethered to the surviving substrate.

Furthermore, the author fundamentally misunderstands the mathematical concept of entropy as used in neurobiology. The manuscript mentions the "entropic brain hypothesis" and casually equates near-critical neural entropy with "filter bandwidth". However, the author fails to grasp the mathematical reality of differential entropy in continuous random variables. As Varley points out, entropy is not a measure of "information" in the colloquial sense of meaningful, transcendent content; it is strictly a measure of statistical uncertainty and unpredictability. An increase in entropy (as seen under the influence of psychedelics) simply means a higher state of unpredictability and randomness in the neural signal. The fact that this neural noise correlates phenomenologically with "expanded" experience is, again, beautifully and parsimoniously explained by the REBUS model (the breakdown of predictive constraints allowing noisy data into the global workspace). To map "neural noise" directly onto "increased cosmic filter bandwidth" is scientifically unsubstantiated and mathematically incoherent.

## 9. Conclusion

The manuscript attempts to solve the mind-body problem by rebranding the complex, non-linear, topological dynamics of the mammalian central nervous system as a metaphysical "interface." While the proposal of a "direction-dependent discriminating test" may be rhetorically clever, it is scientifically barren and philosophically hollow. The author has constructed a false dichotomy, pitting a highly sophisticated, un-falsifiable version of interactionist dualism against a naive, monotonic "production" model of the brain that no contemporary neuroscientist, computational biologist, or philosopher of mind actually defends.

The empirical phenomena the author cites as proof of dualism—anaesthetic hysteresis, the asymmetrical dissociation of cognitive functions, the psychedelic state, and the terminal gamma surge—are universally recognized, computationally modeled, and mechanistically explained within modern physicalist frameworks such as Predictive Processing, Integrated Information Theory (IIT 4.0), and multivariate information topology. By systematically ignoring these frameworks, the manuscript fails to meet the basic standards of rigorous philosophical scholarship. The proposed test does not discriminate between production and filter models; it merely highlights the author's profound unfamiliarity with non-equilibrium thermodynamics, predictive inference, and complex systems theory.
