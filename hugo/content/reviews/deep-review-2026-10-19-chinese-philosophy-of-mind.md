## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **Chuanxilu dating error (FIXED).** The references said "(Original conversations recorded 1518–1556.)", but Wang died in 1529, as L73 itself states. Raw source (wentchina.com Chuan Xi Lu page, grepped): Xu Ai recorded the talks from 1512; Xue Kan published them in 1518; Nan Daji added the letters in 1524; Qian Dehong published the continuation in 1554 and added Huang Zhi's material in 1556; Xie Tingjie produced the consolidated edition in 1572. 1518–1556 is therefore the publication span. The entry now reads "(Compiled by his disciples and published in stages, 1518–1556.)" The inline "Wang [1518–1556] 1963" stays, because it marks original publication. This was raised in pessimistic-2026-10-12's Unsupported Claims table (chinese L112) and never queued.

2. **"rather than constitution" contradicted by the primary text and by the article itself (FIXED).** L61 said the two sides were divided by *persistence* "rather than constitution". In Huiyuan's dialogue of 404 they disagree about what spirit is:
   - The sceptic: 精麁一氣始終同宅…宅毀則氣散而照滅. Refined and coarse are one *qi*; when the dwelling is destroyed, the *qi* disperses and the illumination goes out. Also 神雖妙物故是陰陽之化耳: spirit, however subtle, is only a transformation of yin and yang.
   - Huiyuan: 神者何耶。精極而為靈者也 (spirit is refinement carried to its limit, become numinous) and 感物而非物故物化而不滅 (it responds to things without being a thing, so it does not perish when things transform).
   - The article itself calls Fan Zhen's thesis "nearer a supervenience claim", which is a constitution claim.

   Rewritten so that persistence drives the dispute and the rival accounts of what spirit *is* serve that question, with the Huiyuan exchange given as the instance. The scoped thesis is unchanged and the "Nobody asked why…" sentence is kept. The section is neither reverted nor inflated, so both halves of the 07-27 stability note hold.

3. **Internal inconsistency: Wang "came closest of any Chinese thinker" to a consciousness-centred metaphysics (FIXED).** Since 07-27, L63 of the same article has named the Faxiang school's Yogācāra "consciousness-only" doctrine as Chinese. That school is more consciousness-centred than Wang on any reading. Rescoped to "came as close as any Confucian thinker". Lu Xiangshan's "the universe is my mind" makes a strict Confucian superlative contestable too, so "as close as" is used rather than "closest". Wang's role as "the sharpest available test" is unchanged.

### Medium Issues Found

- **"their Confucian and Daoist opponents denied it" (FIXED → "their non-Buddhist critics").** In the primary text both parties quote the *Zhuangzi*. The sceptic quotes 人之生氣之聚, and Huiyuan cites 大宗師 and the *Wenzi*. Radich calls Fan Zhen's text "a debunking Confucian riposte" and says Huiyuan and others appealed to "Chinese classics (with an emphasis on the neo-Daoist canon)". The Daoist classics were common ground, so they cannot label one side.
- **"Both sides conducted the dispute in the shared vocabulary of *qi*" over-generalised (FIXED by scoping).** This holds for the Huiyuan round (verified above). Radich, however, records "a subset of contributors … who use 'consciousness' (shi 識) in a recognizably Buddhist sense, or shishen … /shenshi (神識, 'spirit-cum-consciousness') to refer to the transmigrating entity". He also says they "ground their arguments more in Buddhist scripture" than earlier contributors. The article now says so, and adds that the term still functioned as "the thread of continuity" (Radich): first between incarnations, later between the deluded and the liberated state. So the persistence framing survives, and the point strengthens the article's own Buddhist-coverage scoping.
- **"Chinese Buddhism imported an Indian analysis of mind wholesale" (FIXED).** "Wholesale" overstates the case for Chan, Huayan and Tiantai, which are the standard examples of sinicised schools. Now reads "built on an analysis of mind imported from India—most directly in the Faxiang school…".
- Grammar: "nearer a supervenience claim than to either" → "nearer to a supervenience claim than to either".
