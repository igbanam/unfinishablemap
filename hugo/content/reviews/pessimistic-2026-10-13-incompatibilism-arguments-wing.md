## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist (Patricia Churchland)
"Ultimate source", "settling" and "the agent herself" (source-vs-leeway L36, L82) are folk-psychological posits with no neural counterpart. Decision neuroscience models choice as evidence accumulation to a bound, and nothing in such a model is a candidate for an originator distinct from the accumulation. The wing's taxonomy, she would say, organises a dispute about an entity that the mature science will not contain. *Map response available:* this is bedrock (the 07-19 stability note), but it presses hardest exactly where source-vs-leeway L82 claims Tenet 3 *requires* an originator (item 3). The tenet does not require one, so the Map is exposed there more than it needs to be.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist (Daniel Dennett)
Dennett would welcome consequence L91: "If compatibilism were correct … the Map's whole apparatus of an agent-causal interface would be unmotivated." He would read it as an admission that the interface exists to rescue a variety of free will not worth wanting. That reading is available only because the sentence misstates the Map's own motivation. Tenet 3 rests on self-stultification and mental causation (tenets L91–93), not on free will (item 3). On the manipulation argument, Dennett is a soft-liner: the knob-turning neuroscientist *is* the relevant difference. The manipulation article's account of the soft line (L58) gives that soft-line position to Mickelson, whose actual argument is different (item 4).

### The Quantum Skeptic (Max Tegmark)
Consequence L97 says the Map "posits a minimally owned [miracle]". A Lewisian divergence miracle is a *violation* of the actual laws. Tenet 2 (tenets L65) promises influence "without injecting energy or violating conservation laws", with Born statistics intact. Either the interface breaks a law, and the Map should say which, or it does not, and the word "miracle" is wrong. There is a further disanalogy. Lewis needs his miracle *because* the world is deterministic. In the indeterministic world the Map posits, doing otherwise needs no miracle at all. The analogy runs backwards (Critical Issue 6).

### The Many-Worlds Defender (David Deutsch)
Consequence L99 says branching would "dissolve the very alternative-possibility structure the Consequence Argument makes salient". On Everett every alternative is realised, which is the richest alternative structure there is, and "could have done otherwise" gets a literal branch-relative reading. *Not queued*: the 07-19 stability note (#2) books this as an honestly hedged bedrock disagreement.

### The Empiricist (Karl Popper's Ghost)
The manipulation article admits (L68) that the Map's post-decoherence proposal is "constructed to leave those regularities statistically intact precisely so that no detectable anomaly arises". That is the second horn of Pereboom's wild-coincidences dilemma. If agent-caused choices conform to the physical probabilities, the conformity is either an unexplained coincidence or a law that leaves the agent nothing to do. Two paragraphs later the article escapes through Tenet 5, but Tenet 5 governs simplicity, and the coincidence argument is about likelihood. The [07-09 review](/reviews/pessimistic-2026-07-09-free-will-deepening-cluster/) already noted that the cluster answers every opponent with the same Tenet 5 move. The new finding is worse: the move is now aimed at arguments it does not govern (item 2).

### The Buddhist Philosopher (Nagarjuna)
"The causal buck never stops with her" (source-vs-leeway L36) treats the absence of a self-standing originator as a defect. Dependent origination treats it as how things are. Pereboom's hard incompatibilism, which keeps ethics and drops basic desert, is closer to the Madhyamaka position than the Map's wide-source libertarianism is. *Not queued*: bedrock. The source-vs-leeway page already says the taxonomy does not establish that the sourcehood condition is met.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The formal Consequence Argument is stated in a form that needs agglomeration. High.
- **File**: [topics/the-consequence-argument-for-incompatibilism.md](/topics/the-consequence-argument-for-incompatibilism/) L50
- **Quote**: "Alpha and the powerlessness of the past and laws give us `N(P0 ∧ L)`; repeated application of Beta yields `NA`—no one has any choice about `A`."
- **Problem**: Alpha (from `□p` infer `Np`) cannot yield `N(P0 ∧ L)`, because `P0 ∧ L` is not necessary. In van Inwagen's version, Alpha is applied to the *necessitated conditional* `□(P0 → (L → A))`, and the past and the laws enter as two separate premises, `NP0` and `NL`, discharged by two Beta steps. The SEP's step list ("Arguments for Incompatibilism" §5, raw HTML curled) runs: (3) `N(H ⊃ (L ⊃ P))` by Alpha; (4) `NH`, fixity of past; (5) `N(L ⊃ P)` by Beta; (6) `NL`, fixity of laws; (7) `NP` by Beta. Getting `N(P0 ∧ L)` from `NP0` and `NL` *is* agglomeration, the principle that L56–62 then show to be invalid via McKay–Johnson. So the article's own reconstruction contains the step its next section refutes. A reader cannot tell that van Inwagen's version was built to avoid that step. Taking `N(P0 ∧ L)` as a single premise is a later repair: SEP credits that Beta-2 version to Finch & Warfield 1998.
- **Recommendation**: Priority item 1.

### Issue 2: The coin counterexample gives the wrong reason why `Np` holds. Medium.
- **File**: same, L62
- **Quote**: "no one has any choice about either: no one made the coin fail to land heads, and no one made it fail to land tails."
- **Problem**: `N` is defined at L43 as "no one has, or ever had, any choice about whether p". That is an ability to falsify, not a question of who brought `p` about. By the article's "no one made it" test, `N(p ∧ q)` would come out true as well, since no one made the coin land neither way, and the counterexample would collapse. `Np` holds because no one could have *ensured* heads: a toss of a fair coin might land tails. The 07-19 deep review confirmed that p and q are assigned as McKay–Johnson assign them. It did not test the stated reason, and its stability note #3 covers citation metadata only.
- **Recommendation**: Fold into priority item 1 (same section, same skill).

### Issue 3: Tenet 5 is aimed at objections that are not parsimony arguments. High (reasoning mode).
- **Files**: [topics/the-manipulation-argument-and-hard-incompatibilism.md](/topics/the-manipulation-argument-and-hard-incompatibilism/) L70; [concepts/source-versus-leeway-incompatibilism.md](/concepts/source-versus-leeway-incompatibilism/) L82. Same string family: [topics/event-causal-libertarianism.md](/topics/event-causal-libertarianism/) L90.
- **Problem**:
  - Pereboom has two objections, and the wing describes both correctly elsewhere. The disappearing-agent (luck) objection is a *conceptual* argument against event-causal libertarianism (manipulation L48). The wild-coincidences objection is a *likelihood* argument against agent causation: choices free of the physical probabilities would match observed statistics only by "coincidences so wild as to make it incredible", which is Pereboom's phrase as quoted in Timpe 2007, APQ 44(2). Manipulation L50 states this correctly.
  - Yet manipulation L70 calls Pereboom's rejection of agent causation "a **parsimony-and-plausibility** judgement". Source-vs-leeway L82 calls "Pereboom's case that indeterministic sourcehood is mere luck" a "plausibility-and-parsimony judgement". Both then discount it with Tenet 5.
  - Tenet 5 says "Simplicity is not a reliable guide to truth" (tenets L131) and rules out "using parsimony as a decisive argument" (L147). It does not speak to likelihood or conceptual arguments. Manipulation L70 also widens the tenet silently: "Tenet 5 holds that simplicity *and antecedent plausibility* are unreliable guides".
  - The two pages contradict each other. Manipulation L50 says Pereboom *grants* that agent causation "answers the disappearing-agent worry". Source-vs-leeway L82 aims the luck objection at the Map's agent-causal sourcehood anyway.
  - The sibling is worse. `event-causal-libertarianism` L84 says the Map's own considered view is that event-causal libertarianism "succumbs to the luck objection". L90 then calls that same claim "a plausibility-and-parsimony judgement rather than a theorem" which the Map may decline. The Map relies on the argument against one rival and discounts it as parsimony against another.
- **Why the stability notes do not pre-empt this**: three deep reviews ratified the phrase as "Mode Three framework-boundary marking" (manipulation 07-19; source-vs-leeway 07-19, "Counterarguments Considered"; event-causal-libertarianism 08-19, L62). Their fences protect the *verdict*: do not re-flag "the Map's reply to Pereboom is not decisive", and do not re-flag Pereboom rejecting the luck reading from outside the tenets. Those reviews checked the symmetry of the Tenet 5 move, that it buys no positive support. None of them checked whether the objection is the kind of argument Tenet 5 governs. This finding leaves the verdict alone and corrects the classification. The fix makes the Map's position *more* exposed, not less, so it cannot be an over-claim regression.
- **Reasoning-mode classification (editor-internal)**: an in-framework reply that actually appeals to a Map commitment, achieved by re-describing the opponent's argument so the commitment applies.
- **Recommendation**: Priority item 2.

### Issue 5: Two soft-line and hard-line citations misstate their authors' arguments. Medium.
- **File**: [topics/the-manipulation-argument-and-hard-incompatibilism.md](/topics/the-manipulation-argument-and-hard-incompatibilism/) L58, L60
- **Problem**:
  - **Mickelson (Demetriou) 2010.** The article says she argues that "the involvement of a designing agent, or the bypassing of the agent's own development, marks a real distinction", with the seam "usually at the 3→4 transition". Her abstract (OpenAlex, from the AJP record) says something else. The argument's resilience is "the ill-gotten gain from an ambiguity in the description of the causal relations found in the argument's foundational case". Every coherent reading of the case "involves a type of manipulation which undermines the victim's agency". And manipulation arguments using "the suppressive variety of manipulation" offer no threat. Her seam lies at the *suppressive manipulation* in Case 1. It is not at 3→4, and it does not turn on whether a designer is present. The paragraph then answers her with Pereboom's "tune the intermediate cases" rejoinder, which does not touch a diagnosis aimed at the foundational case.
  - **Jeppsson 2020.** The article says she grounds "responsibility in the agent's own deliberative standpoint rather than in facts about what caused the agent". Her abstract (OpenAlex) makes an epistemic claim about *which intuitions to trust*. Manipulated characters in fiction can seem responsible "because we identify with such characters, and therefore focus on their options and the reasons for which they act". We "have no reason to trust the incompatibilist intuitions that arise when we regard manipulated agents from a much more detached perspective". The gloss turns a claim about the evaluator's perspective into a thesis about what grounds responsibility: the epistemic/metaphysical equivocation this skill's checklist flags.
- **Recommendation**: Priority item 4.

### 1. Consequence Argument: the formal sketch needs agglomeration (L50), and the coin gloss gives the wrong reason (L62). High.
- **File:line**: `obsidian/topics/the-consequence-argument-for-incompatibilism.md:50`, `:62`
- **Exact strings**:
  - L50: `Alpha and the powerlessness of the past and laws give us `N(P0 ∧ L)`; repeated application of Beta yields `NA`—no one has any choice about `A`.`
  - L62: `no one has any choice about either: no one made the coin fail to land heads, and no one made it fail to land tails.`
- **Defect**: Alpha cannot yield `N(P0 ∧ L)`. Van Inwagen's version applies Alpha to the necessitated conditional and takes `NP0` and `NL` separately, so the sketch smuggles in the agglomeration step that L56–62 report as invalid (SEP §5 step list). At L62, "no one made it" is the wrong test for `N`. By that test `N(p ∧ q)` is also true and the counterexample fails. The right reason is that no one could have *ensured* heads.
- **Fix**:
  - L50 becomes: `Alpha, applied to that necessitated entailment (rewritten as `□(P0 → (L → A))`), gives `N(P0 → (L → A))`.
