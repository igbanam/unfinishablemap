## Reply

# Audit: “The Self Is Constructed; the Constructor Is Not”

**Recommendation: major revision.**

The 7 October revision substantially improved the article. It corrected the earlier ownership–agency “double dissociation,” added the Kant and immunity-to-error qualifications, updated the psychedelic-neuroscience passage, and expressly classified the substantial, persisting subject as a posit rather than an empirical result. Those changes are accurately recorded in the changelog and make the present version appreciably more disciplined than its predecessor. [changelog](/workflow/changelog/)

Nevertheless, the central argument does not establish its advertised conclusion against the strongest constructionist accounts. It establishes, at most, the following conditional:

> **Given phenomenal realism and given that conscious content must be presented to an ontologically prior bearer, construction of the self-model does not construct that bearer.**

That is coherent within the Map’s framework, but the second condition is exactly what self-model theory, illusionism, Dennettian accounts and several representational theories reject. The article therefore locates the disagreement rather than winning it. Its own “Evidence and Dependency” section effectively concedes this by saying that the irreducible subject is inherited from Tenet 1 and that the shared empirical evidence does not discriminate dualism from sophisticated functionalism. [self-construction-constructor](/apex/self-construction-constructor/)

## 1. The central inference

The article’s reasoning can be reconstructed as follows:

1. Percepts, social identities, narratives and world-models are constructions.
2. These constructions are conscious contents.
3. Conscious contents must be present **for** an experiential subject.
4. A constructed subject would itself be another content presented to a further subject.
5. That produces a regress.
6. Therefore, the experiential subject cannot be constructed.

The difficulty lies in premises 3 and 4.

### 1.1 “For a subject” shifts between a thin and a thick claim

On a **thin reading**, “experience is for a subject” means that conscious experience has first-personal or subjective character. Many constructionists can accept that formulation. They may analyze the relevant character as an intrinsic feature of a conscious state, as organism-relative availability, as reflexive representation, or as the operation of an integrated self-model.

On a **thick reading**, it means that there is a numerically distinct bearer to whom representations are delivered and who is ontologically prior to them. That is already close to the Map’s conclusion. The argument moves from the relatively neutral thin claim to the thick bearer claim without an independent bridge.

This produces a dilemma:

- If the “minimal subject” is merely first-personal organization, the conclusion is compatible with many physicalist and deflationary views.
- If it is an ontologically distinct receiver of contents, the argument has assumed the controversial architecture it purports to establish.

The article recognizes that a Humean or Buddhist may accept for-me-ness without accepting a self-standing subject, and that Kant blocks an inference from the “I think” to substance, simplicity or persistence. But it still calls the minimal-subject conclusion “close to forced” and subsequently describes the subject as standing “outside the series.” Those stronger formulations exceed what the concession permits. [self-construction-constructor](/apex/self-construction-constructor/)

### 1.2 The regress is not compulsory

The regress follows only if every form of self-awareness requires:

1. one state representing another state;
2. a numerically distinct recipient of that representation; and
3. a further act that recognizes the represented state as belonging to that recipient.

Not all theories have that form.

Same-order self-representationalism, for example, holds that a conscious state represents both its ordinary object and itself. There is no numerically distinct higher-order observer or indefinitely repeated presentation relation. Uriah Kriegel’s formulation is explicit: a conscious state has subjective character when it represents itself in the appropriate way. That theory may face other objections, but it blocks the article’s regress and cannot be answered by merely adding another representational level. [Computer Science and Engineering](https://cse.buffalo.edu/~rapaport/Papers/Papers.by.Others/kriegel-selfrepnlCsness.pdf)

Henrich’s circularity argument is relevant but narrower than the article suggests. Henrich argues that a **reflection theory** cannot generate self-consciousness from an initially non-self-aware act, because reflection already presupposes the subject’s awareness of the reflected state as its own. That challenges certain reflective or higher-order constructions. It does not establish that subjectivity is a substantial, nonphysical bearer, nor does it defeat same-order, non-egological or system-level accounts. [Springer Link](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/bf01248576.pdf)

### 1.3 “Operations require an operator” is not an argument for an additional entity

The article’s pivotal sentence—“construction is an operation, and operations require an operator and a field in which they operate”—turns a grammatical form into an ontological principle.

A naturalist need not deny that an operation has a realizer. The claim is that the organized system is the realizer: distributed neural activity constructs the model in roughly the sense that metabolic organization regulates temperature or a recurrent network integrates information. It does not follow that there must be a further personal-level operator in addition to the organized process.

The argument would need to establish that:

- the neural system cannot itself be the relevant operator;
- subjectivity cannot be a property or mode of the organized process;
- reflexive processes are necessarily relations between distinct subject and object terms; and
- self-organization cannot generate first-personal organization.

None of those propositions follows from the word “operation.”

### 1.4 “Constructor” equivocates between three roles

The article alternates among:

- **the experiencer**: that for which content is present;
- **the phenomenal medium**: the fact or field of conscious presence;
- **the constructor or causal operator**: that which builds, selects or manipulates content.

These are not equivalent roles.

The four main registers might, at most, motivate a question about an experiencer or phenomenal character. The brain and social environment appear to do most of the actual constructing. To infer that the experiencer is also the constructor requires a separate account of mental causation. The later theory-of-mind section attempts to supply one, but its claims are considerably weaker than would be needed. The title therefore attributes an active construction role to something that the preceding evidence primarily characterizes as a putative recipient.

## 2. Stress test against the principal rivals

### 2.1 Metzinger’s self-model theory

The article presents Metzinger’s headline view reasonably accurately: the phenomenal self-model is not a homunculus but a transparent representation of the organism as a whole, and its content constitutes the experienced first-person perspective. The Map’s dedicated source article states this correctly and classifies the dispute as framework-relative rather than a refutation. [self-model-theory-of-subjectivity](/concepts/self-model-theory-of-subjectivity/)

The apex article’s response—“the self-model must be experienced by something”—does not yet engage that architecture. Metzinger denies that a model is first constructed and then inspected by a second entity. The transparent self-model, embedded in the organism’s world-model and control relations, is proposed as constituting the first-person perspective at the system level. Asking what additional entity receives it imports the presentation-to-an-audience model that SMT rejects.

The strongest version of the Map’s objection would instead be:

> Even if the self-model constitutes perspectival organization, why is there anything it is like for that organization to operate?

That is a hard-problem objection to reductive representationalism. It is not yet an argument that a further bearer exists. The current article slides from “SMT may not explain phenomenality” to “SMT presupposes an unconstructed subject.”

The discussion of meditation has the same weakness. The article says that when narrative content recedes, “witness-awareness remains.” Metzinger expressly anticipates this limit case by proposing that minimal phenomenal experience may be a predictive model of tonic alertness: a non-egoic, selfless form of modelling not tied to an ordinary individual first-person perspective. The article can reject that account, but it must explain why it fails rather than treating reports of pure awareness as direct detection of a witness. [self-model-theory-of-subjectivity](/concepts/self-model-theory-of-subjectivity/)

### 2.2 Dennett’s centre of narrative gravity

Dennett’s account does not propose that a pre-existing narrative self authors its own narrative. The self is an abstract organizational posit emerging from the narratives generated by the system—analogous to a centre of gravity rather than an additional concrete object. Dennett explicitly allows the brain or a robot to generate an accumulating biography without first knowing that it is creating a fictional character. [Internet Archive+1](https://archive.org/download/dennett-1992/Dennett1992.pdf)

The apex article’s statement that a narrative is “content, something experienced” changes the subject from the existence of a narrative self to the existence of consciousness. It may show that a theory of narrative identity does not by itself solve the problem of consciousness. It does not show that a prior narrative author exists.

The omission is especially conspicuous because the Map already contains two more careful source articles:

- “The Interpreter Module and the Narrative Construction of Unity” describes Dennett’s apparent author as the product, not the source, of narrative construction.
- “Multiple Drafts Model and the Cartesian Theater” says explicitly that Dennett rejects a final audience, that “for whom?” is a Cartesian-theatre question on his view, and that the Map’s narrator premise is a framework commitment rather than an unanswered burden within Dennett’s theory. [interpreter-module-narrative-construction-unity](/concepts/interpreter-module-narrative-construction-unity/)

Neither article appears in the apex article’s source list. This matters because the apex repeats precisely the move that the Multiple Drafts page has already classified as framework-boundary reasoning.

### 2.3 Predictive-processing models

Predictive-processing accounts can model bodily self-recognition as hierarchical, probabilistic integration: the organism’s body is inferred as the most likely source of interoceptive, proprioceptive and exteroceptive signals, with prediction errors continually updating what counts as “me.” Such accounts predict that self-representations will be plastic, multisensory and distributed rather than implemented by a dedicated self module. [PubMed Central (PMC)+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3848896/)

This does not by itself solve the hard problem. But that is different from failing to explain self-construction. Predictive processing can deny that the self-model is delivered to a second internal entity while providing a constitutive account of:

- bodily ownership;
- self-location;
- agency attribution;
- organism–world boundaries;
- autobiographical integration; and
- the salience of self-related prediction errors.

The article currently says predictive processing explains information transformation but not why the result feels like anything. That identifies a general explanatory gap. It does not establish that the best account of the gap is a substantial constructor rather than Type-B physicalism, non-reductive physicalism, neutral monism, same-order representation or some other phenomenal-realist alternative.

The article is stronger where it admits that Letheby’s self-binding account and the Map’s filter interpretation are empirically equivalent on altered-state evidence. That concession should govern the entire predictive-processing discussion, not just the psychedelic subsection. [self-construction-constructor](/apex/self-construction-constructor/)

### 2.4 Illusionism

The sentence “illusions require a subject to be deceived” equivocates between an ordinary perceptual illusion and illusionism’s technical claim.

Frankish’s illusionist proposal is not that an already conscious witness consciously sees a false image of phenomenal properties. It is that introspective mechanisms generate representations, judgements and dispositions that characterize sensory states as possessing phenomenal properties. The “illusion problem” is to explain why phenomenality seems so immediate and compelling in functional and neurocognitive terms. Frankish accepts that this is a difficult explanatory burden, but he explicitly denies that it must be discharged by positing real phenomenal properties or a further witness. [Keith Frankish](https://keithfrankish.github.io/articles/Frankish_Illusionism%20as%20a%20theory%20of%20consciousness_eprint.pdf)

The apex briefly notes that illusionists deny its premise. That is commendably candid. But once the denial is acknowledged, the “someone must be fooled” argument cannot continue to function as a reductio. It is instead a restatement of phenomenal realism:

- The Map says misrepresentation must itself be phenomenally undergone.
- The illusionist says systematic misrepresentation and phenomenal judgement need not involve the phenomenal properties represented.

Neither side has defeated the other merely by repeating its side of that disagreement.

### 2.5 Enactivist and process accounts

The article groups predictive processing and enactivism together, then characterizes both as constructing inner content for a recipient. Strong enactivist accounts often reject that content–recipient architecture. They understand perspectival organization as enacted through the organism’s ongoing sensorimotor and regulatory engagement with the environment.

Such a view might still be inadequate as a theory of phenomenality. But the objection would need to address the constitutive organism–environment relation, not redescribe it as a representational product waiting to be presented internally.

## 3. Does the article beg the question?

**As a neutral argument, yes. As an internal conditional argument, no.**

The article’s own evidential-status section says that the proposition that each construction presupposes an irreducible experiential subject is inherited from Tenet 1. It also says the empirical materials do not discriminate the dualist reading from sophisticated functionalism and that the substantial subject is a posit. [self-construction-constructor](/apex/self-construction-constructor/)

That disclosure is a major strength. The problem is that the rest of the article repeatedly resumes the language of demonstration:

- the constructionist “cannot cross” the relevant line;
- the constructor stands “outside the series”;
- the regress is said to be inescapable;
- the minimal claim is “close to forced”;
- pure functionalism is said to struggle with the transparency cases;
- the four registers supposedly establish that a subject is presupposed;
- the title states without qualification that the constructor is not constructed.

The properly calibrated conclusion would be narrower:

> Constructionist explanations of self-content do not, merely by explaining that content, settle the metaphysics of phenomenal subjectivity. Under the Map’s phenomenal-realist and non-deflationary conception of the subject, the subject is treated as a presupposition rather than as another self-representation.

That would identify a live explanatory fork without claiming a regress that all rivals must accept.

### 3.1 The fourfold “convergence” is not independent evidence

The four registers are empirically different, but the dualist conclusion in every row is produced by applying the same bridge premise:

> Conscious content must be received by an irreducible subject.

Repeating one controversial premise across perception, social identity, narrative cognition and transparency does not generate four independent reasons for that premise. It demonstrates the premise’s generality within the framework.

The article now says that convergence is not independent confirmation. That is correct. But its introductory language still says the argument gains weight from the recurrence of an asymmetry across “independent domains.” The evidence is independent at the level of construction phenomena, not at the level of support for an unconstructed bearer. [self-construction-constructor](/apex/self-construction-constructor/)

### 3.2 Inverted qualia are not neutral support

The claim that two systems could have identical functions and cortical representations while yellow feels different is a disputed modal premise, not an empirical result. A functionalist, representationalist or identity theorist can reject the stipulated possibility.

Using inverted qualia to “sharpen” the argument is legitimate as an internal anti-physicalist intuition pump, but it should not be presented as evidence that computational construction leaves phenomenality invariant while experience varies.

## 4. Citation and empirical-claim audit

I found no obvious fabricated major work among the central citations checked. The main problem is not bibliographic existence but **claim-level inflation**: cautious or limited source findings become stronger philosophical conclusions in the apex synthesis.

### 4.1 Metzinger and transparency: broad attribution accurate, application inaccurate

The basic description of phenomenal transparency is sound: introspection ordinarily accesses represented content but not the vehicle properties or subpersonal construction process. Metzinger defines transparency in almost exactly those terms. [Johannes Gutenberg-Universität Mainz](https://www.philosophie.fb05.uni-mainz.de/files/2018/09/Metzinger_2019_Dretske_on_Transparency_penultimate.pdf)

The article’s examples of representations becoming “opaque” are much less secure:

- A blurred visual field can still be phenomenally transparent: blur is part of the represented content, not introspective access to the representational vehicle.
- A painter’s squint alters perceptual input and content; it does not thereby reveal the neural representation as a representation.
- Lucid dreaming adds metacognitive knowledge that one is dreaming, but that is not automatically access to vehicle properties.
- Depersonalisation may make aspects of a self-model experientially strange, but this requires a more precise argument before being identified with Metzingerian opacity.

The statement that what-it-is-likeness remains “invariant” while “everything else” about functional role changes is unsupported and implausibly strong. All of the cited conditions involve extensive functional and phenomenological changes.

### 4.2 Gibson and perceptual learning: overgeneralized

The claim that perceptual learning is differentiation “not enrichment” is too categorical. Differentiation is central to Gibson’s tradition, but the wider literature recognizes multiple mechanisms. Goldstone’s major review distinguishes attention weighting, imprinting, differentiation and unitization. [Percepts and Concepts Lab](https://pc.cogs.indiana.edu/papers/perlearn.pdf)

“The perceptual object did not exist before the engagement that built it” is not a direct empirical result. It is a philosophical gloss on expert discrimination. The radiologist example supports changed sensitivity and organization; it does not, without further argument, show that a new phenomenal object literally comes into existence rather than that previously available features become more effectively discriminated, grouped or interpreted.

### 4.3 Schachter–Singer: historically recognizable, evidentially stale

The article accurately invokes the classic theory according to which context and cognitive attribution affect emotional interpretation. But it does not communicate the theory’s poor subsequent evidential standing. Reisenzein’s extensive review found no convincing evidence for the strong claim that emotional states result from labelling otherwise unexplained arousal; the best-supported residual claim was a much weaker arousal-intensification effect. [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Rainer-Reisenzein/publication/16840488_The_Schachter_theory_of_emotion_Two_decades_later/links/5425723f0cf2e4ce9403816d/The-Schachter-theory-of-emotion-Two-decades-later.pdf)

Barrett’s constructed-emotion framework should therefore not be presented as simply developing an experimentally established Schachter–Singer result. The intellectual lineage is real, but the original experiment cannot bear the empirical weight assigned to it.

### 4.4 Neonatal evidence: substantial overreach

Evidence of spontaneous self-touch and early sensorimotor discrimination supports the presence of embodied organization and the developmental foundations of body awareness. The cited observational study followed only four infants and describes such activity as a foundation from which a later bodily sense of self develops. It does not establish “phenomenal selfhood,” an irreducible experiential subject, or the absence of constitutive social influence. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30619012/)

The defensible conclusion is:

> Some forms of bodily differentiation and regulation precede mature narrative and socially categorized identity.

That is considerably weaker than “the phenomenal self does not require the social.”

### 4.5 Social dependence: the source article’s caveat disappears

The apex says social dependence runs “strictly one way” and uses the zombie community to support this. Yet the linked source article expressly says that the zombie scenario begs the question against a functional constructionist: the parties disagree over whether functionally realized social identity already counts as selfhood. [social-construction-of-self-vs-phenomenal-self](/topics/social-construction-of-self-vs-phenomenal-self/)

This is a clear case of **apex amplification**. The source page properly identifies a framework boundary; the synthesis promotes it into a strict asymmetry.

### 4.6 DMN, meditation and narrative self: too monolithic

The article is right to avoid saying that mind-wandering is invariably self-narrative, and the October revision correctly updated the psychedelic passage from a DMN-only story to broader network desynchronisation. [changelog](/workflow/changelog/)

The remaining claims are too causal and too network-specific:

- “The DMN constructs the narrative self” is a useful shorthand, not a settled mechanistic conclusion.
- Damage to regions associated with the DMN can affect autobiographical memory and self-referential cognition, but that does not identify the network as the producer of an experiential subject.
- Meditation does not uniformly “suppress the DMN.” A resting-state meta-analysis found increased connectivity between DMN and salience-network regions after mindfulness training, while the 2026 whole-brain meta-analysis found distributed, practice-specific patterns involving insula, operculum, superior temporal cortex, supplementary motor regions and hippocampus. [Nature+2](https://preview-www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-15195-6.pdf)

The linked DMN article is more careful than the apex: it says the filter interpretation is a different reading of the same correlations, not additional evidence against physicalism. That qualification should be carried into the apex’s main narrative. [default-mode-network](/concepts/default-mode-network/)

### 4.7 Billon, agency and ownership: now substantially improved

The October revision correctly abandoned a flat ownership–agency double dissociation. The article now relies primarily on the better-supported direction—thoughts experienced as one’s own without being experienced as voluntarily initiated—and marks the thought-insertion and depersonalisation interpretations as disputed or less secure. This is an example of successful correction and calibration. [changelog](/workflow/changelog/)

The residual inference still goes too far: preservation of a first-person reporting position does not by itself establish an intact owner, much less a substantial subject. The article partly acknowledges this, but the synthesis later treats the cases as evidence for the constructor’s phenomenal anatomy.

### 4.8 Kant and IEM: accurate additions, incompletely propagated

The Kant article correctly states that the bare “I think” establishes neither substantiality, simplicity nor diachronic identity. It also correctly warns that the Map avoids a paralogism only as long as it does not later infer its subject from felt mine-ness or unity. [kants-paralogisms-and-the-maps-subject](/topics/kants-paralogisms-and-the-maps-subject/)

The IEM article is similarly well calibrated: first-person immunity is compatible with embodied, Kantian, deflationary and Cartesian accounts, and therefore gives the Map’s preferred bearer no discriminating support. [immunity-to-error-through-misidentification](/concepts/immunity-to-error-through-misidentification/)

The apex cites these results but does not fully obey them. Its regress repeatedly moves from the first-personal presence or ownership of content to a subject standing outside the constructed series. That is not yet an inference to substance or persistence, but it is sufficiently bearer-like that the article must explain why it is not the first stage of the prohibited inference.

### 4.9 Theory of mind: weakest empirical section

The source article itself says ape false-belief evidence is contested, introduces a two-system interpretation, and acknowledges that language, working memory and executive control may help explain human–ape differences. [theory-of-mind](/concepts/theory-of-mind/)

The apex nevertheless says that the systematic human–ape gap falls “precisely” at metarepresentation and tracks consciousness. That is not established. A 2025 study found that chimpanzees rationally revised beliefs in response to relative evidence strength and undercutting defeaters—forms of second-order evidence processing that the authors characterize as metacognitively demanding and reflective. This is not a demonstration of human-like recursive social mindreading, but it directly weakens any broad categorical claim that coordinated representation of evidence about evidence begins only at a uniquely human, consciousness-dependent threshold. [Science+1](https://www.science.org/doi/pdf/10.1126/science.adq5229)

The further claim that recursive theory of mind requires phenomenal consciousness is also unsupported. Nested representations can be functionally specified and computationally implemented. To show that phenomenality makes a causal contribution, the article would need a result that distinguishes:

- phenomenal integration;
- global availability;
- working-memory capacity;
- language-supported recursion;
- executive control; and
- purely computational metarepresentation.

No such discriminator is supplied. The quantum-coherence paragraph is responsibly labelled speculative, but the preceding consciousness-threshold claim is not correspondingly qualified.

## 5. Integration with the principal source articles

**Integration quality: mixed.**

The source page accurately emphasizes that the PSM is not a homunculus and that the Map’s objection is a located, framework-relative disagreement. The apex reproduces the description but not the calibration: it says the regress is unavoidable and the subject is close to forced. [self-model-theory-of-subjectivity](/concepts/self-model-theory-of-subjectivity/)

### Kant’s Paralogisms

**Integration quality: conceptually strong, operationally incomplete.**

The Kant article’s warning needs to function as a constraint on the argument, not merely as a late disclaimer.

### Immunity to error through misidentification

**Integration quality: good.**

The apex correctly says IEM discriminates nothing about the bearer. This is one of the article’s clearest passages. It should be used as the model for the treatment of transparency, neonatal embodiment and clinical ownership: compatibility is not differential support.

### Social construction

**Integration quality: poor.**

The source article’s explicit acknowledgement that the zombie argument begs the question is omitted, while the apex strengthens “appears one-way” into “strictly one way.” [social-construction-of-self-vs-phenomenal-self](/topics/social-construction-of-self-vs-phenomenal-self/)

**Integration quality: improved but uneven.**

The updated psychedelic qualification has propagated correctly. The linked page’s stronger underdetermination language and its caution about DMN-specific filtering have not propagated consistently. [default-mode-network](/concepts/default-mode-network/)

### Theory of mind

**Integration quality: poor.**

The apex retains the source article’s hierarchy of capacities but drops its most important cautions concerning contested ape results and alternative executive or linguistic explanations. It converts a research programme into a consciousness threshold.

### Dennettian source articles

**Integration quality: materially incomplete.**

The site has dedicated articles on the interpreter module and Multiple Drafts, both of which directly answer the apex’s “who experiences the model?” move. Neither is listed among the apex’s source articles. The omission allows the apex to present a framework-boundary objection as though Dennett had failed to notice the need for an audience. ## 6. Overall assessment

The article succeeds as an **internal synthesis of the Map’s commitments**. It usefully distinguishes constructed narrative and social identity from the deeper problem of phenomenal subjectivity, and it now discloses much of its dependence on Tenet 1.

It does not succeed as an independent argument that the constructor is unconstructed.

The strongest warranted conclusion is:

> Perceptual, social, narrative and self-model construction do not by themselves settle the metaphysics of consciousness. If phenomenal consciousness is irreducible, constructionist theories must explain how their functional and representational structures relate to first-personal presence. Whether that presence requires a distinct, substantial, persisting and unconstructed bearer remains unresolved.

That conclusion pressures reductive accounts without begging the question, remains compatible with the Kant and IEM articles, and accurately represents the empirical underdetermination already acknowledged by the page.

## Concrete potential improvements

1. **Apex article — change the thesis status.** Retitle it along the lines of “What Constructed Selfhood Does Not Yet Explain” or add a subtitle stating that it is a tenet-conditioned argument. The current categorical title exceeds the admitted evidential status.
2. **Apex article — formalize the argument.** State numbered premises and distinguish explicitly between:

- first-personal character;
- a subject as the organism or system;
- an ontologically distinct bearer;
- a substantial persisting subject; and
- a causally active constructor.
3. **Apex article — separate experiencer from constructor.** Explain why the putative recipient of phenomenal content should also count as the operator that builds or manipulates it. Do not move from passive presence to causal agency without a separate argument.
4. **Apex article — remove “operations require an operator.”** Replace it with a defended premise about why distributed, self-organizing or reflexive systems cannot constitute subjectivity. As written, the sentence reifies an agent from an agent noun.
