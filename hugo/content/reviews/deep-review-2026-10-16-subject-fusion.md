## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Superlative fails currency (L48).** "The only peer-reviewed philosophical treatment, Cochrane (2021)" is false. Kang, S.-P. (2022), "Shared consciousness and asymmetry," *Synthese* 200(5), art. 413 (doi:10.1007/s11229-022-03890-w), is a peer-reviewed philosophical paper on the Hogan case, written as a reply to Cochrane. The research note listed Kang and still repeated "only" (research note L65). **Fixed**: "A peer-reviewed philosophical treatment, Cochrane (2021), records…" (0 words). The research note keeps the old phrasing, which is acceptable for a dated note.
- **Source/Map conflation, Zohny & Savulescu (L57, L75, L85).** The source reads "if a mind merger occurs gradually over time, psychological connectedness may be preserved, and thus, psychologically the identities composing the merger may persist." The article quoted only from "the identities…", which dropped the qualifier "psychologically". L75 then set the Map's claim about *subjects* against them ("gradualness does not let the originals persist, as Zohny and Savulescu suggest it might"). They make no claim about subjects in the Map's sense, and the Map need not deny that psychological identity carries through a gradual merger. **Fixed**: L57 restores "psychologically" inside the quotation (+1). L75 now reads "does not let the original subjects persist, whatever it preserves of the psychological identities Zohny and Savulescu track" (+5). L85 reads "a gradual merger in which psychological identities persist" (+1).
- **Internal contradiction plus flat statement (L91, Dualism).** The old text was "Consciousness is not built from parts, so C cannot be composed of A and B. Coleman's dilemma states the reason, and it is why fusion at the level of persons is replacement rather than combination." This had two faults. (a) It stated the Map's non-compositional reading as fact, though L39 calls everything framework-internal and L61 names combinationists who reject the premise (tenet-check-2026-10-14 Note 3). (b) It said non-composition is *why* fusion is replacement. L73 says the opposite: non-composition and transitivity give only "at most one" original, and "none" needs the separate pairing premise. There was also a stance problem. Coleman argues that *subjects* cannot combine. He does not argue that consciousness is not built from parts: his panqualityism builds macro-experience from subjectless qualities. **Fixed**: "On the Map's reading, consciousness is not built from parts, so C cannot be composed of A and B; Coleman's dilemma argues the point for subjects. Fusion of persons is therefore not combination, and the pairing premise is what makes it replacement." (+7).
- **Tenet 5 used as a positive reason (L95, Occam).** The old text was "The Map declines it because knowledge here is incomplete". Incomplete knowledge stops simplicity from deciding against the Map. It gives no reason to decline the graded picture. This is the same defect the census-change-points review repaired at its L110/L111. **Fixed** on that model: "The tenet only bars simplicity from deciding; the step rests on the Map's commitment to determinate subjects, not on evidence, and the Map books its cost: a sharp boundary that nothing reads." The page itself states that commitment (L75 "a whole number at every moment"; L89 "the Map's determinate subject", "the fact remains determinate while going unread"). The ground is deliberately *not* the existential reading of coupling, because [coupling-engagement-condition](/concepts/coupling-engagement-condition/) L66 says that reading "restates the Map's commitment more than it supports it". Net +7 for the paragraph after tightening the first sentence (−4).
