## Executive Summary

The tempo void is the inability of consciousness to read the rate at which its own experience runs. Three findings mark its edge. **First**, there is no absolute tempo to read: Poincaré's universal-expansion argument transposes to time, so a uniform change in the rate of mind and world together would go unnoticed, and a change in the mind alone shows up only against an outside reference such as a clock or a moving object. **Second**, even relative tempo shows up only when it changes faster than the mind can adapt; gradual drifts are recalibrated away (Arstila 2012, on caffeine and the keypress-and-flash adaptation). **Third**, a change in one's own rate is credited to the world: a sped-up self sees a slowed world (Noyes & Kletti's accident survivors), and a slowed self sees a sped-up clock (Goody's Parkinsonian patient, via Arstila). The one case in which people report a tempo change, "time slowed down" in danger, is an unresolved three-way dispute. On the memory account (Stetson, Fiesta & Eagleman 2007) the slowing is reconstructed afterwards; on the processing-speed account (Arstila 2012) it is felt at the time; on the relative account (Phillips 2013) it is real but measured against one's own non-perceptual mental activity. Introspection cannot adjudicate, because every report is retrospective and the same words come out of each mechanism. The experimental record splits by condition: free-fall fear brings no gain in temporal resolution, while preparing to act does bring one (Hagura et al. 2012). The void is framework-neutral and gives dualism no evidential gain. It does flag a claim the Map makes twice: that attentional time distortions are "different modes of temporal experience" rather than estimation failures, which is a distinction this void says introspection cannot draw.

## Key Sources

### Stetson, Fiesta & Eagleman (2007), "Does Time Really Slow Down during a Frightening Event?"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0001295 (full text read via Europe PMC PMC2110887)
- **Type**: Paper, *PLoS ONE* 2(12): e1295 [Crossref]
- **Key points**:
  - Participants fell backward "for 31 m before landing safely in a net" [raw]; the fall lasted 2.49 s [raw]. A wrist-mounted "perceptual chronometer" alternated a digit with its negative at a period 6 ms faster than each person's threshold. If temporal resolution rose during the fall, the digit would become readable.
  - Retrospective estimates of their own fall rose 36% (2.17 → 2.96 s) [raw], but in-flight digit reading was no better than in ground controls.
  - Conclusion: "Our findings suggest that time-slowing is a function of recollection, not perception" [raw]; "subjective time is not a single entity that speeds or slows, but instead is composed of separable subcomponents" [raw].
  - ⚠️ **Internal inconsistencies in the primary.** The Methods say the platform was "winched 46 m" and the fall was 31 m, yet the Discussion says "free-falling from 50 meters" [raw]. Figure 2's legend gives the ground-control comparison as "p = 0.86", while the Results text gives "p = 0.98" [raw]. Eagleman's 2008 review then repeats "a 50 meter tower" and "the 3 second fall" [raw]. **Use 31 m / 2.49 s from the Methods.**
  - Stetson does record a verbal report: their fall "seemed to take a very long time" [raw]. That is a *duration* report, not a slow-motion (passage) report, which bears on Arstila's objection below.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits. The single report "time slowed" hides at least two separable mechanisms.

### Arstila (2012), "Time Slows Down during Accidents"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2012.00196 (full text read via Europe PMC PMC3384265)
- **Type**: Hypothesis and Theory paper, *Frontiers in Psychology* 3: 196 [Crossref]
- **Key points**:
  - A realist reply to Stetson: "when we become faster, the world appears slower to us" [raw]. It invokes rapid locus coeruleus–norepinephrine activation.
  - **The void stated by its own literature**: "Without a background feeling about the 'normal' rate of events unfolding in our awareness, we simply cannot know if the rate at which things unfold in a current situation is unusual or not" [raw]; that feeling "is something that need not be accessible to even the most perceptive introspection" [raw].
  - **Adaptation erases slow change**: on the coffee objection, "we can only notice the changes that occur faster than we can adapt to the situation" [raw].
  - Critiques of Stetson: free fall "can be counted as surprising" only doubtfully, since "all those participants that estimated the duration of the fall had seen the fall before" [raw]. The chronometer tests fast, low-level visual limits, while the slowing is central (citing Holcombe 2009). And if perception really sped up without cognition speeding up, the world should seem *faster*, not slower [raw, paraphrased].
  - ⚠️ **Framing check**: Arstila says Stetson "did not report" asking whether time slowed [raw]. That overstates slightly, since Stetson records a verbal duration report (above). The fair form is that Stetson did not elicit a *slow-motion* report.
  - Reports the classic survey figures **[secondary; Noyes & Kletti primaries not read]**: external time slowed for 75% of 85 respondents (1976) and 72% of 101 (1977); faster thought for 68% and 61%.
  - Quotes Noyes & Kletti (1976, p. 23) **[secondary]**: "Not only did elapsed time seem drawn out, but events seemed to happen in slow motion. Yet in contrast to the outward slowing, individuals described their thoughts as speeded up."
  - Quotes Goody's Parkinsonian patient via Sacks (2004) **[secondary; UNVERIFIED at Sacks — the New Yorker URL returned 404]**: "My own movements… seem normal unless I see how long they take by looking at a clock. The clock on the wall of the ward seems to be going exceptionally fast."
- **Tenet alignment**: Bidirectional Interaction (weakly). Arstila's evidence that the effect was experienced at the time, not merely remembered, is that survivors *acted* on fast, complex thought. That is behavioural evidence, compatible with any framework.

### Phillips (2013), "Perceiving the Passing of Time"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9264.2013.00353.x
- **Type**: Paper, *Proceedings of the Aristotelian Society* 113(3pt3): 225–252 [Crossref]; abstract read via OpenAlex
- **Key points**:
  - "Duration distortions familiar from trauma present an apparent counterexample to what we might call the naive view of duration perception" [raw].
  - The counterexample holds only "on the assumption that we perceive duration absolutely". Instead, "once we recognize the constant presence of a stream of non-perceptual conscious mental activity, we can provide an attractive, purely relative account of temporal distortions" [raw].

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

A rate is a ratio, and here both terms lie on the far side of introspection. Experience presents *contents*: a ball arriving, a thought forming, an interval that felt long. It does not present the rate at which those contents are produced against world-time. Dennett and Kinsbourne's content/vehicle distinction says why: the time *of* representing is a vehicle property, and introspection reports what is represented. The void has three layers:

1. **No absolute tempo (Unexplorable).** By Poincaré's argument transposed to time, a uniform rescaling of all processes, mind included, is undetectable by construction, and arguably nothing has happened. If there is a fact about a mind's absolute tempo, no experience could carry it.
2. **Relative tempo only against the world, and only when sudden (Occluded edge).** A change in the mind alone is detectable only through a reference, such as the ward clock, the car, or the ball. Arstila's adaptation argument and the keypress-and-flash recalibration (Stetson et al. 2006, via Arstila and Eagleman 2008) show that the expected timing re-zeroes itself, so only changes outrunning adaptation are noticed.
3. **Outward attribution.** When a rate change is noticed, it is credited to the world. A faster self reports "everything happened in slow motion"; a slower self reports "the clock seems to be going exceptionally fast" [secondary]. One's own tempo serves as the calibration zero. This is the temporal instance of the shape the self-signature note found for accent, voice and style, now folded into `self-opacity` ("The Signature Face").

### Evidence for the Limit

- **Introspection cannot separate the hypotheses.** Stetson (memory), Arstila (processing speed), Phillips (relative to the mental stream), and Buckley's (1) to (4) all predict the same sentence, "time slowed down". Reports are necessarily retrospective, and retrospection is the very medium the memory hypothesis implicates, so no report can exclude it. The case for felt-at-the-time slowing must come from outside the report, from what survivors *did*. That is Arstila's move, and it is a behavioural argument, not an introspective one.
- **Conditions split, and the split is invisible from inside.** Fear in free fall: duration estimates up 36%, temporal resolution unchanged (Stetson). Action preparation: perceived duration up *and* temporal resolution up (Hagura). Oddball: duration up, pitch and flicker unchanged (Pariyadath & Eagleman). The same phenomenal verdict, "that lasted longer", sits on different machinery in each case.
- **No fixed reference within experience.** Whether the oddball expanded or its neighbours contracted is "equally feasible" (Eagleman 2008) [raw]. The experience gives a contrast with no zero point.
- **Tempo is represented per channel.** The Zeitraffer lesion cases alter perceived speed of motion locally. "Time is not a single entity" (Eagleman 2008) [raw] is the third-person face of the first-person absence: there is no single tempo for introspection to read.

### Phenomenology

Try to notice the speed of your experience now. Nothing answers. Experience offers contents and contrasts, such as boredom's drag, absorption's rush, or the frozen second hand, and each is a judgment about an *interval* against an expectation, not a felt rate. The edge is met as a missing dimension, not as an obscured one. When tempo does announce itself, it arrives disguised as a property of the world (the slow train, the fast clock). It is never experienced as "I am running fast." Even Noyes and Kletti's survivors, who reported faster thoughts, described those as many thoughts in a short world-interval [secondary], which is again a ratio to the world.

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods
None for absolute tempo. For relative tempo, there is the in-flight probe design (Stetson's chronometer, Hagura's flicker and letter tasks), which measures capacity during the event rather than relying on memory afterwards. Arstila's objection stands: a probe of low-level resolution may miss a central rate change.

### Indirect Methods
- **Behavioural inference**: Arstila's argument from successful complex action during a seconds-long accident.
- **Dissociation mapping**: which temporal measures (duration, resolution, flicker, pitch, reaction time, iconic capacity) move together under which manipulations. The Jones et al. click-train paradigm is the cleanest global-rate handle.
- **Clinical contrast**: Zeitraffer and akinetopsia for channel-local rate; Parkinsonian bradykinesia for a slowed self against an intact world.
- **Apophatic description**: specify tempo by what experience cannot contain (an absolute rate) and by where it surfaces (sudden relative change, credited outward).

### What AI Might See
For a computational system the void is total and easy to see. A computation's internal state sequence is the same at any execution speed. A language model generating tokens slowly under heavy load and quickly when idle cannot tell the difference except through timestamps supplied as text, which is an outside reference, Goody's ward clock in symbolic form. Egan's Copies running "seventeen times more slowly" dramatise this. The case sharpens the human one into a discriminator, though one that cannot be tested. On a functionalist view, a uniformly slowed emulation has *identical* experience, so tempo is no part of phenomenal character at all. On any view that makes tempo an intrinsic phenomenal parameter, the slowed emulation's experience differs, and nothing available from inside or outside could show it. That is the Unexplorable core, here stated for minds whose execution rate is under an engineer's control.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet
**Occam's Razor Has Limits.** The simplest reading, that experience runs at a rate one can feel and "time slowed" reports a change in it, is false twice over. The report merges separable mechanisms (Stetson; Pariyadath & Eagleman; Eagleman 2008), and there may be no absolute rate for it to report (Poincaré; Phillips).

### Other Tenets (graded per the operation-void wing standard: compatible unless a discriminator is named)
- **Dualism: compatible, no evidential gain.** Physicalist internal-clock models and dualist accounts meet the same limit. If anything, the relational structure is what a deflationary account predicts. The one dualist-friendly reading is weak: Arstila's realist account needs a background "sense of normal speed" that is phenomenal but not introspectible, which is a phenomenal posit, not a dualist one.
- **Bidirectional Interaction: compatible.** Hagura shows the agent's preparation altering perceptual tempo, and Arstila's survivors' fast purposeful action is the strongest anti-epiphenomenal datum here. Both have neural readings; no discriminator.
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction: cautionary constraint.** If the interface acts through discrete selection events (cf. `stapp-quantum-mind`), those events have a rate. This void entails that introspection cannot read it, so felt tempo can never be offered as evidence about an interface's event rate. Any article drawing on the Map's quantum material should state that limit.
- **No Many Worlds: no connection found.**
