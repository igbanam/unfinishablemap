## Executive Summary

The causal relevance of content is the fourth of the four "property-based" problems of mental causation in the SEP's taxonomy. The other three are property dualism, anomalous monism and exclusion. The Map has dedicated pages for anomalous monism and exclusion ([anomalous-monism-and-the-denial-of-strict-psychophysical-laws](/concepts/anomalous-monism-and-the-denial-of-strict-psychophysical-laws/), [causal-exclusion-argument](/concepts/causal-exclusion-argument/)). Property dualism is covered across the Map's dualism pages, and the SEP says it re-runs the interaction arguments that [pairing-problem](/concepts/pairing-problem/) treats. **The Map has no page on the fourth problem, and no live page mentions program explanation, Jackson and Pettit, triggering causes or the content-relevance literature by name.**

The problem runs as follows. If content is fixed partly by relations to the environment, and behaviour is produced by the intrinsic features of the brain, then content looks causally idle. Six response families are standard: intrinsic surrogates and narrow content, Dretske's structuring causes, broad behaviour, explanatory practice, Jackson and Pettit's program explanation, and Yablo's proportionality defence of wide causation.

Tested against this literature, the acquaintance page's answer at L104 ("wide content is fixed by the vehicle's causal history and so is efficacious at one remove") **does not survive as worded**. The inference from *fixed by the causes of the vehicle* to *efficacious* is the inference the literature's own vending-machine example blocks. It does survive in Dretske's structuring-cause form, at two costs: the relevance it secures is past-tense, and it covers learned indicator content but not plainly social externalism.

A cleaner answer is available from the Map's own hybrid view of content, explained in [the hybrid route below](#hybrid-route). There, wide content includes a narrow, phenomenally constituted component and inherits that component's efficacy. On this route the externalist parity objection does not add a second debt. It folds into the content-efficacy posit the Map already records.

On parity (question 3), the debt is general in form, because physicalists face the same "qua" problem. It is special in payment, because the physicalist ways out (program explanation, determinables, subset inheritance) require physical realization, which the Map denies to consciousness.

## Key Sources

### SEP, "Mental Causation", §7 "Problem IV: Externalism" (Robb, Heil & Gibb 2023)
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mental-causation/
- **Type**: Encyclopedia
- **Key points**:
  - The problem: if content is "broad", "it is indeed hard to see how this content could figure in a causal account of her actions" (§7.1). The sources cited for it are Stich 1978, Kim 1982, Fodor 1980, 1987 and 1991, and Jackson & Pettit 1988.
  - The vending-machine analogy (after Dretske 1998). A coin's being a quarter "depends on its having the right sort of history", yet "This is something the vending machine cares nothing about"; the coin affects the machine "not qua quarter, but only qua possessor of a particular kind of intrinsic makeup" (§7.1).
  - Four responses are surveyed: intrinsic causal surrogates and narrow content (§7.2), reasons as structuring causes (§7.3), broad behaviour (§7.4), and the appeal to explanatory practice (§7.5).
  - §3 ("The Ascent to Properties") frames all four property-based problems as threatening "a form of epiphenomenalism: even if minds and mental events are causes, they are not causes as (or qua) mental". It also says these features are "no less problematic than mind–body interaction is for the Cartesian dualist".
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral survey. §3 supplies the parity point the task asks about.
- **Quote**: "If intentional properties are relevant at all, then, they are apparently relevant only to what happened in the past during the learning process." (§7.3, stating an objection to Dretske)

### Dretske, *Explaining Behavior* (1988); "Mental Events as Structuring Causes of Behaviour" (1993a)
- **Type**: Book; book chapter
- **Key points**:
  - The distinction (1988, p. 43, verbatim snippet): "triggering cause of a process ( the cause of the C which causes M ) and what I have been calling its structuring cause ( the cause of C's causing M )".
  - The thermostat case (p. 86): the drop in temperature "initiates a process which has M as its outcome . But it does not cause C to cause M."
  - The thesis (1993a, abstract and opening paragraph): "psychological explanations provide structuring causes of behaviour, while biological explanations provide triggering causes of behaviour".
  - Mechanism, from SEP §7.3's summary: a state is "recruited" during learning as a cause of movement because of what it indicates, so its relational content is relevant to the fact that it causes that movement.
  - Dretske admits (1996 reply, abstract) that he "did not extend" the account to novel behaviours and denies that it "is not extendible", citing his ch. 6.
- **Critics** (SEP §7.3): Smith 1990, Block 1990, Baker 1991, Horgan 1991, Kim 1991, Mele 1991; later Hofmann & Schulte 2014. The SEP records two objections. One is screening-off during learning: "Even during the learning process, the states of Lilian's brain would seem to be sensitive only to local, intrinsic features of one another, features that screen off external goings-on". The other is past-only relevance, quoted above.
- **Tenet alignment**: Dretske is a naturalist; the view is physicalist. It is the closest literature analogue of the acquaintance page's "at one remove" answer.

### Jackson & Pettit, program explanation (1988, 1990a, 1990b; reprinted 2004)
- **Type**: Papers (*Mind*; *PPR*; *Analysis*)
- **Key points**:
  - The 1990a abstract places the problem in "the argument that broad content is explanatorily irrelevant to behaviour because doppelgangers behave alike while possibly differing in broad content".
  - Relevance can come without efficacy: "some cases where an instance of F is causally relevant to an instance of G are cases of relevance without efficacy" (1990a, 2004 reprint p. 60).
  - From the 1988 paper (reprint p. 109): "we can and often do explain by citing a feature which causally programs without causing". From p. 113: "Functional state explanations , then , are program explanations".
  - **Dualism parallel**: the 1990a paper sets functionalism's content problem beside "the familiar objection to dualism that the interactionist variety of dualism has to give way to an epiphenomenalist variety and so much the worse for dualism!" (reprint p. 48). Its footnote cites Jackson's "Epiphenomenal Qualia" for "reservations about the decisiveness of this argument when ( and only when ) directed at qualia".
  - The paper then claims "a major difference between dualism and functionalism" (reprint p. 51). **The reason given is not read (snippet truncated): LEAD.**
  - The 1988 paper considers the history reply directly (reprint p. 106): "What that cup and various samples of water did to me in the past is in part responsible for those narrow states which lead me to reach for that cup. The substantial doppelganger challenge, the one that calls for a ..." **The continuation is unread: LEAD, and the single most important passage to read before drafting** (see [Guards](#guards)).
  - The SEP (§6.4) classes program explanation as an "inheritance" solution: "Causal relevance in this sense is an explanatory notion", a "lower-grade" relevance inherited through realization.
- **Tenet alignment**: Physicalist (functionalist). Useful for parity because the authors themselves draw the dualism parallel.

### Yablo, "Wide Causation" (1997)
- **URL**: http://www.mit.edu/~yablo/wc.pdf (author's copy; the journal version is *Philosophical Perspectives* 11, issued as *Noûs* 31(s11))
- **Key points**:
  - Yablo names two threats, "from below" (exclusion) and "from within" (externalism), and argues they are linked through the principle "proportionality: causes must be proportional to their effects" (§3).
  - WITHIN's charge, in Yablo's words: "Regardless of whether my desire had been for water or twater, as long as I stayed intrinsically the same the behavioral results would not have been any different" (§3).
  - Proportionality is a necessary condition only: "X does not cause Y unless it is proportional to it" (§6).
  - On inclusion (§6): "When one state is included in another, any influence that the first has on subsequent events is included in the influence had by the second."
  - "an extrinsic state need not be extrinsic through and through; it can have intrinsic parts or aspects" (§16). Yablo calls an attitude **thick** when it includes its intrinsic counterpart, the "battitude" [A], as a determinable, and **thin** when it is noncommittal about the thinker's internal contribution.
  - The verdict splits by kind of attitude. On thick attitudes "WITHIN has a point" (§16). On thin attitudes: "Thin attitudes have nothing to fear from WITHIN" (§16).
  - WITHIN, he argues, rests on a "superproportionality" that would also strip ordinary causes of their status (§§17–19): "intentional causes incorporate unneeded detail. But all intuitive causes, intentional or not, are like this" (§19).
- **Tenet alignment**: Yablo's answer to BELOW treats mental properties as determinables of physical ones, which the Map declines at `causal-exclusion-argument` L67. His WITHIN argument concerns attitudes and their intrinsic counterparts and does not need that physicalist premise.

### Other sources, by role
- **Content is idle; intrinsic surrogates do the work**: Stich 1978; Fodor 1987, ch. 2 (SEP narrow content §3.1: "the causal powers of an entity ... must be intrinsic features of the entity"); Fodor 1991 (a weaker modal version). - **Content explanations were never causal**: Owens 1993 (abstract) uses Fodor's concession "to invert his argument and cast doubt on the received model of folk psychological explanation as causal explanation by intentionally individuated states".
- **Local causation is compatible with broad individuation**: Burge 1986 and 1989, per SEP narrow content §3.1 (LEAD at primary).
- **Explanatory practice**: Segal & Sober 1991, Burge 1993 (SEP §7.5). The SEP's objection is that the strategy "would at best seem to be addressing only the 'whether' question, not the 'how' question".
- **All macro-properties are inefficacious**: Segal 2009 (abstract). Psychological properties, "along with paradigmatically causally efficacious macro-properties, such as toughness, are causally inefficacious in respect of their possessor's typical effects", and "the distinction between epiphenomenalism and overdeterminationism may be more terminological than real". Note that the same author co-wrote Segal & Sober 1991, which defends the efficacy of content.

- **The qua formulation**: the SEP (§6.3) credits Horgan 1989 ("Mental Quausation") with the point that "a causally relevant property is relevant to some particular property of the effect". Horgan is unread. The word "quausation" is his title; "qua problem" is the task's label, not a term found in any source read here.

## Major Positions

### Content epiphenomenalism / intrinsic-surrogate eliminativism
- **Proponents**: Stich (1978, 1983); in its narrow-content form, Fodor (1987, 1991).
- **Core claim**: causal powers are intrinsic, twins share them, and so broad content cannot be what produces behaviour.
- **Relation to site tenets**: the Map's hybrid already denies that *phenomenal* content is broad. For wide intentional content the Map has no stake in denying the argument's conclusion.

### Structuring causes (Dretske)
- **Core claim**: content explains why a state causes a movement, not what triggers the movement.
- **Relation to site tenets**: secures relevance through history alone, which matches the acquaintance page's phrase "efficacious at one remove". Carries the past-only cost.

### Program explanation (Jackson & Pettit)
- **Core claim**: content is causally relevant but not efficacious. It programs for the presence of efficacious realizers.
- **Relation to site tenets**: needs realization. It cannot serve the Map's account of consciousness, which is not physically realized. Whether it can serve an epiphenomenalist's nomically correlated qualia is the open question flagged under the p. 51 LEAD.

### Proportionality and wide causation (Yablo)
- **Core claim**: WITHIN's standard proves too much, and thin wide attitudes are proportional causes.
- **Relation to site tenets**: the most direct vindication of wide content's causal relevance. It does not by itself discriminate wide content from an epiphenomenal quale, because proportionality is only a necessary condition.

### Explanatory practice and broad behaviour (Burge; Segal & Sober; Enc)
- **Core claim**: what our best causal explanations cite is causally relevant, and wide content is relevant to behaviour described widely.
- **Relation to site tenets**: on this standard an epiphenomenalist's qualia also come out relevant, since ordinary explanation cites experiences. The standard dissolves epiphenomenalism rather than separating it from interactionism.

## Key Debates

### Are causal powers intrinsic?
- **Sides**: Fodor and Stich (yes) against Burge and Yablo (no, or not in the sense needed).
- **Current state**: ongoing (Gaukroger 2017 lead). Fodor himself retreated in 1995.

### Is structuring relevance real causal relevance to present action?
- **Sides**: Dretske against Kim 1991, Baker 1991, Horgan 1991 and others (SEP §7.3).
- **Current state**: ongoing (Hofmann & Schulte 2014 lead).

### Is relevance-without-efficacy enough, or is it epiphenomenalism renamed?
- **Sides**: Jackson & Pettit (enough); Kim's freeloader worry; Segal 2009 (the distinction may be "terminological").
- **Current state**: open. The SEP §6.4 sentence "If such a weakening seems to amount to epiphenomenalism" marks the worry.

## Does "Efficacious at One Remove" Survive? {#one-remove}

The acquaintance page's answer at L104 has two clauses. The first: "wide content is fixed by the vehicle's causal history and so is efficacious at one remove". The second: "an epiphenomenal phenomenal constituent has no causal history in the vehicle at all".

**1. As worded, it does not survive.** The "so" needs an inference from *content is fixed by the causes of the vehicle* to *content is efficacious*, and the vending-machine case blocks it. The coin's value is fixed by its history, and the minting caused the coin, yet the machine "cares nothing about" the value (SEP §7.1). Twin Earth gives the same result for content. H2O caused Oscar's tokens and XYZ caused Twin-Oscar's, so each content-fixing fact lies in its vehicle's causal history. The twins still behave alike, which is exactly the doppelganger premise Jackson & Pettit report (1990a abstract) and Yablo's WITHIN (§3). The worded answer equivocates. It shows that the *relata* that fix content were causes of the vehicle. It does not show that the *content* made a difference to what the vehicle does.

**2. It does survive in Dretske's form, at a price.** Dretske's structuring causes are the literature's worked-out version of relevance through the vehicle's causal history. The asymmetry the page wants then holds. An epiphenomenal quale causes nothing, so it can never have figured in the recruitment that wired C to M, and it cannot be a structuring cause. Three costs follow, in rising order of severity for the Map:

- **(a) Scope.** The account was built for learned indicator content. Burge's social externalism varies the community while holding the individual's history fixed, and it is not plainly covered (Map's analysis, LEAD). Extending the account to inference, where nothing is reinforced token by token, is the "novel behaviour" gap Dretske answered by pointing to his ch. 6 (1996 reply, abstract; ch. 6 unread).
- **(b) Screening-off during learning** (SEP §7.3, first objection).
- **(c) Past-only relevance** (SEP §7.3, second objection). This cost is sharper for the Map than for Dretske. The efficacy-of-evidence premise (acquaintance L102) concerns whether *this* inference or report is made *from* this evidence, which is a token basing relation in the present tense. A structuring cause explains why a type of state has the inferential role it has. It does not say that this token was produced by the evidence's evidential features now. A Dretskean reading of basing would need stating, and that would be the Map's own construction. Acquaintance L56 already says of the causal theory of basing that the Map "has stated that theory nowhere".

**3. The weaker standards do not secure the asymmetry.** This is the Map's synthesis, not any source's claim.

- *Counterfactual relevance* (SEP §7.2). Content enters the counterfactuals because, in the agent's environment, intrinsic state co-varies with content. On a property dualism with biconditional psychophysical laws, a nomically correlated epiphenomenal quale supports the same counterfactuals. The SEP's freeloader worry (§5.3, Kim 1998) applies to both, or to neither. Robinson's parity argument at acquaintance L86 is the voice-channel form of the same move.
- *Proportionality* (Yablo). Proportionality removes WITHIN's objection, but because it is only a necessary condition it confers no causal status. It is neutral between wide content and the quale.
- *Explanatory practice*. Ordinary explanation cites experiences, so this standard makes the epiphenomenalist's qualia relevant and dissolves the view instead of discriminating it.
- *Program explanation*. If programming requires realization, the epiphenomenalist's quale is excluded, and so is the Map's consciousness. Jackson & Pettit claim a "major difference" between dualism and functionalism (p. 51), and that may be exactly this point (LEAD).

**Upshot.** The parity answer depends on which standard is used. The efficacy-of-evidence premise must be read with a causal-relevance standard strong enough to exclude nomic danglers and weak enough to admit wide content. Of the standards above, Dretske's historical standard and a realization-based program standard meet that condition, and the counterfactual and practice standards do not.

A further constraint comes from the J&P/Segal distinction. If the premise demanded *efficacy* in the strict sense, functionalist content would fail it (Jackson & Pettit concede content is relevant without being efficacious), as would macro-properties generally (Segal 2009). The premise would then reduce to absurdity. So L102's "causally contribute" has to mean relevance, and the writer must say which relevance.

## The Hybrid Route {#hybrid-route}

The Map has a second answer that needs no historical claim. It is the Map's own synthesis and should be presented that way.

The Map holds a hybrid: wide intentional content, narrow phenomenal content, and phenomenal intentionality as basic (`content-externalism` L20 and L42; `swampman` L53–L59). The SEP's narrow-content entry records the matching argument, "(1) There is pervasive intentional content that constitutively depends on phenomenology alone. (2) Phenomenology constitutively depends only on narrow factors" (Horgan and Tienson 2002, p. 527, quoted at SEP narrow content §3.4).

On that view a wide belief *includes* a narrow, phenomenally constituted component, in Yablo's sense that an extrinsic state "can have intrinsic parts or aspects" (§16). Read this way, the Map's wide attitudes count as **thick** in Yablo's terms (the Map's mapping, not Yablo's). Yablo's verdict on thick attitudes is that "WITHIN has a point" (§16). Thick attitudes "really are in danger of being knocked out of proportion with typical behavioral effects by their intrinsic counterparts", and whether that happens "depends on the details of the case". The hybrid route accepts that verdict and moves the efficacy-of-evidence premise onto the included component:

- the evidential features that must do causal work are the narrow, phenomenally constituted ones, the features the subject is presented with. Inference among externally individuated beliefs is then made *from* their evidence because their included narrow components drive it. This is the SEP §7.2 line that surrogates "might in fact play a role in ensuring" content's efficacy, with the Map's phenomenal content as the surrogate. Yablo's inclusion principle (§6), "When one state is included in another, any influence that the first has on subsequent events is included in the influence had by the second", lets the wide state be credited with that work in the derivative sense;
- the route transmits work only when the included component that does the work is the one carrying the evidential features. For the epiphenomenalist, what does the work is the physical vehicle, while the evidential features of phenomenal evidence are phenomenal. The quale neither includes, nor is included in, that vehicle; it merely co-varies with it by law. So inclusion cannot carry the vehicle's work to the quale. The asymmetry the acquaintance page needs then rests on inclusion rather than on history.

**The payoff for the Map.** On the hybrid, the externalist parity objection adds no new debt. It reduces to the posit the acquaintance page already prices at L106–L108: whether consciousness acts in virtue of what it presents (Tenet 3's "toward patterns that express its content"; `positions/quantum-interface#^mechanism-debt`). WITHIN never reaches the Map's content clause, because the content that clause concerns is narrow by the Map's own lights.

**Costs.** (i) The route concedes WITHIN for wide content. It works only if the narrow components carry the evidential relations, and Boghossian 1992 on externalism and inference is the hazard (LEAD, below). (ii) The answer is tied to phenomenal intentionality, which is contested: the SEP narrow-content entry notes that "Both premises of this argument are controversial". (iii) Yablo states the inclusion principle in §6 while discussing pain and brain state as determinable and determinate, and he goes on to refine it with proportionality. The Map can use it for wide and narrow content within the mental. It should not import it for the mind–brain relation, which `causal-exclusion-argument` L67 declines.

## Is the Content Clause a Dualist-Specific Debt? (Parity) {#parity}

**General in form.** The SEP says mental properties have "up to four features" that make their efficacy puzzling, externalism among them, and calls them "no less problematic than mind–body interaction is for the Cartesian dualist" (§3). The threat it names is that minds "are not causes as (or qua) mental". Jackson & Pettit set functionalism's content problem beside the objection that drives interactionist dualism into epiphenomenalism (reprint p. 48). Segal 2009 extends inefficacy to macro-properties such as toughness. Physicalists of every stripe therefore owe an account of how content is relevant *qua* content. The Tenet 5 point stands: the existence of the debt is no special evidence against dualism.

**Special in payment.** The physicalist ways of discharging the debt all run through realization: program explanation (inheritance; SEP §6.4), Yablo's determinables, and the subset strategy. The Map's [causal-exclusion-argument](/concepts/causal-exclusion-argument/) already declines the subset and determinable routes because consciousness has no physical realizer. Program explanation is closed for the same reason, so the Map cannot pay its instance of the shared debt in the shared currency. Its payment is its own: selection at indeterminacies, where no sufficient physical cause exists for content to be excluded by (`causal-exclusion-argument`, route 3). That payment is available but not shown to be actual, which is the mechanism debt.

**Net.** The externalism problem specifically is not a dualist debt. The Map can answer it with the shared literature, best through the hybrid route. What remains is one debt, the "in virtue of what it presents" clause. It is a dualist-shaped instance of the general qua problem, and the shared literature does not discharge it.
