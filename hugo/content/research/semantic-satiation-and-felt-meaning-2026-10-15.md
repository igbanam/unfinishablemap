## Summary

**Verdict: FOLD-IN.** The literature is richer on the competence side than the reviewer saw and thinner on the phenomenal side than the Map's pages imply, and the two sides have never been measured together at trial level on the same items. That combination settles the scope: a short paragraph in [phenomenal-constitution-thesis](/concepts/phenomenal-constitution-thesis/)'s "Constituting versus Accompanying" section, a one-clause addition to [P-TU1](/positions/thought-and-understanding/#p-tu1)'s *Would shift if*, and corrections to two existing citations in [recognition-void](/voids/recognition-void/) and one figure in [memory-anomalies](/topics/memory-anomalies/). No new article; no `pending_articles` entry.

**The dissociation question, answered.** Satiation is a dissociation of felt meaning from *accuracy*-competence and a mild degradation of *speed*-competence, not a joint decline. Tian and Huber's category-matching accuracy never interacted with repetition in any of four experiments and was *higher* for the repeated category; what "gradually slowed" was a priming advantage of about 10–20 ms that reversed into a deficit of about the same size by the last third of a 20-trial block (Table 2: repeated-match 595 → 620 ms against non-repeated 604 → 609 ms). That is the loss of a facilitation, not a failure of competence, and the reviewer's worry that Tian and Huber's slowing "point[s] toward competence falling" does not survive the full text. Moulin and colleagues' word-copying induction is the one study that pairs a phenomenal report with a same-item competence measure: post-experimental recognition of the alienated words was unimpaired ("if anything ... better"), and the modal justification for the episode was "I knew it was spelt right, but it seemed wrong" (43.3%). Both are shallow competences — category matching, recognition memory — and neither is paired with a *meaning*-character report at trial level. So the induced zero case [P-TU1](/positions/thought-and-understanding/#p-tu1) wants is *constructible* from existing paradigms but has not been *built*.

**What it lends Tenet 1.** Nothing beyond what the [evidential discipline](/project/evidential-status-discipline/) already allows. Under the constitutive reading, satiation is the understanding-versus-processing distinction induced on demand (meaning lapses for the subject while processing continues); under the accompaniment reading, a fluency signal has dropped out of a still-contentful state. The record is consistent with either, and Başoğlu's 2026 paper — the one philosophical treatment of satiation found — argues that the reports are mostly about *sub-semantic* properties (handwriting, spelling, letters), which is the deflationist's reading made explicit. The honest gain is procedural: [P-TU1](/positions/thought-and-understanding/#p-tu1)'s discriminability is rated "indirect" because its zero case has to be found; satiation shows it could be induced, and the position should say what result would count.

## The Dissociation Question

Is satiation a dissociation of felt meaning from competence, or a joint decline? The table lists every study read, with its repetition manipulation, whether it took any phenomenal measure, what competence it measured, and the result.

Three things follow.

1. **No study pairs a trial-level report of felt meaning with a competence measure on the same items.** The competence-side literature (Smith & Klein through Ströberg) was built precisely to escape "report bias" (Tian & Huber's phrase) and takes no phenomenal measure at all. The phenomenal-side literature (Severance & Washburn through Moulin et al.) measures competence once, post hoc, as recognition memory. The brief's unsettled question is therefore unsettled in the record, not merely in the reviewer's reading.

2. **Where competence is measured, it survives.** Accuracy never falls with repetition in any speeded study read; the only degradations are in speed (a reversed priming advantage of tens of milliseconds) and in priming magnitude (relatedness and N400 effects shrink). Smith & Klein's 30-repetition condition still showed positive priming. Moulin et al.'s subjects recognised the alienated words as well as the others, and 43% of them said in so many words that they still *knew* the spelling was right. Satiation is a dissociation, not a joint decline.

3. **What lapses is not cleanly "felt meaning".** Moulin et al.'s justification counts are dominated by form-level strangeness (handwriting, spelling, "a string of letters"); Başoğlu reads the same data as evidence that the reports "point out some sub-semantic properties of the stimulus". The competence-side studies that locate the effect at a semantic level (Kounios 2000, Ströberg 2017) are contradicted by Frenck-Mestre 1997 and Pilotti 1997, and Tian & Huber's associative account sits between the two, so even the locus is contested. The Map should therefore not describe satiation as "felt meaning driven to zero"; it should describe it as felt familiarity-and-meaning lapsing while form-level strangeness takes its place.

## The Three Questions for the Map

### 1. Dissociation or joint decline?

Dissociation, with the qualification in point 3 above. Competence as accuracy survives; competence as speed degrades by an amount that would be invisible outside a reaction-time laboratory; priming magnitude falls. The reviewer's Tian & Huber worry inverts on the full text: "gradually slowed" names a reversed facilitation, and the same paper reports accuracy *rising* in the repeated condition.

### 2. What does it show about the constitutive reading versus the accompaniment reading?

Less than either side would like, and exactly as much as the Map's discipline predicts. Under PCT, the subject for whom "the" has become "a string of letters instead of a whole word" is a subject for whom the content has lapsed *as content* while a processing capacity continues — the understanding-versus-processing distinction the Chinese Room verdict rests on, produced in the laboratory rather than imagined. Under the accompaniment reading, the content is untouched (the subject still categorises, still recognises, still knows the spelling is right) and what has dropped out is a noetic feeling of fluency or familiarity, which is how [noetic-feelings-void](/voids/noetic-feelings-void/) already files it. The data do not adjudicate, because the surviving competence is shallow (category matching, recognition) and the lapsed character is reported in form-level terms. Başoğlu's paper is worth registering as the dialectical state of the art: it argues that satiation fails to support *semantic perceptualism* (the Nes/Brogaard/Bayne claim that meanings are perceptually experienced) because its effects are explicable at sub-semantic and associative levels. That is the deflationist's argument applied to the perceptual version of the liberal thesis, and the Map's constitutive reading is downstream of the liberal thesis, so the paper is a reason for the Map not to lean on satiation. It is not a refutation of PCT, which does not require that meaning be *perceived*.

## Tenet Assessment

- **Tenet 1 (Dualism)**: neutral. Satiation is a dissociation case of the kind [P-TU1](/positions/thought-and-understanding/#p-tu1)'s grade C already discounts; its phenomenal interpretation is contested (Başoğlu 2026 reads the reports as sub-semantic) exactly where the aphantasia and insight cases are. It lends irreducibility nothing beyond "consistent with".
- **Tenet 2 (Minimal Quantum Interaction)**: not engaged.
- **Tenet 3 (Bidirectional Interaction)**: not engaged. That competence survives the lapse of felt meaning is, if anything, a small datum the epiphenomenalist can use (processing proceeds without the feeling); PCT's reply — that what proceeds is processing, not understanding — is the conditional reply the PCT page already makes.
- **Tenet 4 (No Many Worlds)**: not engaged.
- **Tenet 5 (Occam's Razor Has Limits)**: mildly engaged. The simplest story (perceptual habituation, Pilotti 1997; bottom-up saturation, Zhang 2024) is contradicted by the N400 studies that varied case and format to exclude it (Kounios 2000, Ströberg 2017), a small instance of simplicity being unreliable with an incomplete mechanism.

## Gaps in Research

- **No same-item pairing exists.** The decisive experiment — trial-level felt-meaning (not peculiarity) ratings against a competence measure with transfer and error detection, on the same items — was not found in OpenAlex (330 post-2009 hits for the phrase, 154 post-2020, none matching) or by web search. Its absence is the finding.
- **Lambert & Jakobovits 1960's measure** is described three ways by three secondary sources: semantic-differential polarity (the standard account, matching the title), "ratings of lexical validity" (Tian & Huber), and lexical decision (Başoğlu). PsycNet blocked the abstract and the McGill dissertation download returned HTML. The note uses the polarity description and flags it.
- **Smith & Klein 1990** was read only through Tian & Huber and Ströberg; the reviewer's Crossref metadata is confirmed but no quotation from the paper itself is tagged [raw].
- **Severance & Washburn 1907** page range (182–186) rests on two reference lists; Crossref and OpenAlex record the first page only.
- **Esposito & Pelton 1971** verdict is quoted via Ströberg; the review itself was not fetched.
- **Gasparri & Murez** full text was blocked at PhilPapers; it is cited by Başoğlu as 2021 and dated 2019 online by OpenAlex, and the note does not rely on it.
- **Zhang et al. 2024** was read for its abstract and the bottom-up claim; the two-column PDF interleaved badly and the body was not quoted.
- **The 2022 valence study** (Journal of Psychological Science, Chinese; modified speeded category matching, satiation absent for negative words) surfaced in web search but was not fetched; it does not pair a phenomenal measure either.
- **The Map's "semanticisation falsifier" at PCT L72** named in the brief does not exist at that line; the nearest falsifier text is [cognitive-phenomenology](/concepts/cognitive-phenomenology/) L206, which the fold-in does not touch.
