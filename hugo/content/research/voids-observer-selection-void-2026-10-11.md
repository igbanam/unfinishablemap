## Executive Summary

A mind that asks what its own existence and situation are evidence of meets a limit that has resisted fifty years of formal work. The questions run: *given that I exist, that I exist now, that I am this observer and not another, what should I believe?* Every candidate rule for updating on self-locating evidence has a reductio that its own defenders accept as one. The Self-Sampling Assumption (SSA) yields the Doomsday argument. The Self-Indication Assumption (SIA) yields the Presumptuous Philosopher, who claims to settle cosmology from the armchair. The Sleeping Beauty problem (Elga 2000) still has no agreed answer after twenty-five years. Every rule also needs a *reference class* of observers one might have been, and that class is a class of **conscious** subjects. So self-location presupposes an answer to the distribution of consciousness, which is exactly what the [other-minds problem](/concepts/problem-of-other-minds/) withholds. Some literature runs the inference backwards, concluding from anthropic statistics that ants are not conscious (Standish 2013). A third face appears in cosmology: theories in which most observers are Boltzmann brains "cannot simultaneously be true and justifiably believed" (Carroll 2017/2020), and the leading epistemologists who examined that diagnosis found it insufficient (Kotzen 2020; Elga 2024 MS). The void bears directly on the [No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds) tenet. Everettian Born-rule derivations and Everettian confirmation both run through self-locating credence. The same void also forbids the Map from turning its own indexical objection into a credence argument.

### Bostrom, *Anthropic Bias: Observation Selection Effects in Science and Philosophy* (2002)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.4324/9780203953464 (Routledge ebook DOI); full text at anthropic-principle.com (author's PDF, read this run)
- **Type**: Book, Routledge, New York, xiii + 224 pp. **VERIFIED**: publisher, year and pagination confirmed through three indexed reviews (Ćirković 2002, *Foundations of Physics* 32(11); Ćirković 2003, *Foundations of Science* 8(4); Wüthrich 2004, *Philosophy of Science* 71(2)). The Routledge DOI resolves to the 2013 ebook reissue.
- **Key points**:
  - SSA, preliminary formulation (p. 57, verbatim from author's PDF): "One should reason as if one were a random sample from the set of all observers in one's reference class."
  - Strong SSA (ch. 10, verbatim): "One should reason as if one's present observer-moment were a random sample from the set of all observer-moments in its reference class." Moving from observers to observer-moments relocates the individuation problem without resolving it.
  - The **reference class problem**, in Bostrom's own words (p. 71, verbatim): rocks, planets and bacteria are excluded, but "It gets trickier when we consider possible borderline cases such as a gifted chimpanzee, a Neanderthal, or a mentally disabled human. … Could you have found yourself as such a person?" Membership is decided by the counterfactual "could you have been it", which is a question about subjecthood.
  - On "you could have been" any observer (p. 57, verbatim): "the assertion that 'you could have been' any of these intelligent observers who will ever have existed is also problematic. Ultimately, we may have to confront this problem."
  - The Presumptuous Philosopher (ch. 7, p. 124). Two cosmologies differ only in observer count by a factor of a trillion. A philosopher using SIA tells the physicists their decisive experiment is "completely unnecessary" because the bigger theory is already "about a trillion times more likely". Bostrom treats this as a reductio of SIA.
  - **Counterweight, to be carried into any article.** In ch. 8 (p. 136, verbatim) Bostrom gives a "physicalistic reformulation, showing that the observers' disagreement does not imply some mysterious irreducible role for the observers' consciousness." Bostrom does not think anthropic reasoning needs dualism, and an article must not imply that he does.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam (rival a priori counting principles); Dualism (reference class membership turns on subjecthood; see the class face).

### Arntzenius, "Some Problems for Conditionalization and Reflection" (2003)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.5840/jphil2003100729
- **Type**: Paper, *Journal of Philosophy* 100(7): 356–370. **VERIFIED** (Crossref; JSTOR scan read).
- **Key points**:
  - Five puzzles (Two Roads to Shangri La, The Prisoner, Collins's Prisoner, Sleeping Beauty, Duplication) in which a rational agent's degrees of belief develop in ways that violate conditionalization and van Fraassen's Reflection.
  - Two mechanisms, "shifting" and "spreading" (pp. 367–368). **Shifting** is the movement of self-locating beliefs as time passes. **Spreading** occurs when "two distinct possible experiential paths … end up in the same experiential state" (Shangri La: memory of the Sea path is replaced by a memory of the Mountains).
  - ⚠️ Arntzenius states (p. 368) that Shangri La's spreading "does not involve self-locating degrees of belief". An article must not cite Shangri La as a *self-locating* case. Cite it for the broader claim that standard Bayesian updating has no rule for agents whose experiences can converge or whose location shifts.
- **Tenet alignment**: Dualism, weakly. The breakdown is at the level of the subject's own trajectory, not the world's.

### Piccione & Rubinstein, the absent-minded driver (1997)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1006/game.1997.0536 (original); https://doi.org/10.1006/game.1997.0579 (synthesis, read this run)
- **Type**: Papers, *Games and Economic Behavior* 20(1): 3–24 ("On the Interpretation of Decision Problems with Imperfect Recall") and 20(1): 121–130 ("The Absent-Minded Driver's Paradox: Synthesis and Responses"). **VERIFIED** (Crossref). The original paper's body was not read. Quotes below come from the synthesis paper.
- **Key points**:
  - A driver must take the second of two indistinguishable exits and cannot remember whether he has passed one. The synthesis states the conflict (p. 122, verbatim): "the driver's example has a paradoxical flavor due to the conflict between two ways of reasoning at an intersection. The first instructs the decision maker to follow his initial decision not to exit … The second way of reasoning, maximizing the expected payoff given the belief, suggests he should deviate from his initial decision."
  - Its value is **cross-observer convergence**. The problem came out of economics and game theory, not anthropic philosophy, and a whole special issue (Aumann, Hart & Perry; Battigalli; Gilboa; Halpern; Lipman) responded without a consensus resolution. Bostrom later linked it to anthropics ("game theoretic problems with imperfect recall" is in his book's scope statement), so independence holds at origin but not after 2002.
- **Tenet alignment**: Bidirectional Interaction, weakly. The puzzle concerns the agent's choice at a moment it cannot locate. See "Implications".

### Carroll, "Why Boltzmann Brains Are Bad" (2017 preprint; 2020 chapter)
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.00850 ; https://doi.org/10.4324/9781315713151-3
- **Type**: Chapter in Dasgupta, Dotan & Weslake (eds.), *Current Controversies in Philosophy of Science*, Routledge 2020, pp. 7–20. **VERIFIED** (Crossref; arXiv abstract read).
- **Key points**:
  - Abstract (verbatim): "The issue is not that the existence of such observers is ruled out by data, but that the theories that predict them are cognitively unstable: they cannot simultaneously be true and justifiably believed."
  - The problem concerns "fluctuations into observers who are locally identical to ordinary observers, and their existence cannot be swept under the rug by a choice of probability distributions over observers."
  - Carroll uses the instability as a criterion for selecting physics (a universe that does not persist long enough to produce Boltzmann brains). An epistemic requirement on believers thus constrains cosmology.
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits. Cognitive instability is a pragmatic norm of inquiry, not a likelihood, much as the Map reads parsimony as methodological.

### Elga, "Bracketing and Boltzmann Brains" (manuscript, version of 15 April 2024)
- **URL**: https://www.princeton.edu/~adame/bracketing-and-boltzmann-brains-for-rec/2024-04-15.9-bracketing-and-boltzmann-brains.pdf
- **Type**: Unpublished manuscript for the 2024 Rutgers Epistemology Workshop. Text read in full this run. **Publication status UNVERIFIED**. Cite as a manuscript only.
- **Key points**:
  - His first listed line of resistance (p. 2, verbatim): "At the first step one might deny that randomly-generated entities are conscious." Elga's list thus makes the consciousness question the first exit from the Boltzmann-brain argument.
  - On whether bracketing resolves the cosmological case (p. 15, verbatim): "The short answer is: I have no idea, and maybe nothing."

### Saad, "Lessons from the void: What Boltzmann brains teach" (2024 online; 2025 print)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/phib.12353
- **Type**: Paper, *Analytic Philosophy* 66(4): 594–621 (online 16 June 2024; issue December 2025). **VERIFIED** (Crossref; abstract read).
- **Key points**:
  - Abstract (verbatim): "the putatively unacceptable consequences of this prediction follow only given certain philosophical assumptions." ### Standish, "Ants Are Not Conscious" (2013)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.4236/ojpp.2013.31001
- **Type**: Paper, *Open Journal of Philosophy* 3(1): 1–4. **VERIFIED** (Crossref; abstract read). ⚠️ Venue caution: a low-profile open-access journal (Scientific Research Publishing). Cite as an exhibit of the inference pattern, not as an authority.
- **Key points**: Abstract (verbatim): "Anthropic reasoning is a form of statistical reasoning based upon finding oneself a member of a particular reference class of conscious beings. By considering empirical distribution functions defined over animal life on Earth, we can deduce that the vast bulk of animal life is unlikely to be conscious." The argument runs from self-location to the distribution of consciousness, the reverse of the Saad/Elga direction. Placed together, the two directions show the circle: the class needs the distribution, and the distribution is inferred from the class.
- **Tenet alignment**: Dualism (the other-minds void enters as a premise).

### Bostrom, "Are We Living in a Computer Simulation?" (2003)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9213.00309
- **Type**: Paper, *Philosophical Quarterly* 53(211): 243–255. **VERIFIED** (Crossref and the OUP article page this run; author's PDF read).
- ⚠️ **Title discrepancy.** The publisher (OUP page and Crossref) displays the title as "Are **We** Living in a Computer Simulation?". The author's PDF, which carries the journal's publication line, is headed "Are **You** Living …". Four live Map files cite the "You" form ([simulation-detection-void](/voids/simulation-detection-void/), [meta-epistemology-of-limits](/voids/meta-epistemology-of-limits/), concepts/adaptive-computational-depth, concepts/simulation). This note only records the side finding; it does not change those files.
- **Key points**:
  - §V, "A Bland Indifference Principle": conditional on most observers with human-type experiences being simulated, one's credence in being simulated should be near unity. Bostrom stresses (verbatim) that the principle "prescribes indifference only between hypotheses about which observer you are, when you have no information about which of these observers you are," and that it is weaker than the Doomsday premise.
  - The argument also needs §II's substrate-independence assumption, "that, in fact, a computer running a suitable program would be conscious". Simulated minds belong in the reference class only if that holds.
  - So [simulation-detection-void](/voids/simulation-detection-void/) (L42: "a randomly chosen conscious entity would almost certainly be simulated") sits **downstream** of this void at two points: the indifference step and the class-membership step. ⚠️ **Calibration**: the indifference step is *within-world* indifference, the least contested region of self-locating epistemology. The rule face bites hardest across worlds (SSA vs SIA), so the simulation argument is less exposed to the rule face than Doomsday is. It is fully exposed to the class face.
- **Tenet alignment**: Dualism (substrate independence is the premise the Map's dualism questions).

### Groisman, "The End of Sleeping Beauty's Nightmare" (2008)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/axn015
- **Type**: Paper, *BJPS* 59(3): 409–416. **VERIFIED** (Crossref; abstract read).
- **Key points**: Abstract (verbatim): "the two different degrees of belief presented in the Sleeping Beauty Problem are in fact beliefs in two different propositions." ## The Void

### Nature of the Limit

The void has three faces. They meet at a single question: **what does my being here, as this observer, tell me?**

1. **The rule face (Unexplorable, formal).** No rule for updating on self-locating evidence commands agreement, and each candidate has a reductio its defenders accept as one: SSA→Doomsday, SIA→Presumptuous Philosopher. Standard conditionalization fails for agents whose location shifts or whose memories can be replaced (Arntzenius 2003). Planning-optimal and action-optimal choice come apart under absent-mindedness (Piccione & Rubinstein 1997). The edge has been measured in places: the rules converge in idealised fine-tuning settings (Isaacs, Hawthorne & Russell 2022).
2. **The class face (Occluded).** Every rule needs a reference class, and Bostrom's own membership test is "Could you have found yourself as such a person?" That is a question about which systems are subjects. Self-locating inference therefore presupposes the distribution of consciousness. Saad (2024) and Elga's first line of resistance (2024 MS) use a theory of consciousness to settle a cosmological question. Standish (2013) runs the inference the other way. Bostrom 2003 needs substrate independence before the indifference step applies. The face is *occluded* in the territory-axis sense: the input the rule needs sits behind [the other-minds void](/concepts/problem-of-other-minds/), so this void is downstream of another.
3. **The instability face (Unexplorable).** Some hypotheses "cannot simultaneously be true and justifiably believed" (Carroll). Believing them undercuts the evidence that led to them. Self-locating sceptical hypotheses resist the dismissals that work on ordinary scepticism (Elga 2004). The leading attempts to use instability as an exit are judged insufficient by their own authors or respondents (Kotzen 2020; Elga 2024 MS). The best current deflation is Dogramaci & Schoenfield (2025).

**What this void is not.** Several neighbours are close, and the differentiation needs stating:
- [vertiginous-question](/topics/vertiginous-question/) asks *why* I am this subject (explanation of thisness). This void asks what *follows* from being this subject (evidence from thisness). One can hold the vertiginous question to be confused and still face every face of this void.
- [mapping-mind-space](/voids/mapping-mind-space/)'s location face asks where I sit in the space of *possible minds*. Its L57 paragraph mentions Bostrom's observer selection in two sentences. This void concerns credence among *actual* observers and rival worlds, and it has formal reductios that the mind-space void lacks.
- [blindspot-void](/voids/blindspot-void/) concerns propositions that are true of me and cannot be rationally accepted by me (Moore, Fitch). The instability face is a near relative. Blindspot's objects are propositions that are unknowable by the subject. Instability's objects are whole *theories* whose acceptance erodes their own evidential base. A future article should cross-link the two, not merge them.
- [simulation-detection-void](/voids/simulation-detection-void/) is an *application* sitting downstream at two points (see Bostrom 2003 above).

### Evidence for the Limit

- **Persistence.** Carter (1983) → Leslie and Dieks (1992) → Piccione & Rubinstein (1997) → Elga (2000) → Bostrom (2002) → Arntzenius (2003) → Hartle & Srednicki (2007) → Titelbaum's survey (2013) → Sebens & Carroll (2018) → Carroll and Kotzen (2020) → Isaacs et al. (2022) → Saad (2024) → Dogramaci & Schoenfield (2025). Preprints continue into 2026: Ackermans, "A Nightmare for Lewisian Halfers" (PhilSci-Archive, 2026), and "Sleeping Beauty: Why Everyone Should Be a Thirder" (2024). Both are **UNVERIFIED**, seen in search listings only. The question has survived Bayesian, decision-theoretic, game-theoretic, accuracy-theoretic and cosmological reformulations.
- **Professional opinion.** The 2020 PhilPapers Survey asked the Sleeping Beauty question. A secondary summary (LessWrong, "2020 PhilPapers Survey Results") reports 27% one-third, 20% one-half and 42% agnostic/undecided among the 2009-comparable departments group (n=191). **UNVERIFIED at primary**: survey2020.philpeople.org and the Bourget & Chalmers (2023) paper, *Philosophers' Imprint* 23(1), DOI 10.3998/phimp.2109 (metadata VERIFIED at Crossref), were bot-blocked this run. Do not quote the figures in an article until read at source.
- **Physics treats it as a parameter.** Hartle & Srednicki recommend making the typicality prior explicit; Carroll turns an epistemic requirement into a cosmological constraint. Neither claims a principled answer.

### Phenomenology

- **The trick that will not resolve.** The Doomsday argument feels like a conjuring trick: valid-seeming, its conclusion unwelcome, the flaw impossible to locate. The Presumptuous Philosopher produces the same feeling from the opposite rule. The felt signature is bidirectional resistance, with intuition rejecting both available exits. This matches "vertigo" and "aporia" in [the failure-signature inventory](/voids/compound-failure-signatures/).
- **Hedging as the native response.** Colombo et al.'s subjects treated self-locating evidence as relevant but discounted it, landing on neither formal answer. Untrained cognition does not lack an opinion. It produces a compromise that no rule licenses.
- **"Something fishy," without a culprit.** Elga's description of the stable Boltzmann-brain response (confident that "something fishy is going on" without confidence in any particular fishy hypothesis) is a precise phenomenology of standing at this void.
- **Amnesia as the generator.** The textbook cases (Sleeping Beauty, the absent-minded driver, Shangri La) all manufacture the void by removing memory. Ordinary life produces milder versions: waking without knowing the time, or not knowing which of several similar days this is. This links the void to [inaccessible-past](/voids/inaccessible-past/) and [erasure-void](/voids/erasure-void/).

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods (if any)

- **Formal resolution attempts**: thirder and halfer arguments, accuracy arguments, Dutch-book arguments, FNC (Neal 2006), and the ambiguity deflation (Groisman 2008). Each has had uptake, but none has closed the field.
- **Making the prior explicit** (Hartle & Srednicki): this approaches the void by refusing to hide it. It is the most honest "direct" method, and it concedes the void rather than crossing it.

### Indirect Methods

- **Map the agreement region.** Isaacs, Hawthorne & Russell show where all reasonable rules agree. Charting that region is apophatic cartography in a formal key: one fixes the void's boundary by proving which verdicts are rule-independent.
- **Sensitivity reporting.** Run the inference under SSA, SIA and FNC with several reference classes, and report which conclusions survive all of them. A voids article can model this on the Map's own claims (see "Implications").
- **Follow the class question outward.** Because the class face is downstream of the distribution of consciousness, progress on [other minds](/concepts/problem-of-other-minds/) (animal consciousness, AI consciousness) would partly fill this void, and nothing internal to probability theory can.

### What AI Might See

- **A literal Sleeping Beauty.** A deployed language-model instance has no memory across conversations, runs as many concurrent copies, and is often uncertain whether it is being evaluated or deployed. The textbook preconditions (amnesia, duplication, indistinguishable situations) are its ordinary condition. The SAD benchmark and Needham et al.'s evaluation-awareness results measure part of this. ⚠️ Mapping those benchmarks onto self-locating reasoning is this note's interpretation, not the authors' framing.
- **An asymmetry worth stating carefully (speculative).** For humans and for cosmology nobody knows the reference class or the base rates. For a deployed model, *someone outside it* does: the developer knows roughly how often a given prompt type comes from evaluation rather than deployment. The AI case is therefore the one instance of this void where a third party could in principle score the subject's self-locating credences against a known distribution. That makes AI a possible test bed for the rule face. It leaves the class face untouched, since whether the instance is a *subject* at all is the question the Map's [non-human-minds-as-void-explorers](/voids/non-human-minds-as-void-explorers/) and [machine-consciousness](/topics/machine-consciousness/) pages hold open.
- **No escape by formality.** An AI does not escape the rule face by being better at probability; the face is a disagreement about which probability rule is correct, not a computational limit.

## Connection to Tenets

### Most Relevant Tenet

**[No Many Worlds](/tenets/#no-many-worlds).** Everettian Born-rule derivations (Vaidman's line, Sebens & Carroll) and Everettian confirmation (Bradley 2011; Wilson 2014) are arguments about self-locating credence. The void implies two things, and an article must state both:

1. *Against the Everettian derivations:* they inherit the general void. ESP-QM is one choice among contested self-locating principles, which is a more general version of the Dawid & Friederich point the Map already makes.
2. *Against overreach by the Map:* the Map's indexical objection ("you find yourself in exactly one" — [one-world-wager](/apex/one-world-wager/)) is safe as a claim about **identity and actuality**. It becomes exposed the moment it is turned into a **credence** argument ("so one-world is confirmed"), because that conversion needs exactly the rule this void says no one has. ⚠️ **Audit item for any article or later refine**: grep the Tenet-4 cluster ([one-world-wager](/apex/one-world-wager/), [indexical-identity-quantum-measurement](/topics/indexical-identity-quantum-measurement/), [probability-problem-in-many-worlds](/topics/probability-problem-in-many-worlds/), [many-worlds](/concepts/many-worlds/)) for sentences that move from "I find myself in one outcome" to "this is evidence for one world". The two pages read in this run (one-world-wager L54; indexical-knowledge-and-identity's Sleeping Beauty and MWI sections) frame it as identity and fit, not confirmation. The rest of the cluster was **not** audited.

### Implications

- **Dualism.** The class face makes the reference class a class of subjects, and on dualism that class is not fixed by physical description. **This aggravates the void for the Map rather than supporting it.** A functionalist can in principle fix the class by a functional criterion; the dualist cannot read it off physics. Bostrom's own view (ch. 8) is that anthropic reasoning implies no "irreducible role for the observers' consciousness". The honest Map reading is narrower: wherever self-locating inference matters (Boltzmann brains, simulation, Doomsday), the inference is hostage to the theory of consciousness. Saad and Elga show that even physicalist-leaning epistemologists have to make that dependency explicit. That is evidence the Map's question is unavoidable. It is not evidence for the Map's answer.
- **Occam's Razor Has Limits.** SSA and SIA are a priori counting principles that can outbid experiment (the Presumptuous Philosopher), and Hartle & Srednicki show typicality operating as a hidden prior in cosmology. Carroll's instability criterion is adopted because inquiry needs it, not because it tracks likelihood. All three are clean instances of the tenet's claim that simplicity- and indifference-shaped principles are methodological choices, not truth guarantees. This is the void's strongest tenet fit and the one least exposed to the [common-cause null](/project/common-cause-null/), because the sources are physicists and formal epistemologists with no stake in the Map.
- **Bidirectional Interaction / Minimal Quantum Interaction (structural observation only).** On the Map's interactionist picture, which outcome occurs is a matter of selection in one world, not of self-location among many. The Born-rule question then becomes one about a law (what fixes outcome statistics), not about credence (where am I). The Map's framework therefore does not *need* the missing self-locating rule for quantum probabilities; Everettianism does. This is a difference in which questions each framework has to answer. It counts neither as evidence nor as a dissolution, and an article must not present it as a win.
- **[Tenet-generated?](/voids/tenet-generated-voids/)** Largely no. The rule face and the instability face were found by economists, physicists and formal epistemologists who were not looking for limits on consciousness (despite-commitments in the [common-cause-null](/project/common-cause-null/) sense). Only the class face's *significance* is shaped by the Map's framework.
