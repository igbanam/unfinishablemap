## Executive Summary

Gendler (2008a, 2008b) introduced *alief*: an associative, automatic, arational state with representational, affective and behavioural content. She used it to explain **belief-discordant** behaviour, such as the visitor who believes the Grand Canyon Skywalk is safe yet trembles and clings to the rail. Three rival families of view compete with hers:

- **Deflationary** views say no new kind is needed. Ordinary beliefs, desires, imaginings and sub-personal representations do the work (Kwong 2012; Currie & Ichino 2012; Mandelbaum 2013; Muller & Bashour 2011).
- **Propositional** views say implicit attitudes are unconscious beliefs (Mandelbaum 2016; Bendaña 2021) or near-beliefs (Levy 2015, "patchy endorsements").
- **Vagueness** views say the subject neither determinately believes nor determinately fails to believe (Schwitzgebel 2001, 2010).

Schwitzgebel explicitly exempts the glass-floor case. He grants that the trembler "still believes" the floor is solid (2010, p. 549 n. 5). The skywalk is therefore the one case in which every party agrees about what is believed.

The task's framing needs one correction. On Gendler's taxonomy, overriding an alief means holding an *alief's* guidance in check, not a belief's. That is the control over "emotion-type states" which Soter (2025) *borrows* to build back-end doxastic voluntarism. So the skywalk is the source of Soter's mechanism, not an instance of back-end control over belief.

The case becomes an instance only on propositional accounts, where the overridden implicit attitude is itself a belief. Whether "acting on belief against alief" is the case `doxastic-voluntarism` L107 predicts is therefore **taxonomy-relative**. The article's contribution is to say so.

The binding calibration holds throughout. A dual-process physicalist account explains the trembling, the override and the felt effort with no experiential term. The page is tenet-coherent and does not raise the evidence for any tenet.

- **Definition**, p. 642, verbatim: "A paradigmatic alief is a mental state with associatively linked content that is representational, affective and behavioral, and that is activated—consciously or nonconsciously—by features of the subject's internal or ambient environment. - **Author stance**, p. 642: "Since I incline towards physicalism, this means that I think alief is also a physical state."
- **Visibility**, p. 646: "it is precisely when they are belief-discordant that aliefs tend to be evident to us."

Alief is thus involuntary on *psychological* grounds and belief on *conceptual* grounds, which matches the page's two grounds for involuntarism (§two-grounds).
- **Norms of coherence**, p. 651: "But if I believe that P and alieve that not-P, something is amiss."
- **The well-functioning aliever**, p. 651: "the well-functioning aliever is one whose aliefs and beliefs largely coincide (or one whose ability to suppress contrary impulse is strong)". The parenthesis is Gendler's own nod to override capacity.
- **Indirect cultivation**, p. 652: "if we use such tricks to cultivate beliefs, we need to cover our tracks; if we use them to cultivate aliefs, we can do so under conditions of full disclosure."
- **The key sentence for the back-end question**, pp. 660–661, across a page break: "...it is only through a process of conscious or habit-governed inhibition that representations whose accuracy we endorse come to play a distinctive role in governing our actions."
- **Conclusion**, p. 663: "Belief plays an important role in the ultimate regulation of behavior. But it plays a far smaller role in moment-by-moment management than philosophical tradition has tended to stress."
- **Ethics**, p. 663 n. 62: "Aristotelian ethics is an ethics of alief, whereas Kantian ethics is an ethics of belief." This links to the akrasia page's Aristotle section and its enkratic agent.

## Major Positions

### Alief as a sui generis state (Gendler 2008a, 2008b, 2012, 2014)

- **Core claim**: belief-discordant behaviour needs a distinct state. It is associative, automatic and arational, has bundled representational, affective and behavioural content, is involuntary in practice, and changes with habit rather than evidence.
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral. Gendler inclines towards physicalism (2008a p. 642). The distinction is framework-neutral, and the Map may use it without inheriting her physicalism. She gives no support to dualism either.

### Deflationary pluralism (Kwong 2012; Currie & Ichino 2012; Mandelbaum 2013; Muller & Bashour 2011)

- **Core claim**: the Gendler cases are explained by beliefs, desires, imaginings and perceptions, plus some sub-personal representations. No single new kind is needed.
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral. Note the dispute's shape: it is about kind-individuation in psychology, and the tenets do not adjudicate it. Tenet 5 should not be used to favour alief over the deflationist, or the reverse.

### Implicit attitudes as beliefs or near-beliefs (Mandelbaum 2016; Bendaña 2021; Levy 2015 as a midpoint)

- **Core claim**: implicit attitudes are propositionally structured. They are unconscious beliefs on Mandelbaum's and Bendaña's views, and "patchy endorsements" on Levy's.
- **Relation to tenets**: this view determines whether override counts as back-end doxastic control (see the next section).

### In-between belief (Schwitzgebel 2001, 2010)

- **Core claim**: on a phenomenal-dispositional account, broad dispositional splits, as with Juliet and Kaipeng, are cases of neither believing nor failing to believe. Narrow excused splits, such as the glass floor, leave the belief intact.
- **Relation to tenets**: neutral. Schwitzgebel's dispositionalism is already engaged on the Map; see the Dormancy-void research (`research/voids-dormancy-void-2026-09-17`, L68).

### Dual-process and dual-attitude psychology (Evans & Stanovich 2013; Gawronski & Bodenhausen 2006)

- **Core claim**: Type 1 defaults stand unless Type 2 intervenes, or associative activation runs separately from propositional validation.
- **Relation to tenets**: this is the rival named in the calibration rule. It explains the trembling (Type 1 default), the override (Type 2 intervention) and the felt effort (the opportunity-cost and control-signal accounts already on `concepts/mental-effort` §Modulation) with no experiential term.

## Key Debates

### Is alief a natural kind?

- **Sides**:
  - For: Gendler, and Danón (LEAD).
  - Against: Kwong, Currie & Ichino, Mandelbaum 2013, Muller & Bashour, Nagel, Doggett.
  - Modified: Brownstein & Madva (FTBA, norm-sensitive).
- **State**: open. The SEP frames it as "whether putative aliefs form a unified kind".

### Are implicit attitudes beliefs?

- **Sides**:
  - Yes: Mandelbaum 2016; Bendaña 2021.
  - No: Madva 2016; Levy 2015 (sui generis); Gendler (alief).
  - Neither, determinately: Schwitzgebel 2010.
- **State**: open. The SEP "Belief" entry's verdict is "remains controversial".

### Do implicit measures track behaviour-guiding states at all?

- **Evidence**: correlations of .14–.37; Oswald 2013 "poor predictors"; Forscher 2019, where changes do not mediate behaviour; Schimmack 2021; Gawronski et al. 2022, separating implicit bias from bias on implicit measures.
- **State**: the measure-level evidence is weak. **The article should build its argument on the skywalk/precipice cases, not on IAT data.**

## The Map's Question: Is Acting on Belief Against Alief the Case the Back-End Speculation Predicts?

The relevant text is `concepts/doxastic-voluntarism` L107. It was read at source on 2026-10-20 and says: "The Map speculates that if control over belief is ever felt as effort, it is at the back end, in holding a belief's guidance in check, where [mental-effort](/concepts/mental-effort/) places effort generally."

L86 gives Soter's mechanism, in which the control mechanisms that block "emotion-type states" "can block a belief's guidance too."

What the research supports, as the article's main argument:

1. **The answer depends on the taxonomy.**
   - **On Gendler's taxonomy**, the skywalk visitor who walks forward is holding an **alief's** guidance in check, not a belief's. That is control over an emotion-type state, the source domain Soter extends to belief. The case shows what back-end control *would feel like* but is not an instance of control over belief.
   - **On Mandelbaum's or Bendaña's view**, the overridden implicit attitude *is* a belief. Juliet's deliberate striving (Schwitzgebel 2010, p. 532) then *is* holding a belief's guidance in check, which is an instance of L107.
   - **On Schwitzgebel's view**, Juliet is in-between, so her override is a case of neither. The skywalk keeps its belief and goes with Gendler's reading.
   - The article should set this out as a three-row table and not pick a row.
2. **The skywalk is the cleanest case** because every party grants the belief (Schwitzgebel 2010, p. 549 n. 5). Juliet's case is contested at the level of what she believes.
3. **The front/back split fits the phenomenology without testing it.** In the precipice cases the subject does not struggle to *form* the belief that the glass holds. Hume's man "knows himself to be perfectly secure". The struggle comes at the point of action. This matches the L107 and `phenomenology-of-resistance-across-domains` L63 picture: nothing to push on at acquisition, with effort where a held state's guidance is checked. A physicalist account predicts the same split, so the fit is not evidence.
4. **Felt effort requires noticing.** Gendler (2014) says associative interference is often "phenomenologically invisible". Greenwald & Banaji (1995) say implicit effects are reduced by focusing attention on the task. Juliet's striving is intermittent because "it's impossible to constantly maintain such self-conscious vigilance." The speculation should therefore be stated conditionally: effort appears where the rival guide is noticed and checked, and is absent where the interference goes unseen. The article must not claim felt effort in implicit-bias cases generally.
5. **There is a parity point for the voluntarism page.** Gendler gives belief a conceptual involuntarism and alief a practical one (2008a p. 651). She also observes that alief can be cultivated indirectly "under conditions of full disclosure" while belief-cultivation must "cover our tracks" (p. 652). This yields a neat asymmetry for `doxastic-voluntarism` §two-grounds and for `self-control-as-skill-and-scaffolding`. Duckworth, Gendler & Gross (2016) is Gendler's own later work on situational strategies.

**Calibration (binding)**: none of points 1–5 raises the evidence for Dualism or Bidirectional Interaction. A Type 1/Type 2 or prefrontal-regulation account explains all three facets: the trembling, the override and the effort. The page is tenet-coherent, in the same register as `doxastic-voluntarism` §map-reading ("it removes a defeater and adds no evidence").
