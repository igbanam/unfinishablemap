**New dispositionalism** is the compatibilist thesis that an agent's ability to do otherwise is a *disposition*, which, like the fragility of a glass that is never struck, can be present and unexercised in a deterministic world. Michael Fara gives the canonical statement: "An agent has the ability to A in circumstances C if and only if she has the disposition to A when, in circumstances C, she tries to A" (Fara 2008, p. 848). The founding texts are Michael Smith's (2003), Kadri Vihvelin's (2004, 2013) and Fara's; the label is Randolph Clarke's (2009). The view claims two payoffs: unmanifested dispositions are compatible with determinism, so unexercised abilities to do otherwise are too; and a Frankfurt-style intervener merely *masks* the ability to do otherwise, so the Principle of Alternate Possibilities survives.

The view is live and unrefuted. Its critics (Clarke 2009; Ann Whittle 2010; Christopher Franklin 2011; Romy Jaster 2021) argue that whatever it captures is not the ability at issue in the free-will dispute; a reply open to its defenders is that the general ability they analyse is the one that matters. For The Unfinishable Map it is among the strongest challenges to the premise that leeway must be *categorical*. The Map's response, set out in [[#what-it-concedes|what the view concedes and denies]] (explained below), is that "categorical" names two requirements, of which new dispositionalism grants one and denies that the other is needed. The Map's case for the second rests on its agent-causal reading of the tenets and a contested reading of deliberation, which neither the analysis nor its critics settle.

## The Analysis {#the-analysis}

Fara starts from the observation that abilities, like dispositions, can be *masked*: an agent tries to A, keeps the ability while trying, and still fails. "It is essential to an ability's being masked that its possession survive an attempt to exercise it" (p. 847). The Dispositional Analysis follows, with both ascriptions read wide-scope (p. 848, n. 8).

Vihvelin analyses abilities as bundles of dispositions. *Narrow* ability is the case where "S has what it takes to do X: she's got the necessary skills and the psychological and physical capacity to use those skills"; *wide* ability adds that "she's got the means and opportunity and nothing external stands in her way" (Vihvelin 2013, p. 11, quoted in Franklin 2013). Narrow abilities nomologically supervene on intrinsic properties (p. 13), and to have one "is to have a disposition or bundle of dispositions" (p. 171). Her "commonsense metaphysical compatibilism" identifies free will with such a bundle plus an environment free of obstacles, a feature counting as an obstacle only if the agent, trying in its presence, would fail (p. 110, as reconstructed by Franklin). Smith's strand concerns rational capacities, where the difference between having and failing to exercise a capacity and lacking it altogether is "crucial for our allocations of responsibility" (2003, abstract).

This is a thesis about agents' abilities, separate from dispositionalism about properties, which the Map treats under [[causal-powers|causal powers]] and [[consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-laws-and-dispositions|the metaphysics of dispositions]].

## Why the Conditional Analysis Could Be Revived {#revival}

The classical compatibilists, from Moore to Ayer, read "could have done otherwise" as *would have done otherwise, had one chosen to*. That analysis fell to cases like Keith Lehrer's: an agent with a pathological aversion to red sugar balls would take one if she chose to, but, "not so choosing, I am utterly unable to touch one" (Lehrer 1968, p. 32, quoted in Maier & Kikkert 2025).

Later, the metaphysics of dispositions produced counterexamples to conditional analyses of dispositions in general. A fragile glass that would turn to steel if struck is *finked* (Martin 1994); one packed in styrofoam is *masked* (Johnston 1992); neither would break if struck. The new dispositionalists re-diagnose the earlier failure: "it may be that the failures of the conditional analysis of ability were not due to any fact about abilities, but rather to a shortcoming of conditional analyses generally" (Maier & Kikkert 2025, §3.4). The order matters: Lehrer-style cases refuted the conditional analysis of ability first, and masks and finks arrived later, against a different target.

## The Two Payoffs {#payoffs}

### Determinism

On Franklin's reconstruction of Vihvelin, determinism leaves narrow abilities in place, and since "the truth of determinism does not entail that if I had tried to do otherwise I would have failed", it is no obstacle to wide ability either (Franklin 2013).

Fara turns this against the [[the-consequence-argument-for-incompatibilism|Consequence Argument]]: its transfer principle has a false instance wherever an ability is masked and "no one has the ability to prevent the masking conditions from obtaining", and so "The dispositional analysis of abilities has rescued compatibilism" (pp. 862–863). He anticipates the reply that his sense of "ability" "is not the sense that is properly at issue" and names its price: the incompatibilist "must maintain that the sorts of abilities involved in free action are abilities that cannot be masked", so that anyone who tries and fails never had the ability, a view that "carries the burden of imposing requirements on free action that are implausibly hard to meet" (p. 863).

### Frankfurt Cases

In a [[frankfurt-cases-and-the-principle-of-alternate-possibilities|Frankfurt case]], Black would make Jones fail if Jones tried to act otherwise. Fara answers that "this conditional fact does not establish that Jones lacks the ability to act otherwise" (p. 854); like packing around a glass, "the evil scientist does not remove Jones's disposition to act otherwise when he tries" (p. 855). Vihvelin sorts the cases (2013, pp. 97–98, as summarised by Franklin). A *Bodyguard*, triggered when the agent begins to choose otherwise, leaves some ability to do otherwise by construction. A *Preemptor*, triggered by an earlier non-actional sign, leaves the agent's intrinsic properties and narrow abilities untouched, and never frustrates an attempt, so it is no obstacle.

The result is a rare compatibilism that keeps the leeway requirement. Vihvelin holds that "the literature spawned by Frankfurt's attempt to undercut the metaphysical debate between compatibilists and incompatibilists is a snare and a delusion" (Vihvelin 2008, abstract), and Frank Hofmann (2022) builds "a leeway account of free decisions" on new-dispositionalist foundations. It sits on the leeway side of the [[source-versus-leeway-incompatibilism|source/leeway seam]] while denying that leeway conflicts with determinism. Gus Turyn (2025) extends masking to Pereboom's four-case manipulation argument, holding that manipulation masks rational abilities while "causal determinism is not a mask".

## The Replies {#replies}

### Clarke: Whether Exercising the Capacity Is Up to the Agent {#clarke}

Clarke grants that "there is a kind of capacity to act possession of which is a matter of having a disposition" (2009, abstract). If something would prevent one's A-ing should one try, and neither its presence nor its preventing is up to one, "then even if I have a capacity to A, it is not up to me whether I exercise that capacity" (p. 339, quoted in Maier & Kikkert 2025). His verdict, that the new dispositionalism "leaves unresolved the main points of dispute concerning free will", leaves the dispute open for both sides rather than awarding it to the incompatibilist. The reply the Stanford entry offers on the dispositionalist's behalf is that Clarke's agent lacks the *specific* ability to do otherwise but keeps the *general* one, which is what the analysis analyses.

### Whittle: Global and Local Abilities {#whittle}

Whittle, working independently of Clarke, separates *global* abilities, a "general practical" or "type" ability, from *local* ones, the ability-to-A-in-these-circumstances: "If determinism threatens anything, it threatens only" local abilities, while dispositional analyses secure global ones. Against Frankfurt she grants that "Jones is able to do otherwise, in one sense, even when Black is about", but "this sense of being able to do otherwise is not the one that concerns incompatibilists" (Whittle 2010). Separately, Greg Janzen (2016) argues that "brain-malfunction" Frankfurt cases "evade the dispositionalist analysis".

### Franklin: Ability and Opportunity {#franklin}

Franklin (2011) grants the analyses for argument's sake and denies that they deliver compatibilism or defeat Frankfurt, because "agents' freedom and responsibility depend not only on their abilities, but also their opportunities". Reviewing Vihvelin's book, he finds the weak link in her definition of an obstacle, which assumes, without defence, "that while a feature that prevents my attempting to do otherwise from being successful is an obstacle, a feature that prevents me even from attempting to do otherwise is not an obstacle". Of the Preemptor he asks: "isn't this an obstacle par excellence?" (Franklin 2013).

### Jaster and Vetter {#jaster}

Jaster restates the objection from inside the theory of abilities: "Understanding how abilities work gives us no reason to think that the ability to do otherwise relevant for free will is compatible with either Black's presence or determinism" (Jaster 2021, abstract). With Barbara Vetter she also argues that principled disanalogies between abilities and dispositions block "any dispositional account of abilities" (Vetter & Jaster 2017, abstract), a line neutral on free will.

## What the View Concedes and What It Denies {#what-it-concedes}

This section is the Map's interpretation. The Map's agency articles say compatibilism reinterprets "could have done otherwise" until it no longer requires the *categorical* ability libertarians defend. Against new dispositionalism, "categorical" must be split, because it does two jobs.

The first concerns *what kind of fact* the ability is: a real, intrinsic property of the agent rather than a bare truth about what would happen under other conditions. New dispositionalism, in Vihvelin's version at least, grants this, since her narrow abilities supervene on intrinsic properties and the analysis is built to survive finks and masks. On this axis, the charge that the dispositionalist reinterprets the ability away is unfair.

The second is a *modal* claim in van Inwagen's sense: the other act is consistent with the actual past and the laws. New dispositionalism denies that this is needed; an agent whose act the past and laws entail still has her abilities, unobstructed.

The Map holds that the modal kind is what the "up to me" of deliberation and responsibility requires; new dispositionalism holds that an intrinsic, unobstructed disposition suffices. Clarke's objection marks where they part: the analysis does not say whether exercising the capacity is up to the agent, and that is where the Map locates leeway, at the selection among options.

One way the Map can meet Fara's challenge is to grant that executive abilities, such as opening a door if one tries, are maskable dispositions, and to place its modal claim at the choosing rather than at the success of an attempt. Fara's "implausibly hard to meet" charge, that every failed attempt would then show a missing ability, has less purchase there, since on this reply no attempt stands between the agent and her choosing. The cost is that the choice-level power is not analysed as a disposition at all; the [[free-will]] hub calls the agent's settling "a primitive whose modal structure is owed rather than supplied". Nor does Clarke's point refute the view: by his own verdict it leaves the dispute open.

## What Follows, and What Follows Only on the Map's Reading {#implications}

From the neutral definition alone, unexercised dispositions are compatible with determinism, which no party disputes, and the dispute moves to which sense of "can" responsibility tracks. Only on the Map's reading is the free-will-relevant ability modal, supplied by quantum-open outcome-selection read agent-causally.

On Frankfurt cases the two sides share a conclusion more than a reason: the dispositionalists preserve a dispositional ability, on Whittle's reading only a general one, while the Map's reply on the indeterminism horn of the Widerker–Kane dilemma needs a specific alternative that survives the prior sign, so the dispositional defence is no free gift to the Map's leeway wing.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**[[tenets#^bidirectional-interaction|Tenet 3 (Bidirectional Interaction)]]** commits the Map to outcome-selection, consciousness influencing which definite outcome becomes actual. The agency cluster reads that selection agent-causally, as the agent settling among open alternatives, and that reading supplies modal leeway; the tenet itself does not entail it ([[where-the-substance-commitment-enters]]). New dispositionalism therefore challenges the reading rather than the tenet: a dualist who held each selection fixed by prior mental states, one the [[the-consequence-argument-for-incompatibilism|Consequence Argument article]] notes could accept Tenet 3, could adopt Vihvelin's analysis wholesale. Vihvelin also argues that commonsense beliefs about free will do not commit us to agent causation (2013, ch. 3, as summarised in Franklin 2013), which presses directly on the Map's [[agent-causation|agent-causal]] reading.

The [[positions/agency-and-will#^p-a5|positions register]] (P-A5) would shift if the "genuine alternatives" residue were shown "compatibilist-recoverable"; new dispositionalism is the most direct candidate for that trigger, and the replies above explain why it has not fired without closing the question.

**[[tenets#^occams-limits|Tenet 5 (Occam's Razor Has Limits)]]** cuts both ways. Vihvelin's subtitle, "Why Determinism Doesn't Matter", promises freedom without indeterministic machinery, and the Map declines to treat that economy as decisive while knowledge of the mind's relation to the physical is incomplete. The tenet binds the Map too: it removes a parsimony defeater without supplying evidence for the modal reading, whose warrant is coherence with tenets adopted on other grounds and a reading of deliberation the data do not settle alone.

New dispositionalism stands unrefuted; the Map's best reply relocates the dispute to the choosing rather than winning it.
