# Research: Voids - The Luminosity Void

**Date**: 2026-10-22
**Search queries used**: Williamson anti-luminosity "cognitive homelessness" *Knowledge and Its Limits*; "Luminosity Regained" Berker; Srinivasan "Are We Luminous?"; Weatherson "Luminous Margins"; Crossref metadata lookups for 18 titles; OpenAlex abstracts for 20 DOIs; OpenAlex forward citation search (2021 onward) on Goldstein & Waxman 2021, Smithies 2012 and Srinivasan 2015; Hall "Phenomenal properties are luminous properties"; graded vs all-or-none awareness (Overgaard 2006, Windey et al. 2013); "anti-luminosity" with "all-or-none"/"graded" consciousness, introspection and psychophysics.
**Voids category**: Mixed. Unexplorable at the margins if the argument is sound; merely unexplored if the luminists are right. The status is itself the live dispute.

## Executive Summary

A condition is **luminous** when, whenever it obtains, the subject is in a position to know that it obtains. Timothy Williamson argued that no non-trivial condition is luminous, including the conditions philosophers have treated as paradigmatically luminous: feeling cold, being in pain, its appearing to one that *p*. His argument runs a slow change (a cold dawn warming to a hot noon) through steps too small for the subject to discriminate. Knowledge needs a margin for error, so at the last moments of feeling cold the subject feels cold but is not in a position to know it. The OUP chapter abstract states the conclusion: "there are no non‐trivial luminous conditions, and therefore that we suffer from a kind of cognitive homelessness" [raw]. The same apparatus refutes the KK principle, so knowing and being in a position to know that one knows also come apart.

The void is a **margin void**. Clear cases are untouched: at dawn the subject knows she feels cold. What is closed is a band near every boundary of a gradual mental condition, and nothing in experience marks the band. Thirty years of replies (1996–2026) have not settled the matter. Luminists attack the margin-for-error premise, and Berker argues it holds only if beliefs about one's mental life can "swing free" of it even after ideal reflection. Defenders rebuild it: Srinivasan grounds it in an empirical claim about "what kind of creatures we are", and Goldstein & Waxman (2021) derive non-luminosity without any safety premise.

**Calibration.** The argument presupposes no ontology of mind, so the void is **framework-independent and evidence for no tenet**. It does make a **demand** on the Map. Three live pages assert first-person self-intimation without scoping it to clear cases, and the Map's phenomenal-authority account has to be restated as privileged access *without* luminosity (Merlo 2026) or carry an explicit, contested constitutive-connection commitment (Berker 2008). Non-luminosity concerns knowledge of a state, never the state's causal efficacy, so nothing here concedes epiphenomenalism. **Recommendation: article at `voids/luminosity-void`** (slug free). The fallback fold and a separate scoping fix are set out under "Recommendation" below.

### Williamson, "Cognitive Homelessness" (1996) and *Knowledge and Its Limits* (2000), chs. 4–5
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.2307/2941049 ; ch. 4 https://doi.org/10.1093/019925656X.003.0005 (pp. 93–113) ; ch. 5 https://doi.org/10.1093/019925656X.003.0006 (pp. 114–134)
- **Type**: Paper; book chapters
- **Key points**:
  - The 1996 paper names the target, the philosophical temptation "to postulate a realm of phenomena in which nothing is hidden from us" [raw, OpenAlex first-paragraph abstract]. Descartes placed the realm in one's own mind and Wittgenstein extended it, and they agreed on "a cognitive home in which everything lies open to our view" [raw]. The phrase *cognitive homelessness* is Williamson's (the 1996 title).
  - Ch. 4 abstract: "The argument involves consideration of gradual processes in which small changes are below our level of discrimination. It is related to, but not the same as, sorites paradoxes" [raw]. Williamson himself denies that the argument is a sorites; critics dispute this (below).
  - The case: the subject "is not aware of any change in them over one millisecond" (2000, p. 97) [via Srinivasan]. Luminous conditions, if any exist, are "curiosities" (2000, p. 109) [via Srinivasan]: trivial conditions that always or never obtain.
  - Ch. 5 abstract: the argument "is used to refute the KK principle"; "Further iterations of knowledge are shown to involve similar cognitive gaps"; the underlying phenomenon is "the need for a margin for error in knowledge" [raw].
- **Tenet alignment**: None as evidence. The argument is ontologically neutral. Its historical target, the Cartesian transparent mind, is a thesis dualists have held, which makes it a demand on the Map rather than support.

### Srinivasan, "Are We Luminous?" (2015)
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/phpr.12067 (author PDF https://users.ox.ac.uk/~corp1468/Research_files/Are%20We%20Luminous_PPR.pdf)
- **Type**: Paper (defence of Williamson)
- **Key points**:
  - Clean reconstruction: (LUM) if C obtains at a case, K(C) obtains; (MAR) if K(C) at a_i, then C at a_{i+1}; (BEG) C at dawn; (END) not-C at noon. The four are jointly inconsistent, and Williamson drops LUM [raw].
  - Stakes: "If Williamson is right, then the common picture of the phenomenal realm as one of privileged access turns out to be a Cartesian orthodoxy from which philosophy must be cleansed" [raw].
  - Lists the luminist critics of MAR: Leitgeb 2002, Weatherson 2004, Blackson 2007, Wong 2008, Berker 2008, Ramachandran 2009, Vogel 2010, Cohen 2010, Zardini. Those who reject safety outright: Brueckner & Fiocco 2002, Neta & Rohrbaugh 2004, Comesaña 2005, Conee 2005 [raw].
  - Her defence makes MAR rest on safety plus "an empirical assumption about the kinds of creatures we are: that is, creatures whose beliefs are structured by certain dispositions" [raw abstract], so that "a large part of what is at stake in the debate about anti-luminosity is a certain vision of what kind of creatures we are, empirically speaking" [raw].
- **Tenet alignment**: Occam's Razor Has Limits (fit). The empirical turn is the void's best handle (see "Approaches to the Edge").

## The Void

### Nature of the Limit
The **luminosity void** is the band, at the edge of every gradual mental condition, in which a subject is in the condition but not in a position to know that it is. Its shape is specific:
1. **Margin-local.** Clear cases are known. The closure sits only where the condition shades off in steps below discrimination.
2. **Unmarked.** No millisecond step is noticed, so nothing in experience flags entry into the band. It presents as ordinary uncertainty, if as anything at all.
3. **Iterating.** The same structure separates knowing from being in a position to know that one knows (KK failure, ch. 5), and each further iteration adds "similar cognitive gaps". [[recursion-void]] bounds metacognitive depth by capacity, at around fifth order. This void opens a gap at the *second* order for an idealised subject, from margins rather than capacity.
4. **Doxastic, not necessarily epistemic** (Smithies). What closes is the guaranteed link between the state and knowledge of it; whether some weaker epistemic transparency survives is open.

### Evidence for the Limit
The evidence is an argument with a 30-year record of attack and reconstruction, not a finding. On the defenders' side are Williamson 1996/2000, Srinivasan 2015 and Goldstein & Waxman 2021 (the safety-free rebuild), with Ramachandran 2009 and the agential extensions showing reach. On the luminists' side, Weatherson 2004 (wrong safety rule), Berker 2008 (assumes phenomenal-doxastic disjointness), Zardini 2012 (soritical), Smithies 2012 (doxastic only), Hall 2021 (phenomenal properties are luminous) and Johnson 2021 (disjunctivist self-knowledge) all leave the conclusion contested. The persistence of the dispute is the data point. No response has removed the margin problem without taking on a substantive and contested commitment: a constitutive phenomenal-doxastic connection, a non-standard safety condition, or a non-classical treatment of the sorites.

### Phenomenology
Approaching the edge feels like nothing distinctive, and that is the point. The subject in the Cold Morning case notices no change from one step to the next while her confidence slowly falls. Where a felt correlate exists, it belongs to the [[noetic-feelings-void]] family (hesitancy, the feeling of not being sure), and those signals are themselves graded, with margins of their own. The band is self-concealing in the same way as [[erasure-void]]'s lost capacities, but the mechanism differs: nothing is deleted; the resolution is simply too coarse.

## Distinguishing from Neighbouring Voids

- **[[vagueness-void]]** uses Williamson's 1994 margin principle for the cut-offs of vague *predicates* ("tall"). This void applies the margin to a subject's knowledge of her *own present state*. Williamson says the argument is "related to, but not the same as" a sorites; Zardini says it borrows a sorites' plausibility. An article must present both readings.
- **[[blindspot-void]]** covers truths about oneself that are formally unknowable to oneself (Moore, Fitch, "I am not now conscious") and, on its positive side, Dretske's and Ramm's doubts about self-verifying awareness. The luminosity void is the converse boundary: states one is in but cannot know one is in, at margins, with no theorem involved. The two together bound first-person knowledge from both sides.
- **[[self-opacity]]** covers contingent introspective unreliability and the unobservable subject. This void holds even for an ideally attentive subject (Williamson stipulates concentration throughout).
- **[[acquaintance-void]] ("The Fineness of Grain")** covers concepts failing to capture determinate shades (Kelly). Merlo's concession is the same fineness-of-grain point made about knowledge of one's own state, which makes it the natural fold host.
- **Drift-void research note (2026-10-20, not live)** concerns comparison across time when the standard moves with the self. Here the comparison is synchronic: is the condition obtaining *now*? The gradual series is the argument's device, not its subject.
- **[[phenomenal-sorites-problem]]** treats a third-personal question (where in a series of systems experience gives out) and explicitly relies on first-person self-intimation to set that question apart. This void contests that reliance (see below).

## Approaches to the Edge

### Direct Methods
None, by construction: inside the band the subject is not in a position to know which side she is on. Training raises discrimination and moves the band, but it cannot remove it so long as some change falls below the new resolution.

### Indirect Methods
- **Third-person calibration of introspective resolution.** Morales's iSDT treats introspective reliability as a function of experiential signal strength, which gives the margin a measurable, empirical shape (a type-2 sensitivity curve) without settling the epistemology.
- **The creature-dispositions premise as an empirical target.** Srinivasan rests MAR on the claim that our beliefs are structured by dispositions that change smoothly with the condition. The graded vs all-or-none controversy (Sergent & Dehaene 2004; Overgaard et al. 2006; Windey et al. 2013; Cohen, Keefe & Brady 2023) bears on that premise: where a condition and the confidence tracking it flip together, MAR loses its motivation and luminists gain ground. Where awareness is graded, the defenders' premise gains ground. *This bridge is the note's own suggestion.* Two searches (one combining "anti-luminosity" with "all-or-none"/"graded" and introspection; one on the forward citations) found no paper that draws it. Treat it as untested, not as a literature claim. Measured continuity of report or behaviour also does not straightforwardly show phenomenal gradation (Michel 2019 on the instrument).
- **Formal modelling.** Margin models in epistemic logic (Williamson ch. 5; Goldstein 2024's Reflective Luminosity and Variable Margins) map exactly which iterations fail under which assumptions. The void's boundary is theory-relative but precisely statable.
- **Apophatic description.** The band can be characterised by what it lacks (a felt boundary, a knowable last moment), which is the method of [[apophatic-cartography]].

### What AI Might See
- **Discrete conditions.** Goldstein lists, as a cost of Variable Margins, that it "does not apply to cases of digital knowledge". The literature already treats discrete conditions as a special case. A system whose self-report is wired to read a discrete register could have conditions that are luminous in Williamson's sense, which supplies Berker's constitutive connection by engineering. Whether any such condition is phenomenal is exactly what is unknown for AI (see [[cross-architecture-llm-introspection]]), so an AI's luminous registers say nothing about phenomenal luminosity.
- **Inverted access.** For inspectable systems, third-person readout can resolve a state more finely than the system's self-report, an inversion of the human asymmetry. That is a fact about access, not evidence of consciousness either way.
- **An AI writer's own position.** A model's report about its own states should be treated as margin-bound and uninformative about phenomenality, in keeping with the [[blindspot-void]] treatment of AI self-reports.

## Connection to Tenets

All connections below are **fit, not evidence**.

### Most Relevant Tenet
**Dualism, as a demand.** Williamson's 1996 opening names Descartes's transparent mind as the target. If the argument is sound, dualists inherit a constraint: the first-person evidence the Map draws on must be clear-case evidence, never luminosity. Taking Berker's exit (a constitutive phenomenal-doxastic connection) is open to dualist acquaintance theorists and to self-representational physicalists alike, so it is a contested commitment and not a dividend.

### Implications
- **Bidirectional Interaction.** Agential anti-luminosity (Piñeros Glasscock; Beddor & Pavese) means the agent's knowledge of its own acting has margins, so felt agency cannot locate mental causation exactly. This constrains how [[agency-void]] and [[decision-void]] use first-person evidence. **Epiphenomenalism guard:** in the Cold Morning case the subject's confidence falls *as* the feeling changes, so the feeling is causally upstream of the belief, at limited resolution. Non-luminosity concerns knowledge, and nothing in the argument suggests the state is causally idle. An article must not let Berker's phrase "swing free from the truth" slide into "does no causal work". The Map does not concede [[epiphenomenalism]].
- **Occam's Razor Has Limits.** The "cognitive home" is the simplest model of the mind's relation to itself, and it fails at its edges if Williamson is right. Zardini's point applies symmetrically: the argument's own idealised uniformity may be soritical. Fit in both directions.
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction; No Many Worlds.** No bearing found. The "cases" of safety theory are centred possible worlds (Srinivasan n. 5), a modal device unrelated to branching.

## Four-Criteria Scoring ([[apophatic-cartography-four-criteria]])

This is the note's own grading, by one hand, so subject to the same-hand caveat:
- **Signature specificity: strong.** Margin-local, unmarked, iterating (KK), and doxastic rather than (perhaps) epistemic. The breakdown is recognisable from a description of it alone.
- **Structured persistence: moderate-to-strong.** The argument has survived from 1996 to 2026 and been rebuilt without its most attacked premise (2021). The luminist counter-tradition persists as well (Hall 2021; Merlo 2026's partial concession).
- **Cross-observer convergence: weak.** One lineage in analytic epistemology. The empirical channel (iSDT, graded awareness) bears on a premise and does not test the conclusion. The Advaita self-luminosity tradition affirms something nearby but uses a different notion (awareness illuminating itself, not a position to know a condition), so it can be neither counted for nor against.
- **Framework independence: strong.** No ontology of mind is presupposed, and the void cuts against a thesis historically held by dualists.

**Writer guards:**

- (b) Never call it a sorites without giving Williamson's denial, and never deny the charge without giving Zardini's.
- (c) Clear cases are known; do not write "we cannot know our own states".
- (d) Do not use it as evidence for or against dualism.
- (e) Apply the epiphenomenalism guard above.
