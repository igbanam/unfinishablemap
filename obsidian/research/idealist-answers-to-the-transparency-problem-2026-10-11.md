## Executive Summary

Five Map pages charge idealism with a "transparency problem": why a wholly mental reality appears from outside as exactly impersonal, lawful physical structure. The Q3 page (L38) calls this "not lighter than" the dualist's interface problem. The pages give no idealist answer, yet idealists have given several, and they hold up well. Broken into parts, the charge looks like this:

1. **Order.** Why is there law at all, and why these laws? Every metaphysics owes this. Yetter-Chappell (2025, ch. 5) argues that "any account of laws available to materialists is equally available to the nontheistic idealist", and the Map's own [[idealism]] page (L119) already concedes a "parity result" on it.
2. **Form.** Why does the world look impersonal and mathematical? Structural realism answers this: physics describes only structure (Russell 1927; Eddington 1928), so whatever physics describes will look impersonal. The Map holds that premise itself ([[intrinsic-nature]] L37: "Structural description is the method of physics, and structure all it can express").
3. **Austerity.** Why would a cosmic mind run on dynamics as free of reason as physics? This residue is real and belongs to idealism alone. Chalmers (2019) calls it the "austerity problem". The obvious escape, giving the cosmic mind extra mental structure, costs "supra-natural structure" that is causally idle.
4. **Explanatory objection.** Anti-realist (phenomenalist) idealism leaves the order among possible experiences unexplained (Chalmers 2019). This stays serious, and Christmann (2026) argues that it pushes even Yetter-Chappell's realist idealism back toward a global subject.

Idealism also does not simply *trade away* the interface debt. Yetter-Chappell's view keeps "psychophysical bridging laws", and Kastrup's identity view takes on the subject-constitution (decombination) problem. The Map carries an appearance-shaped debt of its own as well: its selection channel is "posited to average to exactly the Born distribution" ([[observational-closure]] L54).

**Verdict**: the comparison should be restated as an itemised list without weights. Only austerity, the explanatory objection and the constitution problem belong to idealism alone. **Recommendation**: a short `concepts/` page (1,600–2,000 words) titled **"The Lawful-Appearance Problem"** ([details below](#recommendation)). The name "transparency problem" is the Map's own coinage, absent from the idealism literature, and it collides with at least three other Map uses ([naming collision below](#naming)).

## Naming Collision {#naming}

"Transparency problem" already has three other live senses on the Map:

- `concepts/self-reference-paradox` L84, §"The Transparency Problem": Metzinger's transparent self-model.
- `voids/predictive-construction-void` L55, "**The transparency problem.**": successful predictions vanish from experience. This is a live page; its source note is `research/voids-predictive-construction-void-2026-04-15` L129.
- The phenomenal-transparency family: [[phenomenal-transparency-opacity-spectrum]], the "transparency thesis" (Harman/Tye) on several pages, and the archived transparency void.

In the wider philosophy of mind, "transparency" means the transparency of experience (Moore, Harman, Tye) or the transparency method for self-knowledge. A web search for "transparency problem" with idealism or Kastrup found no use of the phrase for the idealist's debt, so the phrase is the Map's coinage. The idealism literature names parts of the problem itself: Chalmers's "austerity problem" and his "explanatory objection" (2019).

**Proposed name: the lawful-appearance problem.** It covers both halves of the charge, lawfulness and appearance. It matches the existing "appearance debt" headings (mechanism-costs L95, "The Appearance Debt"; dualism-cartography L99, "Q3 owes the appearance debt"). A grep of `obsidian/` and `archive/` finds no existing use. Keep the Q3 slug `q3-q4-sliding-boundary-and-transparency-problem` so its URL stays stable, and gloss the old name once in prose ("what earlier versions called the transparency problem").

## The Problem, Decomposed {#decomposition}

| Part | Question | Specific to idealism? | Strongest idealist answer | What remains |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 Order | Why does appearance follow laws, and these laws? | No. Every view takes some laws as basic, and dualism adds psychophysical laws as well ([[psychophysical-laws]] L135, Schaffer's "t-shirt problem") | Berkeley: divine will. | T4 Explanation of order (anti-realist idealism) | What explains the coherence of possible experiences? | Yes, for phenomenalism | God (Berkeley); the tapestry (Yetter-Chappell); primitive laws (Pelczar) | Christmann 2026: no ground for the synchronic co-consciousness pattern |

## Key Sources

### Chalmers (2019), "Idealism and the Mind-Body Problem"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.4324/9781315717708-28 (preprint: https://consc.net/papers/idealism.pdf)
- **Type**: Handbook chapter, read in the author's preprint. Verified at Crossref: *The Routledge Handbook of Panpsychism*, ed. W. Seager, Routledge, issued 2019-12-10, pp. 353–373 (Crossref's title string carries a stray footnote "1")
- **Key points**:
  - Realist idealism holds that "what it is for physical facts p to obtain is for certain structural roles to obtain". It is "naturally understood as a sort of realism about the physical world, rather than a sort of anti-realism": "The physical world really exists out there, independently of our observations; it just has a surprising nature." Such views are "highly congenial to epistemological structural realism, which says roughly that science reveals the structure of the physical world but not its intrinsic nature."
  - The explanatory objection to phenomenalism: "the truths about possible experiences demand explanation, and phenomenalism gives them none". Cosmic and micro phenomenalism do not escape it: "one can still reasonably raise the question of what explains the order among the cosmic appearances or the micro-appearances."
  - The austerity problem ("A related issue specific to cosmic idealism is the austerity problem"). The cosmic mind "looks extremely austere, and very much unlike a mind as we normally think of it. Its basic experiential structure and dynamics is very much the structure and dynamics of physics. There seems to be little or no rationality in this structure. … It is not really clear why, if there is to be a cosmic mind, it should be as austere as this." The impure alternative has its own cost: "the world has more structure than physics suggests, and we have to postulate supra-natural structure and dynamics beyond what natural science suggests", and "one might worry that they will be entirely epiphenomenal with respect to the universe we observe."
  - The constitution problem "is a very serious one". Emergent or autonomous cosmic/macro idealism "has very much the flavor of dualist views of the mind–body relation, and it suffers from analogous problems of interaction". His footnote 17 classes Albahari and Yetter-Chappell (then forthcoming) as emergent views of this kind.
  - Overall verdict: "cosmic idealism is the most promising version of idealism, and is about as promising as any version of panpsychism"; "Idealism is not significantly less plausible than its main competitors."
- **Tenet alignment**: conflicts with Tenet 1 (Dualism) at the limit. Neutral on Tenets 2–4; Chalmers files consciousness-collapse views under dualism, not idealism. Fits the spirit of Tenet 5: he opens his conclusion with "I do not claim that idealism is plausible", and then judges every rival implausible too.
- **Quote**: "It is not really clear why, if there is to be a cosmic mind, it should be as austere as this."

### Yetter-Chappell (2025), *The View from Everywhere: Realist Idealism without God*
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1093/9780197795057.001.0001
- **Type**: Book (OUP, New York; Crossref issued 2025-02-26). Chapters verified at Crossref: "Berkeley without God" 1–23; "The Phenomenal Tapestry" 24–64; "Idealism and the Mind-Body Problem" 65–83 (.003.0003); "Perception" 84–117; "Science, Structure, and Spacetime" 118–147 (.003.0005); "The Virtues of Idealism" 148–194
- **Key points** (from the Crossref abstracts; the chapter bodies were not read):
  - Reality is "a vast unity of consciousness that binds together experiences as-of every object from every perspective". Chapter 1 asks us to "think of reality as Berkeley's God, minus: minus beliefs, desires, will . . . minus everything but his vast collection of sensory experience and the relations that structure these experiences."
  - On order: "There must be something outside of us that can sustain objects when we are not perceiving them, and account for the regularity of our perceptions. But this needn't be a god in any recognizable sense."
  - Ch. 5: "any account of laws available to materialists is equally available to the nontheistic idealist, for physical laws are—rightly—neutral as to the metaphysical nature of that which they govern". The view fits both substantivalism and relationalism, and the chapter closes on "worries about ontological and theoretical profligacy".
  - Ch. 3: "Idealism (per se) is not a position on the mind-body problem. It is compatible with analogues of all the major positions on the mind-body problem." She favours a nonreductive solution plus "a novel externalist account of psychophysical bridging laws". **So idealism keeps a mind–body problem.**
  - Ch. 6: "If our world is an idealist world, we live in David Chalmers's perceptual Eden."
- **Tenet alignment**: conflicts with Tenet 1. Her retained bridging laws are a structural counterpart of Tenet 3's interaction question, now posed between minds.

### Kastrup (2017), "An Ontological Solution to the Mind-Body Problem"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.3390/philosophies2020010
- **Type**: Paper, *Philosophies* 2(2): 10, read in full
- **Key points**:
  - Abstract: "The universe we see around us is the extrinsic appearance of phenomenality surrounding—but dissociated from—our alter."
  - He lists lawfulness as a datum his view must meet. His Fact 3 reads: "Reality normally unfolds according to patterns and regularities—that is, the laws of nature—independent of personal volition."
  - **On impersonality** (Explanation 3): dissociation "explains rather simply why we do not have personal volitional control over the laws of nature".
  - **On stability** (§6, the third objection): he asks "how can the laws of nature be so stable, self-consistent and predictable?" and answers "The fallacy here, of course, is that of anthropomorphization". He concludes that "the laws of nature are akin to the archetypes of universal consciousness".
  - **On austerity** (§9): outside its alters, universal consciousness "may unfold, at least partly, along lines that we may describe as instinctual or naturalistic." This is his answer to T3.
  - "The physical is simply the verifiable contents of perception, a particular category of experience."
- **Quote status**: the string "matter is the extrinsic appearance of mental processes" does **not** occur in this paper (0 hits; "extrinsic appearance" occurs 29 times). Do not reintroduce it as a quotation. Verified alternatives are the abstract sentence above and "The inanimate universe we see around us is the extrinsic appearance of these thoughts." (§11).
- **Tenet alignment**: conflicts with Tenet 1. Turns Tenet 3's interaction into mental–mental relations. Silent on Tenets 2 and 4.

### Berkeley (1710; 1734 text), *A Treatise Concerning the Principles of Human Knowledge*, §§30–31, 60–65
- **URL**: https://archive.org/details/treatiseconcerni00berk (Open Court reprint, 1904)
- **Type**: Primary historical text
- **Key points**:
  - §30: "the set rules or established methods wherein the Mind we depend on excites in us the ideas of sense, are called the laws of nature" (the OCR misreads one character in "laws"). §31 gives the purpose: "a sort of foresight which enables us to regulate our actions for the benefit of life".
  - §60 raises the transparency problem as the "eleventh" objection: "to what purpose serves that curious organization of plants, and the animal mechanism in the parts of animals"? "The like may be said of all the clockwork of nature".
  - §65 answers it. Connexions among ideas are "only of a mark or sign with the thing signified", and "the reason why ideas are formed into machines, that is, artificial and regular combinations, is the same with that for combining letters into words." Mechanism is a grammar that finite minds can read.
- **Significance**: the Map presents the question as a fresh debt. Berkeley set it out and answered it in 1710, with a *teleological* answer: the order exists so that finite minds can read it.
- **Tenet alignment**: conflicts with Tenet 1. Chalmers classes Berkeley as a "cosmic/macro phenomenalist".

### The structural-realist premise: Russell (1927) and Eddington (1928)
- **Russell**, *An Outline of Philosophy*, p. 163: "Physics is mathematical, not because we know so much about the physical world, but because we know so little: it is only its mathematical properties that we can discover."
- **Eddington**, *The Nature of the Physical World*, pp. 275–277. He sets out to show "how it comes about that within the whole domain of experience a selected portion is capable of that exact metrical representation which is requisite for development by the scientific method". His conclusion: "the stuff of the world is mind-stuff". The symbols of physics "bear to it the same relation that the bursar's accounts bear to the activity of the college."
- **Relevance**: these texts supply the premise of the T2 answer, not idealism itself; Russell was a neutral monist. The same premise drives the Map's [[russellian-monism]] and [[intrinsic-nature]] pages.

### Mørch (2017), "The Evolutionary Argument for Phenomenal Powers"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1111/phpe.12096 (*Philosophical Perspectives* 31(1): 293–316; read at abstract)
- **Key point**: the phenomenal powers view holds that "phenomenal properties produce and thereby (metaphysically) necessitate their effects in virtue of how they feel". Chalmers (2019) carries the idea over to idealism: "The Mørch-style move of saying that cosmic experiential states ground the relevant dispositions is particularly attractive in this context".
- **Relevance**: this is the idealist's best route to laws that are *less* brute than on physicalism or dualism, because lawful dispositions would follow from what experiences are like. If it works, T1 tilts toward idealism instead of resting at parity.

### Christmann (2026), "Global subject pressure in nontheistic idealism"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0034412526101681 (*Religious Studies*, First View, pp. 1–12; Crossref issued 2026-07-07; abstract verified)
- **Key points**: Yetter-Chappell's view "cannot sustain anti-subjectivism without admitting a globally unifying subject-like ground", in part because of "the absence of any adequate ground for the synchronic pattern of co-consciousness relations"; and "theistic idealism is comparatively superior".
- **Relevance**: this is a slide *within* idealism, from nontheistic toward a global subject, and it mirrors the Q3 slide. On this argument the T4 order debt pushes Yetter-Chappell back toward Berkeley's God.

## Major Positions (the idealist answers)

### The divine-order answer (Berkeley; Foster and Robinson in the Berkeleyan mould)
- **Core claim**: lawful order is the regular will of a cosmic mind, arranged so that finite minds can read it (§§30–31, 65).
- **Relation to site tenets**: the Map rejects the posit. But the posit is of the same *type* as the Map's own: a fundamental source of order on the mental side, where the Map has fundamental psychophysical laws. It cannot be scored as worse in kind.

### The structural-realist answer (Russell's and Eddington's premise; Chalmers's realist idealism)
- **Core claim**: the impersonal, lawful look of the world is an artefact of what physics can express.
- **Relation to site tenets**: the Map holds the premise ([[intrinsic-nature]] L37; [[ontic-structural-realism]] §"Why the Map Prefers the Epistemic Reading"). The Map therefore cannot press T2 without weakening its own epistemic structural realism. What remains is Newman's problem, which the Map's OSR page already says "cuts both ways".

### Laws as regularities of mind-at-large (Kastrup)
- **Core claim**: the laws are archetypes of universal consciousness. Expecting them to be unstable anthropomorphises the cosmic mind, and dissociation explains why we cannot will them away.
- **Relation to site tenets**: this matches the Map's own concession at idealism.md L119 ("a parity result, not an argument for dualism"). The Map's remaining objection is the relabelling question at analytic-idealism L54, and Chalmers's austerity problem is its sharpest form.

### Laws are neutral about what they govern (Yetter-Chappell)
- **Core claim**: Humean, governing and dispositional accounts of laws all carry over unchanged to an idealist base.
- **Relation to site tenets**: this undercuts dualism-cartography L99's claim that "why this regularity rather than another" is "a Q3-specific debt".

### Grounding laws in phenomenal powers (Mørch, as Chalmers carries it over)
- **Core claim**: experiential natures ground the dispositions that physics records.
- **Relation to site tenets**: it competes with the Map on the Map's own ground (mental causation), and has an extra cost of its own, the causal-powers reading of experience.

## Key Debates

### Austerity versus excess baggage
- **Sides**: Chalmers (2019) sets the dilemma. Kastrup takes the pure, austere option and explains the austerity by the absence of self-reflection outside alters.
- **Core disagreement**: a cosmic mind whose structure matches physics exactly is barely a mind. A richer one carries structure that does no work.
- **Current state**: open. This is the part of the charge that the Map can press most legitimately.

### Does idealism escape the interface?
- **Sides**: Kastrup's identity view takes on subject constitution, which the Map treats at [[open-individualism-and-the-de-combination-problem]]. Yetter-Chappell keeps bridging laws (ch. 3). Chalmers judges emergent cosmic/macro idealism to have "analogous problems of interaction".
- **Current state**: no realist idealism surveyed gets rid of a mind–mind relation debt. The idealist's real trade is an interface between mind and matter for a constitution or bridging debt between minds.

### Pressure toward a global subject
- **Sides**: Christmann (2026) against Yetter-Chappell (2025).
- **Current state**: one recent critique, with no reply yet located.

## Assessment: The Map's Debt Comparison {#assessment}

Claim by claim:

1. **Q3 page L38**: "The transparency problem is not lighter than the interface problem; it is a different debt with a different shape. Trading the first for the second is precisely what the slide accomplishes."
   - "Not lighter than" is a judgement of weight. The Map's apex [[dualism-cartography]] (L87) has withdrawn "cheaper than" verdicts "for want of a scale". It also records Q3's transparency debt as "read off the cells' own commitments rather than argued independently". "Not lighter" is that same kind of verdict in negated form and should go. "A different debt with a different shape" stays.
   - "Trading" is incomplete. Realist idealists keep a debt between minds (Yetter-Chappell's bridging laws; Kastrup's subject constitution). A more accurate statement: the slide converts a mind–matter interface debt into a mind–mind constitution debt and adds a narrower appearance residue (T3).
   - The same page's L86 ("do not pay a heavy recovery debt") weighs the debt again in the MWI analogy.
2. **dualism-cartography L99**: "why this regularity rather than another" is "a Q3-specific debt the other cells do not face in that form". T1 is shared by all views (Yetter-Chappell ch. 5; idealism.md L119), and the qualifier "in that form" carries the whole claim. The residue specific to Q3 is austerity. The page is over its hard limit (5,185/5,000 per the 10-11 todo note), so any fix must not add length. Candidate swap: "why this regularity rather than another" → "why a mind's dynamics would be this austere".
3. **mechanism-costs L99 / L105**: L99 states the question fairly. L105's "minimal interface debt, substantial appearance debt" uses "substantial" as a weight term.
4. **analytic-idealism L54**: the "relabelling" question is fair. Citing Chalmers's austerity problem there would sharpen it.
5. **dualism-channel-width-axis L71 / L83**: these say Kastrup "owes an account of why the intrinsic looks extrinsic". Kastrup 2017 gives one (dissociation plus archetypes). The page should state it before calling it owed.
6. **The Map's own appearance debt.** [[observational-closure]] L54 says the Map's selections are "posited to average to exactly the Born distribution across the ensemble". That is a mental process which must look exactly like physics. The shape is the same as the charge against idealism; the scope is narrower (selection events only, not all of physics); and it is posited, not explained. Fairness requires naming it on whichever page charges idealism.

**Net result**:
- **Specific to idealism**: T3 (austerity), T4 (for anti-realist forms) and C (constitution).
- **Shared by all views**: T1.
- **Dissolved by a premise the Map holds**: T2.
- **The scorecard**: neither side is shown to carry the lighter debt, so the inventory should list items and not weigh them.
- **What survives for the Map**: it can legitimately press austerity, together with Christmann's internal-slide argument.
