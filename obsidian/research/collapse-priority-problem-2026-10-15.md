# Research: The Collapse-Priority Problem

**Date**: 2026-10-15
**Search queries and fetches used**: SEP "Collapse Theories" (full text); "Esfeld Gisin GRW flash theory relativistic quantum ontology arXiv"; "Bedingham relativistic state reduction dynamics arXiv"; "Brody Hughston efficient simulation of quantum state reduction"; "consciousness or free will selects the noise realization in spontaneous collapse model"; "Hameroff Penrose 2014 non-computable not random objective reduction"; arXiv full texts of Tumulka 2006 and 2007, Conway & Kochen 2006, Pearle 1999, Brody & Hughston 2002, Bedingham 2009 and 2010, Esfeld & Gisin 2013, Gisin 2010, Chalmers & McQueen 2021; arXiv-API abstracts of Bassi & Hejazi, Okon & Sebastián, Myrvold 2017; Crossref metadata for every published item below.

**Calibration banner.** This note states a **debt**: what a priority law would have to look like. It does not claim that such a law exists. The Map has no worked model of the mechanism ([[positions/quantum-interface#^p-q10|P-Q10]]), and the bias-without-deviation dilemma stays open ([[positions/quantum-interface#^p-q3|P-Q3]]). The formal frameworks surveyed below are physics written for other purposes. Where this note maps the Map's selector onto them, the mapping is the Map's own construction, framework-internal and coherence-only. No source surveyed connects any of these formalisms to a conscious selector, except the consciousness-collapse models (Chalmers & McQueen; Okon & Sebastián), which assign consciousness the opposite job.

## Executive Summary

The Map's ordering ([[post-decoherence-selection]], [[prebiotic-collapse#the-completeness-tension|prebiotic collapse]]) gives objective reduction four jobs: whether a collapse occurs, when, in which basis, and, wherever no interface is present, which outcome. It gives consciousness one job: in an interface-grade brain it preempts the token outcome inside the Born corridor. The "collapse-priority debt" is the missing law that combines the two processes. Seven loci on four pages name the debt, and no page states its content.

The literature supplies more structure than the corpus has used. (1) **GRW already factors each collapse into WHEN, WHERE and HOW** (SEP's own words). The WHEN is a Poisson clock that does not depend on the state. The WHERE is a sample from a Born-like density. So "preemption inside the corridor" has an exact formal home: the selector supplies the WHERE sample at hits inside the interface and leaves the clock alone. Tumulka's relativistic GRW keeps the same split. (2) **In CSL the realised noise is, in Pearle's word, "the chooser"**. Brody & Hughston show that one reduction model can be rewritten exactly as a Born-distributed terminal outcome variable plus an independent Brownian motion. On that representation, "the selector fixes the outcome and physics fixes the trajectory" is a well-defined statement. Brody & Hughston expressly assign the split no physical significance, so treating it as ontology is a further posit. (3) **The delay/rate clause is needed for a reason no corpus page states**. A selector that controlled timing could bias outcomes while every single collapse still obeyed the Born rule for the state at its own moment. The no-delay clause is what keeps the corridor from being evaded through timing. (4) **The spacelike-ordering clause meets a known result**. In relativistic collapse models the joint probability law can be covariant, but the *coming-into-being* of realisations cannot be (Esfeld & Gisin; Gisin; contested by Tumulka, who calls the question meaningless). A selector acts on realisations, so a covariant priority law has three visible shapes, each with a cost: a preferred foliation; no temporal locus for selection; or a precedence keyed to event *type* (interface events condition non-interface events). The type-keyed rule is covariant in form. It turns any intention-conditioned deviation into a signalling channel wherever the selected degrees of freedom are entangled with an unsettled spacelike partner, so it leans on interface locality.

## Key Sources

### Ghirardi & Bassi, "Collapse Theories", *Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy* (substantive revision 30 Sep 2025)
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/qm-collapse/
- **Type**: Encyclopedia (full text fetched; authors from the entry's copyright block)
- **Key points**:
  - GRW's hitting process is specified by three independent answers, which the entry labels HOW, WHERE and WHEN. HOW: the wavefunction is multiplied by a Gaussian. WHERE: the hit centre has probability density "given by the square of the norm of the state" after localisation. WHEN: "the question WHEN is answered by assuming that the hittings occur at randomly distributed times, according to a Poisson distribution, with mean frequency" *f*.
  - "This implies that hittings occur with higher probability at those places where, in the standard quantum description, there is a higher probability of finding the particle if a measurement were performed".
  - Relativistic programme: Tumulka (2006) is described as the first relativistic reduction model satisfying all relativistic requirements, "free from divergences and foliation independent", but only for non-interacting particles. Bedingham (2011) gets a covariant CSL-type field theory by adding an auxiliary relativistic field.
  - On spacelike events, the entry reports (citing Ghirardi & Grassi 1996 and Ghirardi 2000, not read here) that "the very formal structure of the theory is such that it does not allow, even conceptually, to establish cause-effect relations between space-like events." It also reports that GRW and CSL, "just as standard quantum mechanics, exhibit only outcome dependence".
  - It reports Myrvold's (2017) no-go: a relativistically invariant Markovian collapse theory that ensures vacuum stability and excludes superluminal signalling "cannot be consistently constructed without the introduction of nonstandard degrees of freedom."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral physics. Supports Tenet 4 (single outcomes) and supplies the baseline the prebiotic ordering assumes.
- **Use**: The WHEN/WHERE/HOW factorisation is the exact place where "OR fixes when and in which basis; the selector fixes which" can be written down (see *Angle (i)* below).

### Pearle, "Collapse Models" (1999), in Breuer & Petruccione (eds.), *Open Systems and Measurement in Relativistic Quantum Theory*, Lecture Notes in Physics 526, 195–234
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/9901077 · DOI 10.1007/BFb0104404 (Crossref)
- **Type**: Review chapter (arXiv full text read)
- **Key points**:
  - The founder of CSL frames collapse dynamics as supplying a chooser: "standard quantum theory (SQT) is a theory of choices without a chooser: more is a chooser."
  - In the toy model the operator fixes the menu and the noise picks: "the operator A determines the choices while B(t) is the chooser."
  - The probability rule "gives the probability density for B(t) to be the actual noise that occurs in nature", and "the statevectors with the largest norm are the most likely to occur."
  - On non-Markovian versions: "at the ensemble level, there is no effect of the future upon the present."
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral. It aligns structurally with Tenets 2–3 only through the Map's own reading.
- **Use**: This is Pearle's vocabulary, not the Map's. In CSL the outcome is fixed by which noise realisation occurs, and the probability rule fixes the measure over realisations. "The selector fixes the noise realisation within its own measure" is therefore a precise gloss of corridor preemption. Its limit: in CSL the same realisation also fixes the trajectory, and so the effective *timing*, of the collapse. The selector may not touch timing on the Map's ordering, so a further split is needed (next source).

### Brody & Hughston, "Efficient Simulation of Quantum State Reduction", *Journal of Mathematical Physics* 43(11): 5254–5261 (2002)
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0203035 · DOI 10.1063/1.1512975 (Crossref)
- **Type**: Paper (arXiv full text read)
- **Key points**:
  - For the energy-based stochastic reduction equation they give a closed-form solution "in terms of a random variable representing the terminal value of the energy and an independent Brownian motion."
  - The terminal variable H takes each eigenvalue with the Born transition probability. The observed "signal" is ξ_t = σHt + B_t, "a ‘noisy’ representation of the information encoded in the random variable H."
  - "the ‘true value’ of H is ‘hidden’ until the reduction process is complete." H and B "can be thought of as representing independent state variables for the reduction dynamics."
  - Caveat in the source itself: "We assign no a priori physical significance to H", and both variables "are introduced as an ansatz for obtaining a solution".
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral physics.
- **Use**: This is the cleanest formal statement of the decomposition the priority law needs. It splits the reduction exactly into a Born-distributed outcome variable and an independent noise that carries the trajectory, with the rate set by σ and the spectrum. On this representation, "the selector supplies H, and physics supplies B and σ" is a well-defined priority rule. It changes neither rate nor timing distribution, and if the selector's H-choices are Born-distributed the marginal is exactly Born. Two cautions for the article. (a) Promoting H to the selector's locus is an interpretive posit that the authors decline. (b) The closed form is shown here for the energy-based model, whose collapse operator commutes with the Hamiltonian. Whether general CSL (mass-density collapse with a non-commuting Hamiltonian) admits the same exact split was not established in the sources read (see Gaps).

### Bedingham, "Dynamical state reduction in an EPR experiment", *Journal of Physics A* 42(46): 465301 (2009)
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.2327 · DOI 10.1088/1751-8113/42/46/465301 (Crossref)
- **Type**: Paper (arXiv full text read)
- **Key points**:
  - It gives a relativistically invariant continuous reduction model of a spacelike EPR measurement. The equations "are solved explicitly in terms of random variables with a priori known probability distribution in the physical probability measure."
  - In that solution the hidden variables s_a "represent the true outcomes for the isospin eigenvalues of each particle after the measurement process". These are jointly distributed (perfectly anti-correlated for the singlet) and masked by independent Brownian motions.
  - "the use of the physical probability measure induces a corresponding correlation between the stochastic processes to which the particle states are coupled."
  - On frames: "We stress that the choice of spacetime foliation is analogous to an arbitrary gauge choice." It also argues against Conway & Kochen that a relativistic reduction mechanism is possible.
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral physics. It bears on Tenet 4 (a relativistic one-world collapse is constructible).
- **Use**: This is the relativistic analogue of the Brody–Hughston split, and it shows the problem plainly. The outcome variables of spacelike-separated wings are *one joint variable*. A selector at wing 1 that fixes s_1 thereby fixes s_2. Two selectors would contend for one variable. Ordering is "gauge", but a selector's act is supposed to be a physical fact.

### Tumulka, "A Relativistic Version of the Ghirardi–Rimini–Weber Model", *Journal of Statistical Physics* 125(4): 821–840 (2006)
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0406094 · DOI 10.1007/s10955-006-9227-3 (Crossref). Quotations are from arXiv v2.
- **Type**: Paper (arXiv full text read)
- **Key points**:
  - Primitive ontology of flashes; the flash set "is random with distribution determined by the initial wavefunction." The model "is nonlocal and violates Bell’s inequality though it does not make use of a preferred slicing of space-time or any other sort of synchronization of spacelike separated points."
  - Law of the flashes: waiting times are independent exponentials with expectation τ. The new flash locations are then drawn from a |Ψ|²-type density on hyperboloids. The clock/location split of nonrelativistic GRW survives.
  - Nonlocality: for spacelike-separated flashes the joint distribution does not factorise. Either flash's distribution can be read as depending on the other's realisation.
  - Generations: "the theory neither presupposes nor provides a grouping of the flashes into generations", so it supplies no earlier/later between spacelike flashes.
  - The trade-off: "our model suggests that one should be able to avoid a preferred slicing if one is willing to pay the price of a certain deviation from quantum mechanics."
- **Tenet alignment**: Supports Tenet 4 (definite outcomes, single history). Covariant only at the level of the joint law (see Esfeld & Gisin).

### Tumulka, "Comment on 'The Free Will Theorem'", *Foundations of Physics* 37(2): 186–197 (2007)
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0611283 · DOI 10.1007/s10701-006-9098-0 (Crossref). Quotations are from arXiv v2.
- **Type**: Paper (arXiv full text read)
- **Key points**: In rGRWf, in one frame the first flash f_A has the marginal Born distribution and f_B the conditional one; in another frame the roles reverse. "Who influences whom is frame dependent. There is no objective fact about who “really” influenced whom. There is no need for such a fact." And: "Whether nature chooses the space-time point fB first, and fA afterwards, or the other way around, does not seem like a meaningful question to me." - **Use**: This is the sharpest statement of the position the Map *cannot* simply adopt. The Map's selector is precisely something that "chooses", and its corridor differs by frame: the marginal in one frame, the conditional in another.

### Conway & Kochen, "The Free Will Theorem", *Foundations of Physics* 36(10): 1441–1473 (2006)
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0604079 · DOI 10.1007/s10701-006-9068-6 (Crossref)
- **Type**: Paper (arXiv full text read; the Janus section)
- **Key points**: In a toy "hexagonal universe", "Janus freely decides the result of the first experiment on any given day, and then uses the SPIN axiom to fill in the results for the other hexagons on that day." The construction proves consistency only: "the Janus models show the consistency of this physics, but cannot be “the” explanation for the physics, since there is both a left and a right Janus model." The paper also claims that GRW-type mechanisms cannot be made relativistic. Tumulka (2007) and Bedingham (2009) contest that claim.
- **Use**: Janus is an uncannily close model of the Map's selector. A free chooser fixes one outcome and the law fills in the correlated rest, and *which face chooses* is a symmetry-breaking fact the physics does not fix. Cite the toy as an analogy only. Its relativistic conclusion is disputed.

### Esfeld & Gisin, "The GRW Flash Theory: A Relativistic Quantum Ontology of Matter in Space-Time?", *Philosophy of Science* 81(2): 248–264 (2014)
- **URL**: https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.5308 · DOI 10.1086/675730 (Crossref). Quotations are from the arXiv draft of 7 Oct 2013.
- **Type**: Paper (arXiv full text read)
- **Key points**:
  - A frame-independent account of *how* spacelike Bell outcomes come about would need functions that do not exist: "neither rGRWf, nor any other theory, can account for the occurrence of the Alice-flash and the occurrence of the Bob-flash in a Lorentz-invariant manner."

- **Type**: Book chapter (arXiv full text read: abstract, §2, CSL section)
- **Key points**:

## Major Positions: Candidate Orderings

The 2026-10-08 review asks the article to treat "the three candidate orderings" but does not list them. The reconstruction below is this note's own. It gives three orderings plus two variants found in the literature.

### Ordering A: Reduction-first (outcome-complete OR)
- **Proponents**: Standard GRW/CSL and DP read as complete theories; no consciousness role.
- **Core claim**: The physical noise fixes every outcome, including inside brains.
- **Relation to tenets**: This is the "noise already selected" horn of the completeness dilemma at [[prebiotic-collapse#the-completeness-tension|prebiotic L126]]. It leaves no work for a selector ([[ensemble-level-epiphenomenalism]]) and fails Tenet 3.

### Ordering B: Selection-first (consciousness sources the collapse)
- **Proponents**: von Neumann–Wigner ([[von-neumann-wigner-interpretation]]); Chalmers & McQueen (super-resistance); Okon & Sebastián (IIT-dependent CSL operator); Kremnizer & Ranchin (lead, not read).
- **Core claim**: Consciousness (or its physical correlate) triggers collapse and fixes the basis. The Born rule picks the token.
- **Relation to tenets**: It reverses the Map's division of labour, since consciousness sets occurrence, timing and basis while chance sets the outcome. It conflicts with the prebiotic ordering ("Consciousness initiates no collapse"), is exposed to the Zeno falsifier (Chalmers & McQueen's own abstract), and is the direction [[thoughtful-local-friendliness-and-the-artificial-friend]] L81 says the laboratory case would push the Map toward.

### Ordering C: Nested substitution (the Map's preemption)
- **Proponents**: The Map ([[post-decoherence-selection]], [[prebiotic-collapse]]); no external proponent found.
- **Core claim**: OR's clock, operator and rate fix whether, when and in which basis. Inside interface-grade events the realisation of the outcome variable is supplied by the selector, constrained to the measure OR would have used.
- **Formal home**: GRW/rGRWf WHERE-sampling; the Brody–Hughston terminal variable H; Bedingham's s_a.
- **Relation to tenets**: This ordering is the minimality claim of Tenet 2 and the causal claim of Tenet 3, and it presupposes Tenet 4. Its open costs are the four clauses below.

### Variant: Concurrent additive dynamics
- Several collapse operators in one stochastic equation (Chalmers & McQueen's combined form). There is no priority question, because all act together, but the rates add. A consciousness term therefore changes timing, which collapses this variant into Ordering B's costs. Where the operators fail to commute there are no joint eigenstates, so a basis conflict replaces the priority question.

### Variant: Internal non-random selection with a race (Orch-OR)
- One reduction law whose token choice is "non-random (albeit non-computable)", with a race against environmental reduction that masks it. Priority is decided by which reduction reaches threshold first. The Map rejects the race: its selector substitutes within whatever reduction occurs.

## Angle (i): The Noise-Realisation Formalisation

**Statement.** In every surveyed dynamical-reduction model the token outcome is a function of a realised random object. In GRW it is the hit centre. In CSL it is the noise path, Pearle's "chooser". In the Brody–Hughston representation it is the terminal variable H, and in Bedingham's EPR solution the variables s_a. "Preemption inside the Born corridor" can be stated without metaphor: *within interface-grade events, the selector fixes the outcome-determining part of the realisation, and the probability law the model already contains is the corridor the selector must respect.*

**What this buys.**
1. The corridor stops being a metaphor. It is the model's own probability rule: the GRW WHERE density, Pearle's P_t{B}, the law of H.
2. Exact marginal preservation is automatic *if* the selector's choices over trials follow that law, which is desideratum 2 of the [[born-preserving-causal-efficacy|toy-model desiderata]].
3. It links to the [[agency-budget]] skeleton. A minimum-entropy coupling between conscious state C and outcome supplies a joint law P(C, H) with Born marginal on H. Drawing H from P(H | C) at interface events, and leaving B to physics, writes the coupling into a reduction model. As far as this search found, the corpus has not made that connection before.

**What it does not buy.**
- It does not answer P-Q3. Whether a selector constrained to the model's measure does causal work or merely relabels chance is the bias-without-deviation dilemma itself, and the formalisation relocates it unchanged.
- It does not supply the selection principle (desideratum 5; [[selection-criterion-problem]]).
- The CSL realisation also fixes the trajectory, so the selector must act on the *outcome part* only. That split is exact in GRW/rGRWf (clock independent of location) and in Brody–Hughston's energy-based model, but it is not shown for general CSL (see Gaps).
- Making H the selector's locus contradicts nothing in the physics. It does add metaphysics the physicists explicitly decline ("no a priori physical significance").

**Where the outcome is "decided".** On the B-driven representation the outcome emerges gradually as the noise accumulates. On the H+B representation it is fixed from the start and "hidden" until reduction completes. The filtrations coincide, so the dynamics cannot say which representation is fundamental. The priority law therefore has to *choose a representation as ontology*, and the physics does not supply that choice. The article should present this as one of the primitives the law costs. The Map's preemption reads naturally on the H+B representation, and reduction-first reads naturally on the B-driven one.

## The Delay/Rate Clause

The anchor page asks "whether selection can delay reduction or alter its rate". The Map answers no (the PDS lead assigns timing to OR). Neither the corpus nor any source found says *why* the corridor needs the answer to be no. Here is the reason, offered as this note's own analysis, framework-internal and checkable.

- **Timing is a bias channel.** Between collapses the state evolves unitarily, so the Born weights change over time (for example in an oscillating two-level system). Suppose a selector could choose *when* an interface collapse happens, and so which instantaneous weights apply. It could then raise the frequency of a preferred outcome while every single collapse obeyed the Born rule for the state at its own moment. Per-collapse corridor compliance would be satisfied and the aggregate would be biased relative to the physical clock. The no-delay, no-rate clause is therefore what makes the corridor binding.
- **Zeno is the same channel at high rate.** Frequent triggered collapse freezes evolution. That is why simple consciousness-collapse models fall to the quantum Zeno effect (Chalmers & McQueen). It is also why the prebiotic page's former Zeno sentence ("prolong certain superpositions") conflicted with the ordering ([[quantum-zeno-effect]]; Stapp-Zeno is demoted at [[positions/quantum-interface#^p-q4|P-Q4]]).
- **In the formal homes the clock is already independent of the outcome.** GRW and rGRWf have Poisson or exponential waiting times that do not depend on the state. In Brody–Hughston the reduction rate is set by σ and the energy gaps, and the Brownian motion is independent of H. "The selector does not touch the clock" is therefore a natural restriction in these models.
- **What does not forbid rate modulation.** No-signalling (Gisin; Bassi & Hejazi) forbids only state-dependent nonlinearity in the ensemble dynamics. A rate changed by an external mental parameter would keep the dynamics linear. The prohibition comes from minimality (Tenet 2) and from the bias-laundering argument above, not from relativity. The article should not claim more. This bears on the "rate-modulation model" at [[comparing-quantum-consciousness-mechanisms]] (the review's carried item 5).

## Angle (ii): Relativistic Collapse and What "First" Could Mean

**The finding.** In rGRWf and relativistic CSL the *probability law* can be covariant: Tumulka's joint flash distribution, Bedingham's foliation-independent equations. The *order in which realisations come about* cannot be made covariant (Esfeld & Gisin; Gisin). Tumulka replies that no such order is needed: "There is no need for such a fact." For a theory with no chooser, Tumulka's reply is available. For the Map it is not, because the Map's selector is a chooser of realisations. Its corridor also differs by frame. Where the selected outcome is correlated with an unsettled spacelike partner, the corridor is the marginal in a frame where the selection comes first and the conditional in a frame where it comes second (Tumulka 2007's EPR walk-through, applied to a selector).

**Three shapes for a covariant priority law, with costs:**
1. **Preferred foliation.** Selection happens in the order of a privileged slicing, possibly derived from the wavefunction rather than added. Esfeld & Gisin argue that becoming requires this anyway. Cost: Lorentz invariance at the level of becoming. The SEP's report that collapse theories admit no cause-effect relations between spacelike events is then overridden for selection events specifically.
2. **No temporal locus (Tumulka's stance).** Only the joint distribution of whole histories is lawlike, so a selector "chooses" a history, or at least the joint outcome of every correlated event. Cost: the selector becomes global and non-local. That fits poorly with Tenet 2's minimality and with a brain-local interface, and it is the "Janus" structure Conway & Kochen say cannot be "the" explanation.
3. **Type-keyed precedence.** Interface events condition non-interface events, never the reverse, whatever the frame. The rule mentions event *type*, not time order, so it is covariant in form. It is also what interface locality already does in the generic case: detectors settle Bell outcomes physically, and brains select downstream inside their own future light cones ([[multi-agent-born-preservation-problem]], "Where the Interface-Locality Answer Runs Out"). Costs: (a) two interface events with correlated outcomes (cross-brain entanglement, nested agents) need a further rule, such as joint selection or a tie-break, which is the multi-agent debt again; (b) the **intention-signalling hazard**, stated below.

**The intention-signalling hazard (this note's derivation; verify before article use).** [[positions/quantum-interface#^p-q9|P-Q9]] and [[self-concealing-interface]] locate the corridor's empirical exposure in a deviation conditioned on intention: I(outcome ; intention | physical state) > 0. Under type-keyed precedence, suppose the selected outcome is entangled with an *unsettled* spacelike partner. Then an intention-conditioned bias at A becomes an intention-conditioned bias in B's statistics, and the subject chooses the intention freely. That is a superluminal signal. So the conditional-statistical channel and type-keyed precedence can coexist only where the selected degrees of freedom have no unsettled spacelike partner. That restricts the channel to exactly the interface-local cases. The priority debt and the P-Q9 exposure therefore constrain each other, and the article should say so. (Check against [[causal-consistency-constraint]] and the multi-agent page's demand 3 before stating it in Map voice.)

**Within one brain.** Two interface reductions in one brain can be spacelike-separated only within sub-nanosecond windows, since the brain is about 0.15 m across. A single unified subject selecting both would be a non-local selector across that region. The point is small but real. It is the spacelike version of the unity question and needs one sentence, not a section.

## Angle (iii): What the Priority Law Costs in Primitives

Per [[positions/methodology-and-calibration#^p-m1|P-M1]], adding structure that removes a defeater raises no evidential tier. A priority law adds at least these posits:

1. **An interface predicate** I(e): which reduction events are interface-grade. Without a principled criterion it is a free parameter ([[brain-interface-boundary]]).
2. **A representation-as-ontology choice**: the outcome-variable (H+B) reading of the reduction made real, where the physics is neutral between that reading and the noise-driven one.
3. **A corridor law**: the selector's choices follow the model's own probability rule, unconditioned ([[positions/quantum-interface#^p-q2|P-Q2]]), and also conditioned on any freely settable variable wherever the precedence rule exposes them to spacelike partners.
4. **A clock-independence clause**: the selector never affects whether or when a reduction occurs, nor its rate.
5. **A spacelike precedence rule**: one of the three shapes above, with its cost.
6. **A selection principle** (desideratum 5): still unfilled.

None of these raises credence, external grade or discriminability. Formalising 1–5 in a toy model would at most move *model maturity* for the relevant positions. On P-Q10's own terms, a worked toy model that survived review would tighten several positions by one band. The article must not suggest the law exists.

## Schematic Shape of the Law (debt statement, not a result)

> For each reduction event e whose occurrence, time t_e and basis {P_i} are fixed by the objective-reduction law: if I(e), the outcome index is supplied by the selector, subject to (corridor) Pr(i_e = i | everything publicly conditionable at e) = Tr(P_i ρ_e), and (clock) the law of t_e is independent of the selector; otherwise the outcome is sampled by the reduction law's own randomness. For spacelike-separated events with correlated outcome variables, [precedence rule: foliation | joint history | type-keyed + interface locality].

Every bracket and predicate in that sentence is currently unfilled. That is the content of the debt.

## Key Debates

### Is rGRWf "relativistic" if becoming is not?
- **Sides**: Tumulka (joint distribution suffices; order is meaningless) vs Esfeld & Gisin and Gisin (realisation/becoming needs a foliation; the theory is "as Lorentz-invariant as possible, but ... not more"). Conway & Kochen originally denied that relativistic GRW-type mechanisms are possible. Tumulka and Bedingham rebutted this.
- **Core disagreement**: whether a theory owes an account of how actual events come about, beyond their joint probability.
- **Current state**: Open. The Map is committed to actualisation events, so it sits on the Esfeld–Gisin side by construction and inherits their foliation dilemma.

### Does consciousness source collapse or select within it?
- **Sides**: Chalmers & McQueen and Okon & Sebastián (source and basis) vs the Map (token only, inside OR). Penrose (internal non-random choice, with a race).
- **Current state**: No external author defends the Map's nested ordering as found in this search. The Map's version is novel and unworked.

### Can a stochastic dynamics' randomness be "owned" by anything?
- **Sides**: Brody & Hughston (H and B as an ansatz with no physical significance) and Pearle (the noise as "chooser", with an ontological flavour). Frigg & Hoefer on GRW probabilities as propensities vs Humean chances is a lead, not read here.
- **Current state**: Unsettled in the physics literature. The Map must take a side.
