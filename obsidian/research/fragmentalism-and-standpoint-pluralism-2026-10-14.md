## Summary

Fragmentalism is Kit Fine's name for one of two "non-standard" ways of being a realist about tense. Fine lays out four principles that McTaggart's argument shows to be jointly inconsistent — Realism (reality is composed of tensed facts), Neutrality (no time is privileged), Absolutism (the constitution of reality is not relative to a standpoint) and Coherence (reality is not constituted by incompatible facts) — and sorts the theories of time by which principle they drop. Anti-realism drops Realism; standard realism (presentism, the moving spotlight) drops Neutrality; **external relativism** drops Absolutism; **fragmentalism** drops Coherence. Fine's fragmentalist takes reality to be "irredeemably incoherent": incompatible tensed facts all absolutely obtain, but only facts that "cohere" belong to the same "fragment" (a maximally coherent collection), and coherence is a primitive relation. Section 12 of the same chapter transposes the whole apparatus to subjects — First-Personal Realism, First-Personal Neutrality — and asks, without endorsing first-personal realism itself ("I do not know what to think on the question", p. 311), which non-standard form it should take. His answer is the same as for tense: fragmentalism over relativism.

List's quadrilemma is the subject-case of exactly this structure. His **non-fragmentation** ("the totality of facts that hold in any given world are compossible") is Fine's Coherence restricted to a world, and his **one world** is what distinguishes a fragmented single reality from Fine's relativist "multiple realities". So the fourth claim of the quadrilemma denies Fine's Coherence and nothing else: a fragmentalist drops non-fragmentation, keeps one world, and lets all subjects' first-personal facts obtain absolutely without co-obtaining. External relativism is not a horn of the quadrilemma in its own right; where it lands depends on whether relative obtaining is factive, which is the design choice Lipman 2023 makes central.

The literature since 2015 has split fragmentalism in two along a line that decides everything for the Map. Merlo 2022 distinguishes **structured** fragmentalism (Fine's own: *metaphysical* reality is neutral and fragmented, but *mere* reality — what is the case, full stop — is oriented to one standpoint, which requires denying that what is really the case is the case) from **unstructured** fragmentalism (Lipman, Loss, Simon, Iaquinto and Torrengo: every fragment's facts obtain simpliciter, so what is the case is itself incoherent). Unstructured fragmentalism faces Merlo's tensed-belief-explosion problem; structured fragmentalism avoids it at the price of a non-factive notion of reality, which Lipman and Loss both find unintelligible. Carried to subjects, structured fragmentalism *is* the "privileged-fragment" option the Map's pages gesture at: every subject's first-personal facts are really the case, only mine are the case. That is Merlo's two-level subjectivism, and — as the 2026-10-14 pessimistic review of the subjective-facts article observed — it is the shape of the Map's own posit. The Map's fragmentalist exit is therefore not a promissory note with unknown terms; it is structured first-personal fragmentalism, and its bill is non-factivity.

Jonathan Simon's quantum fragmentalism closes the loop with Tenet 4. He reads the terms of a superposition as states of affairs in different fragments — "a branch is a fragment" — giving a one-world picture in which every outcome obtains and none co-obtain. Iaquinto and Calosi argue it loses phase relations and is "dangerously close to be Everettian Quantum Mechanics in disguise". For the Map this is the live alternative the task brief anticipated, and it cuts both ways: the fragmentalist device the Map uses for subjects is the same device that would give the Everettian a one-world home for branches. A Map that is fragmentalist about subjects and unitist about outcomes owes an argument for the asymmetry, and the indexical objection cannot supply it against a *structured* branch-fragmentalism, which has a fact simpliciter about which branch is the case.

## The Four Views and Their Exits from the Quadrilemma

The mapping that organises everything: List's **non-fragmentation** is Fine's **Coherence** applied within a world; List's **one world** is the denial of Fine's relativist "multiple realities"; List's **first-person realism** is Fine's **First-Personal Realism** (p. 285); List's **non-solipsism** is the subject-case of Fine's **Neutrality** read as a claim about what is real (Hare's egocentric presentist is a standard first-personal realist in Fine's sense, with one privileged standpoint). Fine's **Absolutism** has no separate line in the quadrilemma, which is why external relativism has no horn of its own. List says as much: his claims "do not map exactly onto Fine's".

### (a) Fine's fragmentalism (2005), as refined by Lipman (2015, 2016) and sorted by Merlo (2022)

- **Core claim**: Realism + Neutrality + Absolutism, Coherence dropped. Incompatible tensed (or first-personal) facts all absolutely belong to reality; what structures them is a primitive relation — Fine's "coherence", Lipman's "co-obtainment", Simon's "symmetric coordination relation" — and a fragment is a maximal collection of facts so related. Fine's semantics keeps contradictory *utterances* false by focusing each utterance on the fragment of its own time ("Relative Link", p. 295, per Lipman thesis p. 46); the metaphysics "admits contradictory facts to reality" while the language never asserts both at once.
- **Two internal splits that matter**. (i) Lipman rejects Fine's transitivity and overlap assumptions and reformulates the view with co-obtainment as a sentential connective ("A ◦ B", "A insofar as B"), with the Correia–Rosenkranz point that Fine's "coherence" cannot be ordinary coherence (ordinary compatibility over-generates fragments). (ii) Merlo's structured/unstructured split: Fine's own view keeps *mere reality* oriented to the present (only the present's facts are the case; all times' facts are *really* the case), so it is **structured** and must deny Factivity (Rp → p). Lipman, Loss, Simon and Iaquinto–Torrengo are **unstructured**: every fragment's facts obtain simpliciter, so "what is the case" is itself incoherent. The Map's pages have so far used "Fine's fragmentalism" for the unstructured picture — "reality has no complete, jointly consistent description" (vertiginous-question L107) — which is Lipman's view, not quite Fine's.
- **Subject case**: First-Personal Realism + First-Personal Neutrality + Absolutism, Coherence dropped: my first-personal facts and yours all absolutely obtain and never co-obtain. Fine's §12 is explicit that he does not endorse first-personal realism (p. 311) but argues conditionally that, if one is a first-personal realist, fragmentalism beats relativism (p. 314). Lipman's thesis ch. 4 develops the unstructured subject version: the "neon arrow" of subjecthood points at ML in one fragment and at TN in another, and "in the meshed-together picture no one is singled out".
- **Quadrilemma exit**: drops **non-fragmentation**, keeps the other three. List names this horn with Fine and (in §5) Lipman 2023. For the *unstructured* version the exit is clean. For the *structured* version see (d) below.
- **Costs**: List: a fragmented world "would require a significant revision of the way we think about worlds in philosophy, logic, and scientific modelling". Merlo: unstructured fragmentalism explodes belief (every subject's first-personal facts obtain, so one should believe all of them); structured fragmentalism needs non-factive reality. Lipman's own 2023 framework answers the explosion with a logic of *standpoint shifts* whose conclusions "cannot be aggregated into a single conjunction" — the belief-side analogue of co-obtainment — which is also, in effect, Loss's reply (abstract only here).

### (b) External relativism (Fine 2005 §§7–11; Lipman's "metaphysical relativism")

- **Core claim**: Realism + Neutrality + Coherence, Absolutism dropped. Facts belong to reality only *relative to* a standpoint; there are "multiple realities, each relative to a different moment in time" (Lipman's gloss of Fine p. 280), and in the subject case "each subjective reality [is] given relative to a" subject (Fine p. 314, snippet truncated). Fine distinguishes it from *internal* relativism, on which "there is a single reality, which does not vary over time, but in saying how this reality is we must say how things are at each time" (p. 280) — internal relativism is the B-theorist's view, not a form of tense realism.
- **Why Fine and Lipman reject it**: Fine: fragmentalism "avoids this difficulty. Reality is simply a certain way ... there exists no external form of relativity" (p. 310), and if times are not basic constituents of reality that "should apply across the board ... also in the formulation of the reality claims themselves" (p. 310, per Lipman). Lipman: Moore's instability — either "the relativization is idle" (relative obtaining just is obtaining, and incompatible facts obtain simpliciter after all) or the relativised fact fails to capture how things appear.
- **Quadrilemma exit**: not a horn as such. Three readings, by what "obtaining relative to a subject" amounts to: (1) if relative obtaining is **non-factive** — @s(p) does not yield p — then the facts that obtain simpliciter are of List's form (1*)/(2*), "there is a perspective from which it is true to say 'I am in state X'", which List argues are third-personal; this is the **deflationist** exit in a relativist idiom, and List's compossibility reply (pp. 9–10) is addressed to exactly it. (2) If relative obtaining is **factive**, the relativism is unstable in Lipman's sense and becomes fragmentalism: Lipman 2023 shows that factivity plus incompatible standpoints entails that "coherent conceptions can only be incomplete", which is fn 18's "disunified world ... enough to count as a form of fragmentalism". (3) If "multiple realities" are taken at face value — each subject's reality a complete, coherent, centred world — the view is List's **many-worlds** theory of consciousness, which drops **one world**. Fine's observation that the fragmentalist reads the relativist's standpoints as fragments "rather than the other way round" (p. 281) is the formal statement that (2) and (3) are the same facts described from different directions. *This three-way sorting is this note's analysis, assembled from Fine, Lipman and List; no source states it as such.*
- **For the Map**: the subjective-facts article says "the Map's corpus names only the fragmentalist route; it should know the relativist one exists". It should also know the relativist route is unstable on its proponents' own account and resolves, under pressure, into one of the three horns the Map already prices.

### (c) Lipman's standpoint pluralism and appearance-restricted factivity (Lipman 2023 *Ergo* §7; Lipman 2023 *J. Phil.*; Lipman 2026)

- **Core claim** (*J. Phil.*): the world contains standpoints relative to which different facts obtain; a factive operator @s(p); a deflationary notion of adopting a standpoint; two validities, classical (within a standpoint) and external (standpoint shifts), with the rule that shift-conclusions cannot be conjoined. This is unstructured fragmentalism regimented as a logic. The monograph generalises it to "multiple stacks of facts instead of one single stack" (2026 abstract).
- **The appearance-restricted option** (*Ergo* §7, as recorded in the 2026-10-06 note): restrict factivity to appearance facts, so that "the world is the totality of objective facts with, in addition, each appearance fact that obtains relative to some subject or other". Lipman presents this as the coherent, non-fragmentalist egalitarian route; he takes the unrestricted one himself.
- **Quadrilemma exit**: this is the subtle case, and the thesis makes it legible. Appearance facts are subject-less ("it appears that (the sky is blue)", with a counterfactual dependence on ML's state); appearance facts from different subjects have different contents and are compossible. What is *not* an appearance fact is the fact of which appearance-set is lived — the neon arrow. Restricting factivity to appearance facts therefore keeps **non-fragmentation**, **non-solipsism** and **one world** and drops **first-person realism in List's sense**: there is no fact simpliciter that *this* standpoint is oneself, only @s(s is oneself) for each s, and that is a (1*)-type fact. It is realism about phenomenal facts combined with deflation about the indexical fact. Lipman's thesis treats the arrow as the thing that fragments, which is why his own route is unrestricted factivity and fragmentation.
- **The specialness problem, Lipman's version**: wide-scope relativity — @t1[t1 is the only factual time] — lets every standpoint be privileged relative to itself. This is the same move as Merlo's "which point of view gets to be privileged is, itself, a subjective matter" (2016: 324, via List) but with the privilege distributed to every standpoint instead of claimed for one. The 2026-10-06 note's record of Builes fn 46 and Merlo fn 19 against it stands.
- **For the Map**: appearance-restricted factivity is the view the Map's "the 'I' is a void, the phenomenal facts are real" posture would become if the void were read as *no fact there*. The Map reads it as *a fact we cannot cross*, which keeps it in first-person realism and so in the quadrilemma. The distinction between those two readings is the distinction between exits (c) and (d).

### (d) Privileged-fragment fragmentalism (the Map's "collapses back towards Hare" option)

- **Does it exist in the literature?** Not under that name. The structure exists exactly, as Merlo's **structured** fragmentalism carried from times to subjects: all subjects' first-personal facts are *really* the case (metaphysical reality is neutral and fragmented); only mine are *the* case (mere reality is oriented to one standpoint). Merlo 2022 fn 1 says the whole analysis applies "mutatis mutandis" to "'first-personal' fragmentalism (as prefigured in Fine (2005: § 12))", and Merlo 2016 fn 19 (2026-10-06 note) names "a non-factive conception of the fundamental (Merlo 2013)" as his preferred way to reconcile parity at the fundamental level with a totality of facts "oriented towards one point of view". Fine's own fragmentalism is of this kind for time (Merlo, citing Fine 2005: 297 and Fine 2020: 407 fn 3). So the "privileged fragment" is privileged in *mere* reality only.
- **Relation to Hare**: Hare's egocentric presentism denies that others' experiences bear presence at all; there is nothing at any level on which parity holds except functional sentience. Structured first-personal fragmentalism grants full parity at the level of what is really the case and privileges one subject only at the level of what is the case. It is Merlo-shaped, not Hare-shaped. "Collapses back towards Hare" (egocentric-presentism L81) is right about mere reality and wrong about metaphysical reality.
- **Quadrilemma exit**: at the level of metaphysical reality it drops **non-fragmentation** (R(I am in X) and R(I am in Y) for distinct subjects). At the level of mere reality all four claims hold *only* if "non-solipsism" is read as "more than one subject is *really* real", since only one subject's first-personal facts are the case. List's quadrilemma does not distinguish the two levels; the structured fragmentalist's answer to it is that the quadrilemma is sound for mere reality (where she is, in the relevant sense, a one-subject theorist) and sound for metaphysical reality (where she is a fragmentalist), and that the two verdicts are compatible because R is not factive.
- **Costs**: non-factivity, which both Lipman ("we lose a natural understanding of what reality consists in") and Loss ("misguided, to say the least") reject; Merlo's own concession that the subjective/objective "fine line" may not be "coherently sustained" (2021, per the 2026-10-06 note); and the Builes/Merlo specialness objection now points the other way — the view *keeps* "I am special simpliciter" (in mere reality) and so inherits Fine's "metaphysical chauvinism" charge (p. 302) for that level, answered only by Merlo's "subjectively so".
- **Benefits**: no belief explosion (one should believe only what is the case, and that is one subject's facts); a fact simpliciter about which subject is lived, which is what the vertiginous question and P-I2 need; one world; and a principled description of other minds as really-the-case rather than fictional (contrast List's reading of Hare as "fictionalism about the subject worlds of others").

### Simon's quantum fragmentalism and the one-world question

Simon identifies superposition terms with states of affairs in different fragments: spin-up obtains, spin-down obtains, they do not and cannot co-obtain; "a branch is a fragment". He stresses that his fragments can be logically coherent with one another (the incompatibility is physical, not logical), so the view sits near Calosi–Iaquinto–Loss's "coherent unitism" boundary. Merlo files it as unstructured ("smooth fragmentalism"), which means every branch's facts obtain simpliciter — a one-world Everett. Iaquinto and Calosi's objection is physical (phase relations are lost: |ψ⟩ = c₁|↑⟩ + c₂|↓⟩ and |ψ⁻⟩ = c₁|↑⟩ − c₂|↓⟩ have the same terms) and their diagnosis is that any fix makes the view "Everettian Quantum Mechanics in disguise" in its decoherence-only variant.

For the Map the point is structural, not physical. The device by which the Map keeps all subjects' first-personal facts real in one world — obtaining without co-obtaining — is available, term for term, to keep all outcomes real in one world. The Map is fragmentalist about subjects and unitist about outcomes. Nothing in fragmentalism itself licenses that asymmetry; it has to come from the tenets. And the indexical objection does not supply it against a *structured* branch-fragmentalism: a view on which every outcome is really the case but exactly one is the case has a fact simpliciter about which branch is actual, so "why am I this branch?" has an answer there of exactly the kind the Map gives for "why am I this subject?". Tenet 4's resources against that view are its non-indexical arguments (the reach limits recorded at P-I5), plus whatever Tenets 2–3 add: on the Map outcomes are *selected* by a causally efficacious interaction, which is a reason for there to be a fact of the matter about the branch that is not available for subjects, who are not selected by anything.

## Implications for the Map: What Moves

1. **Tenet 4 and `apex/one-world-wager` L48.** The sentence "The Map's commitments pick first-person realism (Tenet 1), non-solipsism, and one world (Tenet 4), and so accept fragmentation: not every fact about conscious experience integrates into a single coherent world-description" is correct but under-specified in two ways the literature now forces. (i) Which fragmentalism: the Map's posit — parity of consciousness across subjects plus a fact simpliciter that *this* subject is the lived one — is *structured* first-personal fragmentalism (Merlo 2022's taxonomy; Fine's own kind), not the unstructured kind the phrase "no single coherent world-description" evokes. Its named cost is a non-factive notion of what is really the case. (ii) The fragmentalist device is available to the Everettian (Simon 2018), so "one world" at L48 means unitism about *outcomes*, and the apex should say that the asymmetry between subjects (fragmented) and outcomes (not) is argued from Tenets 2–3, not from fragmentalism. Suggested replacement for the clause after "accept fragmentation": "— in the structured form: every subject's first-personal facts are really the case, one subject's are the case. ## Tenet Assessment

- **Dualism (Tenet 1)**: neutral. Fine, Lipman, Merlo and List all say first-person realism cuts across the physicalism/dualism line; List says standard dualism *rejects* first-person realism by keeping non-fragmentation and one world. The Map's dualism is therefore not standard in List's sense, and the fragmentalism page should say so rather than let readers infer that dualism delivers the first-person fact.
- **Minimal Quantum Interaction / Bidirectional Interaction (Tenets 2–3)**: these are where the subject/outcome asymmetry lives. Outcomes are selected; subjects are not. That is the Map's only non-question-begging reason to be unitist about branches while fragmentalist about subjects, and it should be stated as such. Iaquinto and Calosi's point that fragmentalism cannot by itself say whether branches *cannot* interact (metaphysical vs nomological impossibility) is a reminder that the Map's selection story, not fragmentalism, carries that weight.
- **No Many Worlds (Tenet 4)**: this is where the tenet pays. The honest statement: the Map keeps one world by fragmenting it (structured), and the price is a non-factive notion of what is really the case. The alternatives on the table are solipsism-at-the-fundamental-level (Hare), unstructured fragmentation with belief explosion (Lipman, Loss), many centred worlds (List), and deflation (Lewis, Perry, appearance-restricted Lipman). Simon shows the one-world/many-worlds line is not where the Map's pages have drawn it: a fragmentalist can be a one-worlder about branches.
- **Occam's Razor Has Limits (Tenet 5)**: directly engaged. Each exit is extravagant in a different currency (worlds, fragments, non-factivity, a privileged subject, or a denied datum), and the quadrilemma makes the choice forced rather than optional. Tenet 5 says simplicity cannot rank them; that is the right thing to say, and it also means the Map cannot use parsimony *against* List's many worlds while itself buying non-factivity.
