## Summary (verdict: FOLD-IN)

Sibley's thesis is real, famous, and exactly as the reviewer described it; it also lends the Map's tenets nothing, and Sibley says so himself in the paper. "Aesthetic Concepts" (1959) argues that no description in non-aesthetic terms is ever logically sufficient for applying an aesthetic term — "graceful", "delicate", "garish" — while granting that aesthetic qualities "always ultimately depend upon" non-aesthetic features and that the concepts are governed *negatively* by conditions (a canvas of pale blue bars cannot be garish). On the page where he anticipates the natural puzzle this raises, Sibley answers it by comparing aesthetic concepts with colour words, which are equally rule-free, and then draws two differences — normally sighted people can miss aesthetic qualities, and we support aesthetic judgments by talking — neither of which is a difference in metaphysics. The claim is cast at the level of concepts and their criteria of application (Sibley's footnote restricts it to *uses* of words; Zangwill's SEP entry notes he "never described the dependence in modal terms"), and the modern consensus, stated plainly by Zangwill, is that this kind of anomalousness coexists with aesthetic properties supervening on non-aesthetic ones. A physicalist can accept every word of Sibley.

That settles the calibration question the seed built in: aesthetics adds nothing here beyond the dependence-without-entailment pattern the physicalist already accommodates for colour and for morals. Worse for a putative article, Sibley's point is orthogonal to the hub's L118 rather than its ancestor. L118 argues that where structure is fully mapped (the 4:5:6 triad) the question *why that structure is experienced as beautiful* remains — the structure-experience gap, which the hub already identifies as the explanatory gap in aesthetic dress. Sibley's point is that structural descriptions do not *license aesthetic predicates*; it would hold in a world of zombie critics whose vocabulary had the same logic, so it cannot bear on Tenet 1. The triad is in fact one of Sibley's own conceded borderline cases, where a non-aesthetic description ("reds and yellows", "bright colors") does carry one to an aesthetic term.

An article would therefore be a history-of-analytic-aesthetics piece (Sibley 1959/1965, Cohen 1973, Kivy 1973/1975, Levinson 1984, Zangwill 2001, Watkins 2020, Sedivy 2025) whose honest lede is "this record is neutral between the Map and its opponents", in a section with 22 slots left (348/370 measured 2026-10-15 with `tools.evolution.state.count_section_files`). The reviewer's cap-free alternative is the right call. The two sentences the hub should take are given under [Potential Article Angles](#potential-article-angles); their job is prophylactic — to stop a future editor or reader citing Sibley as support for L118.

## The Three Questions

### (1) Concepts, perception, or properties — and which touches Tenet 1?

**Concepts, with a perceptual gloss; neither touches Tenet 1.** The thesis is stated as a claim about *terms* and *concepts* and their *conditions of application* (quotations 2–5); Sibley's own footnote 1 restricts it to aesthetic *uses* of words, a restriction Zangwill notes "he and everyone else ignored". The perceptual strand is the account of *taste* as the capacity whose exercise the concepts require (quotation 1) and the "we just see" comparison (6). Sibley makes no claim about the metaphysics of aesthetic properties beyond affirming their dependence on non-aesthetic features, and he never puts that dependence in modal terms (13). Three readings, then:

- *Conceptual reading* (the one the text supports): there is no a priori or criterial route from non-aesthetic predicates to aesthetic ones. This is silent on what aesthetic properties are. It is also, as Sibley concedes, shared with colour concepts (6), and as Shelley adds, with moral concepts (12). A physicalist who holds that aesthetic properties supervene on physical ones with no analytic bridge principles accepts this reading without strain — it is just the "no a priori entailment" that Levine's and Chalmers's physicalist opponents (type-B) already grant for consciousness, and that nobody disputes for morals.
- *Perceptual reading*: taste is a discriminative capacity that normally-sighted people may lack (7). This is a claim about cognitive variation and training (Ransom 2022 treats exactly this as "the puzzle of training"), fully naturalisable. Its first-person strand — one has to see the quality oneself — is the acquaintance intuition, already carried by `topics/aesthetic-testimony-and-the-acquaintance-principle`, where the Map has already calibrated it as corroborating pressure that does not entail dualism.
- *Property reading* (not Sibley's, but the one an article would need): aesthetic properties are not identical with, and not entailed by, physical properties. Levinson's supervenience (18) and Zangwill's "anomalous but supervenient" (14) are the standard articulations, and both are explicitly physicalist-compatible. The 2020s revisions (Watkins's realization relations, Sedivy's particularism, 19–20) move *away* from a clean aesthetic/non-aesthetic base, which is Cohen's objection winning, not a gap opening.

Only a property reading could touch Tenet 1, and the property reading is not in Sibley; where the literature supplies it, it supplies it in a form that coexists with physicalism.

### (2) Does the colour parallel collapse L118 into supervenience-without-entailment?

**It collapses *Sibley's* gap, not L118's — because the two were never the same gap.** The colour parallel (6, 12) shows that non-condition-governedness is a general feature of observational and thick concepts; Zangwill's remark that anomalousness and supervenience coexist (14) shows that the pattern needs no non-physical property. If the hub's L118 were an instance of Sibley's pattern it would collapse exactly as the seed feared.

But L118 does not claim that the 4:5:6 ratio fails to license "consonant" or "pleasing". It claims that with the structure fully specified the question of why that structure is *experienced as* beautiful stands untouched — the structure-experience gap, which the hub's convergence section already names as "the explanatory gap in aesthetic dress, not a new line of evidence". Sibley's point concerns the logic of aesthetic vocabulary and would survive in a community of zombie critics whose words had the same grammar; L118's question would not arise for them. The two gaps come apart in both directions: the triad is a case where Sibley's sufficiency *nearly holds* (his footnote 6 concedes that "bright colors" takes one straight to "gay") yet L118's question is at its sharpest; conversely Sibley's gap is widest for "graceful" and "delicate" whether or not any phenomenal question is in view.

Zangwill's colour remark (15) adds a twist worth recording: at the level of *thought*, aesthetic concepts are *less* like colour concepts than the parallel suggests, because aesthetic thought presupposes dependence on a base while colour thought does not. That asymmetry runs against a dualist use of Sibley, since presupposed dependence is the physicalist's home ground.

### (3) What would an article say that L118 does not?

Honest content: Sibley's thesis, its conceded colour parallel and negative conditions; Hart's defeasible concepts as the foil; Cohen's and Kivy's objections (the distinction cannot be drawn; elegance looks condition-governed; anyone can tell a graceful line); Levinson's recasting as supervenience; Zangwill's anomalousness explained by subjectivity; Watkins and Sedivy dropping supervenience for realization and particularism; and a Relation-to-Site-Perspective section concluding that none of it moves Tenet 1 because the whole dispute is conducted at the level of concepts and criteria. That is a competent survey whose lede must say the record lends the Map nothing. The hub needs only the warning, not the survey.
