# Research: Agent Causation and Acting for Reasons

**Date**: 2026-10-20
**Origin**: P2 `research-topic` task harvested from `reviews/optimistic-2026-10-20-relocation-wing.md` (Expansion, High).
**Search queries used**: SEP "Incompatibilist (Nondeterministic) Theories of Free Will" (curled raw); SEP "Free Will" (curled raw); Crossref and OpenAlex lookups for O'Connor 2000, Clarke 1993/1996/2003/2005/2011/2019, Lowe 2008, Steward 2012, Feldman & Buckareff 2003, O'Connor & Churchill 2004, Martinez 2024, Baker 2017, Runyan 2018, Taggart 2021; NDPR reviews of Clarke 2003 (Carlsson) and Lowe 2008 (Griffith), curled raw; Fischer's *Mind* review and Buckareff's *Auslegung* review of O'Connor 2000 (PDF, pdftotext); Matheson's EJPR review of Mawson 2011 (PDF); Google Books search-within on O'Connor 2000 and Clarke 2003; web searches on O'Connor–Churchill, Clarke 2019, and the wild-coincidence replies.

## Executive Summary

The subject is uncovered and article-sized. The Map says throughout its agency cluster that the agent-cause selects *for* reasons, or that reasons *guide* the selection, but no page says how reasons explain an agent-caused choice. The literature has a precise question here and three worked answers, all verified at primary or publisher level below. (1) **Non-causal**: O'Connor (2000, ch. 5) and Chisholm deny that reasons cause the choice. The reason is identified by the content of the intention the agent causes. O'Connor adds that reasons causally structure the agent's propensities. (2) **Integrated**: on Clarke's view (1993, 1996, 2003 ch. 8) the choice has two necessary causes, the agent and the reason-events, and the reason-events cause it nondeterministically. (3) **Uncaused volition**: on Lowe's view (2008) volitions are wholly uncaused. The operative reason is "the reason which the agent chose to act upon". The Map currently holds pieces of all three without choosing. `agent-teleology` says the reason "causally contributes", which is integrated. P-A2 grounds authorship "in Chisholm's and Lowe's sense", and both deny that reasons cause the choice. `philosophy-of-action-under-dualism` adopts a hybrid "close to Lowe" next to a Davidsonian "causal skeleton". The reviewer's expected conditional mostly holds but needs three refinements (see [The Reviewer's Conditional, Assessed](#the-reviewers-conditional-assessed) below). The most consequential finding is structural. The Map's default Born-preserving selection already has the shape of Clarke's integrated view. That helps against Pereboom's wild-coincidence objection, which the Map currently carries as an undischarged cost. It also imports two objections to the integrated view. The first is that it adds no control over the necessary event-cause (Carlsson 2004). The second is that the contrastive work in the Map's luck reply would then be done by the same event-causal resources event-causal libertarianism already has.

## Answers to the Task Questions

### Q1. O'Connor 2000, ch. 5: his account, and Davidson's "one operative reason" problem: VERIFIED (abstract, two reviews, SEP, snippets); full chapter UNREAD

- Chapter 5 is "Reasons and Causes", pp. 85–107 (Crossref DOI 10.1093/019515374x.003.0005).
- Publisher abstract (Crossref, raw): "Contrary to the causal theory of action, there can be an internal, non‐contrastive explanatory link between a prior motivational state and the intention‐in‐action that the agent causes. Davidson's criticisms of noncausal accounts of reasons explanations are considered and rebutted here when directed against the present account. The account is then enriched with a causal notion of 'structured propensities' to accommodate the fact that some actions, though undetermined, are more likely than others to be performed." (The raw text uses U+2010 hyphens in "non‐contrastive" and "intention‐in‐action", and curly quotes around 'structured propensities'.)
- **The structure** (SEP §3.1, raw): "agents do not cause free decisions; rather, a free decision is a causally complex event, consisting of the agent's causing her coming to have a certain intention." A decision to A is explained by an antecedent desire if (a) the agent had the desire and believed A-ing would satisfy it, and (b) "the intention to A that the agent comes to have in making the decision is caused by the agent and is an intention to A here and now in order to satisfy the desire in question (adapted from O'Connor 2000b: 86)". The wording is the SEP's adaptation, not O'Connor's own. Fischer quotes O'Connor's form of the agent-caused event: "the-coming-to-be-of-an-action-triggering-intention-to-so-act-here-and-now..." (p. 94).
- **On Davidson.** O'Connor's answer to the one-operative-reason problem is that the operative reason is the one the agent-caused intention's content refers to ("in order to satisfy" *that* desire), not the one that caused the choice. Google Books snippet, p. 88: "Davidson's arguments that reasons can explain an action only if they directly produce it are, therefore, unsound. It deserves emphasis that my response to Davidson depends on the thesis that an agent causally initiates his action … and that agent causation is conceptually tied to the agent's having a reason for acting." (The raw snippet hyphenates "Da- vidson".) The reply is explicitly limited to agent-caused actions. It does not rehabilitate Ginet-style non-causalism in general.
- **Reasons do have a causal role, of a restricted kind.** Fischer (*Mind* 2001, raw): "O'Connor believes that reasons do not cause actions, but they are 'structuring conditions on purposive action' (p. 95)", and they "give agents a certain objective, but indeterministic propensity to generate a certain sort of intention in given situations (p. 97)". (pdftotext collapsed some spaces in this line, e.g. "objective,but indeterministicpropensityto generate".) Snippet, p. xiv: "tendency to act in particular ways, which tendency probabilistically structures the basic agent - causal capacity". Snippet, p. 98: "In the end, the agent himself determines the outcome".
- **Contrastivity.** Snippet, p. xiv: "I defend the position that such explanations need not be contrastive in character". This matters for the Map (see Side-Finding 5).
- **Reviewers' readings.** Buckareff (*Auslegung* 26(1), 2003, raw): "In chapter five, O'Connor argues for a non-causal theory of acting for reasons"; "The role of reasons in explaining actions is best regarded as teleological on O'Connor's account" (the raw text has a page break inside this sentence); "The reasons direct action, but they do not cause the action." Buckareff's gloss that the desire "plays no causal role" undersells the structured-propensity layer, which the publisher abstract calls "a causal notion". The article should report both.
- **Critiques** (SEP §3.1, raw): (i) Feldman and Buckareff (2003) argue that the second-order content requirement is not necessary, since one can decide on the basis of a desire without the intention representing that desire. (ii) If the intention is made to represent the desire's *object*, the account collapses into McCann's, and that version fails for decisions made "for many reasons". (iii) The causally-complex-event objection, with the lightning/dry-brush analogy: the explanation's truth would hinge on a feature of the effect without the cited state causing anything. The SEP diagnoses the source as "O'Connor's requirement, like that imposed by most proponents of agent-causal views, that only the agent cause what is directly agent-caused in a free action." Clarke 2003 ends his discussion (snippet, p. 143): "In sum, it does not appear that O'Connor has provided …".
- **O'Connor's later direction: LEAD.** O'Connor and Churchill, "Reasons Explanation and Agent Control: In Search of an Integrated Account", *Philosophical Topics* 32(1) (2004): 241–253, doi 10.5840/philtopics2004321/21 (Crossref title only; PDC and PhilPapers blocked; no abstract obtained). The subtitle suggests O'Connor himself moved toward integration after 2000. Content unread. Do not characterise it beyond its title.

### Q2. Clarke 1996 and 2003 ch. 8: the integrated view, and Clarke's own verdict: VERIFIED (publisher abstracts, NDPR review, snippets); Clarke 1996 UNREAD

- **The view.** Clarke 2003 ch. 8 is "An Integrated Agent‐Causal Account", pp. 133–150 (doi 10.1093/019515987x.003.0008). Abstract (Crossref, raw): O'Connor's view "does not require, in any case of acting freely, that events such as the agent's having certain reasons cause the event that the agent is required to directly cause; the view consequently fails to provide for the reason-explanation of free actions. An alternative agent-causal account is advanced, one that integrates agent causation (as a requirement for free action) with an event-causal theory of action."
- Carlsson (NDPR 2004.10.10, raw): "On this account, a free action has a nondeterministic conjunctive cause, one conjunct of which is the agent herself, and the other is her having certain reasons, etc. Each conjunct is a necessary, but not (even in a nondeterministic sense) sufficient cause of the action." He quotes Clarke p. 24: reason-explanation requires "a certain sort of causal connection between the having of reasons and the action".
- Snippet, p. 145: "impossible, when an agent-causal power is exercised, for the agent alone to cause an event. Causation by appropriate agent-involving events, far from being precluded, will be required in every instance of agent causation." SEP §3.1 (raw) gives the nomological version: whichever decision is made, it is caused by the favouring reasons "only if Leo—the agent—causes that decision".
- **Clarke's own verdict, from his text, not the SEP.**
  1. Ch. 9 abstract (raw): "Assuming that all it requires is possible, an integrated agent-causal view succeeds at this goal and adequately characterizes free will."
  2. Ch. 10 ("Substance and Cause") abstract (raw): "it is concluded that there are, on balance, good reasons to reject this possibility."
  3. Book abstract (raw): "if both free will and moral responsibility are incompatible with determinism, then there is no adequate account of free will."
  4. Snippet, p. 221 (Conclusion): "If that is so, then, given broad incompatibilism, it appears that free will is impossible." (Snippet not re-verified in the 2026-10-20 deep review: the search-within endpoint was rate-limited and its control query also returned 0. The article does not quote it.)
  5. Carlsson (raw): "Clarke finds good, although not decisive, reasons to reject the possibility of such causation. He concludes that the possibility of free will is doubtful, given broad incompatibilism."
  6. Clarke 2011, "Alternatives for Libertarians" (abstract, raw): "concludes on a skeptical note about the viability of libertarian accounts of free will generally."

  So Clarke built the integrated view and holds it to be the only adequate libertarian account under broad incompatibilism. He also holds that the substance causation it needs is probably impossible. **He is not an ally.**
- **Coiner and voice warning.** The SEP incompatibilism entry is by Clarke, Capes and Swenson (2021 revision). Its §3.1 description of the integrated view ("it is said") is Clarke's own voice and does not confirm him independently. The SEP lists "Clarke (1993 and 1996)" among recent agent-causal *advocates* and leaves 2003 out, which is consistent with his later scepticism.
- **1993 versus 1996: LEAD, snippet only.** O'Connor 2000 pp. 76–79 critiques an earlier Clarke version. Snippet, p. 77: "The agent figures into the picture by causing, not the action simpliciter, but the action's being …". Snippet, p. 78: "The agent's causal capacity consists in the ability to make effective an indeterministic propensity of one's reason to bring about A". This reads as a 1993-style view, on which the agent causes the reasons' causing, as distinct from the 1996/2003 conjunctive-cause view. Clarke 1996 (*Philosophical Topics* 24(2): 19–48, doi 10.5840/philtopics19962427) was not obtained. The SEP cites "O'Connor 2000b: 76–70", a typo; the snippets put the discussion at pp. 76–78.

### Q3. Lowe 2008: VERIFIED; the "close to Lowe" hybrid and the causal skeleton pull apart, but only at the upstream link

- Griffith (NDPR 2009.06.15, raw):
  - "Like some noncausalists, Lowe posits uncaused volitions and denies that reasons are causes."
  - "Lowe distances himself from agent causalism by asserting that agents do not cause actions. Instead, free actions are completely uncaused acts of will. Because all agents cause by acting, they cannot cause their own volitions on pain of regress (7)."
  - "As uncaused acts, volitions are not even partly caused by reasons."
- **Lowe's answer to Davidson's problem** (Griffith, raw): "Lowe responds that 'the reason for which the agent acted is the reason which the agent chose to act upon' (183)." This is a third non-causal answer, alongside O'Connor's and McCann's.
- **Reasons externalism.** Book abstract (Crossref, raw): reasons for action are "mind-independent worldly entities rather than the beliefs and desires of agents". Ch. 10 abstract: reasons for action are "needs rather than facts", and they differ fundamentally from reasons for belief. Ch. 7 abstract: the view "falls midway between volitionism and classical agent causalism". (Chapter numbers corrected 2026-10-20: the Introduction, pp. 1–16, is unnumbered, so Crossref's DOI suffix `.003.000N` is ch. N−1. Griffith's "Chapter 3" and "Chapter 7 … (151-154)" and the SEP's "chs. 6–9" for the action theory all confirm this.)
- **No objective probabilities for free choices** (Griffith, raw): "With chance events we can assign objective probabilities. We cannot do so with free choices (190-195)."
- **What pulls apart, precisely.**
  - Lowe keeps *downstream* mental causation. Agents cause action-results by willing, and ch. 1's abstract calls mental causation "distinctively intentional causation". So `philosophy-of-action-under-dualism` L89's minimum ("some version of 'mental states cause movements' must be true") is compatible with Lowe.
  - Lowe denies the *upstream* link, reasons → choice, which is the half of Davidson's skeleton that L43 introduces through the one-operative-reason argument. L89 also calls the standard story "structurally welcome—so far as its causal claim goes", and that causal claim is upstream: reasons cause actions.
  - L95's hybrid ("The agent-substance originates … what she originates is a volition") contradicts Lowe outright, since on Lowe nothing originates the volition.
  - Lowe's denial of objective probabilities for free choices also sits badly with the Map's default Born-preserving selection (P-Q2), which gives choices Born probabilities.
- The queued P2 owns the edits to L77 and `concepts/quantum-indeterminacy-free-will` L91. **No page was edited here.**

### Q5. Does the integrated view reopen the contrastive luck question? PARTLY, and it changes the question's shape (sources verified; synthesis is this note's)

- **The generic objection is unchanged.** SEP §3.2 (raw) presses luck against *all* agent-causal views, giving the Leo case and the duplicate-world argument (Haji 2004; Mele 2005, 2006; van Inwagen 1983, 2000). Integration neither creates this objection nor answers it. **The luck P3 owns that material**; do not duplicate it.
- **Integration-specific pressures.**
  1. **No extra control over the event-cause** (Carlsson, raw): "his integrated account does not provide for any more control over the occurrence of the action-causing events, than does an event-causal account."
  2. **Only partial reason-explanation** (Carlsson, raw): "If the agent's having certain reasons is only a necessary, but not a sufficient immediate cause of her action, it seems doubtful that a reason-explanation of the action can be given. The most we can say is that the agent acted partly for reasons."
  3. **Competing causes** (SEP Free Will, raw): "Given that these two causes seem distinct, is it not possible that the agent cause his decision to φ and yet the agent's reasons simultaneously cause an incompatible decision to ψ?" (The raw text writes φ and ψ in LaTeX.)
  4. **O'Connor's objection to Clarke's earlier version** (snippet, p. 78; LEAD for its exact target): "if C indeterministically causes the agent - causal event , then while the agent - causal event is not entirely random …". If reasons cause the agent's causing, the causal indeterminist's problem returns one level up.
  5. **Ginet 2002** objects to the combination even while "bracketing his rejection of agent causation" (Clarke 2003 snippet, p. 147, citing Ginet 2002: 398–99). Clarke's p. 144 snippet discusses a "bolstering" reading, quoting Ginet's springs analogy ("Think of R1 and R2 as like springs"), and separates the integrated view from a picture on which the agent shifts the prior probabilities of the alternatives ("This is not what is proposed on an integrated agent- causal view").
- **What integration buys.**
  1. Reason-explanation of the event-causal kind. Clarke's ch. 3 abstract: "On two plausible accounts of contrastive explanation, even contrastive rational explanations are available for some nondeterministically caused actions."
  2. An answer to Pereboom's wild coincidences, since frequencies follow the reason-events' propensities. Baker 2017 (*Synthese* 194(8): 3085–3100, doi 10.1007/s11229-016-1094-0) is reported to argue that the objection fails against "reconciliatory integrationist" agent-causalism. That term is Baker's, and it is a **LEAD**: the abstract came only through a search summary, and Springer and PhilPapers were blocked.
- **This note's synthesis, for the article writer.** Integration relocates the contrastive question rather than reopening it. "Why A rather than B?" gets an event-causal contrastive answer, from reasons with propensities. That answer is the same resource event-causal libertarianism uses, and Clarke 2003 ch. 5 uses it to defend ECL against luck (abstract: "nondeterministic causation of an action by its immediate causal antecedents need not diminish at all the type of control relevant to free action"). What the agent-conjunct adds is then a claim about *control and origination* (ch. 9: "greater control than can be secured by any event-causal libertarian account"; p. 220: "a further power to causally influence which of the open alternatives will be made actual"), not about contrastive explicability. Carlsson's point 1 bites exactly there.
- **Handoff to the luck P3** (verified abstracts, for its Q3):
  - Clarke 2003 ch. 9 abstract: "Responses are offered to Peter van Inwagen's challenge to agent-causal views".
  - Clarke 2005, "Agent Causation and the Problem of Luck", *PPQ* 86(3): 408–421, doi 10.1111/j.1468-0114.2005.00234.x (OpenAlex abstract): "A view of this type is defended against a recent and challenging version of the argument from luck."
  - O'Connor 2009a ("Agent-Causal Power", doi 10.1093/oso/9780199558933.003.0007) and O'Connor 2016 ("Probability and Freedom: A Reply to Vicens", *Res Philosophica* 93(1): 289–293, doi 10.11612/resphil.2016.93.1.13) concern agent causation and probability (SEP Free Will raw, on O'Connor 2009a challenging "the more general assumption that freedom requires that agent-causal choices not be governed by statistical laws of any kind").

## The Reviewer's Conditional, Assessed

The reviewer guessed: *on the efficacy-of-evidence premise, the Map's agency cluster is committed to an integrated agent-causal account, or owes a non-causal reading of "for".* That holds in substance. Three refinements:

1. **The premise is theoretical, not practical.** `acquaintance-without-causation` L102 states it for "a downstream belief, inference or assertion" held or made *from* evidence. Extending it to choices made *for* reasons is a parity step that page never takes. A non-causal "for" next to a causal "from" can also be made principled: Lowe argues that reasons for action and reasons for belief differ in kind (ch. 10 abstract). The same commitment arrives independently, though, through the tenet text itself: Bidirectional Interaction has consciousness biasing quantum indeterminacy "toward patterns that express its content" (`tenets` L91, quoted at `acquaintance-without-causation` L108 as what makes the Map's interactionism content-efficacious). If recognised reasons are part of that content, their content is efficacious in the selection. The antecedent of the conditional should therefore be the content clause, with the efficacy premise as a parity reinforcement.
2. **The disjunction has a third option.** Between Clarke (reason-events co-cause the choice) and full non-causalism (Lowe; Ginet; McCann; O'Connor's explanatory link) sits O'Connor's *structured propensity*: reasons causally structure the agent's propensity to cause one intention rather than another without causing the choice. Whether that satisfies "causally contribute to producing it" is the open question the article should answer. Steward's settling-the-residue model is a fourth variant (lead).
3. **The Map's current text already holds incompatible pieces.** See the next section. So the Map does not merely *owe* a choice. It has made several choices, in different places.

**Structural mapping (this note's analysis; not a source claim).** On the Map's default reading, selection preserves Born statistics exactly (`tenets`, Tenet 3 standing note, citing P-Q2). The brain prepares options with physically fixed weights and consciousness settles which becomes actual. If those weights reflect the agent's reasons, this is Clarke's architecture: agent-involving events cause the outcome nondeterministically, and the agent's causing is required for any of them to go through. The article should test this mapping. If it holds, the Map is an integrated agent-causalist by construction. Three things follow. (a) The wild-coincidence reply becomes available. (b) Carlsson's no-extra-control objection becomes the main threat. (c) The Map's case against ECL (P-A2) has to rest on origination and the "further power" (Clarke ch. 9), not on contrastive explicability. If the weights do *not* reflect reasons, and reasons enter only through consciousness's content-directed bias, the view is nearer O'Connor's structured propensity, with the bias as the structuring. It then owes the Feldman–Buckareff and causally-complex-event replies.

## Major Positions

### Causalist standard story (Davidson 1963; Mele 1992)
- **Core claim**: acting for a reason is being caused, in the right way, by one's recognising that reason. The operative reason is the one that caused the act.
- **Relation to tenets**: the Map borrows the downstream causal claim and needs a position on the upstream one. Pure event-causal versions fail P-A2's authorship requirement.

### Non-causal reason-explanation within agent causation (Chisholm; O'Connor 2000)
- **Core claim**: reasons explain an agent-caused intention through its content ("in order to satisfy D") and do not cause it. On O'Connor, reasons structure the agent's propensities.
- **Key arguments**: Davidson's argument fails once the agent causally initiates the act; explanation need not be contrastive.
- **Relation to tenets**: compatible with Dualism and Bidirectional Interaction (the agent still causes the selection). It has to explain how a content-directed bias counts as non-causal with respect to the reasons, and it gives up the Map's contrastive-explicability luck reply.

### Integrated agent-causal account (Clarke 1993, 1996, 2003 ch. 8; with qualifications Mawson 2011, Steward 2012)
- **Core claim**: the free choice has two necessary causes, the agent and the reason-events, and the reason-events cause it nondeterministically.
- **Key arguments**: it gives the event-causal reason-explanation and the agent-causal origination at once.
- **Relation to tenets**: matches the Map's Born-preserving architecture most closely (this note's analysis). Its author thinks it unrealisable, and it faces the no-extra-control and competing-causes objections.

### Uncaused volition with substance causation (Lowe 2008)
- **Core claim**: volitions are uncaused, spontaneous exercises of a rational two-way power "performed in the light of reason". Reasons are worldly items (needs). The operative reason is the one the agent chose to act on.
- **Relation to tenets**: the closest metaphysical cousin of the Map (non-Cartesian substance dualism, an attack on the causal closure argument but not on closure itself), but non-causal on exactly the link this note concerns, and it denies objective probabilities for free choices.

### Non-causal libertarianism (Ginet; McCann; Goetz)
- **Core claim**: concurrent intention content (Ginet), or intention-content that reflects the reason's goals (McCann), fixes the "for".
- **Relation to tenets**: per the Map's own `philosophy-of-action-under-dualism`, a denial of downward efficacy contradicts Tenet 3. Its account of the "for" can still be borrowed.

## Key Debates

### Is causation needed for "acting for a reason"?
- **Sides**: causalists (Davidson, Mele, Clarke) against non-causalists (Ginet, McCann, O'Connor for agent-caused acts, Lowe, Sehon 2005; Schueler 1995, 2003, per SEP Free Will).
- **Core disagreement**: whether intention-content or choice can fix the operative reason without the reason causing anything. The SEP §1.2 frames it as a "clash of intuitions".
- **Current state**: ongoing. Martinez 2024 (lead) surveys causalist options within agent causation.

### Can agent causation and event causation be combined?
- **Sides**: Clarke (yes, conditionally); O'Connor 2000 (critical of Clarke's early version), Ginet 2002 (critical), Carlsson 2004 (critical). O'Connor and Churchill 2004 search for an integrated account (lead).
- **Core disagreement**: whether the agent-conjunct adds control, and whether two causes of one choice can compete.
- **Current state**: open. SEP §3.1: "a matter of some dispute".

### Does agent causation survive the statistics?
- **Sides**: Pereboom (wild coincidences) against Clarke 2010, O'Connor 2009a, Baker 2017, Runyan 2018 and Taggart 2021.
- **Relevance**: the integration question and the statistics question are the same question for the Map, because its selector operates under Born statistics.
