## Executive Summary

Ericsson and Simon (1980; book 1984, rev. 1993) did not claim that people can introspect their mental processes. They claimed that verbal reports are *behaviour with a modelled generating mechanism*. When subjects verbalise information they are already attending to (held in short-term memory, STM), the report is a valid trace of the sequence of thought states. The *analyst* then infers the process from that trace. Reports fail when the instruction asks for information never heeded (reasons, explanations, generalisations, hypotheticals), which forces inference in place of memory.

On this account Nisbett and Wilson's confabulation findings come from their delayed, general and "why" probes, not from verbal report as such. E&S name one point of disagreement, awareness of ongoing processes (p. 245, scan paraphrase). Otherwise they say Nisbett and Wilson's own list of accessible private facts is exactly what the model places in STM.

A 2011 meta-analysis of 94 studies confirms the core prediction: thinking aloud leaves accuracy unchanged (r = −.03), while describe/explain instructions are reactive. Exceptions exist (insight problems, graph comprehension, older adults on Raven's).

For the Map, this is the most precise *third-person, procedural* statement of where the content/process line falls. It is a methods result, neutral between dualism and physicalism. Its scope is heeded, task-directed information, so it is silent on reports of phenomenal character, which is where Schwitzgebel's pessimism bites.

## Key Sources

### Ericsson & Simon (1980), "Verbal reports as data"
- **URL**: https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295x.87.3.215
  - *Psychological Review* 87(3), 215–251 (Crossref).
  - Scan read in full at https://acs.ist.psu.edu/ist597/papers/ericssonS80.pdf. The nd.edu mirror timed out.
- **Key points** (scan pages; paraphrase only):
  - **Reports are data to be modelled.** The authors do not assume a report mirrors process structure or has privileged status as direct observation (p. 217). In their usage "heeded" means attended to, or held in STM (fn. 1).
  - **The Levels** (pp. 219, 227):
    - Level 1 is direct articulation of information already in verbal code.
    - Level 2 is verbal recoding of non-verbal (e.g. visual) information without further processing.
    - Level 3 is articulation after scanning, filtering, inference or generative processes have modified the information. In 1980 Level 3 includes *filtering* instructions (drivers told to report only the hazards they perceive). Explanation is its central case, but not the whole of it.
  - **Predictions** (pp. 226–229):
    - Thinking aloud, unlike explanation, leaves the structure and course of task processes unchanged and may slow them slightly.
    - Explanations cannot be produced without extending what was heeded.
    - Evidence includes Karpf 1973: no accuracy difference, about 50% more time.
  - **Level 3 reactivity can improve performance.** In Gagné & Smith (1962), stating a reason for each Tower of Hanoi move substantially improved transfer (p. 230).
  - **STM cannot hold** automated processes, recognition, retrieval from long-term memory, or neural events. Automation removes intermediate products from report (p. 225).
  - **Probe taxonomy** (pp. 220–224, Table 1):
    - The axes are concurrent vs retrospective, specific vs general, and undirected vs directed, plus hypothetical probes.
    - General "interpretive probing" cannot be relied on for actual thought sequences (p. 221).
    - Directed probes cue what the experimenter expects (p. 222).
    - Immediate retrospection on a specific short episode is reliable; delayed retrospection degrades (p. 226).
- **Tenet alignment**: Neutral.
- **Quotes (abstract, grep-verified)**: "Verbalizing information is shown to affect cognitive processes only if the instructions require verbalization of information that would not otherwise be attended to." and "The inaccurate reports found by other research are shown to result from requesting information that was never directly heeded, thus forcing subjects to infer rather than remember their mental processes."

### Fox, Ericsson & Best (2011), reactivity meta-analysis
- **DOI**: 10.1037/a0021663. *Psychological Bulletin* 137(2), 316–344.
- 94 studies, nearly 3,500 participants. The think-aloud result holds after controlling for task type. Describe/explain procedures raise performance. All procedures add time.
- **Quotes (grep-verified)**: the "think-aloud" effect size "is indistinguishable from zero (r = -.03)"; "procedures that entail describing or explaining thoughts and actions are significantly reactive, leading to higher performance than silent control conditions."
- **Caution**: the outcome measured is *accuracy*, not report validity.

- **Schooler**: *Psych. Bull.* 137(2), 345–350, DOI 10.1037/a0022390.

- **Schooler (grep-verified)**: "although the think-aloud procedure readily captures substantive verbal thoughts, it is less able to capture inchoate cognitions"; "the nonreactivity of thinking aloud does not necessarily speak to its validity."
- **Ericsson & Fox (grep-verified)**: "thinking aloud involves only focusing on a challenging task while concurrently giving verbal expression to thoughts entering attention."
- **This is the later Ericsson revisit of the Levels.** The 2011 work contrasts think-aloud with directed explanation or description (roughly Levels 1–2 vs Level 3; the mapping is inferred from the abstracts), recast in the reply as non-introspective vs introspective methods.
  - Ericsson & Simon (1998), *Mind, Culture, and Activity* 5(3), 178–186 (DOI 10.1207/s15327884mca0503_3). **Not read.**
  - Ericsson (2002), *Applied Cognitive Psychology* 16(8), 981–987 (DOI 10.1002/acp.925). Its abstract (grep-checked) blames verbal overshadowing on *prescribed* verbalisation.
  - Ericsson (2003), *JCS* 10(9–10), 1–18. Metadata from CASBS and the SEP bibliography; **text not read**.

### SEP "Introspection" (Schwitzgebel), §§3.4, 4.2.3
- **URL**: https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/introspection/
- The entry classes Ericsson & Simon *among introspective methods*. Their convergence evidence (eye movements, latencies, Hamilton & Sanford 1978) supports the view that "people’s reports about their stream of thoughts often accurately reflect those thoughts" (curly apostrophe, grep-verified).
- Counter-evidence follows at once (Flavell et al. 1995; Smallwood & Schooler 2006). Schwitzgebel's pessimism targets *experience*, not the thought stream.

### Reactivity exceptions and adjacent literatures
- **Schooler, Ohlsson & Brooks (1993)**, *JEP: General* 122(2), 166–183 (DOI 10.1037/0096-3445.122.2.166). Grep-verified: "concurrent, nondirective verbalization impaired the solving of insight problems but had no effect on noninsight problems." This is the strongest Level 1–2 exception, and it is domain-limited.
- **Fox & Charness (2010)**, *Aging, Neuropsychology, and Cognition* 17(2), 191–204 (DOI 10.1080/13825580903042668). Thinking aloud *raised* older adults' Raven's scores (d = 0.73 and 0.92). The study is by the meta-analysis's own first author.
- **Ali & Peebles (2013)**, *Proc. CogSci 35*, 1720–1725 (https://eprints.hud.ac.uk/id/eprint/17604/): thinking aloud hindered graph comprehension (summariser only).
- **Verbal overshadowing**: Schooler & Engstler-Schooler (1990), *Cognitive Psychology* 22(1), 36–71 (metadata only). Alogna et al. (2014), *PPS* 9(5), 556–578 (DOI 10.1177/1745691614545653; abstract-verified): robust but timing-dependent (4% vs 16%). It is directed post-event description (Level 3), not think-aloud.
- **Reasons analysis**: Wilson & Schooler (1991), *JPSP* 60(2), 181–192 (DOI 10.1037/0022-3514.60.2.181). Grep-verified: "Analyzing reasons can focus people's attention on nonoptimal criteria". Here Nisbett and Wilson's own camp documents Level 3 reactivity. Present it as convergence.
- **Second-language research**: Bowles (2010, Routledge) includes a reactivity meta-analysis. **Metadata only.**

## The Reply to Nisbett and Wilson {#reply}

All of this section is paraphrased from the scan.

- **Scope (p. 245).** Nisbett and Wilson's accessible items are focus of attention, sensations, emotions, evaluations, plans, and intermediate results. These are exactly the model's STM contents. Any real disagreement concerns the one missing item, awareness of ongoing processes.
- **Three procedural differences (pp. 246–247):**
  1. The probes let subjects answer from a priori theory, as when subjects were told they had taken more shock than average and asked why (Nisbett & Wilson p. 237, as quoted by E&S).
  2. Many probes sought information never in memory: between-subjects comparisons, hypotheticals, and generalisations.
  3. The lag between process and probe was long.
- **Conclusion (p. 247).** Every inconsistency Nisbett and Wilson cite arises under conditions where the model predicts no veridical report. E&S want "introspection" dropped as a charge against such data, and they call careful reports highly reliable. That is *their* claim, not a finding.
- **What the reply concedes.** Subjects report heeded states, not processes; the analyst infers process (pp. 217–218). The *diagnosis* moves from introspective incapacity to procedure, but the content/process asymmetry stands, built into the model.

## Where This Puts the Content/Process Line {#line}

| Reportable with validity (E&S) | Not reportable / invites inference |
|---|---|
| Information heeded during the task, in order | Automated sub-processes, recognition, LTM retrieval, neural events (p. 225) |
| Intermediate results (e.g. partial products in mental multiplication, p. 225) | The transitions between them: the "how" as such |
| Concurrent verbalisation; immediate retrospection on a specific short episode | Delayed, general, hypothetical or between-condition probes |
| Undirected instructions to verbalise whatever thoughts occur | Directed requests: reasons, explanations, filtering, prescribed description |

Two refinements:

1. **The line is architectural; access to it is procedural.** Procedure decides whether a report draws on memory or on inference. It does *not* move process into the reportable column. Process becomes knowable through third-person reconstruction from a valid trace, never introspectable.
2. **Reactivity and validity are separate axes** (Schooler 2011). Fox et al. measured reactivity. A non-reactive report can still be incomplete (Duncker, as E&S quote him, p. 243) or blind to non-verbal content.

## Major Positions and Key Debates

- **Process-access scepticism** (Nisbett & Wilson 1977), covered at [[process-content-distinction]]. Wilson (1994), "The proper protocol" (DOI 10.1111/j.1467-9280.1994.tb00621.x), engages the revised book. **Content not retrieved; do not characterise it.**
- **Conditions of access** (Smith & Miller; White): ask when reports are accurate. Protocol analysis is the most worked-out answer.
- **Is think-aloud introspection?** Ericsson & Fox (2011) say no. Schooler (2011) and the SEP entry say yes. **Present the dispute; do not settle it.** On either answer, its success covers thought-stream content only.
- **Is Level 1–2 verbalisation non-reactive?** On average, yes. The exceptions cluster where processing is non-verbal or insight-dependent. No 2020s update to Fox et al. was found.
- **Did E&S refute Nisbett and Wilson?** No; see the reply section. White (1988) judged the question open.

## Relation to Site Perspective

**Tenet 1 (Dualism), and only in this way.** The method states which first-person reports carry evidential weight, blocking the slide from "process reports fail" to "verbal reports are unreliable". It supplies **no** evidence for irreducibility: it validates the information content of a task-directed thought stream, which the physicalist model it was built on handles comfortably.

**Tenets 2 and 3.** The method adds only a mechanism-level reason for the line the Map already draws at [[process-content-distinction]]: the mind-to-mechanism interface is not introspectable, because automated and recognition processes leave nothing in STM.

**Tenet 5.** No bearing beyond a shared demand that data come with a theory of the instrument.

## Guards for the Article Writer {#guards}

**Calibration rule (binding).**
- This is a METHODS page, neutral between dualism and physicalism, like [[process-content-distinction]].
- Do not present protocol analysis as vindicating introspection of phenomenal character. Its scope is heeded, task-directed information in [[working-memory]].
- It must not strengthen the content side past the Schwitzgebel caveat at [[process-content-distinction]] L94. Say explicitly that his failure cases (peripheral vision, colour dreaming, inner speech) concern a different object.

**Do not equivocate "content".** E&S mean the *information* held in STM. The Map's pages often gloss content as *phenomenal*; [[introspection]] L71 says "current phenomenal states". Keep the two senses apart, and name the one protocol analysis validates.

**Author stances.**
- **E&S** never claim subjects report processes; the analyst infers them.
- **Ericsson** by 2011 denies think-aloud is introspection; never write that he "defended introspection". His 2003 *JCS* title speaks of "the central problems of introspection as a source of scientific data"; read it before asserting tension with 2011.
- **Smith & Miller and White** criticise the 1977 *formulation*, not argue for privileged access.
- **Schooler** is sympathetic to protocol analysis: his abstract welcomes introspection "finally getting the scientific grounding". Do not cast him as an opponent.
- **Wilson & Schooler (1991)** are convergent evidence for Level 3 reactivity, not a point scored against the 1977 camp.

**Definitions.**
- Level 3 is broader than explanation: in 1980 it includes filtering and inference.
- Reactive does not mean worse. Explanation and description *raised* accuracy in Fox et al. and in Gagné & Smith; the cost is that the report describes a changed process.

**Coiner warnings.**
- E&S did not invent think-aloud. They cite Watson 1920, Claparède 1933 and Duncker 1945; early use is confirmed only via their reference list and secondary histories. Their contribution is the processing model and the Levels.
- The origin of the term "protocol analysis" is **unsettled**, so do not credit it to the 1984 book.
- The conditions-of-access reframing belongs to Smith & Miller.

**Do-not-quote (scan-only or summariser-only; paraphrase with page cites).**
- Every 1980 Level definition (pp. 219, 227).
- E&S's p. 247 conclusion wording ("thoroughly reliable", "careless charge", "terra incognita").
- The Nisbett-Wilson "private knowledge" block quoted on p. 245.
- Duncker as quoted on p. 243.
- Lashley as quoted on p. 216.
- Ali & Peebles (2013).
