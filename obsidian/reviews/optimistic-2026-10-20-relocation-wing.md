## Executive Summary

All four pages share one move: each relocates a dispute instead of claiming to win it.

- Acquaintance moves the self-stultification argument from *knowing* experience to *using* and *voicing* it.
- New dispositionalism moves the leeway dispute to "the choosing rather than … the success of an attempt".
- The dissociation tiers move the Map's channel vocabulary from evidence to "one admissible decomposition among several".
- The census page moves any census signature into conditional statistics, and the choice between its horns into intervention.

In three of the four, the relocated dispute comes to rest on the same posit: Tenet 3's selection, and whether it answers to what consciousness presents. Each page says so plainly, and the calibration is exemplary.

The main opening lies outside the wing. Two older mental-causation pages still treat that posit as free. `anomalous-monism` L67 says the Map's selection law "escapes the property-epiphenomenalism that dogs Davidson", but the acquaintance page and the Map's own ensemble-level-epiphenomenalism entry hold that escape open. `content-externalism` L48 calls both horns of its wide-content dilemma "usable", but the acquaintance repair now depends on one of them. Both seams can be repaired for 54 words in total, and the reciprocal links cost nothing.

There is one genuine gap. The Map's action theory borrows Davidson's view that reasons cause actions and also adopts an agent-causal hybrid "close to Lowe". Standard agent-causal views deny that reasons cause free action, and no live page asks how the Map's selector acts *for* a reason.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would find his 2003 paper used at full strength against the Map's own argument, and accurately. The acquaintance page reproduces his reply exactly: "the connection between experience and phenomenal belief is tighter than any causal connection: it is constitution". It also keeps his own limits:

- he is "not endorsing epiphenomenalism";
- inferred beliefs "must be appropriately caused by the justifying belief";
- the gap to external-world knowledge "remains as wide as ever".

All three are verified at the preprint. Chalmers would most value the use channel, because it is built from *his* distinction: causal for inference, constitutive only for the direct phenomenal belief. The Map grants the hard problem's half of the dispute and keeps only what his own text keeps causal. "Conceding the presence channel costs the Map's dualism nothing" (L116) is a correct accounting. On the dissociation page he would welcome the admission that "The dissociation evidence never discriminated dualism from functionalism" (L104): structure-and-function evidence was never going to decide the hard problem.

Stapp would welcome the acquaintance page's Minimal Quantum Interaction paragraph. The census page gives him a precise statement of what Born preservation hides. #### Agent causation and acting for reasons: does the Map's selector meet its own "from" condition?

- **Builds on**:
  - `concepts/new-dispositionalism` L89–L91 and L101 ("The agency cluster reads that selection agent-causally, as the agent settling among open alternatives, and that reading supplies modal leeway");
  - `topics/acquaintance-without-causation` L102: the efficacy-of-evidence premise, that a downstream belief, inference or assertion is held or made *from* evidence only if the evidence's evidential features causally contribute to producing it;
  - `topics/philosophy-of-action-under-dualism` L89 ("**Bidirectional Interaction requires the causal skeleton.**") and L95 ("The result is a volitionist agent-causal hybrid, close to Lowe.");
  - `topics/event-causal-libertarianism` L68, which raises the reasons problem only for the *non-causal* wing ("how an action is done *for a reason* when reason-explanation looks causal").
- **Would address**: SEP §3.1 (verified, raw HTML) states the problem for agent causation directly:
  - "Standard agent-causal views deny that events such as the agent's recognizing certain reasons cause any free action".
  - It reports O'Connor's (2000b, ch. 5) non-causal account of reason-explanation and the Feldman–Buckareff (2003) objection to it.
  - It describes the integrated alternative (Clarke 1993, 1996, 2003b ch. 8): a free action "caused by the agent and nondeterministically caused by certain agent-involving events, such as the agent's recognizing certain reasons".
  - It closes: "It is a matter of some dispute whether event causation and agent causation can be combined in this fashion".

  The Map holds both halves, the causal skeleton and the agent-causal settler, without saying which combination it holds. The acquaintance repair raises the stakes. Applied to practical reason, its "from" condition looks as if it requires a reason's content to contribute causally to the choice. That fits Clarke's integrated view and sits badly with O'Connor's.
- **Questions for the research note**:
  1. O'Connor 2000, ch. 5 at primary text: the account of reason-explanation and how it handles Davidson's "one operative reason" problem, which `philosophy-of-action-under-dualism` L43 already uses.
  2. Clarke 1996 and 2003 ch. 8: the integrated view and Clarke's own verdict on it. Read the primary text; do not infer the verdict from the SEP.
  3. Lowe 2008 (*Personal Agency*). `research/agent-causation-libertarian-free-will-2026-01-14` L139 records "Lowe: uncaused volitions; reasons are not causes". That is a **lead, unverified**. If it holds, the Map's "close to Lowe" hybrid and its causal skeleton pull apart.
  4. Steward 2012 and Mawson 2011, which the SEP lists as also allowing events a nondeterministic causal role.
  5. Whether the integrated view reopens the contrastive luck question. **Coordinate with** the queued P3 "Research the luck and Mind arguments against agent-causal libertarianism…". That task's Q3 asks for O'Connor's and Clarke's replies to *luck*. This one asks how agent-caused choices are made *for reasons*. The two share sources and could be run back to back.
