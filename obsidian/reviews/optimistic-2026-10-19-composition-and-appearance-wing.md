## Executive Summary

The four pages share a method. Each takes something the Map owes: a composition rule for several subjects, a located fusion step, an account of why mind looks like physics, a Zeno window for the brain. Each then breaks the debt into items and says which conclusions follow from the definition and which only on the Map's reading. Each method is done well, and the calibration discipline is exemplary throughout. The biggest opportunity is that the four barely know about each other. Only one link runs between them (social-choice → fusion). Yet three of them are bound by the same constraint: the Born-statistics posit, under which the Map's selections must average to physics. Social-choice works out what that posit demands with several subjects (fixed weights). Lawful-appearance names it as the Map's own appearance debt. The Zeno page analyses the one channel that escapes it, and that channel pays by looking like physical dephasing. The report proposes three short seam repairs and one research task to fill a gap the Zeno page names itself. One result is new: the question-selection (Process 1) form of the social-choice problem, which no live page poses.

## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)

Chalmers would recognise his own work used carefully and against no straw man. Lawful-appearance reports his austerity problem in his words ("there seems to be little or no rationality in this structure"). It also keeps his comparative verdict intact, "Idealism is not significantly less plausible than its main competitors", instead of recruiting him for dualism. Social-choice uses the structure of the Chalmers–McQueen (2022) collapse model precisely. Consciousness fixes *when* collapse happens and the Born rule fixes *which* outcome, so "no subject's preference enters the outcome law, so there is nothing to compose." That one sentence draws a real boundary between collapse-trigger and outcome-selection models, and the Map pays for its side of it honestly: "The price is giving up outcome-selection, which Bidirectional Interaction commits the Map to." Chalmers would also praise lawful-appearance's lawfulness section for conceding the parity result ("Lawfulness is a shared item"), so the Map does not claim a dualist advantage the hard problem does not supply.

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)

The Zeno page is the most generous careful reading of Stapp's 2000 reply to Tegmark that the Map could give. It finds where Stapp is right: "makes no difference at all" is true *of the state*, matching the Facchi–Pascazio theorem. It then locates the missing condition in his own derivation. His eq. (4) evolves the reduced state by exp(−iHd) alone, so the toy model's x = 0, "which is why the linear terms vanish for any d". It also finds the one reading on which Tegmark's 10⁻²⁰ s figure does not already close the hold, the strip-width window, and labels it "the Map's working, a scaling argument with no number attached." Stapp would welcome the conclusion that "Neither position is refuted". Above all he would welcome the observation that, inside the window, "the asking does the work". That puts the mind's contribution where his Process 1 puts it: the choice of question.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)

Subject-fusion's first-person result is the passage Nagel would single out. "A fission product's memories at least testify coherently, though on the Map's verdict mistakenly. A fusion product's memories cannot be read consistently at all." The fused subject's own point of view cannot settle which subject it is, yet the page holds that a determinate fact remains ("the fact remains determinate while going unread"). That is Nagel's realism about facts beyond the reach of our concepts, applied to the first person itself. Lawful-appearance offers him his own view from nowhere in structural form. Russell's "Physics is mathematical, not because we know so much about the physical world, but because we know so little" explains why the objective description looks impersonal without implying that what it describes is impersonal.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)

Two passages read well to a process philosopher. Lawful-appearance's phenomenal-powers paragraph has laws grounded in how experiences feel, citing Mørch (2017) and Chalmers's judgement that the move suits cosmic idealism. That gives laws an experiential ground without a substance underneath. Subject-fusion's complete-end verdict, "two endings and one beginning", echoes Whitehead's creativity formula as `concepts/process-philosophy` paraphrases it: "the many become one and are increased by one". C is a novel unity that inherits two pasts, not a compound of A and B. **Constraint applied**: none of the four pages uses process-friendliness to move any entity up the five-tier scale. Indeed none makes a tier claim at all. The resonance is praise for coherence only.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)

Kane locates freedom in indeterminacy that occurs *during effort*. Stapp's effortful Zeno hold is the physics-side analogue, and the Zeno page settles on what matters most to an agency theorist: whether the effort makes a difference at the level of the ensemble. Its answer is conditional and exact. Inside the window "the ensemble differs from what it would have been had nothing been asked", so a hold there "would not be an instance of" [[ensemble-level-epiphenomenalism]]. Social-choice protects the agent's standing from a different side. "Status, stakes and leverage are three different things" keeps the agent's moral status from being diluted by how much causal leverage a composition rule happens to grant, and Kane would want that firewall.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)

All four pages end at an honest unread quantity, and McGinn would recognise the pattern. Social-choice: "What it can say is that the law is unwritten." Subject-fusion: "a sharp boundary that nothing reads". Lawful-appearance: "Which list is heavier the inventory cannot say." Zeno: "Whether the Zeno route gives Tenet 3 a working mechanism therefore turns on a quantity no one has computed." None of these is a shrug. Each names exactly what is missing (a pairing law, a located step, a weighing scale, the window ℓ and its crossing time) and stops there.

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)

None of these pages touches minimal-organism consciousness, and none uses tier language, so the five-tier scale is not in play. What Birch would praise is restraint at four points where each page had a structural opening to overclaim and declined it:

- **Zeno**: the escape from ensemble-level epiphenomenalism inside the window could have been counted as support for Tenet 3. The page instead makes the whole question turn on an uncomputed quantity. Its gap list states its own epistemic weight: "In each, nothing has yet come in, which is weaker than a test survived."
- **Lawful-appearance**: the austerity problem could have been pressed into an argument for dualism. The page says "nothing in it is an argument for dualism". It also follows dualism-cartography's withdrawal of comparative cost verdicts "for want of a scale", and it names the Map's own debt "of the same shape".
- **Social-choice**: three calibrations come before any argument (Map-internal synthesis; normative content only on the low-credence P-VS1 reading; the mechanism debt). Its own sharpest result says only that Born preservation "favours" fixed weights, conditional on which Born reading holds.
- **Fusion**: "no evidence discriminates it from its rivals", and the far end "has no human datum at all." After its 10-16 repair, the Occam paragraph books the step's cost and no longer uses Tenet 5 as a reason.

Verdict: tenet-coherent, not evidence-elevating, on all four. The Process Philosopher and the Hardline Empiricist converge on the same passage, lawful-appearance's phenomenal-powers paragraph: Whitehead praises the experiential ground of law, and Birch praises the booking of it as "a parity result, not an argument for dualism". On this scale's test, that convergence means the tension has been resolved honestly.

## Content Strengths

### psychophysical-social-choice-problem
- **Strongest point**: The Born-favours-fixed-weights result (§born-favours-fixed-weights). "A weighted average of rules that each return Born frequencies returns them too." Rules whose weights move with the states inherit no such guarantee, so intensity weighting, lexical priority and accumulation all owe a subject-sensitive balancing law. The 10-16 deep review re-derived the identity.
- **Notable quote**: "so its aggregate is a law without a subject, a social-choice function that no one chooses."
- **Why it works**: The page brings a mature literature (Arrow, Gibbard, Sen, Harsanyi, List–Pettit) to bear on a question no external source poses, and it marks clearly which extensions are the Map's. The Okasha move, under which a vector-valued felt valence would reopen Arrow's problem *inside* each subject, is a striking use of a theory-choice result.

### subject-fusion
- **Strongest point**: The transitivity argument for first-person unreadability (§map-reading, "Unreadable from inside as well").
- **Notable quote**: "A does not experience its ending, and C's experience begins already furnished with two pasts."
- **Why it works**: The page takes a familiar thought experiment (Parfit 1971) and finds something new in it. Fusion is not merely fission reversed: its memory evidence is incoherent rather than merely mistaken. The conditional structure at the complete end ("at most one" from transitivity; "none" only with the pairing premise) is laid out cleanly enough that a reader can see exactly which premise a critic must attack.

### lawful-appearance-problem
- **Strongest point**: The decomposition. Of the four parts of the charge, lawfulness and structural form are shared by every metaphysics. Only austerity, the explanatory objection and the constitution debt belong to idealism.
- **Notable quote**: "The slide from dualism to idealism is therefore best described as a conversion: a mind–matter interface debt becomes a mind–mind constitution or bridging debt, with the austerity residue added."
- **Why it works**: Berkeley's §65 grammar analogy grounds the modern debate historically. The page also turns the charge back on the Map without being asked (§the-maps-own-appearance-debt). Two other pages link its anchors, so any edit must keep them: `topics/q3-q4-sliding-boundary-and-transparency-problem` L84 links `#the-maps-own-appearance-debt` (a fairness guard installed by today's deep review, which relies on the phrases "of the same shape", "narrower" and "posited rather than explained"), and `topics/mechanism-costs-dualism-thickness-quadrants` L103 links `#lawfulness`.

### zeno-dynamics-on-a-decohered-mixture
- **Strongest point**: Two regime-limited truths. Stapp is right about the state and Georgiev is right in the rate regime, and the toy model shows where the boundary falls (projections halve the hop rate only for x ≲ 1.6).
- **Notable quote**: "the hold need not preserve a superposition across the decision, but each projection must arrive inside the quadratic window for the distinction the environment also monitors."
- **Why it works**: The page moves the familiar timing objection from "outlast decoherence" to "fall inside the quadratic window", which is a more exact and more answerable requirement. It reads Georgiev's own simulation set-up (ξ → 0, a different basis) to show that his redundancy sentence concerns a regime his simulations never reach. Five numbers are given, all re-derived, and the toy is labelled as the Map's.

#### The question-selection (Process 1) form of the social-choice problem — NOT COVERED anywhere on the Map
- **Builds on**: `concepts/psychophysical-social-choice-problem` §composition-rule; `concepts/zeno-dynamics-on-a-decohered-mixture` §unconditional and its Relation to Site Perspective ("the mind contributes the asking: which question, and when"); `concepts/process-1-specification-problem` §How the Debt Might Be Paid (the Laskey variant: brain-supplied basis, mind-supplied timing).
- **Would address**: Social-choice divides models into collapse-trigger models (consciousness fixes *when*, so nothing is composed) and outcome-selection models (consciousness fixes *which*, so the rule is owed). The Zeno page analyses a third family that the Bidirectional Interaction tenet "registers without adopting": consciousness fixes *what is asked*. I grepped `process-1-specification-problem`, `quantum-zeno-effect`, `motor-control-quantum-zeno`, `stapp-quantum-mind`, `multi-agent-born-preservation-problem` and `multi-mind-collapse-problem`, and none poses the multi-subject question for this route. The Map's working, checked in this run with a two-state Bloch-vector calculation (a non-selective projection along axis **n** maps **r** → (**r**·**n**)**n**), gives three results:
  1. **Discordant askers.** Two subjects asking incompatible questions of shared tissue give order-dependent results: n-then-m differs from m-then-n. Rapid alternation shrinks the state by cos²θ per round (0.9 → 0.59 → 0.39 → 0.25 at θ = 36°), so each subject's hold is undone by the other's.
  2. **Concordant askers.** On the Laskey variant two subjects pose the same brain-supplied question, so their asking schedules merge and their effects *accumulate* inside the window. In the Zeno page's own toy formula, halving τ takes R/Γ from 0.136 to 0.071 at x = 0.3. Outside the window, nearly nothing changes (0.683 → 0.482 at x = 3, and smaller still at larger x).
  3. **Why the fixed-weights result does not transfer.** That result needs each subject's rule to return Born frequencies in the unconditioned aggregate. A Zeno hold is built to move the unconditional statistics, and a joint schedule is a composition of channels, not a convex mixture of conditionals.
  So on this route the P-SC2 concordant question (accumulate, dilute or renormalise?) gets the opposite answer to the corridor's: accumulation inside the window, against renormalisation under fixed mixtures. That contrast is worth stating. Social-choice's agenda-dependence results are the natural formal analogue (a lead only; no source checked).
- **Estimated scope**: a short addition (~100–130 words) in social-choice §composition-rule, labelled as the Map's working. → Task minted.
- **Tenet alignment**: Bidirectional Interaction (the Process 1 path registered without adopting); Minimal Quantum Interaction (no force term; the asking schedule is the lever).

#### The plural and Zeno forms of the Map's own appearance debt
- **Builds on**: `concepts/lawful-appearance-problem` §the-maps-own-appearance-debt; social-choice §born-favours-fixed-weights; Zeno §unconditional.
- **Would address**: Lawful-appearance names the Map's debt for one subject: "the selections are posited to average to exactly the Born distribution". Two sibling results bear on it, and the page links neither. (a) With several subjects, the posit binds the *composition rule*. Fixed-weight rules inherit it at no cost, and state-dependent rules owe a balancing law (social-choice). (b) The Zeno route has a different appearance shape. Inside the window its aggregate does *not* reproduce the unmeasured statistics. Instead it looks like physical dephasing, which the Zeno page itself concedes: "physical dephasing of the same strength in the same basis would do the same." So the Zeno lever presents as a physical *process* (monitoring) rather than as physical *statistics* (Born frequencies). It escapes the corridor's appearance debt by taking on a detectability exposure: one would have to show that no physical dephaser of the required strength is present. That contrast finishes the section's own taxonomy (corridor, bending, and now question-selection).
- **Estimated scope**: ~60–90 words; headroom 1,164. → Task minted.
- **Tenet alignment**: Minimal Quantum Interaction and Bidirectional Interaction (the tenet paragraph already says "wherever consciousness selects, its selections must reproduce Born statistics", and the Zeno case qualifies "selects").
