## Reply

# Referee Report: “Intrinsic Nature” (The Unfinishable Map, /concepts/intrinsic-nature/)

**Verdict: major revisions.** The article is more candid than most advocacy pieces. It concedes moderate ontic structural realism (OSR), Newman’s problem and the “defeater-removal only” status of its central premise. [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]] Even so, it rests on three unflagged conflations: intrinsic vs. categorical vs. non-structural properties. It gives no hearing to the pure-powers and powerful-qualities replies that dissolve its opening premise. Several citations are wrong or misattributed. And its account of where the non-physical lives contradicts the site’s own bi-aspectual ontology article.

## TL;DR

- **The core premise is stated as near-fact but is contested on three fronts the article mostly ignores.** These are dispositional monism (Bird, Mumford, Molnar: dispositions need no categorical bearer), powerful-qualities identity theory (Martin and Heil) and the anti-quidditist case (Black 2000). The move from “physics is silent about X” to “X is the natural home of the non-physical” is tenet-driven, not argued for.
- **Several citations are misattributed or stale.** The headline Russell quotation comes from *Human Knowledge* (1948), not *The Analysis of Matter* (1927). “Quiddities” is credited to Lewis (2009) when Armstrong (1989) coined the term and Black (2000) first defined it. Pautz’s 2015 “Dilemma” paper is placed in the Alter and Nagasawa volume, but it is an unpublished manuscript. The Howell (2015) epiphenomenalism objection is presented without Alter and Coleman’s 2021 rebuttal.
- **Integration is incoherent at one critical point.** This article says the Map “locates the non-physical aspect at the intrinsic base.” The bi-aspectual ontology article says consciousness is “the missing ontology, not the missing intrinsic nature,” that it acts only at neural interfaces, and that physical objective reduction does the work elsewhere. Neither the intrinsic-nature article nor the bi-aspectual article links to the other. The Russellian monism article sells “substance dualism” as the Map’s advantage, which the bi-aspectual article explicitly disowns.

## Key Findings

1. **Three distinctions are conflated, and the article’s main inference depends on the conflation.** The article runs together:

- intrinsic vs. extrinsic/relational;
- categorical vs. dispositional;
- structural vs. non-structural.

Mass and charge are standard examples of *intrinsic* properties in the Lewisian sense, yet on dispositional essentialism they are wholly dispositional. [nd](https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/nature-s-metaphysics-laws-and-properties/) Pereboom’s distinction between “relatively” and “absolutely” intrinsic properties exists precisely to block this slide (Pereboom’s chapter in Alter and Nagasawa 2015 is ch. 14, “Consciousness, Physicalism, and Absolutely Intrinsic Properties”). [ecampus](https://lyon.ecampus.com/consciousness-physical-world-perspectives/bk/9780199927357) The article never cites Pereboom.
2. **The strongest rivals to quidditism are absent.**

- Bird’s *Nature’s Metaphysics* has a dedicated chapter on “The regress objection,” which answers Armstrong-style complaints that pure powers regress. [oup](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/natures-metaphysics-9780199573110)
- Bird (“The ultimate argument against Armstrong’s contingent necessitation view of laws,” *Analysis* 65(286): 147–155, 2005) charges that Armstrong’s view “generates a vicious regress with respect to necessitation.” Barker and Smart (“The ultimate argument against dispositional monist accounts of laws,” *Analysis* 72(4): 714–722, 2012) reply that “precisely the same regress afflicts Bird’s dispositional-monist theory, and indeed, related views, such as that of Mumford and Anjum.”
- None of this, nor Martin and Heil’s identity theory, appears in the article. Yet the article later concedes that the Map needs a “causally contributing” base. [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]] That is a powers view by another name.
3. **The citation record is mixed:** 7 verified, 4 misattributed or wrong, 3 stale or one-sided, and several unverifiable (table below).
4. **The Map’s distinctive claim (consciousness at the intrinsic base, but distinct and interacting) is asserted, not argued.** It is also inconsistent with the bi-aspectual article’s localised, interface-only account of actuality.

## Major Objections

**M1. The opening premise begs the question against dispositional monism (lead and §”The Structural/Intrinsic Distinction”).**

- *The passage.* “None of this says what *bears* these dispositions”, and later “relations require relata, dispositions require bearers, a role requires an occupant.” [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]]
- *Why it fails.* This is the “categorical-base” thesis that dispositional monists deny: Mumford, Molnar, Bird (2007) and Shoemaker’s causal theory of properties. On their view a fundamental property is *exhausted* by its dispositional essence, so “what physics leaves out” is nothing. [ruhr-uni-bochum](https://dbs-lin.ruhr-uni-bochum.de/philosophy/pla/sites/default/files/reviews/Alexander_Bird_Natures_Metaphysics_Laws_and_Properties2007.pdf)[nd](https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/nature-s-metaphysics-laws-and-properties/)
- *What is missing.* The article treats OSR as the only live rival and files the powers literature under a single Further Reading link (consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-laws-and-dispositions). [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]]
- *What a referee would require.*

- A section stating the pure-powers position.
- Its regress problem: Armstrong; Bird 2007a, “The regress of pure powers?”; and Ingthorsson, “The Regress of Pure Powers Revisited” (*European Journal of Philosophy* 23(3): 529–541, 2015), who argues that Bird’s and Molnar’s accounts “are not equipped to remove the threat of vicious regress.”
- The reply that powers are individuated by their place in a manifestation graph.
- The counter-charge that categoricalism carries its own regress: Bird (*Analysis* 2005) against Armstrong, and Barker and Smart (*Analysis* 2012) showing that “precisely the same regress afflicts Bird’s dispositional-monist theory.”

Only then can “the Map sides with the quidditist” count as a considered verdict.

**M2. The article’s own commitments push it toward a powers ontology, which removes its need for quiddities (“Relation to Site Perspective,” Bidirectional Interaction paragraph).**

- *The passage.* “An intrinsic nature that is merely categorical and inert would re-import epiphenomenalism, so the Map reads the intrinsic base as causally contributing.” [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]]
- *The tension.* That is either the powerful-qualities view (Martin and Heil: properties are at once qualitative and dispositional) or a pure-powers view. [nd](https://ndpr.nd.edu/reviews/the-metaphysics-of-powerful-qualities-powerful-categoricalism-and-the-laws-of-nature/)[blogspot](http://guidetoreality.blogspot.com/2013/09/russellian-monism-and-identity-theory.html) On either, the article’s founding contrast between “dispositional (what physics describes)” and “categorical (what physics omits)” collapses:

- On powerful qualities, the qualitative side is the *same property* as the dispositional side physics already describes.
- On pure powers, nothing categorical is left over.
- *What is required.* The article must say which view it holds and then rewrite the opening, which still presents quiddities as “intrinsic, categorical natures.” [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]] The obvious ally is uncited: Mørch, “The Evolutionary Argument for Phenomenal Powers” (*Philosophical Perspectives* 31(1): 293–316, 2017). She defends “the view that phenomenal properties are intrinsically powerful” and says it is compatible with “physicalism, interactionist dualism and Russellian monism.”

**M3. The “physics does not describe intrinsic properties” claim is overstated and conflated (§”The Structural/Intrinsic Distinction,” bulleted lists).**

- *The passage.* The list puts “mass as resistance to acceleration” under *dispositional* properties and puts all intrinsic natures under “Physics does *not* describe.” [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]]
- *Why it fails.*

- Standard physics and standard metaphysics (Lewis’s own intrinsic/extrinsic analysis) treat rest mass and charge as intrinsic. The article silently swaps “intrinsic” for “categorical/quidditative.”
- Lewis’s humility thesis is about the *identity* of fundamental property-occupants. [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/257637362_Ramseyan_Humility_scepticism_and_grasp) It is not a claim that physics ascribes only extrinsic properties. Lewis held that fundamental properties *are* intrinsic and non-dispositional, and that we cannot know *which* ones occupy the roles. [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/AUTEH)
- The epistemic claim (we cannot identify the occupant) and the metaphysical claim (there is an occupant, and it is not physical) need separate defences. The article runs them together.
- *Recent literature the article ignores.* Alter and Pereboom (2023, *Erkenntnis* 88(4): 1409–1428) argue that Russellian monism does *not* require structuralism about physics, only a conditional: *if* structuralism is true, then there is more to reality than physics describes. [philpapers](https://philpapers.org/citations/NEYAPC)[philpapers](https://philpapers.org/rec/ALTRMA-2) The article’s framing (“the premise that many Map articles build on”) is therefore out of date with the very literature it relies on.

**M4. The Hempel’s-dilemma section is question-begging and internally inconsistent (§”Hempel’s Dilemma and What ‘Physical’ Means”).**

- *Internal inconsistency.* The second horn is first called “unfalsifiable,” then “vacuous.” [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]] These are different charges. The first is epistemic; the second is semantic, as in Hempel’s own “trivial/indeterminate” worry. [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/VICCPA)
- *Begging the question.* “Everything is physical is either a claim about structure (true but partial) or a claim about a complete ontology” assumes quiddities are *not physical*. Russellian physicalists explicitly deny this:

- Montero’s chapter in Alter and Nagasawa (2015) is titled “Russellian Physicalism.” [oup](https://global.oup.com/academic/product/consciousness-in-the-physical-world-9780199927357)
- Stoljar’s “Nagelian monism” and ignorance hypothesis make the same move.
- Brown (2025), cited in the companion Russellian monism article, defends “Russellian physicalism.” [[russellian-monism|concepts/russellian-monism]]

Hempel’s dilemma is about what “physical” means. Assuming an answer to it in order to “explain why the dilemma has force” is circular.
- *Standard replies omitted.* The section leaves out the main responses:

- Melnyk’s currentism: accept the first horn and treat physicalism as a probably-false but best hypothesis.
- The “no fundamental mentality” (NFM) constraint, set out in Wilson, “On characterizing the physical” (*Philosophical Studies* 131(1): 61–99, 2006) and also used by Montero. On it, physical entities “are not fundamentally mental (that is, do not individually possess or bestow mentality).”
- Ney’s attitude-based reading.
- The *via negativa*.

The NFM constraint is directly relevant, because it lets a physicalist accept quiddities while denying they are mental.
- *Unsupported closure claim.* “closure stated over current physics is false” overclaims. Montero’s point is that the closure principle *as stated over current physics* is likely false or unsupported, not demonstrably false. [[causal-closure-debate-historical-survey|topics/causal-closure-debate-historical-survey]][[causal-closure-debate-2026-03-19|research/causal-closure-debate-2026-03-19]]

**M5. The divergence from Russellian monism is asserted, not argued, and fails on the Map’s own premises (“Relation to Site Perspective,” second paragraph).**

- *The passage.* “If consciousness simply *were* the intrinsic nature of physical entities, it would be identical with the physical at the fundamental level and could not causally *select* among physical outcomes.” [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]]
- *Why it fails.*

- The Map’s own structure (quantum mechanics) is indeterministic at exactly the points where it wants selection. Nothing about *identifying* phenomenal properties with quiddities stops those quiddities from contributing at indeterminacies.
- “Already fixed as the inside of whatever the structure does” assumes the structure does something determinate, which the Map denies.
- Russellian monists claim quiddities “play indispensable roles in physical causation” (SEP, Alter and Pereboom). [stanford](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/russellian-monism/) The Map’s companion article quotes this line and calls it merely “schematic.” [[russellian-monism|concepts/russellian-monism]][stanford](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/russellian-monism/) That is a complaint about detail, not a refutation.
- *Redundancy.* The same argument appears nearly verbatim in the bi-aspectual article, [[bi-aspectual-ontology|concepts/bi-aspectual-ontology]] so the redundancy duplicates a weak argument rather than strengthening it.

**M6. Combination-problem analogues and interaction/exclusion worries for the Map’s own position are underplayed.**

- *The asymmetry.* The “What Would Challenge This View” section requires that Russellian monism prove “more economical… without re-importing the combination problem.” [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]] No matching condition is placed on the Map.
- *The Map’s own analogues.* The Map’s position register (cited in the 2026-10-15 changelog) concedes “The Map owes a subject-pairing law and does not have one” (P-SC2) and that subject boundaries are “not readable off physical structure” (P-I3). [[changelog|workflow/changelog]] These are the Map’s analogues of subject-combination and boundary problems. The companion Russellian monism article admits “a pairing-and-unity burden” too, [[russellian-monism|concepts/russellian-monism]] but this article never does.
- *An exclusion-style dilemma at the interface.* Placing a distinct non-physical aspect “at the intrinsic base” that biases quantum outcomes faces one of two outcomes:

- **(a)** the biasing shifts outcome statistics away from the Born rule, which is empirically exposed and needs a stated test; or
- **(b)** it leaves the statistics untouched, which invites the charge that the “selection” is causally idle in every observable respect. That is the same structure as Howell’s contingency objection, which the Map uses against Russellian monism.

The article mentions neither horn.

**M7. The inscrutability section assumes quidditism without flagging it (§”The Inscrutability of Quiddities”).**

- *The passage.* “Two distinct quiddities could occupy the same structural role” is the quidditist permutation premise. [stanford](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/haecceitism/)[[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]]
- *The rival literature.* Black (2000, “Against Quidditism,” *AJP* 78(1): 87–104) calls such swaps “distinctions without differences.” [stanford](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/haecceitism/)[tedsider](https://tedsider.org/teaching/properties/HO_quidditism_v_CE.pdf) Causal essentialists (Shoemaker, Bird) deny them outright.
- *The distinction the Map needs.* Hildebrand, “Two Types of Quidditism” (*AJP* 94(3): 516–532, 2016), separates two views:

- “bare quidditism,” on which worlds with distinct properties and isomorphic structures “must be qualitatively identical”;
- “qualitative quidditism,” which “allows such worlds to have qualitative differences.”

The Map wants acquaintance to reveal a quiddity, so it needs the qualitative version. The article should say so.
- *Missing credit.* Lewis himself noted the potential exception: panphenomenalism plus the identification (“revelation”) thesis. [cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/canadian-journal-of-philosophy/article/abs/ramseyan-humility-the-response-from-revelation-and-panpsychism/B0A2C185AB9E0DFEC2414CE3A9316BCA) The article presents this exception as “the engine of the Russellian programme” without crediting Lewis, who thought it unavailable. [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/AUTEH)

**M8. Location incoherence with the bi-aspectual ontology (see Integration).** This is the most serious structural problem; details are below.

## Minor Objections

1. **Priority.** “The distinction is Bertrand Russell’s (1927) and Arthur Eddington’s (1928).” [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]] The distinction long predates them. Strawson traces it through Arnauld (1641), Locke, Hume, Priestley and Kant. [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/STRRMM-2) Alter and Nagasawa (2015) reprint Leibniz, Kant and James as “Precursors.” [ecampus](https://lyon.ecampus.com/consciousness-physical-world-perspectives/bk/9780199927357) The article also cites Langton’s *Kantian Humility*, which contradicts its own priority claim.
2. **Eddington’s idealism.** He is cited for “world of shadows,” but the article doesn’t mention that he went on to a mind-stuff conclusion. The Map’s own 2026-01-15 research note flagged that “his idealism goes beyond site’s dualism.” [[russellian-monism-2026-01-15|research/russellian-monism-2026-01-15]]
3. **Same-paragraph hedge.** The method-claim paragraph asserts permanence (“that silence is a feature of the method”), then immediately calls permanence “a bet.”
4. **Falsifier (2) is unsatisfiable by stipulation.** The article *defines* physics’ method as structural, so “physics delivered a non-relational… characterisation” cannot happen on its terms. Restate it as a condition with real content, for example “a successful anti-quidditist account of fundamental properties.”
5. **Trend evidence is symmetric.** “Physics has grown more relational rather than less” is offered as support for quidditism. OSR proponents (Ladyman and Ross, *Every Thing Must Go*; French, *The Structure of the World*) read the same trend as evidence that there is nothing *but* structure. [philarchive](https://philarchive.org/archive/AUTEH)
6. **The Newman’s-problem sentence is ambiguous.** It says Newman’s objection targets “Russell’s *epistemic* structuralism, which is the Map’s own position,” then that it “yields an asymmetry in available replies.” [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]] Spell out which side the asymmetry favours and why. As written, it reads as an objection to the Map being turned into a point in its favour.
7. **“Combination problem has resisted solution since James (1890)”** ignores a decade of constructive work (Chalmers’s combination-problem papers, Goff’s phenomenal bonding, Roelofs’s *Combining Minds*, Coleman, Mørch). That work may fail, but it should be engaged.
8. **The OSR treatment is outsourced.** Ladyman, Ross, French and Worrall are never cited; even the epistemic/ontic structural realism distinction is only gestured at.
9. **The SEP is cited as a bare URL.** It should name the authors (Alter and Pereboom) and give the revision date.

## Integration Assessment

**Contradiction on location (critical).** The three pages give incompatible answers to where consciousness sits:

| Article | Where consciousness / the non-physical sits |
| --- | --- |
| intrinsic-nature | “The Map locates the non-physical aspect of reality at the intrinsic base—but as a *distinct, interacting* aspect rather than as the quiddities” [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]] |
| bi-aspectual-ontology | “Consciousness is the missing ontology, not the missing intrinsic nature of the ontology physics already describes.” Also: “Consciousness supplies that actuality where a neural interface exists; elsewhere physical objective reduction fixes outcomes without it.” [[bi-aspectual-ontology|concepts/bi-aspectual-ontology]] |
| russellian-monism | Advantage claimed via “substance dualism… unified mental subjects” [[russellian-monism|concepts/russellian-monism]] |

These cannot all hold:

- The intrinsic base is wherever physical structure is, which is everywhere. Bi-aspectual actuality, by contrast, is *local* to neural interfaces and is supplied elsewhere by physical objective reduction. So consciousness cannot be “at the intrinsic base” in any sense that matches “intrinsic base” as the article defines it, namely the categorical ground of all physical dispositions.
- If consciousness sits at the base but is not the quiddities, the article owes an answer about what the quiddities are:

- (i) physical quiddities sitting alongside consciousness, which needs a second, unexplained intrinsic layer; or
- (ii) no quiddities at all, which concedes OSR or the powers view.
- The bi-aspectual article also says “Physics captures every structural and dispositional fact.” [[bi-aspectual-ontology|concepts/bi-aspectual-ontology]] That is hard to square with intrinsic-nature’s “causally contributing” non-physical base: what is a causal contribution that is not a dispositional fact?
- The Russellian monism article’s “substance dualism sidesteps subject-summing” conflicts with bi-aspectual’s “The Map’s bi-aspectual ontology is not substance dualism.” The bi-aspectual article itself concedes “an open tension” about where the persisting subject enters. [[russellian-monism|concepts/russellian-monism]][[bi-aspectual-ontology|concepts/bi-aspectual-ontology]]

**Missing cross-links.**

- The bi-aspectual article does not link to /concepts/intrinsic-nature/ anywhere in the body or Further Reading, [[bi-aspectual-ontology|concepts/bi-aspectual-ontology]] although intrinsic-nature claims bi-aspectual “frames” its position.
- The Russellian monism article links to intrinsic-nature only in its first sentence and never mentions Hempel’s dilemma or the inscrutability argument that intrinsic-nature delegates to it. [[russellian-monism|concepts/russellian-monism]]
- None of the three links Alter and Coleman’s reply, or an equivalent Map page on it.

**Redundancy.** The “identity precludes selection” argument and the “missing ontology, not missing inside” slogan appear almost word for word in intrinsic-nature and bi-aspectual. [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]][[bi-aspectual-ontology|concepts/bi-aspectual-ontology]] Pautz, Cutter and the fragmentation narrative are repeated across all three. Consolidate the argument in one page and have the others summarise it in a sentence and link.

**Calibration drift.**

- intrinsic-nature calls the Map’s preference for quiddities “a considered preference against a live rival.” [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]]
- bi-aspectual says physical-completeness “demonstrates through the convergence of no-go theorems” that “no hidden physical variables are missing.” [[bi-aspectual-ontology|concepts/bi-aspectual-ontology]] That overstates the no-go literature: nonlocal and contextual hidden-variable theories such as Bohmian mechanics are not excluded.
- bi-aspectual also says Gleason’s theorem shows “there is no alternative” to the Born rule. [[bi-aspectual-ontology|concepts/bi-aspectual-ontology]] Gleason assumes Hilbert-space dimension ≥3 and noncontextual additivity.
- The Russellian monism article calls Howell’s objection “fatal.” [[russellian-monism|concepts/russellian-monism]]

The three pages are written at different confidence levels about overlapping claims.

**Overall positioning.** The Map’s stated strategy is coherent in outline: accept the structuralist premise, reject the identity of consciousness with quiddities, and add an interacting aspect. As currently written, though, the pages do not agree on whether that aspect is *at* the intrinsic base, *beside* it, or *at a local interface* unrelated to it.
