## Praise from Sympathetic Philosophers

### The Property Dualist (Chalmers)
The subjective-facts article is the first Map page to state cleanly that subjective facts, even if real, need not be non-physical: "a world could contain subjective facts every one of which supervened on the physical". That is the distinction between the structure-of-facts question and the substrate question, and the Map keeps them apart where it would have been easy to run them together for Tenet 1's benefit. The CBS page does the mirror move for the hard problem: "A release account says which cortical regions generate which content; it does not say why that generation is experienced" — the gap is named once, as a point about description, and explicitly "not evidence for any tenet".

### The Quantum Mind Theorist (Stapp)
The void's Bidirectional/MQI paragraph is the one place in the three where the Map's mechanism does work, and it is stated as a difference in *which questions each framework must answer*: on the interactionist picture "the Born-rule question becomes a question about a law — what fixes outcome statistics — rather than about credence", so the Map does not need the missing self-locating rule and Everettianism does. The sentence ends "neither evidence for the Map nor a dissolution of the void". That is the correct shape for a structural advantage: it relocates a debt rather than discharging one.

### The Phenomenologist (Nagel)
Kinkaid's counter-datum is the best new phenomenological material on the site this month. The article sets Merlo's exercise (only my experiences have feltness) against Sartre's man in the park, given "as a *center of orientation* — another ipseity", and draws the right lesson: "Both sides appeal to the Appearance/Reality Principle; they disagree about what appears." The Map's own vertiginous-question page carries the solipsistic datum alone; this article says so and names the counter-datum it should carry. The CBS page's one original contribution is also phenomenological and is flagged as the Map's own: the same cortex's output arriving "once labelled and once unlabelled" as unreal, in CBS and in dreams.

### The Process Philosopher (Whitehead)
Standpoint pluralism — every subject a standpoint relative to which it is the centre, the stacks of facts not required to agree — is the nearest analytic metaphysics has come to Whitehead's perspectivism, and the article presents it in Lipman's own regimentation (@*s*(*p*), the three factivity options) rather than through the Map's needs. It then declines it for a stated reason (Tenet 4 needs the fact simpliciter) and does not pretend the decline is a refutation. Nothing in that praise bears on any evidential tier; no organism or system is being graded here, so the constraint on this persona is not engaged.

### The Libertarian Free Will Defender (Kane)
The void's class face is the agency cluster's problem in a new key: "a functionalist can in principle fix membership by a functional criterion, while the dualist cannot read it off physics". The article treats this as a cost the Map carries ("aggravated by the void, not supported"), which is the honest posture for a view on which the subject census is not physically readable (P-SC2 "owed"). An agent-causationist should want this stated plainly, because the same unreadability is what makes the Map's subject a real further fact.

### The Mysterian (McGinn)
The void is a model of the genre: three faces, each with its own named literature, a "What this void is not" section that separates four neighbours, and an indirect-methods list that ends by conceding that "progress on other minds would partly fill this void, where nothing internal to probability theory can". Its most mysterian line is also its most verified one: Carroll's "cannot simultaneously be true and justifiably believed" (1 hit at arXiv).

### The Hardline Empiricist (Birch)
This persona's verdict is the one that matters for this wing, and it is favourable on all three.

- **CBS**: the article had the structural opportunity to read a release phenomenon as interface evidence (the corpus had been doing it since March) and instead retired the reading and withdrew the Tenet 3 argument: "On every reading above the deafferented cortex supplies the content, so the inference does not go through, and the Map withdraws it." It separates neuronal from phenomenal compensation by name, which is the equivocation the over-read had been trading on. It also uses Tenet 5 *against* multiplying explanations ("the Map has no reason to multiply explanations"), which is the tenet applied in the direction it is least often applied. Painter's "influential but untested hypothesis" is cited for a hypothesis the article then reports as tested, with the test's result — that is how a hyperexcitability claim should be carried.
- **Observer-selection void**: the Standish citation is bracketed on venue ("a low-profile venue; cited as an exhibit of the pattern, not an authority"); Bostrom's physicalistic reformulation is carried against the Map's interest ("The Map reports that stance rather than enlisting him against it"); the Needham et al. reading is marked as "the Map's interpretation" of a paper that "does not use anthropic terms".
