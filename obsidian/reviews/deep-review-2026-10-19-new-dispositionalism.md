### Critical Issues Found (calibration and attribution; all correctable inside the framework)

- **C1, the lead overstated the critics' concession.** "Its critics (Clarke; Whittle; Franklin; Jaster) argue that it captures a real capacity but not the ability at issue" is true of Clarke (his abstract grants "a kind of capacity") and of Whittle (global abilities). It is false of Franklin, who grants the analysis only for the sake of argument, and of Jaster, whose 2017 paper with Vetter denies that any dispositional account of abilities succeeds. **Fixed** to "argue that whatever it captures is not the ability at issue in the free-will dispute", which is true of all four (−2 words).
- **C2, a critic's reading was stated as the view's content (L97).** "The dispositionalists preserve the general ability" is Whittle's reading, and SEP §3.4 offers it only on the defenders' behalf ("might contend"). Fara's analysis is indexed to circumstances, and Whittle herself records that Fara claims to analyse the ability pertinent to free will. **Fixed**: "the dispositionalists preserve a dispositional ability, on Whittle's reading only a general one". The conclusion ("no free gift to the Map's leeway wing") now rests on a point every party accepts: the preserved ability is dispositional, not a modal alternative that survives the prior sign. It no longer rests on a contested critic's reading (+7).
- **C3, the "modal" requirement was ambiguous against fixed-past compatibilism (L87).** "Holding the past and laws fixed, the agent can act otherwise" is a sentence Vihvelin affirms in her own sense (ch. 7, "Fixed Past Compatibilism"), so "new dispositionalism denies that this is needed" was false on one reading. **Fixed**: "the other act is consistent with the actual past and the laws", which new dispositionalism does deny is needed (+1).
