## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Source misrepresentation: Sherrington's position on interaction (L27, L51–55, L67, L91, L95).** The article called Sherrington the "agnostic pole" who "asserted a limit of natural science and stopped there", "did not assert that mind causally steers the brain", "took the second option" (suspension of judgment), "asserted no interaction", and was "silent" on Tenets 2 and 3. The 1940 text says otherwise:
  - p. 200: after canvassing parallelism and "the non-possumus inference that mind is an epiphenomenon": "It may be, although we do not know 'how', that mind does influence energy, and so does share in the management of the body."
  - p. 201 (the continuation of the passage the 09-02 review certified as "deflationary"): "How it has counted would seem to be that the finite mind has influenced its individual's 'doing'."
  - p. 232: "the immediate meaning of the finite mind in the body would seem to be to influence acts. Its influence upon acts is what, we would think, has given it survival-value"; "We felt we had to look upon their contact as a 'contact utile'. The 'how' of it, perennial crux, we left."
  - p. 262: "unless mind have working contact — 'contact utile' — with energy, how can energy serve it?"
  - pp. 318–319: "I have therefore to think of the brain as an organ of liaison between energy and mind, but not as a converter of energy into mind or vice versa"; "on these admissions we become committed to dualism."
  - p. 354: our being is "a unity whose behaviour we can follow only by including the two concepts and treating them as having 'contact utile' each with the other."

  Sherrington therefore held a hedged, untheorised working interactionism. He refused the *mechanism* ("The blank of the 'how' of mind's leverage on matter", p. 232) and refused *theology* (inserting "an immortal soul" is a "trespass", p. 355). His dualism is epistemic in scope ("dualism or monism in a limited scope, that is, with no reference to reality", p. 324). **Resolution:** the section is rewritten as "The Agnostic Pole: What Sherrington Did and Did Not Claim". The "agnostic pole" label is kept but defined precisely: agnostic about ultimate reality and about mechanism, *not* about contact. Sherrington's own hedges are quoted as part of the claim, and his own p. 200 phrase ("as though agnosticism should believe, even in the field in which it declares it has no knowledge") is used to name the stance. The license question becomes "what does correlation license?" (Sherrington: a second category plus untheorised contact; the students: evidence, then mechanism). The lead, the gradient sentence in "Two consequences", and the RSP were corrected to match. RSP now says Sherrington stands nearer Tenet 3 than his reputation suggests and claims no more than that. The Tenet 2 sentence records his single remark, that "the physicist releases the individual" into "a probability system" (p. 201), as clearing room for mind without proposing a mechanism.

  The 2026-09-02 ledger line "context confirms the evolutionary-efficacy reading the article had hedged toward" read only the immediate sentence: the next sentence makes the efficacy claim explicit. The 09-02 Stability Note "Sherrington's silence on Tenets 2-3 is a deliberate, correct calibration" is **withdrawn** for Tenet 3 on primary-text grounds. This is a resolution that was incorrect, so re-flagging it is legitimate under the convergence rule.

- **Dropped qualifier / embellishment: Smith 2001 (L77, L81).** The article said Smith argues Eccles's move "did not solve the problem Descartes posed so much as relocate it". It also said the lineage's dualism was, for Smith, "transmitted intellectual culture as much as individual conviction". Neither "relocate" nor the culture-transmission gloss is in the abstract, which is the only level read. The abstract also scopes the verdict: "(at least in the form Eccles espoused)". **Resolution:** re-written to track the abstract: the tradition is "initiated by Thomas Henry Huxley", the mind–brain problem "troubled him throughout his career", and the solution provides, "at least in the form Eccles espoused", "no solution to the problem posed by Descartes". The L81 phrase "relocated, not solved" became "no solution".

- **Characterisation error: Arshavsky 2006 (L79).** The article glossed covert dualism as "an unwillingness to treat consciousness as a tractable biological problem" and ended with an unresolved "religious roots" caveat. The abstract (Europe PMC) locates covert dualism's roots in neuroscience's own paradigm: cognition performed "by neural networks consisting of simple elements". The scare-quoted "Scientific roots" in the title marks that thesis. **Resolution:** gloss re-tracked to the abstract wording ("unwillingness to discuss neural mechanisms of consciousness, leaving the problem of consciousness to psychologists and philosophers"), the network-paradigm thesis added, and the unverifiable religious-roots sentence removed.

- **Wrong page locator: Eccles 1989 "spiritual beings" passage (L65).** It was cited at p. 241 (Pratt-carried aggregator locator, per the 09-01 research note). Google Books search-within on the live index `9GWIAgAAQBAJ` (control: "psychons" returned PA224/PA226) returns "we have to recognize that we are spiritual beings with souls existing in a spiritual world (Section 10.7) as well as material beings with bodies and brains existing in a material world" at **PA253**, in the "Addendum: reflections and imaginings" (contents: Addendum 251, References 259, Index 281). The two snippets overlap and together give the full sentence. **Resolution:** the passage is quoted verbatim with "[...]" for the section cross-reference, located at p. 253 and marked verified. The divine-creation passage stays at p. 237: the book's own index reads "Divine Creation, unique self 237" and "soul 237, 243". Its wording stays marked as secondary-transcribed because the text itself was not snippet-visible. Dead Google Books ids `rM68T7L-lY4C` and `jcDF0QEACAAJ` returned 0 for the control and are not evidence of absence.

- **"Sherrington would have conceded the third and fourth critics' starting point" (L81).** Arshavsky's starting point is that consciousness is neurally tractable and covert dualism obstructs it, which is not something the 1947 "no basis" sentence concedes. **Resolution:** narrowed to Smith: "Smith's verdict that the problem remains unsolved is one Sherrington himself stated in 1947."
