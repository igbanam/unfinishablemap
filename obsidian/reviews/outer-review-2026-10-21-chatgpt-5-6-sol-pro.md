# Audit of “Intrinsic Nature”

**Review date:** 21 October 2026  
**Overall verdict:** **Major revision required.**

## Version and scope

The live page records a last-modified date of **13 October 2026**. The changelog shows, however, that the 13 October change was principally a zero-word integration link to the newly created article on structuralism about phenomenal character. The most recent substantive recalibration of the article’s central argument appears to have been on **7 October**, when “robust and not a temporary gap in knowledge” was softened to “robust across physics as practised,” and the permanent limitation of physics was explicitly reclassified as a methodological bet rather than a theorem. That was a valuable correction, but several later sections still reason as though the stronger, pre-correction claim had been established. [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]]

The article’s defensible core is:

> On a Russell–Eddington structuralist interpretation, present physical theory may underdetermine the absolutely intrinsic realization of its structural-dynamical roles.

What does **not** follow without further argument is that:

1. every property described by physics is relational;
2. dispositions require categorically distinct bearers;
3. physical inquiry is permanently incapable of characterising intrinsic nature;
4. intrinsic categorical properties must exist;
5. consciousness is the one intrinsic nature known directly; or
6. the Map’s distinct, outcome-selecting consciousness belongs at the same “intrinsic base.”

The article is unusually good at conceding some of these limits—especially in its “gap creates room; it does not furnish the room” discipline note—but its argument repeatedly outruns those concessions. [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]]

## 1. The organising taxonomy is the central weakness

### The article collapses four distinctions into one

Its presentation effectively aligns:

- relational with structural and dispositional;
- intrinsic with non-structural and categorical;
- role with physical description;
- role-occupant with quiddity.

Those divisions do not coincide. The contemporary Russellian-monism literature expressly warns that the structural/non-structural distinction cannot simply be identified with the relational/non-relational or intrinsic/extrinsic distinctions. Shape, for example, is normally classified as non-relational and intrinsic, yet physics can characterise shape structurally. This is why the literature often turns instead to the distinction between **relatively intrinsic** and **absolutely intrinsic** properties. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/russellian-monism/)

At minimum, the article needs to distinguish four independent questions:

1. **Relationality:** Does a property depend on relations to other entities?
2. **Structural characterisation:** Is it individuated by a pattern, role or isomorphism class?
3. **Modality:** Is it categorical, dispositional or both?
4. **Epistemic access:** Is it described by present physical theory?

A property can be intrinsic but structurally characterisable, non-relational but dispositional, or categorical without being an unknowable quiddity. Conversely, a relation can be fundamental without requiring independently individuated categorical relata.

### “Dispositions require bearers” does not establish categorical bases

The article’s most important unsupported move is:

> relations require relata, dispositions require bearers, a role requires an occupant.

Taken minimally, this says that a disposition is instantiated somehow. It does not show that the instantiator has a **categorically distinct**, non-dispositional nature. The dispositions literature leaves open whether a disposition can be its own causal basis, whether one disposition can ground another, and whether fundamental properties may be “bare” dispositions without separate categorical bases. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/dispositions/)

Pure-powers theorists give the direct reply the article needs to confront: powers can stand in mutual manifestation relations that constitute stability, objects and apparent bearers. On that view, there is a web of powers rather than a base/substratum architecture. Roselli and Leconte-Chevillard’s 2026 treatment explicitly presents this as a metaphysically robust alternative to non-dispositional substrata. [Springer+1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-026-05466-4)

Powerful-qualities theories pose a second problem. They identify a property’s qualitative and dispositional aspects rather than treating one as the categorical occupant of the other’s role. The Map’s own laws-and-dispositions article adopts this identity theory and says that the qualitative aspect supplies genuine phenomenal causal power. That commitment is difficult to reconcile with *Intrinsic Nature*’s presentation of categorical occupant and dispositional role as separate sides of a fundamental divide. [[consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-laws-and-dispositions|topics/consciousness-and-the-metaphysics-of-laws-and-dispositions]]

**Assessment:** The article currently defines the alternatives in a way that already favours quidditism. Pure powers and powerful qualities are not optional side objections; they directly challenge its basic ontology of role and occupant.

## 2. “Physics describes only structure” remains an interpretive thesis

The historical Russellian starting point is legitimate. Russell wrote that physics is highly abstract, reveals mathematical characteristics, and does not disclose the intrinsic character of its subject matter. But this supports an epistemic underdetermination claim—not automatically the article’s stronger modal conclusion that structural description is all physics could ever express. [Project Gutenberg+2](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/77427/77427-h/77427-h.htm)

The article now concedes that permanence is a methodological bet. Yet it subsequently says that:

- no present or completed physics delivers intrinsic nature;
- both horns of Hempel’s dilemma necessarily miss quiddities;
- the relevant gap may be permanent;
- physics has become more relational rather than less;
- the possibility of physics characterising fundamental nature “has not been achieved.”

The opening caveat therefore does not control the downstream argument. The article alternates between:

1. **Modest claim:** current physics characterises entities primarily through mathematical, nomological and causal roles; and
2. **Strong claim:** physics is constitutively limited to such roles.

Only the first has been established.

Recent philosophy of physics also complicates the claim that advances in physics merely deepen a quiddistic silence. Adlam argues that general-relativistic and quantum-reference-frame formalisms suggest ways in which non-modal features might arise as internal views on modal structure without appeal to quiddities. This does not prove that structuralism is correct, but it shows that physical theory can contribute to the modal/non-modal question rather than merely leaving an invariant categorical remainder. [Springer+1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-025-05190-5)

The article also needs to distinguish:

- **the theory’s representational vocabulary**;
- **the reference of its terms**;
- **the metaphysical nature of the represented entities**.

A theory can characterise mass through its nomological role while successfully referring to a property whose nature is not exhausted by that characterisation. Coleman’s recent formulation, for example, treats science as giving an extrinsic or dispositional characterisation of a property without thereby making the property itself merely extrinsic. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44204-026-00450-8)

**Assessment:** The structuralist premise is a serious philosophical position, but the article presents it too often as a lesson delivered by physics rather than one metaphysical interpretation of what physical theories disclose.

## 3. The treatment of ontic structural realism is improved but still mis-targeted

The article commendably acknowledges moderate OSR: objects can remain as relata while lacking intrinsic identities or quiddities. That concession defeats the simple “relations require relata” response. The Map’s dedicated OSR article states the point even more sharply: an objection aimed at relations without relata “refutes the version nobody needs to hold.” [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]]

But *Intrinsic Nature*’s first proposed falsifier still asks for an OSR that shows that “relations require no relata and structure no occupant.” This is inconsistent with the stronger treatment elsewhere on the site. Moderate OSR need not eliminate relata. It need only deny that relata possess independently individuating categorical natures. That is enough to block the article’s inference from structure to quiddities.

Current OSR literature also contains more developed replies than the article indicates:

- Meyer reconceives apparent objects as modes of a single world-structure.
- Hamlin proposes an ante rem ontology intended to address objecthood, representation and causation.
- Adlam develops modal OSR using relational observables and internal perspectives.
- Jiang offers a structuralist grounding of activity, mechanisms and interventionist causation. [Springer+3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-025-05425-5)

None of these proposals should be treated as having settled the debate. Hamlin’s mathematical-universe route, for example, incurs a substantial collapse problem between mathematical and physical structure. Meyer’s existence monism is metaphysically revisionary. Adlam expressly leaves open questions. But their existence means that the article cannot frame the contemporary choice as quiddistic occupants versus unintelligible free-floating relations.

The dedicated OSR article is substantially more balanced. It recognises that Newman’s problem cuts both ways: if pure structure cannot individuate the physically intended relations, epistemic structural realism cannot simply place the individuator in an unknown base without explaining what that base contributes. It therefore concludes that the Map has an available reply, not a refutation of OSR, and still owes an account of its own individuator. That conclusion should be imported into *Intrinsic Nature*. [[ontic-structural-realism|concepts/ontic-structural-realism]]

**Assessment:** The relevant potential defeater is not “relations without relata.” It is:

> a coherent account of objects, individuation, modality, causation and empirical content in which relata or structures need no independently individuated categorical quiddities.

That stronger structuralist challenge remains open.

## 4. Hempel’s dilemma is being asked to do work it cannot do

The article treats Hempel’s dilemma as if it reinforces the structural/intrinsic gap: current physics is incomplete; future physics is unknown; therefore neither can ground an ontologically complete physicalism.

That is too quick. Hempel’s dilemma is primarily a problem for one way of **defining “physical” by reference to physical theory**. It does not independently establish:

- quidditism;
- the existence of a categorical base;
- permanent physical ignorance;
- the falsity of causal closure; or
- dualism.

The current physicalism literature explicitly treats Hempel’s dilemma as showing, at most, that a particular theory-relative definition is inadequate. Object-based, structuralist, causal-nomological and other accounts remain available. Structuralist definitions are themselves offered as responses to Hempel: physical vocabulary may be restricted to logical, mathematical, causal or nomological terms without requiring a complete present theory. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/physicalism/)

The article therefore moves too rapidly from:

> “A completed physical theory is not presently specifiable”

to:

> “No completed physics could describe intrinsic nature.”

The second conclusion depends on the disputed methodological premise, not on Hempel’s dilemma.

The treatment of closure is also compressed beyond what the cited Montero paper supports. Montero distinguishes several principles: sufficient-physical-cause closure, strong closure excluding nonphysical causes, and two-way closure excluding cross-boundary causation. She also discusses how probabilistic quantum events complicate formulations of sufficiency. Her argument is that closure principles are difficult to formulate and justify—not simply that “current closure is false and future closure vacuous.” [New Dualism Archive](https://www.newdualism.org/papers/B.Montero/Varieties-of-Causal-Closure.htm)

Nor does incompleteness of current physics entail failure of causal closure. A theory may be incomplete in content while all physical events nevertheless have physically sufficient or probability-fixing antecedents. Conversely, a completed structural physics might fail strong closure if nonphysical causes overdetermine or probabilistically influence its events. These questions must be separated.

**Assessment:** Hempel’s dilemma can support epistemic modesty about the extension of “physical.” It cannot be used as independent evidence that physics omits categorical quiddities.

## 5. Quiddity inscrutability is conditional, not a general consequence of intrinsic nature

The article’s role-swapping argument assumes that:

1. quiddities are individuated independently of their causal-nomological roles;
2. the connection between a quiddity and its role is contingent;
3. distinct quiddities could exchange roles;
4. all external access proceeds through those roles; and
5. no conceptual, scientific or acquaintance-based route fixes the identity of the occupant.

That describes a strong **recombinatorial quidditism**. It is not entailed by the bare existence of intrinsic properties.

Non-recombinatorial quidditists can hold that a property has an intrinsic nature while its relation to its nomological role is necessary. Dispositional essentialists identify properties through their powers. Powerful-qualities theorists deny the separation between quality and power. Smith’s taxonomy explicitly distinguishes individuation quidditism from recombinatorial quidditism, and Lewis’s 2009 contribution is associated specifically with “Ramseyan Humility,” not with establishing every form of quiddity inscrutability. [Springer+1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s12136-022-00524-1.pdf)

The 2026 symposium literature shows that this is an active dispute:

- Liu argues that unknowable quiddities and unknowable combination principles threaten Russellian monism’s explanatory fruitfulness.
- Wu replies that ignorance of fundamental entities need not prevent understanding the mechanisms connecting them to observable phenomena.
- Wishon disputes the assumption that every Russellian view is committed to exhaustive physical structuralism or inaccessible quiddities.
- Coleman argues that the debate strengthens non-mysterian panpsychist or panqualityist versions rather than mysterian Russellian physicalism. [Springer+3](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44204-026-00447-3)

The article’s statement that the inscrutability “may be a permanent boundary” is permissible as a hypothesis. Presenting the boundary as the natural result of accepting intrinsic nature is not.

### The introspective exception contains an unargued bridge

The article says that phenomenal consciousness may be the one intrinsic nature known directly because we know pain by feeling it and red by seeing it. It commendably notes that representationalists, eliminativists and phenomenal structuralists dispute this.

But even granting direct acquaintance with phenomenal qualities, two conclusions remain distinct:

1. **Acquaintance conclusion:** we know the intrinsic character of a phenomenal property.
2. **Russellian conclusion:** that phenomenal property is, constitutes, or reveals the quiddity occupying a fundamental physical role.

The first does not entail the second. A dualist can accept acquaintance while holding that phenomenal properties are additional properties rather than the categorical realizers of mass, charge or spin. A structuralist about phenomenology can deny that acquaintance reveals a non-structural quality. A Russellian monist needs an additional identification or constitution premise.

Lewis’s possible exception to humility similarly requires more than acquaintance: it depends on a suitable panphenomenalism plus a revelation or identification thesis. [JSTOR+1](https://www.jstor.org/stable/pdf/26445206.pdf)

**Assessment:** The article correctly identifies the hinge, but it should state the missing bridge as an explicit premise rather than allowing “direct acquaintance” and “knowledge of a physical quiddity” to run together.

## 6. The presentation of Russellian monism is too narrow and too adversarial

The standard contemporary characterisation of Russellian monism combines three theses:

1. structuralism about physics;
2. realism about quiddities; and
3. the relevance of quiddities to phenomenal consciousness.

The third thesis does not require every Russellian monist to identify consciousness straightforwardly with particle quiddities. Quiddities may constitute consciousness, partly constitute it, ground it, or combine with structural facts in its constitution. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/russellian-monism/)

This matters because the related *Russellian Monism* article says that the programme’s variants “share only the structuralist premise.” Under the standard taxonomy, views that reject realism about quiddities or their relevance to consciousness are not straightforward variants of Russellian monism at all. The page has grouped Russellian monism with a wider family of structural-gap views and then treated the family’s breadth as internal fragmentation. [[russellian-monism|concepts/russellian-monism]]

There is genuine disagreement among panpsychist, panprotopsychist, cosmopsychist, neutral-monist and Russellian-physicalist proposals. But diversity within a research family is not by itself evidence against the shared framework. The site should distinguish:

- underdetermination by the structuralist premise;
- multiple legitimate completions of a research programme;
- mutually inconsistent theories;
- and views only loosely adjacent to Russellian monism.

### “Identity blocks causal selection” is tenet-relative

The article’s sharpest divergence says that if consciousness simply were the intrinsic nature of physical entities, it could not select among outcomes because it would already be the inside of whatever physical structure does.

This is not a neutral objection to Russellian monism. It presupposes the Map’s own conception of consciousness as an additional outcome-selecting variable. A Russellian power monist can instead hold that phenomenal nature is identical with a causally efficacious power. On that view, there need be no second selector standing outside the property’s manifestation. [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]]

The Howell contingency objection remains serious, but it has substantial replies. Some deny that contingent role occupancy would make a property causally irrelevant; others deny that the quiddity-role relation is contingent. Hawthorne, Chalmers, Mørch, Gundersen, Kadić, and Alter and Coleman represent these two families of response. The related Map articles call the contingency argument a “fatal weakness” without adequately presenting this literature. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/russellian-monism/)

The proper conclusion is therefore narrower:

> Russellian identity does not provide the particular cross-domain, quantum-outcome-selection role required by the Map’s Tenets 2–4.

That shows incompatibility with the Map’s explanatory specification. It does not establish that identity theories lack causal efficacy.

### The comparison of burdens is asymmetric

The article mentions the combination problem but gives comparatively little space to the Map’s own debts. Its direct comparison page is fairer: it calls Minimal Quantum Interaction a mechanism sketch rather than a complete theory, acknowledges the interaction problem, recognises the decoherence challenge, and concedes that the Map incurs additional evolutionary and causal-closure commitments. [[russellian-monism-versus-bi-aspectual-dualism|topics/russellian-monism-versus-bi-aspectual-dualism]]

Likewise, the Map’s fundamental-subject strategy avoids micro-subject combination only by taking unified subjects as primitive. It then owes accounts of:

- why such subjects exist;
- how each subject pairs with one body;
- how one subject couples across a spatially distributed neural system;
- how its influence preserves the relevant statistics;
- and what psychophysical law connects qualitative character to particular outcomes.

The site’s *Russellian Monism* article now acknowledges much of this parallel ledger, but *Intrinsic Nature* does not import it. [[russellian-monism|concepts/russellian-monism]]

**Assessment:** Russellian monism has substantial combination, constitution and causal-integration problems. The article nevertheless overstates their decisiveness while assessing the Map under a more permissive “location plus future mechanism” standard.

## 7. Tenet-protective bracketing

The article contains several good safeguards: it calls OSR a live rival, marks permanence as a methodological bet, concedes the contested character of introspection, and explicitly says that an ontological remainder is not positive evidence for consciousness. These are meaningful improvements, not cosmetic hedges. [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]]

There are nevertheless five ways in which the framing still protects the site’s commitments.

### 7.1 The concession is not propagated

The article concedes that permanent physical silence is not a theorem, but later arguments treat it as a settled boundary. The hedge is local; the dependency remains global.

### 7.2 Rival taxonomies are introduced in Map-friendly language

“Role versus occupant” and “disposition versus bearer” make quiddities appear mandatory before pure powers, powerful qualities or moderate OSR have been heard.

### 7.3 The central objection to Russellian monism presupposes Tenet 3

The demand for an independently acting selector is the Map’s theory of mental causation. Russellian monism’s failure to supply that selector is counted as a weakness without first establishing that causal selection must take that form.

### 7.4 Defeater scope is narrowed immediately

The article says that successful eliminative OSR would remove only the ontological-remainder route while leaving Dualism standing. That may be true of the site’s dependency graph, but rhetorically it reduces the significance of a defeater before assessing it. Given how extensively “physics is ontologically partial” is used across the site, loss of the remainder argument would have wider consequences for motivation, coherence and evidential presentation even if the formal Dualism tenet survived. [[ontic-structural-realism|concepts/ontic-structural-realism]]

### 7.5 Problems are outsourced asymmetrically

Russellian monism’s combination, parity and epiphenomenalism problems are invoked as reasons to prefer the Map. The Map’s pairing, psychophysical-law, individuation, Born-rule, decoherence and interface-unity problems are mostly left to linked pages. A concept article may legitimately delegate detail, but it should not count delegated objections against one side while treating delegated answers as sufficient for the other.

**Assessment:** The article no longer argues by simple exclusion, but its comparative burden allocation remains tenet-protective.

## 8. Integration with the bi-aspectual ontology is conceptually incomplete

The integration is extensive at the level of links and vocabulary. It is not yet clear at the level of ontology.

### What exactly is “at the intrinsic base”?

The article describes intrinsic nature as the categorical base that bears physical dispositions. It then says that the Map locates consciousness there **as a distinct interacting aspect rather than identifying it with physical quiddities**.

This generates a dilemma:

- If consciousness is the categorical realizer of physical dispositions, the view approaches Russellian monism or powerful-quality monism.
- If consciousness is ontologically distinct from whatever realizes those dispositions, it is not the intrinsic nature of the physical in the original Russellian sense. It is an additional co-fundamental entity or property interacting with physical structure.

“Located at the intrinsic base” then becomes a metaphor for fundamentality rather than a specified metaphysical relation.

### “Actuality” is presently another role

The bi-aspectual article expressly says that “actuality” names the role of determining which possibility becomes real and is not a mechanism. But *Intrinsic Nature* argues that roles require occupants and that structural description does not tell us what occupies them. The same question therefore applies:

> What is the intrinsic nature of actuality, as opposed to its outcome-selecting role?

Calling consciousness “actuality” does not answer this. It restates the function assigned to consciousness. [[bi-aspectual-ontology|concepts/bi-aspectual-ontology]]

This creates a reflexive version of the article’s own criticism of physics: physics allegedly gives only the structural role of matter, while “actuality” currently gives only the causal role of consciousness.

### The status of “aspect” is unclear

The bi-aspectual article denies that structure and actuality derive from a neutral common substrate and insists that they are ontologically distinct. In ordinary dual-aspect theories, “aspects” are aspects of a shared underlying reality. Without such a bearer, the Map needs to specify whether structure and actuality are:

- substances;
- fundamental properties;
- powers;
- categories of event;
- domains;
- complementary descriptions;
- or terms in a primitive psychophysical relation.

Otherwise “bi-aspectual dualism” risks functioning as a label that combines monist vocabulary with dualist commitments without clarifying their formal relation. [[bi-aspectual-ontology|concepts/bi-aspectual-ontology]]

### An apparent contradiction with powerful qualities

*Intrinsic Nature* and *Bi-Aspectual Ontology* argue that identifying consciousness with physical intrinsic nature would leave it unable to perform independent causal selection. The laws-and-dispositions article, by contrast, adopts powerful qualities and says that identity between qualitative and dispositional aspects is precisely what gives phenomenal character causal power and lets the present quality select its manifestation. [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]]

This may be reconcilable if the site distinguishes:

- **having causal power**, which identity may ground; from
- **being an additional selector among physically open outcomes**, which the Map requires to be ontologically distinct.

But that distinction is not currently stated. As written, one page treats identity as the solution to causal efficacy while another treats identity as the reason genuine selection is impossible.

**Integration verdict:** High cross-link integration; incomplete metaphysical integration. The three articles use the same vocabulary but do not yet supply a single stable account of what the intrinsic base, actuality, powerful quality and physical structure are, or how they relate.

## 9. Citation and reference audit

### 9.1 The Russell quotation should not appear in quotation marks

The sentence attributed to Russell—

> “The physical world is only known as regards certain abstract features of its space-time structure”

—does not appear to be a verbatim sentence from *The Analysis of Matter*. It resembles a compression of several passages. Russell’s verifiable formulations include “Physics, in itself, is exceedingly abstract” and that physics “does not tell us anything as to the intrinsic character” of its material. Elsewhere he says matter is known only through “certain very abstract characteristics.” The existing sentence should be labelled a paraphrase or replaced with an exact, page-specific quotation. [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]]

### 9.2 Lewis is miscredited

The claim that the term or idea of quiddities is “standardly credited to Lewis (2009)” is misleading. The quidditism literature clearly predates Lewis’s paper; Robert Black’s *Against Quidditism* appeared in 2000, and the underlying “whatness” terminology has much older roots. Lewis’s distinctive contribution here is the Ramseyan Humility argument, not the introduction of quiddities into metaphysics. [PhilPapers+1](https://philpapers.org/rec/BLAAQ)

### 9.3 The Pautz reference conflates two manuscripts and a book

Reference 6 describes Pautz’s 2017 reply and parenthetically presents the 2015 *A Dilemma for Russellian Monists about Consciousness* as though it appeared in the Alter and Nagasawa volume. The 2015 work is an unpublished manuscript whose copy identifies itself as a rough first draft dated 15 December 2015. The 2017 work is a separate unpublished reply to Roelofs’s comments. Neither should be bibliographically represented as a chapter in *Consciousness in the Physical World*. [[intrinsic-nature|concepts/intrinsic-nature]]

### 9.4 Hempel’s dilemma lacks its central sources

The references include Montero but neither an original source for Hempel’s formulation nor the major later discussion associated with Crane and Mellor. Since the article builds an entire section around the dilemma, it should cite the standard literature and distinguish the original dilemma from Montero’s separate analysis of closure principles. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy+1](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/physicalism/)

### 9.5 The Eddington attribution is under-documented

The “world of shadows” presentation is broadly consistent with Eddington’s rhetoric, but the article gives only a whole-book citation. Because the phrase is doing historical and argumentative work, it should have a chapter/page citation and be clearly marked as quotation or paraphrase.

### 9.6 The bibliography is stale relative to the argument

The older works remain relevant, but the bibliography does not represent the current state of the debates it surveys. It omits:

- Alter and Pereboom’s recent work on the structuralist premise;
- recent distinctions between recombinatorial and non-recombinatorial quidditism;
- Adlam, Meyer and Hamlin on contemporary OSR;
- Roselli and Leconte-Chevillard on powers ontology;
- the 2026 Liu–Wishon–Wu–Coleman debate about mysterian and non-mysterian Russellian monism;
- the replies to Howell from necessitarian and non-necessitarian Russellian monists. [Springer+7](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11229-025-05425-5)

# Concrete potential improvements

## “Intrinsic Nature”

1. **Replace the opening categorical assertion with a conditional formulation:** “On a Russell–Eddington structuralist interpretation, physics characterises fundamental entities primarily through structural-dynamical roles and may leave their absolutely intrinsic nature underdetermined.”
2. **Add a four-axis taxonomy** separating relational/non-relational, structural/non-structural, dispositional/categorical, and physically characterised/not physically characterised. Do not present these as equivalent divisions.
3. **Replace the current Russell quotation** with a verified verbatim passage, edition and page number, or remove the quotation marks and label the existing sentence as a paraphrase.
