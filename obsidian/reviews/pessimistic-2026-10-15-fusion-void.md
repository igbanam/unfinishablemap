## Executive Summary

The page's quotations are clean. What fails is the *reading* of three sources, plus one example. (1) The common-cause gate, which carries the page's primary tenet connection, is described as "parsimony implemented" with a "winning model". But the cited model averages both causal structures on every trial, and its fitted prior for a common cause is 0.28, which leans *against* the simpler hypothesis. The same section's "no experience of nearly fusing" is contradicted by the page's own research note ("badly dubbed speech feels *wrong*"), and the McGurk sentence assigns individual differences to "weightings" where the cited authors put them in the gate itself, as a disparity threshold. (2) The lead's example, eye and hand, is precisely the case where Hillis found component information is **not** lost, so the most truncation-exposed sentence on the page states the opposite of its own §Component Destruction. (3) The "measured edge" sits at the modality line, but a 2012 study found mandatory fusion into metamers *across* two senses. Separately, "destroyed" and "information the system no longer carries" assert more than discrimination data can show. (4) The Dualism paragraph says fusion "does not touch why the combined content is *experienced* as one", yet the source quoted two sections earlier goes on, in the same sentence, to derive phenomenal unity from access to the fused product. All four fixes fit in 217 words against 1005 of headroom.

### The Quantum Skeptic

"Your Tenets 2/3 paragraph (L94) is hedged, so I will only point out that it aims at the wrong parameter. Under maximum-likelihood combination, a 'slight shift in weighting' moves the estimate *slightly*: the combined estimate is a linear function of the weights. It does not yield 'a different perceived world'. The only flip-like point in this machinery is the common-cause decision near threshold. And Wozny, Beierholm and Shams (2010) found that most observers *sample* the causal structure in proportion to its probability, which is a stochastic selection step. If you want a cheap lever, that is where the leverage is. A precision weight, meanwhile, is implemented as gain across large neural populations. 'Small in parameter space' is not 'small in physical intervention', and the minimality tenet is about the latter."

Recorded under Language Improvements and as a subject idea. Not a priority, because the paragraph disclaims any evidential claim.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

"Notice what Körding's ideal observer actually does: it never collapses to one causal hypothesis. You have imposed your collapse reflex on a mechanism that, in the very paper you cite, declines to collapse. And the parsimony gloss is backwards too: the system's fitted prior *disfavours* the one-cause story (0.28). It fuses only when the evidence earns it, which is how a razor should be used."

The first half is correct about Körding (Issue 1). The Map has a fair rejoinder from the same literature, which the page should use: Wozny et al. (2010) found most observers best described by *probability matching*, which samples one structure per trial, so the 'winner' is drawn by lottery rather than chosen by margin. Whichever account is right, "winning model", read as a decision, is unsupported by the source the page cites.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: The common-cause gate is described against its own sources
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/fusion-void.md`
- **Location**: L90 (the primary tenet bearing), with L46 and L68
- **Problem**:
  - (a) L90 reads "The common-cause gate is parsimony implemented: perception continually chooses between a one-cause and a two-cause model of its inputs, and consciousness receives only the world the winning model implies." The cited paper (Körding et al. 2007, full text) says the model "does not simply decide ... but considers both possibilities on each trial". It also reports a fitted common-cause prior of 0.28, a standing lean *toward two causes*. "Parsimony implemented" and "winning model" both read a decision-plus-simplicity-preference into a model that has neither. The research note's own gap list says the prior "is described from general knowledge of the paper; only the abstract was verified". The unverified description fed straight into the tenet paragraph.
  - (b) The paragraph's closing advice, that felt singleness "should be discounted" whenever it is offered "as evidence of anything", overreaches on the page's own sources: Ernst & Banks, Alais & Burr and Körding all headline *near-optimal* combination. A near-optimal estimator's output is good evidence of a common cause in the conditions it is tuned to. Tenet 5's caution belongs at the edge of that tuning: the ventriloquist's stage and the dubbed film. That relocation is a *better* Tenet 5 argument, and it matches the 2026-10-15 calibration installed at `concepts/census-change-points` L111 ("the tenet only bars simplicity from settling the matter").
  - (c) L46: "There is no experience of nearly fusing." This is an unsourced phenomenological universal, and the page's own research note (L143) records the counterexample: "badly dubbed speech feels *wrong* without the wrongness being locatable in either sense". Wallace et al. (2004) also show the unity verdict is reportable trial by trial, with near-threshold non-unity trials leaving a content signature (repulsion, higher variability). The defensible claim is narrower: at most a sense that something is off, never the comparison.
  - (d) L68: "Different minds run visibly different audiovisual weightings". Mallick et al. place the individual difference in a *disparity threshold* for fusing (NED model) plus sensory noise, which is the common-cause gate, not the weights. They also warn that raw frequencies "confound individual differences and stimulus differences". Corrected, the sentence *strengthens* the page, because the durable personal parameter becomes a setting of the gate the page has just described as hidden.
- **Severity**: High (a, b: primary tenet bearing contradicted by a verified source), Medium (c, d)
- **Recommendation**: The replacements are given verbatim in the Priority List, item 1. Net +107.

## Counterarguments to Address

### "The common-cause gate is the razor running sub-personally"
- **Current content says**: perception implements parsimony, and its verdicts should be discounted.
- **A critic would argue**: the gate's fitted prior favours two causes, its behaviour is near-optimal, and so it is a case of simplicity *working*. That makes it an awkward illustration of a tenet saying simplicity is unreliable.
- **Suggested response**: The tenet's clause is "with incomplete knowledge". The gate is reliable inside the statistics it was tuned on and as compelling as ever, and wrong, on inputs built to mimic them. That is the tenet's claim exactly, with a measured instance (item 1's L90 rewrite).

### "Nothing is lost; the components were never mental contents"
- **Current content says**: the Hillis cross-modal result "absorbs that objection and sharpens the void" (L60).
- **A critic would argue**: the reply shows only that access *could* have been retained.
- **Suggested response**: The reply is stronger than the page states. Single-cue stimuli are discriminable when presented alone and stop being discriminable once combined, so a personal-level capacity is measurably lost on combination. The reply earns "lost to perceptual judgment", which is why Issue 3(b) rescopes rather than retracts.

### Falsifiers
- **Current content says**: nothing explicit. The page ends on "Approaches to the Edge".
- **A critic would argue**: a void without stated falsifiers is "not even wrong".
- **Suggested response**: The page's empirical edge already *was* corrected (Prsa), which is the best evidence that the void makes testable claims. A short "What would challenge this" paragraph would name trained discrimination of within-modality metamers and an introspective single-cue report that predicts discrimination.
