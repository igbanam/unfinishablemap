## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found (all FIXED in this file)

1. **Tollock et al. (2025) mischaracterized: "brief", "mild", "non-terminal", "trigger-linked" and the 4.1% "terminal" subtype.** The raw abstract (Crossref JATS, DOI 10.1093/geroni/igaf122.2914) reports event *types* (orientation 67.8%, old memories 34.8%, functional abilities 27.7%, nonverbal 25.1%, and "terminal lucidity (memories consistent with near-death experiences) (4.1%)") and lists *potential* triggers. It reports **no durations, no severity grading and no timing relative to death**. The article had stated that the events were "predominantly mild and non-terminal" and "mostly brief, modest", that only 4.1% "met a terminal-lucidity definition", and that the episodes' "predominantly brief, trigger-linked profile" favoured neurochemical mechanisms. These claims appeared in the lead, Modern Systematic Data, Materialist Counter-Proposals, levels 1–3, the levels summary, Current Research Gaps and the Trajectory. Level 3 used the 4.1% as a *proximity-to-death* measure, but the category is defined by *content* (NDE-like memories). The error ran *against* the Map's thesis, so the defect is source fidelity, not slippage. **FIXED**: all loci now report what the abstract says. Level 3 now uses Batthyány and Greyson's retrospective median survival (2–24 h) and states that the prospective abstract reports no timing. The thesis was recalibrated, not reversed (see Calibration below).
2. **"The first prospective study" was contested (×4).** MacLeod (2009, *Palliative & Supportive Care* 7(4):513–516) prospectively observed 100 consecutive hospice deaths, and Batthyány and Greyson 2021 call it "the only prospective study published to date". Gilmore-Bykovskyi et al. (2025, *The Gerontologist* 65(9), gnaf178, online 1 Aug 2025) is a peer-reviewed prospective study of advanced dementia with video recording. **FIXED**: the wording is now "the 2025 prospective studies", and "came entirely from retrospective" is now "almost entirely". Gilmore-Bykovskyi et al. was added in one sentence (nine validated episodes in three of 20 participants across 539 observations, with marked verbal recovery in every one) and as reference 19. This also balances the frequency picture, since the two studies disagree on how common episodes are.
3. **Misattribution: "loss-of-function lesion premise" was credited to Christopher (2022).** The phrase is **Masi's** and appears verbatim in the Masi (2023) abstract (Europe PMC full text PMC10641890). Christopher 2022 contains **zero** occurrences of "lesion" (full PDF grep). The 2026-06-05 ledger said "both attributions stand", but that was asserted without a grep. **FIXED**: the phrase is now credited to Masi. Christopher is retained with his stance labelled ("presses terminal lucidity against it from an explicitly soul-based dualism"; his explanation draws on mediumship reports).
4. **Batthyány & Greyson (2021) sample description fabricated or conflated.** The article said "survey of 900 nursing and medical staff ... in Austria, Germany, and Switzerland" and "witnessing at least one episode at rates between 61% and 100%". The full paper (UVA DOPS PDF of record) has no "900" and no 61–100% witness rates. In the paper, 221 respondents accessed an online survey and 187 completed it. Respondents were formal *and informal* caregivers, recruited through directories in nine European countries and the US plus online forums. The sample was *selected* on having witnessed an episode, so a witness rate cannot be computed from it. The 61% figure matches an unrelated family-caregiver pilot (A-LIST, N = 480), not this paper. **FIXED**: the paragraph was rewritten to match the paper and the 61–100% sentence was deleted.
5. **Dropped qualifier "reported by observers" (×2).** The abstract says complete remission "was reported by observers of the lucid episode". The article had "More than 80% of cases showed complete remission" and "dataset found that more than 80% showed". **FIXED** in both places.
6. **Unverifiable Murray quotations.** "implies that consciousness can exist independently of the brain" and "a speculative leap" appear in no accessible raw source (AEI repost teaser, Science & Culture, Skeptic, Emerson Green, Mind Matters; the WSJ is paywalled). The 06-05 ledger's "accurate" verdict for "speculative leap" does not survive a raw grep of the Science & Culture page it named. "Newtonian physics in 1887" was quoted but the original reads "as Newtonian physics *was* in 1887". **FIXED**: the 1887 comparison is now paraphrased without quote marks and the unverifiable concession sentence was removed. The WSJ title "Can Science Reckon with the Human Soul?" (16 Oct 2025) was verified.
7. **Autopsy overstatement / internal inconsistency.** L96 said "in many cases verified by autopsy" while L98 said "some cases". The PSI Encyclopedia notes that such patients "cannot easily be made subjects of ... autopsies", and Batthyány & Greyson and Mashour have none. **FIXED**: now "in some historical cases documented at autopsy". The levels summary now names "historical autopsy cases and retrospective surveys".
8. **Internal contradiction in The Evidential Trajectory.** The lead-in said "evidential force has grown substantially" and the closing line said "growing visibility has not been matched by growing confirmatory force". **FIXED**: the lead-in now reads "visibility and evidence base have grown".
9. **Prediction 3 attributed unsupported specifics to the Batthyány dataset.** "patients recognised family members and recalled specific events from years or decades prior": the B&G paper has no "recogni", "decades" or "autobiograph". **FIXED**: observers' reported return of memory is now credited to B&G and the recognition detail to the case-report literature generally.
