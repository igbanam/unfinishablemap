## Executive Summary

The page's best work holds. The Verification Paradox is a real and well-placed idea, and the "Conversely" paragraph (L71) honestly admits the void collapses on one horn. Four problems remain.

1. **A false verbatim quote.** L63 quotes Nisbett and Wilson as saying subjects are "unaware of their own unawareness". The 1977 paper does not contain that string. The 2026-07-17 deep review de-quoted the same phrase on `voids/mood-void`, but the fix never reached this page, which is now the only live page with the quoted form.
2. **The void is stated more broadly than the argument supports.** The core asymmetry claim (L61, "Emotional disclosure resists this") ignores counterexamples on the page itself: dangerousness, listed at L41, is open to non-affective checks. The lead (L37) offers two options, irreplaceable access or systematic illusion, and leaves out the middle position that the page's own cited source defends.
3. **A report limit is used to claim a fact about content.** In the Phenomenology section (L77), the poverty of articulation is used to assert that propositional language is "categorically wrong-shaped" for what emotions disclose. That conflicts with L41, where what emotions disclose is evaluative properties that predicates name.
4. **A Relation claim the register no longer allows.** L83 says epistemic emotions' role "would be inexplicable" if they were epiphenomenal. Both the page it cites (`topics/emotion-and-dualism` L89) and P-MC1 concede that the phenomenal-concept version of epiphenomenalism survives. The co-optimization page (revised 10-19) says the Map does not claim co-optimization is refuted.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist
Churchland's rejection of "disclosure" vocabulary is bedrock, and the page frames it fairly ("Under materialism ... reduces to a question about functional states and their reliability", L81). Her sharper point is about the evidence the page itself uses. It anchors its sceptic in confabulation research (L63), and then in the next section it uses the "overwhelming" sense of knowing (L75) as the void's signature. If introspective feelings of insight are as unreliable as L63 says, the felt depth of affective contact tells us nothing about what was contacted. The page uses the confabulation finding against emotional knowledge and then sets it aside when describing the phenomenology. That is a reason to scope L77 (Issue 3), not a bedrock disagreement.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist
Dennett would aim at L77. "It was beautiful" captures almost nothing of the experience, but that is equally true of "it was red". Heterophenomenology records the subject's conviction that words fall short, and treats that conviction as data about the subject, not as a discovery about what was disclosed. The page moves from that datum to a claim about the content: propositional language is "categorically wrong-shaped" for it. Dennett calls this pattern inflating an intuition into metaphysics, and this time the charge sticks without any physicalist premises (Issue 3). He would also note that L83 repeats a self-stultification move whose reach the Map has since narrowed (Issue 4).

### The Quantum Skeptic
The page makes no quantum claim, which is correct for a void. The only exposure is indirect. L83's "real causal work" by epistemic emotions needs, on the Map's account, the quantum interface, and the register records that interface's mechanism debt ([[positions/quantum-interface#^mechanism-debt|the mechanism debt]]). For Tegmark, curiosity's role in directing inquiry is fully explained by classical neural dynamics on timescales vastly longer than decoherence. He presses no further, because the page does not use quantum mechanics.

### The Many-Worlds Defender
Tenet 4 does not come up, and nothing requires it to. Deutsch's objection is epistemological. The Verification Paradox (L59–L63) assumes that knowledge needs a verified source. On Popper's and Deutsch's fallibilism, no source is ever verified. Knowledge grows by conjecture and criticism, and an emotion's verdict is a conjecture that can be criticised like any other. On that view, circular verification is a problem only for a justificationist epistemology the page never defends. The page should at least note that the void is sharp only under a source-based conception of justification. Smithies' accessibilism (L45) is such a conception, so the page has an ally to name.

### The Empiricist
The complaint that the void cannot be falsified is bedrock, and I do not re-raise it. The Popperian adds something new that supports Issue 2. L61 itself lists "predictive success" as an independent check for vision, and fear passes that test: fear of snakes predicts envenomation. Much emotional disclosure is therefore falsifiable in the page's own sense. What escapes testing is the evaluative residue, whatever awe or grief would disclose beyond any descriptive base. The page should say that the void is located there, and only there.

### The Buddhist Philosopher
Constructivism about the self and about values is bedrock. One historical point is new, though. L55 counts "contemplative practice" among the counter-currents that "preserved affective epistemological claims". The largest contemplative tradition treats the afflictive emotions (kleśa: craving, aversion, delusion) as the paradigm of distorted cognition, and its practice aims to see through emotional disclosure, not to credit it. Some contemplative strands support affective epistemology. *The Cloud of Unknowing* is one, with its priority of love over thought. Others oppose it. Grouping them as one counter-current misstates the record (Issue 6).

### Issue 2: The void is stated more broadly than its argument, in the lead (L37) and in the asymmetry claim (L61)
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/emotional-epistemology-void.md`
- **Location**: L61, "Emotional disclosure resists this." and "The circularity here is tighter and less escapable than the ordinary perceptual case."; L37, "If they do not, then one of the most persistent features of conscious life—the conviction that feelings disclose something real—is a systematic illusion. Either way, a void opens."
- **Problem (L61)**: The page's own test for an external check is cross-verification "by touch, testimony, instruments, and predictive success". Much emotional disclosure passes it. Fear presents things as dangerous, and L41 itself lists dangerousness among the evaluative properties emotions disclose. Fear's verdict on a snake answers to toxicology and to what happens to people who are bitten. One observer's outrage can be checked against others' testimony, just as vision can. The unqualified "Emotional disclosure resists this" is therefore false on the page's own terms. Only the awe and grief examples in the following sentences resist checking. The claim that holds is narrower: for the evaluative residue, whatever emotion seems to disclose beyond any descriptive check, the circularity is tighter. There is also a double standard at L59. Checking emotion against propositional knowledge is said to "beg the question" by making that knowledge "the standard". Checking vision against touch makes touch no standard, only an independent source. Cross-checking is about agreement between independent sources, not about which source has priority.
- **Problem (L37)**: The lead's two horns are not exhaustive. Between "irreplaceable access to truths that reasoning alone cannot reach" and "systematic illusion" sits the mainstream position: emotions are fallible evidence about features other faculties can also check. The page's own cited source sits there too. The McMartin & Pickavance (2024) abstract, raw from Crossref, describes the existing literature as holding that "affective states might do more than position us to receive evidence or function as evidence". It argues that they also "appraise evidence", which is affective *evidentialism*: a rational role inside evidence, not access to truths reason cannot reach. On that middle option no domain is closed and nothing is an illusion. That is exactly the case in which "Either way, a void opens" fails. The page partly admits this at L71 ("The void collapses into a question about the reliability of a particular class of conscious states"), which sits awkwardly with L37's "Either way". The page's own definition of the void, at L59 ("What makes this a void rather than merely an open question is the circularity of verification"), puts it in the difficulty of telling the options apart, not in the content of either option. The lead should say so.
- **Severity**: High (it is the page's central argument, and the fix narrows the void without removing it)
- **Recommendation**: See What to Fix First, item 2. Make the L37 and L61 edits in one pass, so the lead and the paradox describe the same void.

### Issue 6: The history behind the "Unexplored" and "Occluded" claims is overgeneralised (L51, L55)
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/emotional-epistemology-void.md`
- **Location**: L51, "Philosophy has only recently taken seriously the idea that emotions constitute knowledge rather than distort it. The Western tradition from Plato onward has treated emotion as reason's antagonist"; L55, "twenty-five centuries of privileging logos over pathos" and "they were marginalised precisely because they could not translate their insights into propositional form".
- **Problem**: Plato is a counterexample to his own "onward". In the *Symposium* (Diotima's ladder) and the *Phaedrus* (the madness of love), eros is the path to Beauty itself. Aristotle makes feeling "at the right times, about the right things" part of virtue (*NE* II.6), and he analyses emotions through evaluative appearances (*Rhetoric* II). Even the Stoics, the clearest case of devaluing emotion, are, as SEP's *Emotion* entry puts it, the source of the cognitivist identification of "emotions with judgments": false judgments, but judgments, which is not "reason's antagonist". Also on the list are Hume's passions, the moral-sense theorists, Brentano, and Scheler himself (1913), whom the page cites at L43 and who sits awkwardly with "only recently". The causal claim about apophatic traditions has no source, and negative theology was hardly marginal in medieval thought. The page's Occluded category depends on this history.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: Reduce to what the record supports. For example: "A strong strand of Western thought, Stoic and later rationalist, treated emotion as a source of error to be corrected, though Plato's account of eros, Aristotle's ethics of feeling, and the moral-sense tradition dissent." Change "only recently taken seriously" to "only recently made central to epistemology". Cut or hedge "precisely because they could not translate their insights into propositional form". This is about word-neutral.

### Issue 7: Smithies is offered as a reason to favour emotions, but his view favours every conscious state equally (L45)
- **File**: `obsidian/voids/emotional-epistemology-void.md`
- **Location**: L45, "If consciousness grounds all justification, then emotions as conscious states may provide epistemic access that no unconscious process can replicate."
- **Problem**: On phenomenal accessibilism, *every* conscious state, from itches to visual experiences, provides access that no unconscious process can replicate. The premise gives emotions no special standing. It shows only that emotions are *eligible* justifiers, which no party to the debate denies. The section claims to present "The Case for Affective Knowledge", and this paragraph adds nothing to it.
- **Severity**: Medium
- **Recommendation**: "...then emotions, as conscious states, are at least eligible to justify; whether they justify evaluative belief in their own right is what the perceptual model has to show." This is about word-neutral.

## Counterarguments to Address

### The dualist gain at L81
- **Current content says**: Under dualism, the phenomenal character of emotion "is an ontologically distinct feature of reality with its own epistemic affordances", so emotions "may provide genuine access to aspects of reality that no third-person method can reach".
- **A critic would argue**: Being ontologically distinct confers no ability to track anything. If anything, dualism makes the tracking harder to explain: why would a non-physical evaluative character match external evaluative features? This is the affective form of the correlation problem, and it is the inbound [[inheritance-problem-for-the-psychophysical-law#harmony|psychophysical harmony]] debt that the co-optimization page (L97) says interactionism shares "in full".
- **Suggested response**: One clause after "epistemic affordances": "though why those affordances would track anything is the harmony debt every dualist reading owes."

### The Tenet 5 paragraph at L85
- **Current content says**: "The rationalist programme—reduce all knowledge to propositional, evidence-based reasoning—may be a case of parsimony cutting away genuine epistemic territory. The simplest epistemology (reason alone suffices) may not be the correct one."
- **A critic would argue**: This is a straw man. No mainstream epistemology holds that "reason alone suffices"; empiricism counts perception as a basic source. Nor do sceptics of affective knowledge reject it on grounds of parsimony. They reject it because of the evidence for mood-congruent bias, the evidence the page itself cites at L63.
- **Suggested response**: "An epistemology that admits only perception and inference as basic sources may be cutting away genuine territory; the case against affective sources rests on their documented biases, not on simplicity, so Tenet 5 bears only on the residual question of whether parsimony should break the tie." Note that the last clause touches the Tenet-5 tiebreaker NEEDS-HUMAN record (todo L93). Rather than assert the tiebreaker rule, the refine can simply drop that clause.

### "Testing against propositional knowledge begs the question" (L59)
- **Current content says**: Checking emotion against propositional knowledge assumes that knowledge is the standard.
- **A critic would argue**: Cross-checking assumes only that the sources are independent, not that one has priority (see Issue 2).
- **Suggested response**: Fold into the L61 rewrite: say the residue resists independent checks, not that checking would beg the question.
