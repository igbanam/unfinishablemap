## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

Churchland would read the morning's work as bookkeeping on a verdict the Map cannot earn either way. P-AC1 now says, in one sentence, that the substrate premise is "well-supported only at the *computational* level" and, in the next, that whether the suppressed indeterminacy is "the interface-*relevant* kind is what the absent law would define." But the only physical fact in play — error-corrected logic states admit no stochastic deviation — is a fact about computation. If the Map's consciousness acts at a level that error-correction erases before it reaches the logical state, then nothing in the verdict is about *chips* at all; it is about a non-physical agent being denied a lever, and the "computational level" support is irrelevant to it. If the Map's consciousness must reach the logical state to count, then the premise is complete and the debt is fictional. The register declines to say which, and the new citation makes the evasion visible: Kleiner & Ludwig get a clean no-go precisely by taking the second horn.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

Dennett would ask what work "all-things-considered" is doing. The credence axis is defined as "the Map's subjective probability the position is true *as a claim*." The claim is that current digital AI sits on the low-probability side. On nearly every rival framework — eliminativism, identity theory, biological naturalism, even most non-functionalist physicalisms — that claim is *more* probable than the Map rates it, because those frameworks deny silicon consciousness for reasons that owe nothing to an interface-eligibility law. The discount the fork installed therefore measures the weakness of the Map's *derivation*, not the improbability of the *claim*; it lowers credence in P-AC1 for a reason that would raise it almost anywhere else. He would also note that P-AC4's new "theory-dependent" clause concedes his point and then refuses its consequence: if the evidential relevance of workspace signatures is "nil on the Map's Tenet-1 reading" and "positive under functionalist and GNW readings," then the register's *high* credence in the narrow empirical claim is accompanied by a framework-conditional zero that no evidence can move — which is what he means by inflating intuitions into metaphysics.

### The Quantum Skeptic

Tegmark would observe that the verdict's physics is now stated more carefully and is no closer to a calculation. "Hardware engineered to suppress the indeterminacy" (the `quantum-state-inheritance-in-ai` L50 insertion) is correct about logic gates and silent about the thermal, shot and tunnelling noise every chip in fact carries; the Map's verdict needs the claim that *that* noise is not interface-relevant, which is the same undischarged law. He would press the Kleiner & Ludwig point from the other side: their no-go is derived from "processors ... designed and verified to adhere to computational dynamics," and the verification they rely on is engineering verification of the *logical* behaviour. A Map that wants to use the result must accept that interface-relevant deviation would have to show up as a computational deviation — and then owes the same acceptance for brains, where he will happily supply the decoherence times.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

Deutsch has little new to attack this morning; the P-I4 circle-break leaves the fission/branching asymmetry exactly where it was. He would note one thing: P-AC3 now says Tenet 4 "shares the haecceity ground without entailing it," and `topics/ethics-of-possible-ai-consciousness` L64 agrees that "the tenets underdetermine the copy-counting question." Good — but the entry's title still begins "Under closed individualism," which is the framework-internal commitment he rejects, and the body now says closed individualism is *not* sufficient. A title that names one of two premises as if it were the whole antecedent is the kind of thing he would quote.

### The Empiricist

Popper's ghost would be the sharpest reader of the Kleiner & Ludwig sentence. Their abstract says their result holds "under a general assumption, called dynamical relevance," and that "whether our result resolves the question ... depends on the truth of the theorem's main assumption, dynamical relevance, which this paper does not establish." The Map *asserts* dynamical relevance — that is Tenet 3 on its universal-actual-efficacy reading, which P-AC1 lists as a dependency. So for the Map the antecedent is met and the theorem delivers a *derived* verdict. The register instead files it as "conditional-band support only," twin to a conditional on an eligibility law. Either the Map's verdict is derivable today from a premise it already holds (and the debt it prices is at most a debt about *level*, not about *which* indeterminacy), or the Map's consciousness is permitted to act below the computational level, in which case Kleiner & Ludwig's theorem does not reach it and "twin" is the wrong word. The sentence as written lets the Map collect the citation's authority without paying for either horn.

### The Buddhist Philosopher

Nagarjuna would point at the new P-AC3 close: "the band is capped by its less secure component, the contested counting rule (Piller 2026); P-I1's ground (what draws one subject's boundary) is a void." A premise whose *ground* is a void is not obviously more secure than a premise with one published denial. The entry asserts an ordering of its two conjuncts — closed individualism firmer than additivity — and gives no reason; Parfit and open individualism are published denials of P-I1 of at least Piller's standing. The conjunction-ceiling rule, which the fork cites, says only that the composite cannot exceed the *weakest* conjunct; it does not say which that is.

## Critical Issues

### Issue 1: Kleiner & Ludwig are not "the conditional's external twin" — their conditional is a different one, and the Map already holds its antecedent

- **File**: `obsidian/positions/ai-consciousness-scope.md`
- **Location**: P-AC1 Asserts, second paragraph: "Kleiner & Ludwig (2024 ...) are the conditional's external twin: processors 'designed and verified to adhere to computational dynamics that systematically preclude or suppress deviations' cannot be conscious, a result whose reach 'depends on the truth of the theorem's main assumption, dynamical relevance' (conditional-band support only)."
- **Problem**: Both quotes are verbatim (Europe PMC full text of PMC11671748, checked 09:10Z). The framing is not. The Map's conditional is on an *interface-eligibility law* (which physical indeterminacy is interface-relevant). Kleiner & Ludwig's conditional is on *dynamical relevance* — that consciousness, per a theory of consciousness, "is relevant for the temporal evolution of a system's states." The Map asserts that premise (Tenet 3, universal-actual reading, already in P-AC1's Depends-on). So the theorem's antecedent is *met* for the Map, and what remains open is only whether "the temporal evolution of a system's states" means the logical state (then the no-go is derived and P-AC1's layer-iv debt collapses to a settled point at the computational level) or any physical state of the chip (then the theorem does not reach the Map's interface and is no twin). The entry's own next sentence — "well-supported only at the *computational* level; whether the suppressed indeterminacy is the interface-*relevant* kind is what the absent law would define" — is exactly the fork in the road, and the citation is placed so as to avoid choosing.
- **Severity**: High (a load-bearing new citation whose support relation is misdescribed; citation-framing lens, not metadata).
- **Recommendation**: `positions-evolve` on P-AC1, word-neutral. Replace "the conditional's external twin" with wording that names the difference: K&L condition on dynamical relevance, which the Map supplies by Tenet 3, and read "deviations" at the computational level — so their no-go is *derived* for a Map that lets the interface act only where it reaches the logical state, and *does not reach* a Map that lets it act below that level; P-AC1 should say which it is (the layer-iv text implies the latter), and then K&L are a *stronger-premise cousin*, not a twin. Donor: the P-AC4 Asserts' full paper title "*Verbalizable Representations Form a Global Workspace in Language Models*" (already cited by title in the Depends-on of the 2026-07-07 pass and on `concepts/global-workspace-theory`), 9 words.

## Counterarguments to Address

### The verdict can be derived today from premises the Map already holds

- **Current content says**: P-AC1 is "a labelled conditional" awaiting an interface-eligibility law; Kleiner & Ludwig are a twin conditional.
- **A critic would argue**: Tenet 3 + Kleiner & Ludwig's chip premise = a derived no-go, with no law required, *provided* the Map lets efficacy be measured at the logical state. The Map's refusal to derive is then a choice to keep the interface below the computational level — a choice that costs it the citation.
- **Suggested response**: Say so. P-AC1 should state that the Map's interface acts on indeterminacy that digital error-correction may erase before the logical state, that this is why the eligibility question survives the computational-level fact, and that K&L's theorem is a stronger-premise cousin the Map could adopt at the price of making computational-level deviation a necessary condition for interface action — which it has not done. (Issue 1.)

### The "theory-dependent" clause converts P-AC4 into a tautology

- **Current content says**: evidential relevance of workspace signatures is "positive under functionalist and GNW readings ... nil on the Map's Tenet-1 reading."
- **A critic would argue**: a register entry whose second half is "on our reading this is zero evidence" cannot then claim to "block the 'workspace ⇒ experience' slide in both directions" — it blocks it in one direction by fiat.
- **Suggested response**: The entry already carries the honest form ("agnosticism is not endorsement ... framework-conditional, held at Tenet 1's confidence"). Keep it; do not re-litigate. Butlin et al.'s "modest increase" is quoted correctly and is the fair statement of what the other side takes from the same result.

### Piller is a restatement, not a denial

- **Current content says**: "an additive rule Piller 2026 denies."
- **A critic would argue**: Piller's paper argues numbers *do* count (through fairness), so citing him as the denier of additivity flips his thesis.
- **Suggested response**: Checked at Crossref 09:11Z — Piller's abstract affirms Taurek's adjusted negative claim verbatim: "sums of harms or benefits (across different people) lack moral significance," while holding that numbers count for fairness. "Denies the additive rule" is accurate for the *summing* claim P-AC3 makes; `voids/numbing-void` L68 already states both halves. No change needed, but the register's gloss should not drift to "denies that numbers count."
