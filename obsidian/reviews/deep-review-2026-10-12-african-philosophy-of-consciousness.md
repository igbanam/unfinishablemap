## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

1. **Cross-article contradiction about the Map's own tenets (RSP L95).** The article said the cause's ontological distinctness "is the whole of what the [Bidirectional Interaction] tenet asserts." `tenets.md` L89 defines that tenet as causal influence only. L53 says distinctness comes from Dualism and Bidirectional Interaction *combined* (interactionist dualism). The sibling `interaction-problem-across-traditions` L132 says a force ontology *satisfies* Bidirectional Interaction while denying distinctness. **Fixed**: the article now says the tenet "does not assert [distinctness] alone, but ... it and the dualism tenet jointly commit the Map to [it] as [[interactionist-dualism]]", and that a force ontology "can satisfy this tenet while denying that distinctness". L97 now says "cannot be counted as support for interactionist dualism ... a rival to it" (was "support for bidirectional interaction ... a rival to the tenet").
2. **Calibration error about the Map's own tenet (Agada, L97).** The article called Agada's consolationism "a position the dualism tenet excludes". `tenets.md` L53 makes the Dualism tenet irreducibility only ("whether substance, property, or aspect ... What matters is irreducibility"), and `concepts/panpsychism` L186 says panpsychism "largely endorses" that claim. A panpsychist monism shares the tenet's irreducibility claim and rejects the *interactionist* dualism the tenets jointly assert. **Fixed** to say exactly that.
3. **Internal contradiction and tradition splice (Ubuntu, L47).** The Map's reconciliation used the Akan *okra* inside the Nguni Ubuntu discussion ("okra (the soul) may be ontologically distinct ... while still requiring communal engagement"). (a) L54 says it is the *sunsum*, not the *okra*, that "is influenced by social and environmental factors". (b) Importing an Akan term into Ubuntu is the unanimist move the article concedes at L73. The ChatGPT outer review of 2026-08-06 (L194) flagged this splice and no task ever discharged it. **Fixed**: "a subject may be ontologically distinct ...", now labelled "its own proposal rather than anything Ubuntu's sources supply".
4. **Result-direction error (Chisale 2018).** The article listed Chisale as Ubuntu being "contested from within", saying she "deconstructs the gendered Ubuntu enacted in care work". The Crossref/AOSIS abstract reports that Ubuntu caregiving "is not exclusively feminine" and that Zulu elders "linked Ubuntu to communal care where men and women partnered". She contests a gendered *reading* of Ubuntu care using Ubuntu's own practice, so she is not critiquing the tradition. **Fixed**: "drawing on Zulu elders' accounts of communal care, disputes the reading of Ubuntu care as exclusively feminine". The paragraph's framing still holds, since this remains a dispute about how the ideal works in practice.
5. **Searle misattribution (L45).** The text said "groups have consciousness in Searle's collective intentionality sense". Searle's collective intentionality is individually held we-intentions with no group mind, as the sibling `consciousness-and-collective-phenomena` L108 itself says. **Fixed**: "not that groups have minds, nor that individuals share the we-intentions Searle calls collective intentionality".
6. **Source/Map conflation (Ubuntu → consciousness, L41–L43).** The proverb is about personhood. The article had it as "consciousness and selfhood are constituted ..." and "Ubuntu relocates [the hard problem]" without saying this was the Map's extension. The ChatGPT 08-06 outer review (L210) gave the verdict "constitution of phenomenal consciousness, hard-problem reframing ... unsupported Map extrapolations", and that was never discharged. **Fixed**: L41 now says "selfhood is constituted"; L43 now reads "Extended from personhood to consciousness — the Map's extension, not the proverb's — Ubuntu relocates it".
7. **Over-concession: Menkiti stated as settling a disputed question (L79).** "Menkiti's influential account shows what kind of claim that is ... a normative position ..., not a report on what consciousness is" treated one side of a live dispute as settled. Flikschuh 2024 (*Philosophia Africana* 23(1):76–95, accepted version read in full) separates two readings. Oyowe (2022, *Menkiti's Moral Man*) treats ancestors as a social kind sustained by collective intentions. Flikschuh herself sketches a "quasi-materialist and pan-psychic" account on which "the mentalistic aspects" of a person survive biological death, and calls it "no more than ... a sketch". **Fixed**: the article now reports the dispute and keeps the epistemic point ("Either way, belief in continuing social membership does not demonstrate persisting phenomenal awareness"). Flikschuh is presented as a sketch, not as evidence, so no evidential upgrade.
8. **RSP collected readings the body marks as contested (L93).** The RSP listed the Akan okra/honam distinction and the Yoruba *ori* as aligning with the Dualism tenet without qualification. The body marks both as contested: Wiredu at L83, and *ori*-as-awareness as "the Map's" reading at L65–L67. The RSP also kept "the widespread treatment of vital force ... across multiple traditions" after the 08-06 refine had narrowed the body to "the Bantu and Akan material". **Fixed**: "Gyekye's reading of the Akan distinction", "if ori is read as a bearer of experience", "the Bantu and Akan treatment".
