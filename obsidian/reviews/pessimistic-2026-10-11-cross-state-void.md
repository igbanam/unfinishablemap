## Executive Summary

The article's citations hold up. Every quotation I could reach is verbatim, and every DOI resolves to the cited work. The trouble is what the lead study shows. Read and Loewenstein (1999), the lede's "clearest measurement", reads differently in full text than in the abstract the article relied on:
- **No state contrast.** Every group chose after the pain had ended. The groups tested a week later, cold when they chose, still accepted far fewer offers than the description-only group (3.9 and 5.6 of 15, against 9.9; Exhibit 7, p. 12). So the study contrasts acquaintance with description, which is the [[transformative-experience-void]]'s structure. It does not contrast one visceral state with another.
- **The authors reject the article's reading.** They predicted that non-experiencers "would underestimate the pain" (p. 7). They treat the matched intensity ratings as a failure of the rating measures (p. 14), not as an estimate that "crossed intact".

The same pattern holds across the article's other sources. Every hot-cold study it cites locates the cold self's error in the *estimate itself* ("underestimated the severity of social pain"; "underestimation of pain by people who are not experiencing it"). So the master dissociation, *content crosses, weight does not*, rests on one reading that its own authors reject. The "Both Directions" section's hot-side half rests on a review sentence alone.

The tenet section repeats the mirth-void pattern the 10-10 review flagged:
- **The data cannot pick the cause.** A counterfactual ("Were felt weight idle…") is offered as evidence that the felt component does causal work. An epiphenomenalist predicts the same choices, and L67 has already declared the data neutral.
- **The article's own source reverses the asymbolia argument.** The structural explanation it relies on (Noordhof) runs the asymbolia argument in reverse: the missing *disposition* explains the missing felt painfulness.

Finally, the "structural" and "Unexplorable" claims fall to the epistemic/metaphysical equivocation check. The evidence supports a state-indexed, inferentially correctable access limit. The prose asserts a limit "generated by the representing itself" that "cannot be mapped from outside", yet L75 charts it from outside.

## Critiques by Philosopher

### The Eliminative Materialist

"Felt weight" is folk psychology standing in for a measurable variable you already have. Kang and Camerer's full text shows *more value-sensitive activity during real choice in the insula, amygdala, and hippocampus*. Read and Loewenstein's authors explain their own result by encoding mode: sensation-focused subjects "encode some of those sensations in a language which can later be recalled, and then transformed into a memory or a decision" (p. 14). Their week-delayed sensation group priced the pain as the immediate group did. A *description*, properly encoded, carried the decision weight across a week. That is the opposite of L93's "the descriptive content crosses and changes nothing". Replace "weight" with encoding, retrieval and motivational-circuit variables and nothing in the data is left over.

### The Hard-Nosed Physicalist

You have renamed **projection bias** a void. Its formalisers (Loewenstein, O'Donoghue & Rabin 2003, in your own research note and dropped from the article) model it as people predicting future tastes as if they resembled current ones. That is an estimation error, and your sources measure it as one:
- the cold self "underestimated the severity of social pain";
- the torture study shows "an underestimation of pain by people who are not experiencing it";
- *Evaluating Eve* Study 3 traces the effect to "different perceptions of the strength of the visceral state itself".

The *content* does not cross intact. It arrives skewed toward the current state. The only intact-estimate datum is a 7-point rating that Read and Loewenstein themselves call insensitive.

"Three features make this a void rather than a bias" (L39) lists features that biases routinely have. Bidirectionality, resistance to instruction and application to familiar cases are the ordinary profile of projection and hindsight effects.

The Dualism paragraph's Mary also misfires. Released Mary keeps knowing what red is like when she is back in the black-and-white room; the knowledge argument never claimed otherwise. A cold self that cannot *re-instantiate* the weight is the case Lewis's ability hypothesis was built for: knowing what it is like as a bundle of state-dependent abilities to imagine and recall. If anything, the void is grist for the physicalist reply to Jackson.

### The Quantum Skeptic

Nothing here touches physics, and the article correctly forces no link to Minimal Quantum Interaction. But it then claims No Many Worlds is "touched more lightly" because the void is indexical. Indexicality is no evidence against Everett. Everettians *rely* on self-locating facts: the self-locating-uncertainty derivations of the Born rule (Sebens & Carroll 2018; Vaidman; leads, not verified here) are built on exactly "which state I now occupy". The sentence should go. It costs nothing to remove, and removing it funds the repairs below.

### The Many-Worlds Defender

Same point, sharper: "which state I now occupy does epistemic work that no third-person description … substitutes for" is a premise every Everettian accepts. It cannot discriminate between interpretations. Keeping it implies a tenet connection that does not exist.

### The Empiricist

What would show there is no void? The article names its falsifier: "the article's claim would fail if simulation restored the weight as a representation" (L65). It then dismisses Steinmetz et al. because simulation "works by installing a partial state, not by improving a description". That is the article's inference, not the paper's. Steinmetz et al. conclude that mental simulation "may help people anticipate their own and others' future preferences, thereby improving their decision making". The phrase "albeit to a weaker extent" describes the *prior* simulation literature their abstract cites, not their own finding.

Without an operational test that separates "installing a partial state" from "improving a representation", every successful correction is relabelled as re-entry. The claim is immunised. And L75 already concedes cold-side inferential correction: "a correction factor without restored access, as with a known instrument error". So either:
- the void is the trivial claim that one cannot *feel* a state one is not in, which every theory predicts; or
- it is the claim that no cold-side description restores *the decision weight*. Read and Loewenstein's sensation-focus group (stable WTAP over a week) and Steinmetz's simulation results put pressure on that claim.

The article should say which claim it makes, and name the test: a cold-side manipulation that moves predictions toward hot-state choices *without* state signatures (no current-preference shift, no projection onto others).

### The Buddhist Philosopher

You treat "the hot self" and "the cold self" as two vantage points with competing authority (L69–L71). The noting practices your research note mentions, which label "craving, craving" as it arises, deny that either is a self. Each is a passing configuration, and the authority question dissolves rather than splits.

More pointedly, the split is manufactured by switching what "authority" means. The torture study asks who is *accurate*, and the hot state wins. Addiction asks whose verdict is *endorsed*, and the cold planner wins. But on accuracy, Sayette's cold planners were wrong too: they "underpredicted the value they would place on smoking". On accuracy, every case the article cites sides with the hot state.

### Issue 1: The lede's "clearest measurement" is an acquaintance contrast, and its authors reject the article's reading of it (High)
- **File**: `voids/cross-state-void.md`
- **Location**: L37 (lede: "Its clearest measurement comes from Read and Loewenstein (1999) … The estimate crossed; the weight did not."); L43 ("What the abstract establishes is the dissociation: the estimate … crossed to non-experiencers intact"); L65 ("Recent experience narrows it: subjects who had just felt the pain behaved more like hot subjects"); L81 ("having been there does not help, which is the milder and more pervasive form Helton and Register describe"); L95 ("Read and Loewenstein's (intensity matched, weight not)"). It also propagates to `voids.md` L303.
- **Problem**:
  1. **No visceral-state contrast.** All groups stated WTAP after the 30-second immersion had ended (p. 7; Exhibit 1). The week-delayed groups were cold at decision, yet their WTAP (SENS/DEL 3.9, DIS/DEL 5.6) stayed far from the description-only control's 9.9. What separates the groups is whether they had ever felt this pain: acquaintance against description. The design's stated purpose confirms it: "a valid measure of pain sensation and memory will distinguish between those who have experienced the pain and those who have not" (p. 7). This is the [[transformative-experience-void]]'s turf (a novel, "unexpectedly painful" stimulus), not the familiar-state void the lede claims.
  2. **"Intact" is the article's reading, not the authors'.** They "predicted that a control group would underestimate the level of pain" (p. 6) and conclude that WTAP "was the only measure that distinguished between control and experimental subjects" (p. 14). That is, the ratings failed to detect an underestimate, and memory reports may be "highly influenced by personal theories of what pain is". The dissociation is a measurement-validity result.
  3. **The full text cuts against the lede's toothache.** Sensation-focused subjects tested a week later priced the pain like those tested at once (4.7 vs 3.9, Exhibit 7 p. 12; t < 1, p. 10). In this study, a week in the cold did not strip the weight from an encoded memory. Only distraction-coded memory faded (2.6 → 5.6).
  4. **L65 breaks L43's own fence.** L43 says the "finer ordering across conditions is not given in the abstract and is not asserted here". L65 then asserts it: "subjects who had just felt the pain behaved more like hot subjects". That holds only for the distraction arm.
  5. **L81 contradicts its source.** "Having been there does not help" is contradicted by this study, where having been there a week earlier helped a great deal. Helton & Register's abstract also says the relevant understanding "can be achieved by knowledge from first-personal experience", so the attribution is unsupported.
- **Recommendation**: Lead with Sayette et al. 2008, which genuinely contrasts familiar states (cold vs hot smokers predicting a craving session). Keep Read & Loewenstein, reframed as an acquaintance contrast with the authors' own reading stated. Drop L65's ordering clause. Restate L81 as "helps less than it should". Drop Read & Loewenstein from the L95 Dualism parenthetical. Fix the `voids.md` L303 blurb. Exact strings are in Priority items 1a–1e and rider V1.

## Counterarguments to Address

### The cold self's estimate is wrong, not intact
- **Current content says**: the description crosses intact and only the weight fails to cross.
- **A critic would argue**: every hot-cold study measures a cold-side *underestimate*. The one intact-rating datum is a scale its authors call insensitive. This is projection bias, an estimation error.
- **Suggested response**: concede the estimate is skewed. Locate the void in the residue: even a corrected estimate (L75's instrument-error correction) does not restore the felt pull, and no cold-side description has yet been shown to move choices without state-like signatures. That is narrower, and defensible.

### The behavioural difference does not pick out the felt component
- **Current content says**: weight is absent, so choices differ, so the felt component does causal work.
- **A critic would argue**: the motivational state is absent too. The epiphenomenalist predicts identical data, and Noordhof's account (cited by the article) explains the felt absence *by* the motivational absence.
- **Suggested response**: claim only that whatever is missing matters, and mark the phenomenal reading as compatible, not supported (Priority item 3).

### Correction by inference shows the territory is mappable
- **Current content says**: the territory "cannot be mapped from outside".
- **A critic would argue**: L75 maps it, Steinmetz says simulation improves decisions, and Read and Loewenstein's sensation-focused coding carried the weight a week.
- **Suggested response**: separate *charting* (possible, inferential) from *feeling* (impossible, trivially), and state the non-trivial claim with a test (Priority item 4b).
