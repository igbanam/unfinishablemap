## Pessimistic Analysis Summary

### Critical Issues Found

- **Map's reply over-generalised from one strand of the literature (calibration, fixed).** §Map's Reading said "The involuntarist literature answers on any metaphysics: belief answers to truth ... and the limit therefore says nothing about causal impotence." Only the *conceptual* strand gives that answer. The article's own §Two Grounds says the psychological ground (Alston) leaves the limit "a fact about human psychology", and Booth 2017 argues involuntarism is "merely contingently true". On those grounds the limit is a contingent lack of a capacity, which is exactly what the objector presses. A tenet-accepting reviewer would still flag this: the reply is only as secure as the contested conceptual argument, and the article was presenting it as unconditional. **Fixed**: "Conceptual involuntarism answers on any metaphysics ... and so the inability 'reveals no genuine lack in our powers of mind' (Hieronymi 2009). The Map adopts this reply, as the [[assent-void]] does, and inherits its condition: on the psychological ground alone, the limit would be a contingent fact about human minds and the reply would weaken. Either way it removes a defeater and adds no evidence ..." The Hieronymi 2009 phrase is verbatim from the CJP abstract (OpenAlex), already in References. The lead's matching sentence now reads "treats the involuntarism of belief, so far as its conceptual ground holds, as a fact about belief's answerability to truth", so the front-loaded summary and the body agree.

No other critical issues. No factual errors, misattributions, fabricated cites, dropped qualifiers or internal contradictions were found (ledger below).

### Medium Issues Found

Crowe's abstract (OpenAlex, verbatim) grounds his defence in "existential reasons" and says nothing about evidential underdetermination; the body could not be fetched (Springer JS challenge on the CC BY PDF; the research note recorded the same block on 10-07, and only inferred "permissivist-flavoured"). **Fixed** by re-heading the bullet "Limited voluntarism. The SEP files Ginet under §4.1 epistemic permissivism, so his placement is unchanged.
