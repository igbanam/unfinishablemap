# Outer review: “Neurological Dissociations as Interface Architecture”

**Review date:** 16 October 2026  
**Recommendation:** **Major revision**

## Overall judgment

The article has a sound empirical core but draws its architectural map too sharply. Blindsight, pain asymbolia, anosognosia, anarchic-hand phenomena, split-brain findings and spatial neglect do reveal dissociable functions. Collectively, they provide substantial evidence against any undifferentiated picture in which perception, awareness, affect, agency, bodily self-knowledge, attention and interhemispheric integration must always vary together.

They do **not**, however, establish:

1. six discrete “channels” rather than overlapping, dynamically reconfigurable networks;
2. the article’s stipulated directions of transmission between brain and consciousness;
3. a common architecture shared by lesion, pharmacological and functional disorders;
4. an already-unified nonphysical consciousness at the receiving end; or
5. any empirical advantage for the dualist interpretation over global-workspace, higher-order, predictive-processing, recurrent-processing, comparator or other functionalist accounts.

The article now acknowledges the fifth point in its opening and closing sections. That calibration was added on 8 October after an earlier review: the changelog records replacement of an identity-theoretic argument with the concession that functional subsystems and dualist interface channels predict the same dissociations. But the body was not fully rewritten around that concession. It still repeatedly describes the six components as independently confirmed interface anatomy, invokes consciousness as a motor arbiter, treats alien hand as evidence against epiphenomenalism and describes anaesthetic emergence as reopening a dedicated interface pathway. [topics/neurological-dissociations-as-interface-architecture](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/neurological-dissociations-as-interface-architecture/)

There is even a visible taxonomic inconsistency: the article announces **four** interface channels and then lists **six**. More importantly, “channel” oscillates among three meanings:

- a neutral functional dependency;
- a neural pathway or network;
- a bridge between neural activity and an ontologically distinct subject.

The evidence supports the first meaning strongly, sometimes supports the second, and does not establish the third.

## 1. The central inference

The defensible inference is:

> Selective neurological impairments provide evidence that several capacities ordinarily bundled together are functionally differentiable.

The article instead often proceeds as though the data establish:

> Each syndrome severs a particular mind–brain interface channel with a determinate direction and content.

That second inference requires much more than behavioural selectivity. Lesions can produce selective deficits through damage to distributed networks, interruption of coordination, diaschisis, altered precision weighting, threshold effects, compensatory reorganisation or degradation of one representational level while sparing another. A selective symptom does not by itself identify a single anatomical conduit, still less a trans-ontological conduit.

The article should separate five evidential levels:

1. **Observed performance:** what the patient can and cannot do.
2. **Reported phenomenology:** what the patient says is or is not experienced.
3. **Neural explanation:** lesions, disconnections, network changes or computational dysfunction.
4. **Functional abstraction:** the capacity provisionally isolated by the syndrome.
5. **Metaphysical interpretation:** whether the capacity is produced by neural organisation or mediates interaction with a distinct mind.

Most of the cited evidence concerns levels 1–3. The article is reasonably entitled to propose level 4. Level 5 remains radically underdetermined.

The claim that “each component has been independently confirmed” is especially too strong. The components are not independently identified before the cases are classified; rather, the taxonomy is constructed by assigning each heterogeneous disorder to a proposed component. Several syndromes also share mechanisms or covary—for example, anosognosia frequently co-occurs with neglect and proprioceptive loss—so the examples are not independent replications of a pre-specified architecture. [topics/neurological-dissociations-as-interface-architecture](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/neurological-dissociations-as-interface-architecture/)

# 2. Syndrome-by-syndrome empirical audit

## 2.1 Blindsight

### What survives

The classic phenomenon remains highly relevant: following V1 damage, some patients can perform above chance on visual tasks while denying ordinary visual awareness. That is good evidence that behavioural sensitivity, report and normal visual phenomenology can dissociate.

### What is overstated

The article says that visual information travels through a superior-colliculus route, that none of it enters awareness and that the alternative pathways cannot deliver phenomenal content. Each part is too categorical.

A 2025 systematic review of 25 human and animal studies identified multiple candidate routes, including LGN–V5 connections, superior-colliculus pathways, the SC–pulvinar–amygdala route for affective processing and surviving portions of V1. Which routes are involved varies with lesion extent, anatomy, plasticity and time since injury. Blindsight is therefore not well represented as one intact pathway lying below one destroyed phenomenal channel. [PMC+1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13332361/)

The phenomenal interpretation also remains disputed. Phillips has argued that at least some classic blindsight performance is severely degraded conscious vision combined with conservative report criteria. Lau and Nakamura’s October 2026 review is more favourable to genuine performance-awareness dissociations, but still argues that decisive work requires matched yes/no and forced-choice sensitivity measures so that weak perception and response criteria are not confused with absent awareness. [Ian Phillips+2](https://www.ianbphillips.com/files/papers/blindsight.pdf)

The neighbouring Map article already recognises total absence and degraded or minimally conscious vision as live alternatives and says the production and interface readings fit the same data. The synthesis suppresses that qualification when it states that no visual information enters awareness. [concepts/blindsight](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/blindsight/)

### Better conclusion

Blindsight supports a separation among objective visual sensitivity, subjective report, confidence and normal visual phenomenology. It does not yet establish that a V1-dependent channel transmits phenomenal content to a nonphysical subject.

## 2.2 Pain asymbolia

### What survives

Pain asymbolia is important because sensory discrimination, affective response, defensive behaviour, motivation and the sense that a state matters to oneself need not remain fully coupled.

### What is overstated

The article calls the dissociation clean and complete: nociceptive discrimination survives while affective unpleasantness is abolished. The primary source does not justify that idealisation.

Berthier, Starkstein and Leiguarda reported **six** patients with absent **or inadequate** emotional responses. The patients also showed diminished reactions to visual threats and, in five cases, verbal threats. Associated findings included hemiparesis, cortical sensory loss, neglect and body-schema disorders, with anatomically extensive lesions. This was not the selective subtraction of a single pain-affect module from an otherwise intact system. [Stafforini PDF+1](https://pdf.stafforini.com/berthier-1988-asymbolia-pain-sensorylimbic.pdf)

The proposed anterior-insula/ACC necessity is also insecure. Feinstein and colleagues described preserved—and in some respects intensified—pain experience in a patient with extensive bilateral damage involving the insula, anterior cingulate and amygdala, and noted the lack of replication of the original localisation claim. [Springer+1](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00429-014-0986-3)

Contemporary alternatives are not merely generic physicalism. Gerrans’s active-allostatic account models pain as distributed processing organised around a multidimensional self-model. On that account, pain asymbolia need not result from severing a discrete affective channel: it can arise because nociception is no longer integrated into the inferred self as the target of protective regulation. This account was published in 2024 and directly addresses both the dissociations and the temptation to impose an overly modular architecture. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/nc/article/2024/1/niae002/7603561)

The neighbouring pain article is materially better calibrated than the synthesis: it explicitly records the six-patient sample, broad comorbidities, extensive lesions, weak localisation evidence and preserved-pain counterexample. The synthesis nevertheless reverts to “clean separation” and “complete absence.” The changelog itself records that the pain article was corrected on 8 October from “no affective dimension at all” to Berthier’s weaker “absent or inadequate”; that downgrade was not propagated here. [concepts/pain-asymbolia](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/pain-asymbolia/)

### Better conclusion

Pain asymbolia supports multidimensional pain organisation. It does not presently establish either a discrete affective conduit or the claim that consciousness adds an otherwise absent property of awfulness.

## 2.3 Anosognosia for hemiplegia

### What survives

Patients can sincerely maintain inaccurate beliefs about their motor capacities despite paralysis and apparently decisive contrary evidence. The syndrome demonstrates that motor function, local error monitoring, bodily self-representation, general belief updating and explicit awareness can come apart.

### What is overstated

The article treats anosognosia as a damaged “self-model ascending channel” that sends consciousness a coherent but fabricated report. That is one functional description, not a result independently identified by the clinical evidence.

The best prospective study followed 58 right-hemisphere stroke patients. Anosognosia was present in 32% at three days, 18% at one week and 5% at six months, and covaried with proprioceptive loss, neglect and disorientation. The authors characterised it as a multi-component disorder arising from a distributed set of regions. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article-abstract/133/12/3578/309246)

A lesion-disconnection study of 174 patients implicated three interacting systems: a premotor loop, limbic structures and the ventral attentional network. A subsequent lesion-network analysis across 267 lesion locations found both domain-specific networks and a cross-modal awareness network centred on hippocampal and precuneus connectivity. These findings favour distributed monitoring and updating mechanisms over a single self-report conduit. [Iris+1](https://iris.uniroma1.it/bitstream/11573/1343505/1/Pacella_Anosognosia-for-hemiplegia.pdf)

The article’s diagram says the self-model channel bypasses attentional gating. That is particularly poorly supported. Ventral-attention disconnection is one of the major contemporary findings, and Bayesian work in 26 stroke patients found that neglect affected how observed errors updated more general prospective beliefs about motor ability. Attention may not explain every local monitoring failure, but the literature directly contradicts an unqualified claim that the relevant process bypasses attention. [christophmathys.com+1](https://christophmathys.com/files/papers/Kirsch-et-al-2021-Updating-beliefs-anosognosia.pdf)

The rapid-reversal evidence is also presented with insufficient scale information. The neighbouring article reports vestibular improvement in two of four patients and a video intervention in one patient. It appropriately concludes that the intra-cerebral belief-updating account fits at least as well as the interface interpretation and that these observations add no evidence for the tenets. [topics/anosognosia-and-the-reversible-self-monitoring-channel](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/anosognosia-and-the-reversible-self-monitoring-channel/)

### Better conclusion

Anosognosia supports a functional distinction among motor impairment, local monitoring, attention, confidence and higher-order belief updating. The “self-model channel” should be labelled a provisional abstraction, not a discovered route delivering reports to an external consciousness.

## 2.4 Alien and anarchic hand phenomena

### What survives

The literature supports dissociations among movement execution, voluntary inhibition, prospective intention, sense of agency and limb ownership. The distinction drawn by Marchetti and Della Sala between anarchic action and alien ownership is useful.

### What is overstated

The synthesis says that a descending channel from conscious intention to motor execution is severed while motor capacity remains intact. It then interprets the behaviour as what occurs when neural competition proceeds without conscious governance.

The evidence does not identify that causal direction. A 2025 systematic review found that agency disruption was common while loss of ownership was selective, and that persistent anarchic-hand phenomena were associated especially with combined disruption of callosal, frontal, frontoparietal and premotor networks. This is a network-control syndrome, not evidence of an interrupted non-neural command line. [Taylor & Francis Online+1](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/pdf/10.1080/13854046.2025.2557973)

The article’s own dedicated page states the stronger standard answer clearly: frontal disinhibition, affordance-driven action and absent or abnormal predictive/efference signals can account for the syndrome without a nonphysical element. It says that current evidence does not decide between comparator and interface interpretations. The synthesis nonetheless describes a missing control channel through which consciousness normally selects actions. [topics/anarchic-hand-and-action-ownership](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/anarchic-hand-and-action-ownership/)

This also conflicts with the Map’s motor-selection page, which acknowledges that classical noisy accumulators, recurrent control systems and hierarchical policies can perform selection without a conscious selector. That page correctly labels conscious intervention a framework commitment rather than an inference from motor neuroscience. The synthesis converts the commitment back into a clinical result. [concepts/motor-selection](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/motor-selection/)

### Alien hand and epiphenomenalism

The argument against epiphenomenalism is circular. An epiphenomenalist predicts behavioural change after frontal, callosal or SMA damage because the relevant neural control system has been damaged. The accompanying change in agency experience is, on that theory, another neural consequence. Nothing in the lesion selectively removes consciousness while leaving all neural control variables unchanged.

The article calls that reply strained because consciousness tracks the relevant circuitry precisely. But precise covariance is exactly what an identity theorist, realization physicalist or epiphenomenalist expects; it becomes suspicious only after assuming that consciousness is an independent controller. Alien hand therefore provides no independent evidence against epiphenomenalism.

### FND is not the inverse half of a demonstrated double dissociation

Voon et al. studied 16 patients and 16 controls performing an emotional-face task and found altered amygdala responses and greater amygdala–SMA functional connectivity. Edwards et al. offered a hierarchical Bayesian account in terms of priors, precision, attention and sensory evidence. Neither study identifies “conscious selection without executed action” as the inverse of anarchic hand, nor demonstrates that both disorders perturb one latent descending channel from opposite directions. [OUP Academic+1](https://academic.oup.com/brain/article-abstract/133/5/1526/542469)

The Map’s dedicated FND article concedes that the interface localisation is contestable, that relevant sedation and hypnosis evidence comes from small uncontrolled series and that the pattern is fully describable as an intra-cerebral inhibitory or predictive process. Calling FND the same channel under “active functional capture” largely stipulates the desired commonality. [topics/conversion-disorder-as-consciousness-side-fault](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/conversion-disorder-as-consciousness-side-fault/)

### Better conclusion

Anarchic-hand phenomena reveal separability among execution, inhibition, intention, agency and ownership. They do not show that conscious commands normally descend through a causal interface.

## 2.5 Split-brain evidence

### What survives

Callosotomy demonstrates that interhemispheric communication is important for the normal integration of perception, action and report, while also revealing substantial behavioural and neural compensation.

### What is overstated

The synthesis says that perceptual content divides while higher-order unity persists, and interprets this as a partially severed “unity channel” linking the hemispheres to an already-unified consciousness. The current evidence does not settle whether experiential unity persists, divides or varies by content and task.

A major consensus review concluded that the classic two-conscious-agents story has been weakened by later findings, but also that available behavioural evidence remains insufficient to determine whether there is one conscious agent or two. “Social ordinariness” and unified everyday behaviour are not direct measurements of phenomenal unity. [Springer+1](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s11065-020-09439-3.pdf)

The Santander et al. paper is bibliographically accurate and important, but the synthesis overreads it. It studied six adult callosotomy patients, including two partial cases. One partial patient retained about 1 cm of splenium. The result was widespread resting-state interhemispheric functional connectivity and absence of the tested behavioural disconnection signs—not a direct finding of undivided phenomenal consciousness. Functional connectivity measured with BOLD fMRI should also not be redescribed without qualification as “full neural synchronisation.” The paper itself notes that common inputs can create apparent coherence in resting-state measurements. [PNAS+2](https://www.pnas.org/doi/pdf/10.1073/pnas.2520190122)

The neighbouring split-brain article was recalibrated on 15 October and now says plainly that the evidence is compatible with the interface interpretation but does not force it. The synthesis retains the stronger “higher-order unity persists” formulation and has therefore fallen behind its dependency. [topics/split-brain-consciousness](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/split-brain-consciousness/)

### Better conclusion

The PNAS result supports unexpectedly redundant and plastic interhemispheric integration. It provides no direct evidence for a non-neural unity channel or an indivisible experiencer.

## 2.6 Hemispatial neglect

### What survives

Neglect is not adequately described as primary blindness. It involves severe failures to orient toward, act upon, represent or report contralesional information, often despite preservation of some early sensory processing.

### What is overstated

The article presents neglect as a damaged attentional gate with an intact visual system and intact delivery pathways. That is too simple. Neglect is heterogeneous across:

- egocentric and allocentric reference frames;
- perceptual, representational and motor-intentional manifestations;
- visual, tactile and auditory modalities;
- acute and chronic phases;
- arousal, disengagement and reorienting impairments;
- cortical lesions, subcortical lesions and large-scale disconnection.

Recent work continues to implicate distributed frontoparietal and interhemispheric networks rather than one gate. Subcortical neglect can itself arise through cortico-cortical disconnection, and 2025 causal-network work differentiates frontal systems supporting selective attention rather than locating a single access mechanism. [Nature+2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-67381-5)

Predictive-processing studies offer more specific competitors. EEG evidence has found abnormalities at multiple hierarchical levels of prediction-error processing, while a 2025 study associated symptom severity with excessive weighting of predictions in ipsilesional space. These accounts explain biased selection through altered precision and learned expectations rather than a jammed gate between intact processing and consciousness. [ResearchGate+1](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/352505443_Deficits_of_hierarchical_predictive_coding_in_left_spatial_neglect/fulltext/60d23301299bf19b8d9d7ec5/Deficits-of-hierarchical-predictive-coding-in-left-spatial-neglect.pdf)

### The alleged double dissociation with blindsight

The article calls blindsight and neglect a double dissociation, but it has not established one in the technical sense. A formal double dissociation would require commensurable measures of two capacities and evidence that:

- one capacity is impaired while the other is preserved in condition A; and
- the reverse pattern occurs in condition B.

The article instead compares two different syndromes, lesion profiles and task families and then assigns one to “delivery” and the other to “selection.” Both involve mixtures of degraded processing, attention, confidence, report and action. The proposed double dissociation is therefore a theoretical interpretation of two clinical literatures, not an empirical double dissociation demonstrated by the cited evidence.

### Better conclusion

Neglect supports the fractionation of sensory encoding, spatial priority, predictive precision, reorienting, representation, action and report. It does not isolate one bidirectional consciousness gate.

# 3. Anaesthesia and three-route “convergence”

The anaesthesia section is not merely illustrative; it is used to argue that lesion, pharmacological and psychogenic perturbations reproduce the same architecture. That claim is inadequately supported.

## Sanders et al.

The Sanders et al. citation is now correctly attributed—the changelog records that “Mashour and colleagues” was replaced with Sanders et al. on 9 October. The paper does support distinctions among consciousness, connectedness and responsiveness, and it emphasizes that behavioural unresponsiveness does not establish unconsciousness. [workflow/changelog](https://unfinishablemap.org/workflow/changelog/)

It does **not** show that the resulting states are failures of the article’s six channels. Its proposed neurobiology is framed in terms of neuromodulation and corticothalamic connectivity. Recasting its taxonomy as interface-channel failure is the Map’s interpretation.

Neuromuscular blockade is a particularly weak “descending-channel” analogue. It disrupts peripheral execution at the neuromuscular junction; it does not sever a central connection from consciousness to brain. Benzodiazepine amnesia likewise does not cleanly isolate memory from every effect on sedation and experience. These examples illustrate dissociability among consciousness, memory and observable responsiveness, but not a shared anatomical channel scheme.

## Hu et al.

Hu et al. correctly reported a KCC2-dependent recovery mechanism in the ventral posteromedial thalamus. But the work was conducted in **mice**, used minimum responsiveness and righting-related behaviour as consciousness proxies, and found a molecular mechanism associated with recovery under four anaesthetics. It did not identify a pathway by which a nonphysical subject “re-enters” the brain. [Nature+1](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41593-023-01290-y.pdf)

The neighbouring anaesthesia article now acknowledges that the four agents represent three broad mechanism classes plus one within-class comparison. Nevertheless, both it and the synthesis continue to call KCC2 a “dedicated reopening pathway” for the interface. The evidence supports a shared biological emergence mechanism in mice; “reopening the interface” is metaphysical redescription. [topics/anaesthesia-and-the-consciousness-interface](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/anaesthesia-and-the-consciousness-interface/)

## Unsupported pharmacological taxonomy

The synthesis’s “four-component pharmacological dissection”—phenomenal presence, behavioural access, temporal binding and memory encoding mapped onto distinct receptor systems—has no direct external citation on the page. Nor is the xenon–ketamine contrast documented by a cited primary source in this article.

Before claiming convergence, the article would need to establish independently that the same latent variables are being manipulated across:

- focal lesions;
- distributed stroke networks;
- peripheral neuromuscular blockade;
- receptor-level anaesthesia;
- FND;
- dissociative psychiatric conditions.

At present, the six-channel taxonomy is flexible enough to absorb each phenomenon after the fact. That is interpretive unification, not independent replication.

# 4. Missed predictive-processing and higher-order alternatives

## 4.1 Predictive processing and active inference

The article mentions the Bayesian account of FND but treats it as a mechanism that functionally overrides an independently real descending channel. It does not recognise that predictive processing can supply a general competing architecture.

A stronger predictive account would say:

| Syndrome | Predictive-processing interpretation |
| --- | --- |
| Blindsight | Residual low-precision evidence or degraded visual representations can guide forced-choice behaviour without producing confident, stable perceptual inference or report. |
| Pain asymbolia | Nociceptive evidence remains available but is not integrated into an allostatic self-model as an urgent threat to the organism. |
| Anosognosia | High-precision priors about intact ability and impaired weighting of prediction errors prevent belief revision. |
| Anarchic hand | Affordance-driven motor policies execute without the normal prospective model and agency prediction. |
| Neglect | Spatial precision and prediction errors are asymmetrically weighted, producing an ipsilesional priority bias. |
| FND | Overprecise symptom expectations and attention alter perception and motor policy without structural destruction. |

These are not merely speculative translations. Predictive or Bayesian models have been directly developed for anosognosia, FND, pain asymbolia and neglect. [christophmathys.com+3](https://christophmathys.com/files/papers/Kirsch-et-al-2021-Updating-beliefs-anosognosia.pdf)

Predictive processing is broad enough to risk post-hoc flexibility, so invoking it is not automatically explanatory success. A 2026 review of active-inference research stresses the need for more discriminating, theory-driven experiments rather than treating broad consistency as confirmation. But that criticism applies with even greater force to the article’s interface-channel mapping. [ScienceDirect+1](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0149763426002745)

## 4.2 Higher-order accounts

Higher-order theories distinguish a first-order state from a representation of oneself as being in that state. The standard theory family therefore expects some dissociations that the article treats as evidence for an interface:

- first-order visual processing without an adequate higher-order representation or confidence signal in blindsight;
- motor impairment without an accurate higher-order representation of impairment in anosognosia;
- movement production without an appropriate higher-order agency representation in anarchic hand;
- nociceptive representation without the higher-order self-related and affective organisation characteristic of ordinary suffering;
- contralesional processing without sufficient higher-order availability for report or control in neglect.

Higher-order theory does not by itself settle the exact neural implementation, and some applications above remain programmatic. But it directly explains why first-order processing and subjective awareness need not degrade together. The article’s claim that identity theory would expect both to degrade together therefore attacks an undifferentiated identity theory that few contemporary physicalists need accept. Higher-order theories were explicitly developed to account for variation in subjective awareness while relevant first-order information remains present. [Cell+1](https://www.cell.com/trends/cognitive-sciences/fulltext/S1364-6613%2811%2900105-7)

## 4.3 Global-workspace and recurrent-processing accounts

A global-workspace account predicts that local or specialised processing may continue when broadcasting, recurrent integration or access to flexible report and control is impaired. Blindsight and some neglect phenomena are near-paradigm cases for that distinction. A recurrent-processing account can similarly distinguish feedforward feature extraction from the recurrent activity required for stable perceptual awareness.

These theories predict a multi-stage and multi-component architecture. Therefore, showing that different stages fail separately is not a residual fact that only an interface model can explain. It is close to what these accounts were designed to predict.

The article mentions sophisticated functionalism abstractly but does not test its six proposed channels against any developed functionalist architecture. A serious comparison would ask whether “perceptual,” “affective,” “self-model,” “motor,” “bilateral” and “attentional” functions correspond to:

- independently measured latent variables;
- distinct computational operations;
- distinct network interventions;
- or merely convenient headings imposed on heterogeneous cases.

# 5. Is the functionalist concession carried through consistently?

**No.**

The concession is clear in four places:

- the opening says both functional-subsystem and interface readings fit;
- the identity-theory section admits that developed functionalism predicts the pattern;
- the dualism section calls the result compatibility-grade;
- the bidirectional-interaction section says the evidence does not establish mental causation.

Those are appropriate calibrations. [topics/neurological-dissociations-as-interface-architecture](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/neurological-dissociations-as-interface-architecture/)

But they are undermined by the rest of the article:

1. **The table labels directions metaphysically.** It does not say “visual processing → report/global access”; it says “brain → consciousness” and “consciousness → brain.”
2. **The body repeatedly treats the interface reading as an empirical description.** Information is said to be delivered to consciousness; consciousness receives false reports; conscious intention issues motor selections; bilateral connections bind material into an already-unified experiencer.
3. **The composite diagram places consciousness outside the brain-side components.** A functionalist alternative is not diagrammed, despite supposedly sharing the structure.
4. **The six components are called independently confirmed.** Under the functionalist concession, what has been confirmed is at most a collection of dissociable functions and dependencies, not their status as interface components.
5. **The “For Interface Architecture” section claims support for interface friction.** If developed functionalism makes the same prediction, the clinical pattern cannot support interface friction over ordinary component failure unless an additional discriminating prediction is supplied.
6. **The epiphenomenalism section relies on the descending-channel interpretation that the closing section says the dissociations do not establish.**
7. **The relation-to-tenets section says the evidence settles that “coupling is multi-channel.”** That formulation remains ontologically loaded. # 6. Identity theory and the target of criticism

The section “Against Identity Theory” should be substantially rewritten.

The conditional “if consciousness were identical to neural processing, damage should degrade both together” is not a consequence of contemporary identity theory in general. An identity theorist can identify:

- visual experience with a particular recurrent or globally integrated neural state;
- pain affect with one distributed neural configuration;
- agency experience with predictive and comparator processes;
- awareness of deficit with higher-order monitoring networks.

Damage may then abolish the identified conscious state while leaving other neural processing intact. That is not an embarrassment for identity theory; it is what a selective neural identity predicts.

The article partly acknowledges this by saying it targets only “naive identity.” But a section refuting a position immediately conceded to be naive does little dialectical work. Worse, it allows the surrounding prose to imply that the dissociations put pressure on physical identity more broadly.

A defensible replacement would be:

> These dissociations reject any one-dimensional psychoneural identity according to which consciousness simply tracks total processing capacity. They do not challenge identities between particular conscious states and particular organised neural states.

That claim is modest but genuine.

# 7. Tenet-protective bracketing

## Dualism

The article now says dualism is not established, but it continues to use a dualist ontology as the default grammar of the empirical sections. “Consciousness receives,” “consciousness commands” and “already-unified consciousness” are not neutral descriptions. The concession is therefore bracketed rather than integrated: the rival is admitted at the end while the dualist reading structures every explanation.

## Bidirectional interaction

The descending direction is not independently found in alien hand. It is inferred by assuming that ordinary action selection is performed by consciousness and then treating disruption of action selection as disruption of conscious causation. The motor-selection dependency explicitly admits that a conscious selector is a tenet-driven commitment, not a result of the neuroscience. [concepts/motor-selection](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/motor-selection/)

## Minimal quantum interaction

The bandwidth paragraph has two problems.

First, the modern approximately 10-bits-per-second estimate is associated with Zheng and Meister’s 2025 analysis of behavioural throughput, not established by the Zimmermann and Nørretranders references cited here. The Map’s newer bandwidth article now cites Zheng and Meister directly, making the synthesis’s sourcing stale. [Cell+1](https://www.cell.com/neuron/abstract/S0896-6273%2824%2900808-0)

Second, behavioural information throughput is not a measure of the causal bandwidth of a mind–brain interface. It does not show that conscious causation is quantum, minimal or confined to the six proposed components. The inference is an analogy laid on top of the data.

## No Many Worlds

The Many-Worlds paragraph has no evidential connection to the clinical cases. An Everettian can treat the patient’s lesion, symptoms and examination results as determinate relative to the observer’s branch and perform exactly the same neuropsychological inference. Branch-relative facts remain diagnostically usable. Nothing about the identification of dissociable functions presupposes collapse or a metaphysically unique world.

This paragraph appears solely because the article must be related to every tenet. It should be removed or explicitly marked as having no bearing on interpretation of the evidence.

## Limits to Occam’s Razor

It is fair to say that nature need not implement the simplest functional architecture. But the article’s proposed symmetry is incomplete. Functionalism pays for differentiated neural and computational organisation. Interactionist dualism pays for that same organisation **plus** psychophysical bridge relations, a rule assigning channel direction and an account of why particular neural configurations couple to a nonphysical subject.

The clinical evidence does not show that both theories incur the same explanatory cost. Nor does a preference for parsimony predict a one-channel brain; distributed physicalists already expect substantial functional complexity.

# 10. Final evaluation

The article should not be discarded. It has the basis of a useful synthesis, provided its thesis is changed from:

> Neurological dissociations empirically map six channels of a mind–brain interface.

to:

> Neurological dissociations reveal several partially separable functional dimensions that any adequate theory of consciousness—dualist or physicalist—must model.

That neutral result is substantial. It constrains theories that treat awareness, affect, bodily self-knowledge, agency, attention and integration as one indivisible capacity. It also creates a useful research programme: determine whether the apparent dimensions are genuinely independent, identify their neural and computational organisation, and seek interventions whose outcomes differ between production and interface models.

What it does **not** currently provide is affirmative evidence for interactionist dualism. The article’s closing compatibility grade is therefore correct, but the article has not yet been rewritten so that every section obeys it.
