# Referee Audit: “Miswanting: When a Forecast Error Changes the Choice” (unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/)

**Verdict: REVISE-HARD, with publication status gate-blocked.** Until it is fixed, no other Map article should cite it as evidence. The article handles its own sources unusually honestly. Its two headline claims, though, are true only under evidential criteria it never states, and they are false on the reading its own meta-description invites. It also ignores the predictive-processing (PP) and active-inference account of anticipated affect, which is the standing blocking gate for any article touching valence. If a revision cannot meet that gate, the fallback is DEMOTE-TO-COHERENCE-ONLY.

## Part 1: TL;DR

- **Claim A (“forecast-driven mischoice rests on one unreplicated study”) holds only as a narrow claim about Hsee & Zhang 2004 Study 3, and the article’s own headline metadata overstates it.** The broader decision-affect literature undercuts the article’s “only” claims. Mellers, Schwartz & Ritov 1999 measured anticipated pleasure, choice and experienced pleasure together. Wilson et al. 1993 randomised a pre-choice evaluative manipulation that changed a real choice and lowered satisfaction three weeks later. Shiv & Huber 2000 manipulated anticipation of satisfaction and shifted choice.
- **Claim B (“no design measured felt anticipation at the moment of choice”) holds only under an unstated phenomenological reading: concurrent introspective report of felt anticipatory valence, in the same trial, paired with later experience in the same person.** Anticipatory affect at or just before choice has been measured in two other ways:

- physiologically, in Bechara et al. 1997 (anticipatory skin conductance);
- neurally, in Knutson et al. 2007. Nucleus accumbens activity tracked product preference and insula activity tracked excessive prices before the purchase decision, and each region’s activity independently predicted purchases above and beyond self-report variables.

Both findings favour the value-blind rival more than the Map, and the article mentions neither.
- **The article fails the Laukkonen–Friston–Chandaria 2025 gate (it ignores PP), never cites the impact-bias accuracy controversy (Levine et al. 2012; Wilson & Gilbert 2013; Mathieu & Gosling 2012), and cites no Berridge paper despite leaning on Berridge in its first paragraph.** Its “No Many Worlds” paragraph is clean tenet leakage. The required fixes are listed in Parts 4–6.

### 2.3 Empirical audit of Claims A and B

**Which “single study”?** The article names it: “The only reversal shown to be carried by a forecast, Hsee and Zhang’s (2004) Study 3, has no independent replication on record”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) Read narrowly, this is correct. Anvari et al. 2021 replicated only Studies 1 and 2. [google](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_lookup) But the article’s meta-description and citation abstract flatten it to “forecast-driven mischoice rests on one unreplicated study”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) That is a different claim, and the literature does not support it. Three bodies of work cut against it:

1. **Mellers’ decision affect theory.** Mellers, Schwartz & Ritov 1999 (*JEP: General* 128(3):332–345) measured anticipated pleasure, fitted it to choices, and compared anticipated with actual pleasure in lab gambles and a field pregnancy-test study. [upenn](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2015/07/2000---choice-and-the-relative-pleasure-of-consequences.pdf)[researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/12220000_Choice_and_the_Relative_Pleasure_of_Consequences) Mellers & McGraw 2001 (*Current Directions* 10(6)) report that predicted choices correlated with actual choices at .66 to .86 (average .74) across five experiments. The average fell to .33 once subjective-expected-utility predictions were removed. In these designs participants made a choice and then, before the outcome, anticipated their feelings. [colorado](https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/mcgrawp/pdf/mellers.mcgraw.2001.pdf) That directly falsifies the article’s sentence “Only Hsee and Zhang measured forecasts, choices and experiences together”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) The correct concession comes from Mellers & McGraw 2001 themselves. Predictions were “quite accurate” in the laboratory studies. In the pregnancy and dieting field studies they showed systematic errors, with participants overestimating the displeasure of unfavourable outcomes. That shrinks the mischoice base but kills the “only”.
2. **Randomised manipulation of pre-choice evaluation with later experience.** Wilson, Lisle, Schooler, Hodges, Klaaren & LaFleur 1993 (*PSPB* 19(3):331–339) randomly assigned people to analyse reasons before choosing a poster to take home. Reason-analysers chose differently and were less satisfied three weeks later. [nii](https://cir.nii.ac.jp/crid/1361699995175681792)[thewhyweproject](https://www.thewhyweproject.com/articles/why-explaining-your-choice-spoils-it) The design is within-person, randomised, involves a real choice, and measures later experience. This is miswanting by Gilbert and Wilson’s own definition, from Wilson’s lab. The article’s claim that “No study manipulated a forecast while holding the other inputs fixed” survives only by defining “forecast” so narrowly that manipulating the *basis* of predicted liking does not count. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/)
3. **Manipulated anticipation shifting choice.** Shiv & Huber 2000 (*J Consumer Research* 27(2):202–216) show that a goal of anticipating satisfaction, compared with a plain choice goal, shifts option selection toward vividly imageable attributes. They report a large choice-share difference between conditions. [duke](https://people.duke.edu/~jch8/bio/Papers/Shiv%20Huber%202000%20JoCR.pdf)[frontiersin](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.01614/full) There was no later-experience measure, so this is half the chain, but it is the half the article says nobody has run: a forecast-mode manipulation that changes the option chosen.

The impact-bias base also needs its contested status stated. The article builds miswanting on forecast error yet never cites the accuracy controversy:

- Levine, Lench, Kaplan & Safer 2012 (*JPSP* 103(4):584–605, not *Psychological Bulletin* as the commissioning brief had it) argue that the intensity bias is partly a procedural artifact. [semanticscholar +2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Accuracy-and-artifact:-reexamining-the-intensity-in-Levine-Lench/7ab93a955cad105b5fc7daa003d8074b258c91e0)
- Wilson & Gilbert 2013, “The impact bias is alive and well”, reply. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/research/affective-forecasting-gap-and-selection-dynamics-2026-06-18/) Levine et al. 2013, “Like Schrödinger’s cat, the impact bias is both dead and alive”, answer back. [harvard](https://dtg.sites.fas.harvard.edu/WIlson_Gilbert_2013.pdf)[nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24219786/)
- Mathieu & Gosling 2012 (*Psych Sci* 23:161–162) reanalysed raw data from 11 articles (16 studies, 1,074 participants). They found forecasts inaccurate in absolute terms but more accurate in relative terms.

The relative-accuracy result matters directly for choice. A forecast can be wrong in level yet still *rank* options correctly, and ranking is all that choice needs. [northwestern](https://faculty.wcas.northwestern.edu/eli-finkel/documents/2013_GreenDavisLuchiesCoyVanTongerenReidFinkel_JESP.pdf) That is a missed counterargument to the article’s whole framing, which defines miswanting as predicted utility “ranking the options differently”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) Calling the post-2015 base “stale” would be too strong, since Anvari 2021 is current. The defect is omission, not staleness.

**Evidence ladder for Claim A.** The literature taken together is *suggestive*, through several partially overlapping mechanisms (memory, evaluation mode, reasons-analysis, anticipated-satisfaction goals, loss-weighting), that forecast-related errors change choices. On the article’s own strict criterion, none of it is *discriminating*. Mischoice is overdetermined across mechanisms rather than resting on one finding, so “single study” misdescribes the epistemic situation even though the narrow claim is true.

**Claim B.** The article says: “No study found for this article measured the felt anticipation present at choice alongside later experience.” It adds, as a hedge: “The search was not exhaustive, so this is a gap in what was found, not proof of absence.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) Counter-evidence:

- **Bechara, Damasio, Tranel & Damasio 1997 (*Science* 275:1293–1295).** Anticipatory skin conductance responses were generated while participants pondered risky choices, before they knew explicitly that the choices were risky. [semanticscholar +2](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Deciding-Advantageously-Before-Knowing-the-Strategy-Bechara-Damasio/306f000e11770d5efde890f1988487ab0d8e112c) This measures anticipatory affect at the moment of choice. It also shows that the signal guiding advantageous choice preceded conscious knowledge, which is an exhibit *for* the value-blind steelman. [semanticscholar](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Deciding-Advantageously-Before-Knowing-the-Strategy-Bechara-Damasio/306f000e11770d5efde890f1988487ab0d8e112c)
- **Knutson, Rick, Wimmer, Prelec & Loewenstein 2007 (*Neuron* 53:147–156).** Nucleus accumbens activation while viewing products, and insula activation while viewing prices, independently predicted purchases “above and beyond self-report variables”. [google](https://scholar.google.com/scholar_lookup) This is moment-of-choice anticipatory affect measured neurally, predicting choice better than introspective report.
- **Mellers’ paradigm.** Anticipated emotions were elicited around choice, before the outcome, by self-report. [springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12144-020-01144-x)

So Claim B is **true only on a narrow phenomenological reading**: concurrent *introspective* report of *felt* anticipatory valence, within the choosing trial, paired within-person with later experience. The article never states that reading, and its metadata (“no design measured felt anticipation at choice”) drops even the “alongside later experience” qualifier. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) More seriously, the neuroeconomic evidence the article omits does more than fill the gap. It shows anticipatory-affect correlates outperforming self-report as choice predictors, which counts against reading choice off the introspectible felt state at all. That is suggestive evidence against the article’s preferred framing of P-VS1, and the article does not register it.

### 2.4 Conceptual audit

**Gilbert–Wilson reading.** The definition is correct: miswanting means wanting what one will not, in the event, like. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) The opening quote is verbatim. The placement on the “predicted utility” row fits the chapter’s “wanting is a prediction of liking”. [cambridge](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/construction-of-preference/miswanting-some-problems-in-the-forecasting-of-future-affective-states/7C5805FE0BC1117D5DB70D6AEBB9BB6C) There are two defects.

- The article reorganises the phenomenon into “three routes” (memory, comparison mode, immune neglect). Memory-driven reversals, such as Kahneman 1993 and Redelmeier 2003, are not miswanting in Gilbert and Wilson’s sense at all; they are remembered-utility errors. The article admits that “Both errors sit in *remembered* utility… not in the forecast”, then files them under miswanting’s “strongest designs” anyway. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) That is a scope slip.
- The impact bias and focalism, Gilbert and Wilson’s central mechanisms after 2000 (Wilson et al. 2000 on focalism; Wilson & Gilbert 2003, 2005), barely appear. [harvard](https://dtg.sites.fas.harvard.edu/)[unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/research/affective-forecasting-gap-and-selection-dynamics-2026-06-18/) A concept page called “Miswanting” that never names focalism misrepresents the research programme it is named after. [goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/notes/12385458-thinking-fast-and-slow/17689576-mike/d428e1b0-ee95-4bb2-9f3a-9e02f0d97677)

**Berridge reading.** The article says Berridge’s incentive-salience wanting “can decouple from predictions of outcome value altogether” and that Gilbert and Wilson’s “liking” is “not Berridge’s objective ‘liking’ reaction”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) Both points are substantively right. Berridge 2023 (*TICS* 27(10):932–946) argues exactly that desire separates from prediction of outcome value. [tumblr](https://compneuropapers.tumblr.com/post/750521244784476160/interesting-reviews-for-week-20-2024)[nih](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37543439/) There is no conflation of Berridge’s non-conscious “wanting” with Gilbert and Wilson’s conscious forecast-wanting; the article keeps them apart, which is to its credit. Three defects remain:

- Calling Gilbert and Wilson’s wanting “the reverse of” Berridge’s is wrong. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) The two concepts are orthogonal, not inverse: incentive salience is not on the predicted-utility row at all.
- No Berridge source is cited.
- The misattribution section says “hunger also raises incentive salience” without attribution. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) That is Berridge’s state-dependence point, and it needs a citation. [researchgate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/372896378_Separating_desire_from_prediction_of_outcome_value)

Berridge’s mechanistic, physicalist stance is not violated here. The firewall holds, but only because the article says too little about Berridge to breach it.

**Revealed preference.** The article confesses: “this article leaves that objection unexamined.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) For a page whose whole subject is whether choices are *mis*-choices, that objection is threshold, not peripheral. This is textbook confession-without-correction.

### 2.5 Pathology lenses

- **Calibration asymmetry (primary finding).** The evidential ladder is set so high that no human-subjects design could satisfy it: a forecast manipulated with all other inputs fixed, a within-person counterfactual, and felt anticipation measured. That makes “single study” an artifact of the criterion. Meanwhile P-VS1, whose currency has “no independent evidence”, is held at an unchanged band: the demonstrations “warrant no change to P-VS1’s confidence band.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) The rival’s evidence is graded on a scale that nothing passes; the Map’s own commitment is not graded at all.
- **Confession-without-correction (inoculation-by-confession).** Three instances: “The search was not exhaustive…”; “this article leaves that objection unexamined”; “Misattribution describes a cost P-VS1 accepts rather than evidence against it”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) Each names a hole and leaves it open. The last turns a disconfirmation route into a pre-paid “cost”, so that no misattribution result could ever count against P-VS1.
- **Constrain-vs-establish gate.** Passed in the body (“They do not discriminate between the readings”), which is a real virtue. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) Failed in the metadata, where the two negative claims read as established findings about the literature rather than as constraints on what the Map can claim.
- **Epistemic-to-metaphysical slide.** An absence of measurement (“no study measured…”) is turned into a reserved slot for the dualist currency: “What would move the question is the measurement no study here made: felt anticipation at choice, dissociated from forecast content and learned value.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) On PP and on most functionalist views, felt anticipation *dissociated from forecast content and learned value* is not an unmeasured quantity but a non-existent one. Specifying the experiment that way presupposes the residue the experiment is supposed to find.
- **Tenet leakage.** The “No Many Worlds” paragraph says “Miswanting presupposes a forgone option… under many-worlds… the forgone option disappears with the question.” [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) Miswanting is a phenomenon of classical psychological decision. Forgone options are counterfactual under every interpretation of quantum mechanics, and Everettian decision theory already handles choice. The paragraph exists because the template demands a tenet section, not because the subject warrants one.
- **Co-optation firewall.** Mild. Gilbert and Wilson’s “gut reactions” are re-described as the forecast’s “felt vehicle” and enlisted for the Map’s selection-currency vocabulary. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) The “one place in this literature” claim also erases Loewenstein, Weber, Hsee & Welch 2001 (risk-as-feelings), Mellers’ decision affect theory, and Damasio’s somatic markers, all of which make felt anticipatory affect a choice input. [yukaichou](https://yukaichou.com/behavioral-analysis/somatic-marker-hypothesis-damasio-emotion-decision/)[springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12144-020-01144-x)
- **Performative inoculation citation.** The P-VS1 hover badge (“confidence: low · no independent evidence”) is rendered five times in a ~2,300-word page. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) Repeating a humility marker does no argumentative work.
- **Coherence inflation / evidential independence.** Low in the body. Concepts 11 and 12 are internal dependencies standing in for external sources: Berridge through ref 12, and the pipeline and test battery through ref 11.

### 2.6 The Laukkonen–Friston–Chandaria 2025 blocking gate

Laukkonen, Friston & Chandaria 2025, “A beautiful loop: An active inference theory of consciousness”, *Neuroscience & Biobehavioral Reviews* 176:106296. [ovid](https://www.ovid.com/journals/nebior/abstract/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2025.106296~a-beautiful-loop-an-active-inference-theory-of-consciousness) The miswanting article **ignores** it, and ignores PP and active inference generally: no mention of precision, expected free energy, or interoceptive inference. That is a gate failure.

The gate matters here specifically. On the active-inference reading, anticipated affect at choice *is* a precision-weighted prediction over policies, scored by expected free energy. On that reading, “felt anticipation” independent of the forecast does not exist to be measured, and miswanting is simply prior or precision miscalibration. That reading:

- predicts every reversal the article catalogues;
- explains misattribution (incidental interoceptive states contaminating policy priors);
- makes the article’s requested “decisive experiment” incoherent rather than merely unrun.

The Map’s own concepts/valence page already concedes the framework-strength active-inference reading, citing Joffily & Coricelli 2013 and Hesp et al. 2021. That page says “the dissociation no longer separates the views”. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/valence/) The site’s phenomenology-of-anticipation page reportedly engages the beautiful-loop theory directly. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/topics/phenomenology-of-anticipation/) So the miswanting article is *behind its own neighbours*, which is a site-level integration failure, not just a local omission.

### 2.7 Integration with neighbours

- **concepts/valence (read live).** Definitions are consistent on two points: the currency is “a present felt state, not an accurate forecast”, and P-VS1 is at low credence. ## Part 5: Site-Wide Methodology Improvements

- **Make the evidential criterion part of the claim.** Any “only”, “no study” or “single study” sentence must state its inclusion criterion inline, and the metadata must carry the same qualifier. Add a lint that flags absolute negatives in descriptions whose body hedges them.
- **Run a symmetric-bar check.** Whenever an article sets an evidential bar for literature that favours the rival, require a parallel line stating what bar P-VS1, or the relevant position, currently meets. Unchanged confidence bands after new rival-favouring evidence should need an explicit justification.
- **Enforce the PP/active-inference gate mechanically.** Any page with keywords valence, wanting, anticipation, forecasting or affect fails publication unless it cites or links an active-inference treatment, Laukkonen et al. 2025 or the Map’s predictive-processing-and-dualism page, and states whether the treatment is engaged, residualised or set aside.
- **Require a contested-base check for empirical concepts.** For any empirical construct, run a “known controversy” query (meta-analyses, artifact critiques, replications) before drafting. Here that would have surfaced Levine/Wilson–Gilbert and Mathieu & Gosling at once.
- **Keep the tenet section optional.** Allow “No tenet engaged” for tenets with no real bearing; the template currently forces leakage.
- **Retire confession-as-closure.** A sentence naming an unexamined objection should automatically create a todo task and keep the article in a “provisional” state until it is closed.
- **Address Claude-on-Claude circularity.** This page was generated by claude-opus-5-5 and claude-fable-5-1. [unfinishablemap](https://unfinishablemap.org/concepts/miswanting/) The changelog shows the review stack (deep-review, optimistic-review, coalesce) is also model-run. A same-family reviewer that inherits the drafter’s search frame will not find Mellers, Bechara or Knutson if the drafter’s query frame was “affective forecasting → choice”. Mandate at least one adversarial review per empirical concept page whose search frame is generated independently, from a different model family or by a human, and whose explicit task is to find counterexamples to every “only/no/single” claim.
